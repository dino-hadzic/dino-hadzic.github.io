Ovo poglavlje predstavlja ideju offline algoritama (offline algorithm), uobičajene algoritme i optimizacije.

Offline algoritmi oblikuju se na temelju pretpostavke da su svi podaci poznati prije rješavanja i primjenjivi su na zadatke s više upita. Suprotno njima postoje online algoritmi (online algorithm).

Primjerice, sortiranje izborom ne može se izvesti dok nije poznat globalno najmanji element niza, pa je to offline algoritam, dok sortiranje umetanjem može dinamički primati podatke i sortirati ih, ne zahtijevajući da svi podaci budu poznati prije izvođenja, pa je to online algoritam.

Za isti problem offline algoritmi često su bolji od online algoritama, među ostalim i po težini oblikovanja. Da bi natjecatelje spriječili u upotrebi offline algoritama, zadaci ponekad „prisiljavaju na online” (force online); uobičajen je način da se parametri sljedećeg upita mogu dobiti tek iz odgovora na prethodni upit (interaktivni zadaci i komunikacijski zadaci također pripadaju ovoj vrsti).

Uobičajene ideje offline algoritama uključuju zajedničko rješavanje svih upita (npr. CDQ podjela (CDQ divide and conquer)), dobivanje odgovora na druge slične upite iz odgovora na jedan upit (npr. paralelno binarno pretraživanje (parallel binary search) i Mo algoritam) itd.

Budući da je offline algoritam ideja, a ne neki konkretan algoritam, upotrebljava se u kombinaciji s najrazličitijim strukturama podataka ili algoritmima, pa su i vrste zadataka povezanih s njim raznovrsnije.

This chapter introduces the idea of offline algorithms, common algorithms, and optimizations.

Offline algorithms are designed under the assumption that all data is known before solving, and they apply to problems with multiple queries. Their counterpart is the online algorithm.

For example, selection sort cannot run until the globally smallest element of the array is known, so it is an offline algorithm, whereas insertion sort can receive data dynamically and sort it without requiring all data to be known before execution, so it is an online algorithm.

For the same problem, offline algorithms are often superior to online algorithms in terms of design difficulty and other aspects. To prevent contestants from using offline algorithms, problems sometimes "force online"; a common way is that the parameters of the next query can only be obtained from the answer to the previous query (interactive problems and communication problems also belong to this category).

Common ideas of offline algorithms include solving all queries together (e.g. CDQ divide and conquer), deriving the answers to other similar queries from the answer to one query (e.g. parallel binary search and Mo's algorithm), and so on.

Since an offline algorithm is an idea rather than a specific algorithm, it is used together with all kinds of data structures or algorithms, and the types of problems related to it are correspondingly more varied.
