Uvod

Teorija prstena (ring theory) proučava najrazličitije prstene.

Sadržaj teorije prstena obrađen u ovom članku neraskidivo je povezan s teorijom djeljivosti iz teorije brojeva. Najprije, analogno normalnim podgrupama iz teorije grupa, uvodimo jezgru homomorfizma prstena, koja se naziva idealom prstena; to je zapravo poopćenje pojma broja iz teorije brojeva na opće prstene. Zatim, poopćavanjem pojmova iz prstena cijelih brojeva – prostih brojeva, Euklidova algoritma, rastava na proste faktore itd. – na opće prstene dobivamo pojmove različitih vrsta integralnih domena.

Mnogi rezultati iz teorije brojeva i dalje vrijede u drugim uobičajenim prstenima. Može se reći da se dio teorije prstena bavi upravo pitanjem vrijede li ti rezultati iz teorije brojeva i u općim prstenima; ako ne vrijede, kakva ograničenja treba nametnuti prstenu da bi vrijedili.

Oznake

Kad to ne dovodi do zabune, u ovom ćemo članku izostavljati znak množenja u prstenu, a prsten \((R,+,\cdot)\) pisat ćemo kao prsten \(R\). U prstenu \(R\) neutralni element zbrajanja naziva se i nulom i označava s \(0\); neutralni element množenja naziva se i jedinicom (jediničnim elementom) i označava s \(1\).

Definicija prstena u ovom članku ne zahtijeva jedinicu

Napominjemo da definicija prstena u ovom članku ne zahtijeva postojanje jedinice. Neki tekstovi u definiciji prstena zahtijevaju jedinicu; tada formulacije nekih rezultata iz ovog članka treba malo prilagoditi. Primjerice, u ovom se članku ideal može definirati preko potprstena, dok ga u drugim tekstovima može biti potrebno definirati preko aditivne podgrupe.

Ideali

Analogno slučaju grupa, mogu se uvesti pojmovi potprstena i homomorfizma prstena.

Potprsten

Za prsten \((R,+,\cdot)\) i njegov podskup \(S\), ako je i \((S,+,\cdot)\) prsten, kažemo da je \(S\) potprsten (subring) prstena \(R\).

Primjer: prsten cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\)

Za svaki cijeli broj \(n\) skup \(n\mathbf Z=\{nk:k\in\mathbf Z\}\) potprsten je prstena \(\mathbf Z\).

Homomorfizam prstena

Za prstene \((R,+,\cdot)\) i \((S,\oplus,\odot)\), ako preslikavanje \(\pi\) čuva zbrajanje i množenje prstena, tj. ako za sve \(r_1,r_2\in R\) vrijedi \(\pi(r_1+r_2)=\pi(r_1)\oplus\pi(r_2)\) i \(\pi(r_1\cdot r_2)=\pi(r_1)\odot\pi(r_2)\), kažemo da je preslikavanje \(\pi:R\rightarrow S\) homomorfizam (homomorphism) iz prstena \(R\) u prsten \(S\).

Slučaj kad definicija prstena zahtijeva jedinicu

Ako definicija prstena zahtijeva postojanje jedinice, onda se u definiciji homomorfizma prstena često zahtijeva i da se jedinica preslikava u jedinicu. Za homomorfizme između nenul prstena s jedinicom taj dodatni zahtjev samo osigurava da homomorfizam ne preslikava cijeli prsten s jedinicom u nulu.

Primjer: prsten cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) (nastavak)

Preslikavanje redukcije modulo proizvoljan nenul cijeli broj \(n\), tj. \(\pi:\mathbf Z\rightarrow\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\), gdje je \(\pi(a)=\bar a\), homomorfizam je prstena.

Rasprava o jezgri i slici homomorfizma grupa može se gotovo doslovno prenijeti ovamo. (Relativna) veličina slike homomorfizma određuje je li homomorfizam surjekcija, a trivijalnost jezgre homomorfizma određuje je li homomorfizam injekcija. Jezgra homomorfizma prstena definira se ovako:

Jezgra homomorfizma

Jezgra (kernel) homomorfizma \(\pi:R\rightarrow S\) iz prstena \(R\) u prsten \(S\) skup je \(\{r\in R:\pi(r)=0\}\), u oznaci \(\ker\pi\), gdje je \(0\) neutralni element zbrajanja u \(S\).

Očito su jezgra i slika homomorfizma prstena potprsteni. Obratno, nije svaki potprsten jezgra nekog homomorfizma prstena. Potprsteni koji mogu biti jezgra homomorfizma prstena nazivaju se idealima prstena.

Ideal

Za prsten \(R\) i njegov potprsten \(I\) kažemo da je \(I\) u prstenu \(R\)

  • lijevi ideal (left ideal) ako za sve \(r\in R\) vrijedi \(rI\subseteq I\), gdje je \(rI=\{ra:a\in I\}\);
  • desni ideal (right ideal) ako za sve \(r\in R\) vrijedi \(Ir\subseteq I\), gdje je \(Ir=\{ar:a\in I\}\);
  • ideal (ideal) ako je \(I\) i lijevi ideal prstena \(R\) i desni ideal prstena \(R\).

Ovdje se zahtijeva da ideal \(I\) bude zatvoren i na množenje slijeva i na množenje zdesna elementima prstena \(R\). Taj je uvjet prirodan. Naime, elementi ideala u homomorfizmu prstena preslikavaju se u nulu, a umnožak bilo kojeg elementa s nulom, bilo slijeva bilo zdesna, trebao bi biti jednak nuli; to je upravo tražena zatvorenost. Osim toga, budući da je aditivna struktura prstena Abelova grupa, svaka je podgrupa normalna; multiplikativna je struktura prstena pak vrlo rudimentarna i ne nameće podstrukturama dodatna ograničenja. To pokazuje da je uvjet zatvorenosti na množenje slijeva i zdesna ujedno i dovoljan.

Primjer: prsten cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) (nastavak)

Kao primjer, prije spomenuti potprsten \(n\mathbf Z\) zapravo je ideal prstena \(\mathbf Z\). To je skup svih višekratnika broja \(n\). Umnožak višekratnika broja \(n\) s bilo kojim cijelim brojem opet je višekratnik broja \(n\). Zapravo su svi ideali prstena \(\mathbf Z\) tog oblika; takav se prsten naziva prstenom glavnih ideala. U općem prstenu neki ideali nisu skup višekratnika nekog elementa; postojanje takvih općih prstena upravo je bilo prvotni poticaj za proučavanje ideala (a ne jednostavno višekratnika)4.

Kvocijentni prsten

Kao i kod grupa, na temelju ideala prstena može se na skupu svih susjednih klasa (u smislu aditivne grupe) definirati kvocijentni prsten (quotient ring). Promotrimo skup

\[ R/I=\{a+I:a\in R\}, \]

gdje je susjedna klasa \(a+I=\{a+b:b\in I\}\). Može se dokazati da su, ako i samo ako je \(I\) ideal, operacije

\[ \begin{aligned} (a+I)+(b+I)&=(a+b)+I,\\ (a+I)(b+I)&=(ab)+I \end{aligned} \]

dobro definirane, tj. da rezultat tih operacija ne ovisi o izboru predstavnika susjednih klasa. Uz te operacije \(R/I\) čini prsten. Opet u skladu sa slučajem grupa, može se dokazati prvi teorem o izomorfizmu (first isomorphism theorem) za prstene te postoji prirodni homomorfizam prstena u njegov kvocijentni prsten. To pokazuje da ideali prstena i normalne podgrupe grupa igraju istu ulogu u homomorfizmima odgovarajućih struktura.

Prvi teorem o izomorfizmu

Neka je \(\pi:R\rightarrow S\) homomorfizam iz prstena \(R\) u prsten \(S\). Tada je \(\ker\pi\) ideal prstena \(R\), a \(R/\ker\pi\cong\pi(R)\) potprsten je prstena \(S\).

Prirodni homomorfizam

Za prsten \(R\) i njegov ideal \(I\) preslikavanje \(\pi:R\rightarrow R/I\) zadano s \(\pi(r)=r+I\) surjektivni je homomorfizam iz \(R\) u \(R/I\) i naziva se prirodnim homomorfizmom (natural homomorphism) iz prstena \(R\) u kvocijentni prsten \(R/I\).

Primjer: prsten cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) (nastavak)

Kao primjer, prsten \(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\) klasa kongruencije cijelih brojeva modulo \(n\) kvocijentni je prsten prstena \(\mathbf Z\) po njegovu idealu \(n\mathbf Z\). To ujedno objašnjava značenje oznake \(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\). Gore spomenuto preslikavanje modulo \(n\), \(\pi:\mathbf Z\rightarrow\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\), upravo je ovdje spomenuto prirodno preslikavanje, a odgovarajuća jezgra upravo je ideal \(n\mathbf Z\).

U slučaju prstena vrijede i ostali teoremi o izomorfizmu.

Drugi teorem o izomorfizmu

Neka prsten \(R\) ima potprsten \(A\) i ideal \(B\). Tada je \(A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}\) također potprsten prstena \(R\), \(A\cap B\) je ideal prstena \(A\), \(B\) je ideal prstena \(A+B\) i vrijedi \((A+B)/B\cong A/(A\cap B)\).

Treći teorem o izomorfizmu

Neka prsten \(R\) ima ideale \(I,J\) takve da je \(I\subseteq J\). Tada je \(J/I\) također ideal prstena \(R/I\) i vrijedi \((R/I)/(J/I)\cong R/J\).

Teorem o korespondenciji

Neka prsten \(R\) ima ideal \(I\). Tada između skupa svih potprstena prstena \(R\) koji sadrže \(I\), \(\mathcal S=\{S:I\subseteq S\subseteq R\}\), i skupa svih potprstena kvocijentnog prstena \(R/I\), \(\mathcal T=\{T:T\text{ is a subring of }R/I\}\), postoji bijekcija \(\varphi:\mathcal S\rightarrow\mathcal T\) koja \(S\in\mathcal S\) preslikava u \(S/I\in\mathcal T\). Ta bijekcija čuva relaciju sadržavanja među potprstenima, a ideali prstena \(R\) uvijek se preslikavaju u ideale prstena \(R/I\).

Ti će teoremi igrati temeljnu ulogu u daljnjoj raspravi o strukturi prstena i ideala.

Operacije s idealima

Na idealima prstena mogu se definirati razne operacije. To je slično tome što se na strukturi djeljivosti cijelih brojeva mogu definirati pojmovi poput najvećeg zajedničkog djelitelja i najmanjeg zajedničkog višekratnika.

Operacije s idealima

Neka prsten \(R\) ima ideale \(I,J\). Mogu se definirati sljedeće operacije:

  • zbroj (sum) ideala: \(I+J=\{a+b:a\in I,b\in J\}\);
  • umnožak (product) ideala: \(IJ=\{\sum_{i=1}^na_ib_i:a_i\in I,b_i\in J\}\), tj. skup svih konačnih zbrojeva umnožaka oblika \(ab\);
  • presjek (intersection) ideala: \(I\cap J\).

Lako se provjeri da su rezultati tih operacija opet ideali prstena.

Primjer: prsten cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) (nastavak)

Promotrimo slučaj prstena cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\). Za ideale \(n\mathbf Z\) i \(m\mathbf Z\) dobivamo

\[ \begin{aligned} n\mathbf Z+m\mathbf Z&=\gcd(m,n)\mathbf Z,\\ (n\mathbf Z)(m\mathbf Z)&=(mn)\mathbf Z,\\ (n\mathbf Z)\cap(m\mathbf Z)&=\mathrm{lcm}(m,n)\mathbf Z. \end{aligned} \]

Općenito, za prsten \(R\) i njegove ideale \(I\) i \(J\) uvijek vrijedi

\[ IJ\subseteq I\cap J\subseteq I,J\subseteq I+J. \]

Pomoću tih definicija kineski teorem o ostatcima za cijele brojeve može se poopćiti na opće prstene. No prije toga treba na opće prstene poopćiti još i pojmove poput prostog broja i relativne prostosti.

Maksimalni ideali

Preko strukture ideala prstena mogu se razumjeti svojstva prstena.

Nenul prsten \(R\) uvijek ima dva trivijalna ideala, \(\{0\}\) i \(R\). Ako je prsten \(R\) k tome komutativan nenul prsten s jedinicom, onda je prsten koji ima samo ta dva ideala nužno polje, i obratno1.

Teorem

Neka je \(R\) komutativan nenul prsten s jedinicom. Tada je \(R\) polje ako i samo ako \(R\) ima samo trivijalne ideale \(\{0\}\) i \(R\).

Dokaz

Ako je \(R\) polje, onda za svaki nenul ideal \(I\) možemo uzeti proizvoljan nenul element \(a\in I\); tada za svaki element polja \(r\in R\) vrijedi \(r=(ra^{-1})a\in (ra^{-1})I\subseteq I\), pa je \(I=R\). Obratno, za svaki \(a\in R\) takav da je \(a\neq 0\) može se provjeriti da je \(aR=\{ar:r\in R\}\) ideal, koji je nužno jednak \(R\), pa postoji \(b\in R\) takav da je \(ab=1\); to pokazuje da \(a\) ima inverz, pa je \(R\) polje.

Uvjet komutativnosti prstena ovdje je nužan; inače bi, da bi prsten bio tijelo, trebalo zahtijevati da su i lijevi i desni ideali trivijalni.

Ovaj se rezultat može poopćiti na slučaj kad sam prsten nije polje. No tada umjesto toga treba promatrati kvocijentne prstene i raspraviti uvjete uz koje je kvocijentni prsten komutativnog nenul prstena s jedinicom polje. Da je kvocijentni prsten \(R/I\) polje znači da kvocijentni prsten \(R/I\) ima samo trivijalne ideale, a prema teoremu o korespondenciji to znači da u izvornom prstenu \(R\) ne postoji ideal strogo između ideala \(I\) po kojem se kvocijentira i izvornog prstena \(R\). Takav ideal \(I\) naziva se maksimalnim idealom.

Maksimalni ideal

Za prsten \(R\) i njegov ideal \(M\), ako je \(M\neq R\) i ako su jedini ideali prstena \(R\) koji sadrže \(M\) upravo \(M\) i \(R\), kažemo da je ideal \(M\) maksimalni ideal (maximal ideal).

Teorem

Neka komutativan nenul prsten s jedinicom \(R\) ima ideal \(M\). Tada je kvocijentni prsten \(R/M\) polje ako i samo ako je \(M\) maksimalni ideal.

Primjer: prsten cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) (nastavak)

Na primjer, ideal \(n\mathbf Z\) prstena cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) maksimalan je ako i samo ako je \(n\) prost broj. Za prost broj \(p\) kvocijentni prsten \(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z\) polje je, koje se označava i s \(\mathbf F_p\).

Nema svaki prsten maksimalni ideal, ali nenul prsten s jedinicom uvijek ima maksimalni ideal.

Teorem (Krull)

Za ideal \(I\neq R\) nenul prstena s jedinicom \(R\) uvijek postoji maksimalni ideal \(M\) prstena \(R\) takav da vrijedi \(I\subseteq M\).

Dokaz

Ideja je iskoristiti Zornovu lemu. Promotrimo skup \(\mathcal S\) svih pravih ideala prstena \(R\) (tj. ideala različitih od \(R\)) koji sadrže \(I\). Budući da je \(I\in\mathcal S\), taj je skup neprazan i s obzirom na relaciju sadržavanja čini parcijalno uređen skup. Za svaki lanac \(J_0\subseteq J_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq J_n\subseteq\cdots\) u njemu neka je \(J\) njihova unija; lako se provjeri da je i to ideal. Štoviše, \(J\neq R\), jer bi inače bilo \(1\in J\), tj. postojao bi \(n\) takav da je \(1\in J_n\), što je u suprotnosti s time da je \(J_n\) pravi ideal. Stoga prema Zornovoj lemi postoji maksimalni ideal \(M\supseteq I\).

Maksimalni ideal analogon je ireducibilnog elementa iz teorije djeljivosti. Naime, relacija sadržavanja među idealima odgovara relaciji djeljivosti među cijelim brojevima; nepostojanje ideala koji bi bio nadskup odgovara nepostojanju faktora koji bi ga dijelio. No pojam maksimalnog ideala širi je od pojma ireducibilnog elementa, jer nisu svi ideali glavni.

Prosti ideali

Uvjet da je nešto polje stroži je od uvjeta da je integralna domena. Ideali koji osiguravaju da je kvocijentni prsten integralna domena nazivaju se prostim idealima; oni su analogon pojma prostog broja iz teorije djeljivosti.

Prosti ideal

Za komutativan prsten \(R\) i njegov ideal \(P\), ako je \(P\neq R\) i ako za proizvoljne elemente prstena \(a,b\in R\) iz \(ab\in P\) uvijek slijedi \(a\in P\) ili \(b\in P\), kažemo da je ideal \(P\) prosti ideal (prime ideal).

Ta definicija na prvi pogled djeluje pomalo neočekivano, ali u usporedbi s definicijom prostog broja i ova je definicija prostog ideala prirodna.

Teorem

Neka komutativan nenul prsten s jedinicom \(R\) ima ideal \(P\). Tada je kvocijentni prsten \(R/P\) integralna domena ako i samo ako je \(P\) prosti ideal.

Dokaz

Za komutativan nenul prsten s jedinicom \(R\) kvocijentni prsten \(R/P\) integralna je domena ako i samo ako \(R/P\) nema djelitelja nule. Označimo susjednu klasu \(a+P\) s \(\bar a\). Da kvocijentni prsten \(R/P\) nema djelitelja nule ekvivalentno je tome da iz \(\bar a\bar b=\bar 0\) uvijek slijedi \(\bar a=\bar 0\) ili \(\bar b=\bar 0\). Prema definiciji kvocijentnog prstena to je ekvivalentno tome da iz \(ab\in P\) uvijek slijedi \(a\in P\) ili \(b\in P\).

U prstenu cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) ideal \(n\mathbf Z\) maksimalan je ako i samo ako je \(n\) prost broj, a \(n\mathbf Z\) prost je ideal ako i samo ako je \(n\) prost broj ili \(n=0\). U općem komutativnom prstenu maksimalni ideal uvijek je i prost, dok obrat, naravno, ne mora vrijediti; to se vidi iz svojstava odgovarajućih kvocijentnih prstena.

Teorem

Neka je \(R\) komutativan nenul prsten s jedinicom. Tada je svaki njegov maksimalni ideal nužno prosti ideal.

Uskoro ćemo vidjeti da obrat vrijedi samo u prstenima s dovoljno dobrim svojstvima, dovoljno sličnima prstenu cijelih brojeva.

Glavni ideali

Analogno pojmu podgrupe, i u raspravi o prstenima često treba promatrati ideal generiran nekim podskupom.

Ideal generiran podskupom

Za nenul prsten s jedinicom \(R\) i njegov neprazan podskup \(A\subseteq R\), ako je \(I\) (s obzirom na relaciju sadržavanja) najmanji među idealima prstena \(R\) koji sadrže \(A\), ideal \(I\) naziva se idealom generiranim podskupom \(A\) (ideal generated by a subset) i označava s \((A)\). Tada se \(A\) naziva generirajućim skupom (generating set) ideala \((A)\).

Glavni ideal

Ideal generiran jednim elementom \(a\in R\) naziva se glavnim idealom (principal ideal) i označava s \((a)\). Tada se \(a\) naziva generatorom (generator) ideala \((a)\).

Za skup \(A\) može se dati konstrukcija ideala koji on generira. Najprije definiramo

\[ \begin{aligned} RA&=\{r_1a_1+\cdots+r_na_n:r_i\in R,a_i\in A,n\in\mathbf N_+\},\\ AR&=\{a_1r_1+\cdots+a_nr_n:r_i\in R,a_i\in A,n\in\mathbf N_+\}. \end{aligned} \]

To su zapravo lijevi odnosno desni ideal generiran skupom \(A\). Tada je ideal generiran podskupom \(A\) upravo \(RAR\). Za komutativne prstene sve su tako definirane strukture jednake.

Svi ideali \(n\mathbf Z\) prstena cijelih brojeva glavni su ideali; u nastavku ih često označavamo s \((n)\).

Integralne domene

Integralna domena komutativan je nenul prsten s jedinicom i bez djelitelja nule. Taj je pojam upravo poopćenje prstena cijelih brojeva. No tako dobiveni prsten ne mora imati dovoljno dobra svojstva da bi se svaki rezultat teorije djeljivosti cijelih brojeva mogao doslovno prenijeti. Da bi se rezultati iz teorije brojeva mogli poopćiti, na integralnu domenu mogu se nametnuti dodatna ograničenja. Tri su najčešće vrste integralnih domena Euklidovi prsteni, prsteni glavnih ideala i faktorijalni prsteni; svaki je od tih pojmova strogo sadržan u sljedećem.

Relacija djeljivosti

Najprije ovdje poopćavamo odgovarajuće pojmove iz teorije djeljivosti cijelih brojeva na opće komutativne prstene.

Djeljivost

Neka komutativan prsten \(R\) ima elemente \(a,b\in R\). Ako postoji \(x\in R\) takav da je \(a=bx\), kažemo da \(b\) dijeli (divide) \(a\) i pišemo \(b\mid a\). Tada kažemo da je \(b\) djelitelj (divisor) elementa \(a\).

Asociranost

Neka komutativan prsten \(R\) ima elemente \(a,b\in R\). Ako se oni razlikuju samo za invertibilni element, tj. ako postoji invertibilni element \(u\in R\) takav da je \(a=bu\), kažemo da su \(a\) i \(b\) asocirani (associate).

Relacija djeljivosti relacija je parcijalnog uređaja na prstenu, a relacija asociranosti relacija je ekvivalencije na prstenu. Gledano preko ideala, \(a\mid b\) ekvivalentno je s \((b)\subseteq (a)\), a asociranost elemenata \(a\) i \(b\) ekvivalentna je s \((a)=(b)\). Stoga se pri raspravi o elementima prstena obično ne obazire na razliku među asociranim elementima. Slično kao kod cijelih brojeva, najveći zajednički djelitelj elemenata \(a\) i \(b\) komutativnog prstena definira se kao infimum skupa \(\{a,b\}\).

Najveći zajednički djelitelj

Za komutativan prsten \(R\) i njegove elemente \(a,b\in R\), ako postoji nenul element \(d\in R\) koji zadovoljava \(d\mid a\) i \(d\mid b\) te za svaki \(d'\) koji zadovoljava \(d'\mid a\) i \(d'\mid b\) vrijedi \(d'\mid d\), kažemo da je \(d\) najveći zajednički djelitelj (greatest common divisor) elemenata \(a\) i \(b\) i pišemo \(\gcd(a,b)\).

U integralnoj domeni najveći zajednički djelitelj jednoznačno je određen do na asociranost. Daljnja je rasprava ograničena na integralne domene.

U integralnoj domeni može se uvesti i pojam sličan prostom broju. U teoriji cijelih brojeva postoje dvije ekvivalentne definicije prostog broja, ali u općoj integralnoj domeni te dvije definicije odgovaraju različitim pojmovima:

Prosti element

Neka integralna domena \(R\) ima nenul element \(p\in R\). Ako je \((p)\) prosti ideal, tj. ako \(p\) nije invertibilan i iz \(p\mid ab\) uvijek slijedi \(p\mid a\) ili \(p\mid b\), kažemo da je \(p\) prosti element (prime).

Ireducibilni element

Neka integralna domena \(R\) ima nenul element \(r\in R\). Ako \(r\) nije invertibilan i ako za sve \(a,b\in R\) takve da je \(r=ab\) vrijedi da je \(a\) ili \(b\) invertibilan, kažemo da je \(r\) ireducibilni element (irreducible) ili da je \(r\) ireducibilan. Obratno, ako je \(r=ab\) i \(a,b\in R\) nisu invertibilni, kažemo da je \(r\) reducibilan.

Može se pokazati da je glavni ideal \((r)\) koji odgovara ireducibilnom elementu \(r\) nužno maksimalan među svim pravim glavnim idealima prstena; no u općoj integralnoj domeni nisu svi ideali glavni, pa pojmovi ireducibilnog elementa i maksimalnog ideala nisu ekvivalentni.

Analogno dokazu da je u prstenu glavnih ideala svaki prosti ideal maksimalan, općenito se može dokazati sljedeći rezultat:

Teorem

Neka je \(R\) integralna domena. Ako je \(a\in R\) prosti element, onda je \(a\) nužno i ireducibilni element.

Dokaz

Neka je \(r\in R\) prosti element i neka \(a,b\in R\) zadovoljavaju \(r=ab\). Budući da je \(r\) prost, bez smanjenja općenitosti neka vrijedi \(r\mid a\); tada je \(a=cr=cba\). Budući da u integralnoj domeni vrijedi zakon kraćenja, imamo \(1=bc\), pa \(b\) ima inverz \(c\). To pokazuje da je \(r\) ireducibilan.

Obrat ne vrijedi.

Protuprimjer

U prstenu kvadratnih cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-5}]\) element \(3\) ireducibilan je, ali \(9=3\cdot 3=(2+\sqrt{-5})(2-\sqrt{-5})\), pa on nije prost.

Ovdje dajemo dokaz tog protuprimjera; čitatelji koji nisu upoznati s prstenima kvadratnih cijelih brojeva neka najprije pročitaju odjeljak Prsteni kvadratnih cijelih brojeva. Neka je \(N(\cdot)\) norma na prstenu kvadratnih cijelih brojeva. Za svaki rastav \(3=ab\) vrijedi \(N(a)N(b)=N(3)=9\). Ako \(a,b\) nisu invertibilni, onda su \(N(a)\) i \(N(b)\) veći od \(1\), pa nužno vrijedi \(N(a)=N(b)=3\). No u \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-5}]\) nema takvih elemenata, tj. \(x^2+5y^2=3\) nema cjelobrojnih rješenja. To pokazuje da je \(3\) ireducibilan. Što se tiče toga da \(3\) nije prost, treba dokazati da \(3\) ne dijeli \(2\pm\sqrt{-5}\), što je očito.

Euklidovi prsteni

Povezano štivo: (Prošireni) Euklidov algoritam, Bézoutov teorem

Euklidov prsten integralna je domena u kojoj se može provoditi uzastopno dijeljenje s ostatkom (tj. Euklidov algoritam).

Euklidov prsten

Za integralnu domenu \(R\), ako postoji preslikavanje \(N:R\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbf N\) takvo da za sve \(a,b\in R\) uz \(b\neq 0\) postoje \(q,r\in R\) za koje vrijedi \(a=qb+r\) te \(r=0\) ili \(N(r)<N(b)\), kažemo da je integralna domena \(R\) Euklidov prsten (Euclidean domain, ED). Preslikavanje \(N\) naziva se normom (norm) elemenata Euklidova prstena.

Druge ekvivalentne definicije

Definicija usvojena u ovom članku definira normu samo na nenul elementima. Različiti tekstovi mogu različito pristupati definiciji Euklidova prstena. Primjerice, neki tekstovi mogu dodatno definirati \(N(0)=0\); no u dijeljenju s ostatkom koje slijedi vrijednost \(N(0)\) ne koristi se, pa to nije bitno. Nadalje, definicija na Wikipediji zahtijeva i da norma \(N\) zadovoljava svojstvo: za sve nenul \(a,b\in R\) vrijedi \(N(a)\le N(ab)\). No lako se provjeri da, ako Euklidov prsten \(R\) ima normu \(N(\cdot)\) koja zadovoljava svojstvo iz definicije u ovom članku, onda se može definirati norma \(N'(a)=\min_{b\in R\setminus\{0\}} N(ab)\) koja zadovoljava i dodatno svojstvo \(N'(a)\le N'(ab)\). Stoga su sve te različite definicije ekvivalentne.

Ta je definicija zapravo poopćenje dijeljenja s ostatkom cijelih brojeva. Postojanje norme omogućuje usporedbu relativne veličine ostatka i djelitelja. Tako se pri uzastopnom dijeljenju norma odgovarajućih ostataka stalno smanjuje; budući da norma poprima vrijednosti u prirodnim brojevima, takav postupak nužno završava s \(r=0\). Tako dobivamo Euklidov algoritam u Euklidovu prstenu.

Mogućnost provođenja Euklidova algoritma znači da se u Euklidovu prstenu najveći zajednički djelitelj može učinkovito izračunati. Potpuno analogno teoriji djeljivosti cijelih brojeva može se dokazati da je rezultat Euklidova algoritma nužno najveći zajednički djelitelj te da vrijedi Bézoutov teorem, pri čemu se koeficijenti u njemu mogu odrediti proširenim Euklidovim algoritmom.

Teorem

Za Euklidov prsten \(R\) i njegove elemente \(a,b\in R\) rezultat \(d\) Euklidova algoritma primijenjenog na \(a\) i \(b\) najveći je zajednički djelitelj elemenata \(a\) i \(b\) te postoje \(x,y\in R\) takvi da vrijedi \(d=ax+by\); obratno, svaki element oblika \(ax+by\) višekratnik je elementa \(d\).

Uočimo da su, u jeziku teorije prstena, svi elementi oblika \(ax+by\) upravo elementi ideala \((a,b)\), a taj teorem kaže da je \((a,b)\) nužno glavni ideal \((d)\).

Zapravo je svaki ideal Euklidova prstena glavni.

Teorem

Svaki ideal Euklidova prstena glavni je ideal.

Dokaz

Neka je \(R\) Euklidov prsten i \(I\) njegov ideal. Ako je \(I=\{0\}\), on je očito glavni. Neka je \(I\) nenul ideal. Prema definiciji na prstenu \(R\) postoji norma \(N(\cdot)\), pa možemo uzeti nenul element \(d\) iz \(I\) s najmanjom normom. Tada za svaki \(a\in I\) vrijedi \(a=qd+r\) uz \(r=0\) ili \(N(r)< N(d)\). Budući da je \(r=a-qd\in I\), iz načina na koji je izabran \(d\) slijedi \(r=0\), tj. \(a=qd\in (d)\). To pokazuje da je \(I\) nužno glavni ideal.

Prsteni glavnih ideala

Integralna domena u kojoj su svi ideali glavni naziva se prstenom glavnih ideala. To je vrsta integralnih domena s vrlo dobrim svojstvima, a ujedno i vrlo česta. U tim je integralnim domenama pojam ideala prstena istovjetan pojmu višekratnika kod cijelih brojeva.

Prsten glavnih ideala

Za integralnu domenu \(R\), ako je svaki njezin ideal glavni, kažemo da je ona prsten glavnih ideala (principal ideal domain, PID).

Stoga se posljednji teorem prethodnog odjeljka može iskazati ovako:

Teorem

Svaki Euklidov prsten ujedno je prsten glavnih ideala.

U prstenu glavnih ideala maksimalni ideali upravo su ideali generirani ireducibilnim elementima. Slično kao što su kod cijelih brojeva prosti brojevi i ireducibilni elementi ekvivalentni, u prstenu glavnih ideala ta su dva pojma također ekvivalentna, pa su i maksimalni i prosti ideali potpuno ekvivalentni.

Teorem

Neka prsten glavnih ideala \(R\) ima nenul ideal \(I\). Tada je \(I\) prosti ideal ako i samo ako je \(I\) maksimalni ideal.

Dokaz

Dovoljno je dokazati da je svaki prosti ideal maksimalan. Neka prsten glavnih ideala \(R\) ima nenul prosti ideal \((p)\) i ujedno ideal \((a)\) takav da je \((p)\subseteq(a)\subseteq R\). To znači da \(a\mid p\), pa postoji \(b\in R\) takav da je \(p=ab\). No budući da je \((p)\) prosti ideal, iz \(ab\in(p)\) slijedi \(a\in(p)\) ili \(b\in(p)\). Ako je \(a\in(p)\), onda je \((a)\subseteq (p)\), pa je \((a)=(p)\); ako je \(b\in(p)\), onda je \(b=cp\), pa je \(p=acp\), a budući da je \(p\neq 0\), imamo \(1=ac\), tj. \(a\) ima inverz \(c\), pa je \((a)=R\). To pokazuje da je \((p)\) maksimalni ideal.

Korolar

Neka prsten glavnih ideala \(R\) ima nenul element \(r\). Tada je \(r\) prosti element ako i samo ako je \(r\) ireducibilni element.

Analiza Bézoutova teorema iz prethodnog odjeljka može se prenijeti na prstene glavnih ideala.

Teorem

Neka je \(R\) prsten glavnih ideala i neka su \(a,b\in R\) nenul elementi. Neka je \(d\in R\) generator ideala \((a,b)\). Tada je \(d\) najveći zajednički djelitelj elemenata \(a\) i \(b\), jedinstven do na asociranost; štoviše, postoje \(x,y\in R\) takvi da vrijedi \(ax+by=d\).

Drugim riječima, u prstenu glavnih ideala i dalje vrijedi Bézoutov teorem. Iako najveći zajednički djelitelj postoji u oba slučaja, najveća je razlika između Euklidova prstena i prstena glavnih ideala u tome što se u prvome najveći zajednički djelitelj može učinkovito izračunati Euklidovim algoritmom, dok u prstenu glavnih ideala općenito nema takva učinkovitog algoritma.

Faktorijalni prsteni

Pojam općenitiji od prstena glavnih ideala jest faktorijalni prsten. Teorem o jedinstvenom rastavu cijelih brojeva naziva se osnovnim teoremom aritmetike. Sličan teorem o jedinstvenom rastavu zapravo vrijedi i u nekim integralnim domenama koje nisu prsteni glavnih ideala. Takve integralne domene nazivaju se faktorijalnim prstenima.

Faktorijalni prsten

Za integralnu domenu \(R\), ako se svaki nenul i neinvertibilan element \(r\) može zapisati u obliku \(r=p_1\cdots p_n\), gdje su \(p_1,\cdots,p_n\) ireducibilni elementi koji se mogu ponavljati, i ako je takav rastav jedinstven do na asociranost i poredak, kažemo da je integralna domena \(R\) faktorijalni prsten (unique factorization domain, UFD).

Osnovni teorem aritmetike kaže da je prsten cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) faktorijalni prsten.

Prije smo dali protuprimjer ireducibilnog elementa koji nije prost; u integralnoj domeni \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-5}]\) koja se u njemu pojavljuje teorem o jedinstvenom rastavu više ne vrijedi. No u svim faktorijalnim prstenima ireducibilni i prosti elementi ekvivalentni su.

Teorem

Za faktorijalni prsten \(R\) i njegov nenul element \(a\in R\), \(a\) je prosti element ako i samo ako je \(a\) ireducibilni element.

Dokaz

Dovoljno je dokazati da je svaki ireducibilni element prost. Za ireducibilni element \(r\), ako \(r\mid ab\), onda postoji \(c\in R\) takav da vrijedi \(ab=rc\). Budući da je \(R\) faktorijalni prsten, \(a,b,c\in R\) mogu se rastaviti na umnožak ireducibilnih elemenata. Usporedbom lijeve i desne strane, iz jedinstvenosti rastava slijedi da je \(r\) nužno asociran s nekim ireducibilnim faktorom elementa \(a\) ili \(b\), pa \(r\) dijeli jedan od elemenata \(a\) i \(b\). To pokazuje da je \(r\) i prost.

Svaki prsten glavnih ideala faktorijalni je prsten.

Teorem

Svaki prsten glavnih ideala faktorijalni je prsten.

Dokaz

Neka je \(R\) prsten glavnih ideala i neka \(r\in R\) nije ni nula ni invertibilan. Da bismo pokazali da se \(r\) može jedinstveno rastaviti na umnožak niza ireducibilnih elemenata, postupamo u dva koraka: najprije dokazujemo postojanje rastava, a zatim njegovu jedinstvenost.

Postojanje rastava prilično je prirodno. Ako je \(r\) već ireducibilan, nema ga potrebe dalje rastavljati; inače nužno postoje \(r_1,r_2\) takvi da je \(r=r_1r_2\) i da \(r_1,r_2\) nisu invertibilni. Dalje, ako su i \(r_1\) i \(r_2\) ireducibilni, također nema potrebe dalje rastavljati; inače se onaj od \(r_1\) i \(r_2\) koji nije ireducibilan može dalje rastaviti, pa se \(r\) može zapisati kao umnožak više elemenata. Dakle, dok god u umnošku nisu svi faktori ireducibilni, postupak rastavljanja može se nastaviti. Rastavljanje nužno završava nakon konačno mnogo koraka. U protivnom aksiom izbora jamči da se iz \(R\) može izdvojiti beskonačan lanac elemenata \(\{r_{(i)}\}_{i=0}^\infty\) takav da je \(r_{(0)}=r\) i da \(r_{(i+1)}\mid r_{(i)}\) vrijedi za sve \(i\in\mathbf N\), pri čemu su sve te relacije djeljivosti stroge, tj. u lancu nema asociranih elemenata. U jeziku ideala to odgovara strogo rastućem beskonačnom nizu ideala \(I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset \cdots\subset I_{i}\subset\cdots\subset R\), gdje je \(I_i=(r_{(i)})\). Lako se provjeri da je unija tih ideala \(I=\bigcup_{i=0}^\infty I_i\) opet ideal, pa je nužno glavni. Neka je \(a\) generator glavnog ideala \(I\); tada postoji \(n\in\mathbf N\) takav da je \(a\in I_n\). Stoga je \(I=(a)\subseteq I_n\). To pokazuje da takav strogo rastući beskonačan niz ideala ne postoji, pa gornji postupak rastavljanja nužno završava u konačno mnogo koraka.

Zatim dokazujemo jedinstvenost rastava. Možemo provesti indukciju po broju faktora u rastavu. Ključni korak indukcije jest provjeriti da, ako je \(r=p_1p_2\cdots p_n=q_1q_2\cdots q_m\) i \(n\le m\), onda je \(p_1\) nužno asociran s nekim \(q_j\). Ovdje treba iskoristiti prethodni rezultat: u prstenu glavnih ideala svaki je ireducibilni element prost. Znamo da je \(p_1\) ireducibilni element u \(R\), pa je on i prost, te se za umnožak na desnoj strani induktivno može pokazati da nužno postoji neki element \(q_j\) takav da \(p_1\mid q_j\). Stoga postoji \(c\in R\) takav da je \(q_j=p_1c\), a budući da je \(q_j\) ireducibilan i \(p_1\) ireducibilan, prema definiciji \(c\) može biti samo invertibilan, pa je \(p_1\) asociran s \(q_j\). Tada se pomoću zakona kraćenja na lijevoj odnosno desnoj strani mogu pokratiti \(p_1\) i \(q_j\), a invertibilni element za koji se oni razlikuju pomnožiti s bilo kojim preostalim elementom. Prema pretpostavci indukcije \(p_2\cdots p_n\) i \(q_1\cdots q_{j-1}q_{j+1}\cdots q_m\) nužno imaju jednak broj ireducibilnih elemenata, jednakih do na asociranost. Time je teorem dokazan.

Naposljetku, postojanje najvećeg zajedničkog djelitelja vrijedi i u faktorijalnim prstenima.

Teorem

Neka faktorijalni prsten \(R\) ima nenul elemente \(a,b\in R\) koji se mogu rastaviti u obliku \(a=up_1^{r_1}\cdots p_n^{r_n}\) i \(b=vp_1^{s_1}\cdots p_n^{s_n}\), gdje su \(u,v\) invertibilni elementi, \(p_1,\cdots,p_n\) međusobno različiti ireducibilni elementi, a \(r_i,s_i\) prirodni brojevi. Tada je jedan njihov najveći zajednički djelitelj \(d=p_1^{\min\{r_1,s_1\}}\cdots p_n^{\min\{r_n,s_n\}}\).

To zapravo pokazuje da je svojstvo postojanja najvećeg zajedničkog djelitelja slabije od vrijeđenja teorema o jedinstvenom rastavu2.

Primjer: prsteni kvadratnih cijelih brojeva

Povezano štivo: Kvadratna polja

Razumijevanje apstraktne algebre ne može bez primjera. Upravo zato što je istraživanje velikog Fermatova teorema zahtijevalo proučavanje svojstava jedne klase algebarskih cijelih brojeva postupno se razvila današnja teorija prstena3. Ovdje raspravljamo o najjednostavnijim algebarskim cijelim brojevima, kvadratnim cijelim brojevima. Dokazi mnogih rezultata u ovom dijelu zahtijevaju složena znanja iz algebarske teorije brojeva, pa su izostavljeni.

Kvadratni cijeli broj (quadratic integer) kompleksan je korijen kvadratne jednadžbe \(\alpha^2+b\alpha+c=0\) s cjelobrojnim koeficijentima i vodećim koeficijentom jedan. Svi kvadratni cijeli brojevi, i samo oni, imaju oblik

\[ \alpha=a+b\omega,~(a,b\in\mathbf Z) \]

gdje je

\[ \omega=\begin{cases} \dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2},& D\equiv 1\pmod 4,\\ \sqrt D,& D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4, \end{cases} \]

pri čemu \(D\) nema kvadratnih faktora.

Analiza

Prema formuli za rješenja kvadratne jednadžbe znamo da se korijen te jednadžbe nužno može zapisati kao

\[ \alpha=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4c}}{2}. \]

Kad je \(b=2k+1\) neparan, taj se korijen može zapisati kao

\[ \alpha=-k-\frac{1\pm\sqrt{4(k^2+k-c)+1}}{2}. \]

Inače, kad je \(b=2k\) paran, taj se korijen može zapisati kao

\[ \alpha=-k\pm\sqrt{k^2-c}. \]

Odatle se zaključuje da kvadratni cijeli brojevi nužno imaju gornji oblik.

Lako se provjeri da za takav \(\omega\) skup \(\mathbf Z[\omega]=\{a+b\omega:a,b\in\mathbf Z\}\) čini prsten. On se naziva prstenom kvadratnih cijelih brojeva (quadratic integer ring), a njegovo polje razlomaka upravo je kvadratno polje \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt D)\). Kad je \(D>0\), svi su kvadratni cijeli brojevi realni, pa se on naziva i realnim prstenom kvadratnih cijelih brojeva; kad je \(D<0\), svi kvadratni cijeli brojevi osim cijelih brojeva kompleksni su, pa se on naziva i imaginarnim prstenom kvadratnih cijelih brojeva.

Svi prsteni kvadratnih cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z[\omega]\) integralne su domene. Među njima, za \(D=-1\), \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-1}]\) (ili u oznaci \(\mathbf Z[\mathrm{i}]\)) naziva se i prstenom Gaussovih cijelih brojeva; za \(D=-3\), \(\mathbf Z\left[\dfrac{1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}\right]\) naziva se prstenom Eisensteinovih cijelih brojeva.

Za kvadratni cijeli broj \(a+b\omega\) može se definirati njegov konjugat (conjugate) kao \(a+b\bar\omega\), gdje je

\[ \bar\omega=\begin{cases} \dfrac{1-\sqrt{D}}{2},& D\equiv 1\pmod 4,\\ -\sqrt D,& D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4, \end{cases} \]

Napominjemo da su za \(D>0\) kvadratni cijeli brojevi realni, pa se ovaj pojam konjugata ne podudara u potpunosti s pojmom konjugata kompleksnog broja, ali su oba posebni slučajevi pojma konjugata algebarskog elementa iz teorije polja. Konjugirani kvadratni cijeli brojevi korijeni su iste kvadratne jednadžbe s cjelobrojnim koeficijentima.

Na prstenu kvadratnih cijelih brojeva može se definirati norma

\[ \begin{aligned} N(a+b\omega)&=(a+b\omega)(a+b\bar\omega)\\ &=\begin{cases} a^2+ab+\dfrac{1-D}{4}b^2,& D\equiv 1\pmod 4,\\ a^2-Db^2,& D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4. \end{cases} \end{aligned} \]

Norma kvadratnog cijelog broja uvijek je cijeli broj. Posebno, za \(D<0\) norma je uvijek prirodan broj. Norma čuva multiplikativnu strukturu, tj. \(N(ab)=N(a)N(b)\).

Invertibilni elementi (jedinice) prstena kvadratnih cijelih brojeva upravo su oni elementi čija je norma \(\pm1\). Za \(D>0\) to je isto što i promatranje rješenja Pellove jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm1\) ili \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm4\). Za \(D<0\) lako se provjeri da su, osim dvaju posebnih slučajeva – u prstenu Gaussovih cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z[\rm{i}]\) invertibilni su elementi \(\{\pm1,\pm\rm{i}\}\), a u prstenu Eisensteinovih cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z[\omega]\) invertibilni su elementi \(\{\pm1,\pm\omega,\pm\omega^2\}\) – u svim ostalim slučajevima invertibilni elementi samo \(\{\pm1\}\).

Norma \(N(\alpha)\) definirana na prstenu kvadratnih cijelih brojeva može se upotrijebiti da se dokaže da je on Euklidov prsten. Za \(D>0\) kao normu iz definicije Euklidova prstena treba uzeti njezinu apsolutnu vrijednost \(|N(\alpha)|\). Pomoću tako dobivene norme može se dokazati da su, za \(D<0\),

\[ D=-1,-2,-3,-7,-11 \]

odnosno, za \(D>0\),

\[ D=2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 21, 29, 33, 37, 41, 57, 73 \]

prsteni kvadratnih cijelih brojeva koji odgovaraju tim cijelim brojevima Euklidovi prsteni s obzirom na normu \(|N(\cdot)|\). No norma iz definicije Euklidova prstena ne mora biti gore definirana norma. Primjerice, za \(D=14,69\) odgovarajući prsteni kvadratnih cijelih brojeva također su Euklidovi prsteni, ali treba upotrijebiti drugu normu. Za \(D<0\) može se dokazati da su gore navedeni slučajevi svi Euklidovi prsteni među prstenima kvadratnih cijelih brojeva.

Složenijim metodama može se utvrditi i je li neki prsten kvadratnih cijelih brojeva prsten glavnih ideala. Može se dokazati da su za \(D<0\) samo za

\[ D=-1,-2,-3,-7,-11,-19,-43,-67,-163 \]

odgovarajući prsteni kvadratnih cijelih brojeva prsteni glavnih ideala. Usporedbom s gornjim rezultatom vidimo da slučajevi poput \(D=-19\) daju primjer prstena glavnih ideala koji nije Euklidov prsten. Za \(D>0\) zasad nema potpunog rezultata.

Može se, međutim, dokazati da su u prstenima kvadratnih cijelih brojeva pojmovi faktorijalnog prstena i prstena glavnih ideala ekvivalentni. Gornji rezultat pokazuje da, primjerice, \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-5}]\) nije prsten glavnih ideala, pa stoga nije ni faktorijalni prsten. Prije smo već primjerom izravno dokazali da u njemu nema jedinstvenog rastava, tj.

\[ 9=3\times3=(2+\sqrt{-5})\times(2-\sqrt{-5}). \]

Istim se primjerom može pokazati da ni ideal \((3,2+\sqrt{-5})\) nije glavni. Uskoro ćemo vidjeti da je jednostavan primjer faktorijalnog prstena koji nije prsten glavnih ideala prsten polinoma \(\mathbf Z[x]\).

Iako mnogi prsteni kvadratnih cijelih brojeva nisu faktorijalni prsteni, svi su oni Dedekindove domene. To znači da se svaki netrivijalni ideal u svakom prstenu kvadratnih cijelih brojeva može jedinstveno rastaviti na umnožak niza prostih ideala. No ako sam prsten kvadratnih cijelih brojeva nije prsten glavnih ideala, ti prosti idealni faktori ne odgovaraju nužno prostim elementima, pa teorem o jedinstvenom rastavu (tj. rastavu brojeva na umnožak prostih brojeva) više ne vrijedi: to je i bio prvotni poticaj za proučavanje ideala umjesto brojeva.

Prsten polinoma

Povezano štivo: Uvod u tehnike s polinomima

U algoritamskim natjecanjima često se susreću razne operacije s polinomima. Množenje, invertiranje, dijeljenje s ostatkom i druge operacije s polinomima mogu se promatrati kao poopćenje operacija s brojevima na prsten polinoma. Jezikom apstraktne algebre svojstva odgovarajućih operacija u prstenu polinoma mogu se brže razumjeti.

Polinom

Za nenul komutativan prsten s jedinicom \(R\), polinom (polynomial) nad \(R\) formalni je zbroj

\[ \sum_{k=0}^{n}a_kx^k = a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+a_nx^n, \]

gdje je \(n\in\mathbf N\) i za svaki \(k\) vrijedi \(a_k\in R\). Ti \(a_k\) nazivaju se koeficijentima (coefficient) polinoma, a odgovarajući \(a_kx^k\) članovima (term) polinoma. Broj \(k\) u članu \(a_kx^k\) naziva se stupnjem (degree) tog člana.

Polinom čiji su svi koeficijenti nula (tj. nulelement) naziva se nulpolinomom (zero polynomial) i označava s \(0\). Za ostale polinome bez smanjenja općenitosti neka je \(a_n\neq 0\), tj. \(a_nx^n\) je član najvišeg stupnja među članovima s nenul koeficijentom. Tada se prirodan broj \(n\) naziva stupnjem (degree) polinoma, član \(a_nx^n\) u kojem se nalazi naziva se vodećim članom (leading term), a \(a_n\) se naziva i vodećim koeficijentom (leading coefficient). Polinom čiji je vodeći koeficijent jednak jedan (tj. jedinici) naziva se normiranim (monic) polinomom. Stupanj nulpolinoma ne određuje se ili se dogovorno stavlja \(-\infty\).

Simbol \(x\) koji se pojavljuje u zapisu polinoma naziva se neodređenicom (indeterminate) polinoma. Sam po sebi nema nikakvo značenje ni područje vrijednosti. Postoji samo zato da svojim eksponentom označi položaj koeficijenta. Stoga se polinom može zapisati i kao niz nad \(R\)

\[ (a_0,a_1,...,a_{n-1},a_n,0,0,\cdots). \]

No takav niz može imati samo konačno mnogo članova različitih od nule. Ako su nizovi koeficijenata dvaju polinoma jednaki, kažemo da su polinomi jednaki. To znači da su im, nakon dopunjavanja članovima s koeficijentom nula, formalni zbrojevi potpuno jednaki. U nastavku više ne razlikujemo zapise formalnih zbrojeva jednakih polinoma: po potrebi čitatelj može sam dopuniti nule koje nedostaju među koeficijentima.

Ponekad treba element prstena uvrstiti umjesto neodređenice polinoma. Primjerice, neka je \(f(x)\) polinom nad \(R\) i \(a\in R\); rezultat uvrštavanja \(a\) u polinom \(f(x)\) jest \(f(a)\). Njegovo je značenje: zamijenimo li u formalnom zbroju polinoma \(x\) s \(a\), dobivamo aritmetički izraz u \(R\), a \(f(a)\) je rezultat izračuna tog izraza u \(R\).

„Polinom” i „polinomijalna funkcija”

Čitatelj ne bi smio brkati ta dva pojma. Polinom je samo konačan niz koeficijenata i ne postaje automatski funkcija. Iako ovdje opisano uvrštavanje elemenata prstena umjesto neodređenice doista preslikava polinom u polinomijalnu funkciju, to preslikavanje ne mora biti injekcija. Primjerice, \(f(x)=x^p-x\) kao polinom nad poljem \(\mathbf F_p\) očito nije jednak nulpolinomu; ali \(f(x)\) kao polinomijalna funkcija \(\mathbf F_p\rightarrow \mathbf F_p\) identički je jednak nuli (to je mali Fermatov teorem). Iako su ta dva pojma različita, mnogi se pojmovi za polinomijalne funkcije mogu poopćiti na polinome; primjerice, po uzoru na derivaciju, neodređeni integral i kompoziciju polinomijalnih funkcija mogu se definirati (formalna) derivacija, neodređeni integral, kompozicija itd. polinoma. Te formalne operacije ne ovise ni o kakvoj topološkoj strukturi, ali i dalje vrijede mnoga pravila računanja.

Za polinome

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x)&=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+a_nx^n,\\ g(x)&=b_0+b_1x+\cdots+b_{n-1}x^{n-1}+b_nx^n, \end{aligned} \]

zbrajanje polinoma definira se kao

\[ f(x)+g(x) = (a_0+b_0)+(a_1+b_1)x+\cdots+(a_{n-1}+b_{n-1})x^{n-1}+(a_n+b_n)x^n, \]

a množenje polinoma kao

\[ f(x)g(x) = a_0b_0+(a_1b_0+a_0b_1)x+(a_2b_0+a_1b_1+a_0b_2)x^2+\cdots, \]

gdje je koeficijent uz \(x^k\) jednak \(\sum_{i=0}^ka_{k-i}b_i\). Uz tako definirano zbrajanje i množenje može se dokazati da svi polinomi nad \(R\) čine prsten, u oznaci \(R[x]\).

Stupanj polinoma \(f(x)\) označava se s \(\deg f(x)\). Polinomi stupnja nula konstantni su polinomi; oni zajedno s nulpolinomom čine ulaganje prstena \(R\) u \(R[x]\). Očito \(R\) ima djelitelje nule ako i samo ako \(R[x]\) ima djelitelje nule.

Teorem

Prsten polinoma \(R[x]\) integralna je domena ako i samo ako je \(R\) integralna domena.

U prstenu polinoma \(R[x]\) nad integralnom domenom \(R\) rezultati zbrajanja i množenja zadovoljavaju

\[ \begin{aligned} \deg(f(x)+g(x)) &\le \max\{\deg f(x),\deg g(x)\},\\ \deg(f(x)g(x)) &= \deg f(x) + \deg g(x). \end{aligned} \]

Ovdje stavljamo \(\deg 0 = -\infty\). Stoga su invertibilni elementi prstena polinoma nužno samo oni invertibilni elementi među njegovim konstantnim polinomima. Nijedan polinom stupnja jedan ili više nije invertibilan.

Daljnja je rasprava ograničena na polinome nad integralnim domenama.

Dogovor

U nastavku ćemo bez razlikovanja upotrebljavati izraze „polinom nad prstenom \(R\)” i „polinom u prstenu polinoma \(R[x]\)”. Primjerice, da je polinom ireducibilan nad prstenom \(R\) znači da je polinom ireducibilan u prstenu \(R[x]\). Nadalje, ako je \(R\) potprsten od \(S\), polinom nad \(R\) automatski je i polinom nad \(S\); ni to više nećemo posebno naglašavati.

Prsten polinoma nad poljem

Među prstenima polinoma nad integralnim domenama najjednostavnija svojstva ima, naravno, prsten polinoma nad poljem. U prstenu polinoma \(F[x]\) nad poljem \(F\) koeficijenti se mogu dijeliti, pa se može definirati dijeljenje s ostatkom. Neka je norma nenul polinoma \(f(x)\) jednaka \(N(f(x))=\deg f(x)\). Tada se za polinom \(f(x)\) i nenul polinom \(g(x)\) iz \(F[x]\) očito može provesti dijeljenje s ostatkom

\[ f(x)=g(x)q(x)+r(x), \]

gdje su \(q(x),r(x)\in F[x]\) te \(r(x)=0\) ili \(\deg r(x)<\deg g(x)\). To pokazuje da je svaki prsten polinoma nad poljem Euklidov prsten.

Teorem

Prsten polinoma \(F[x]\) nad poljem \(F\) Euklidov je prsten, dakle i prsten glavnih ideala i faktorijalni prsten.

U algoritamskim natjecanjima, zbog preciznosti računanja, često se promatra prsten polinoma \(\mathbf F_p[x]=(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)[x]\), pri čemu modul \(p\) mora biti prost broj. Takav prsten dopušta Euklidov algoritam i slične operacije. No prsten polinoma \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)[x]\) za proizvoljan modul \(n\) nije čak ni integralna domena.

Vrijeđenje dijeljenja s ostatkom znači da korijen polinoma uvijek odgovara njegovu linearnom faktoru.

Korijen

Korijen (root) polinoma \(f(x)\) element je \(\xi\in F\) za koji vrijedi \(f(\xi)=0\).

Teorem

Za polinom \(f(x)\) nad poljem \(F\) i element polja \(\xi\in F\), \(\xi\) je korijen polinoma \(f(x)\) ako i samo ako \(f(x)\) ima linearni faktor \((x-\xi)\).

Dokaz

Prema dijeljenju s ostatkom postoje \(q(x),r(x)\) takvi da vrijedi \(f(x)=q(x)(x-\xi)+r(x)\) i \(\deg r(x)<\deg(x-\xi)=1\). Stoga je \(r(x)\) konstantan polinom ili nulpolinom; stavimo \(r(x)=c\), pa nužno vrijedi \(f(x)=q(x)(x-\xi)+c\). Uvrstimo \(x=\xi\); tada je \(0=f(\xi)=c\), tj. \(f(x)=q(x)(x-\xi)\).

Pojam korijena može se poopćiti na višestruke korijene.

Višestruki korijen

Ako polinom \(f(x)\) ima faktor \((x-\xi)^k\), a \((x-\xi)^{k+1}\) ne dijeli \(f(x)\), kažemo da je \(\xi\) korijen kratnosti \(k\) (root of multiplicity \(k\)) polinoma \(f(x)\). Ako je \(k>1\), korijen \(\xi\) naziva se višestrukim korijenom (multiple root) polinoma \(f(x)\); ako je \(k=1\), korijen \(\xi\) naziva se jednostrukim korijenom (simple root) polinoma \(f(x)\).

Teorem

Ako polinom \(f(x)\) nad poljem \(F\) ima (moguće ponovljene) korijene \(\xi_1,\cdots,\xi_k\), onda on nužno ima faktor \((x-\xi_1)\cdots(x-\xi_k)\). Nadalje, ako je polinom \(f(x)\) nad poljem \(F\) stupnja \(n\), onda on ima najviše \(n\) korijena (brojeći kratnosti).

Dokaz

Dovoljno je uočiti da je \(F[x]\) faktorijalni prsten.

Iako za polinome nad poljem vrijedi teorem o jedinstvenom rastavu, ne postoji opći način da se utvrdi je li zadani polinom reducibilan. Slučajevi malog stupnja razmjerno su laki. Primjerice, svi polinomi prvog stupnja ireducibilni su. Nad posebnim poljima svi su ireducibilni polinomi prvog stupnja. Takva se polja nazivaju algebarski zatvorenim poljima. Nad takvim poljem svaki polinom stupnja većeg od nula ima korijen, pa se svaki polinom stupnja većeg od jedan može dalje rastaviti. Jedan je takav primjer polje kompleksnih brojeva \(\mathbf C\). Nad poljem realnih brojeva \(\mathbf R\) pak postoje ireducibilni polinomi drugog stupnja; nad poljem racionalnih brojeva \(\mathbf Q\) struktura ireducibilnih polinoma još je složenija. Stranica Teorija polja sadrži više rasprave o polinomima nad poljem racionalnih brojeva i nad konačnim poljima.

Svi gornji rezultati odnose se na polinome nad poljem. Polinomi nad općenitijim integralnim domenama često se mogu svesti na taj slučaj.

Promotrimo sada prsten polinoma \(R[x]\) nad faktorijalnim prstenom \(R\). Pri izravnom računanju u \(R[x]\) mnoge su operacije ograničene jer se koeficijenti često ne mogu dijeliti. Razmotrimo stoga proširenje prstena \(R\) do njegova polja razlomaka \(F\) i rastavljanje polinoma \(f(x)\) iz \(R[x]\) u \(F[x]\). Znamo da je \(F[x]\) faktorijalni prsten, pa se iz rastava polinoma \(f(x)\) u \(F[x]\) može zaključiti rastav u \(R[x]\). Srećom, takva je ideja uvijek provediva.

Gaussova lema

Za faktorijalni prsten \(R\) i njegovo polje razlomaka \(F\), ako je \(f(x)\in R[x]\) i ako u \(F[x]\) vrijedi \(f(x)=A(x)B(x)\), onda nužno postoje \(s,t\in F\) takvi da je \(a(x)=sA(x)\in R[x]\), \(b(x)=tB(x)\in R[x]\) i \(f(x)=a(x)b(x)\). Stoga, ako je \(f(x)\) stupnja većeg od nula ireducibilan u \(R[x]\), onda je ireducibilan i u \(F[x]\).

Dokaz

Neka je \(f(x)\in R[x]\) reducibilan u \(F[x]\) i neka je \(f(x)=A(x)B(x)\). Neka su \(r_a\) i \(r_b\) redom najmanji zajednički višekratnici nazivnika svih koeficijenata polinoma \(A(x)\) odnosno \(B(x)\); tada su \(\tilde a(x)=r_aA(x)\) i \(\tilde b(x)=r_bB(x)\) polinomi nad \(R\). Stavimo \(r=r_ar_b\); tada je \(rf(x)=\tilde a(x)\tilde b(x)\). Ako je \(r\) invertibilan u \(R\), možemo uzeti rastav \(f(x)=(r^{-1}\tilde a(x))\tilde b(x)\), koji očito zadovoljava zahtjeve leme.

Inače, ako \(r\) ima ireducibilni faktor \(p\), treba dokazati da se taj faktor može pokratiti na objema stranama jednakosti tako da svi koeficijenti ostanu u integralnoj domeni \(R\). Uočimo da je \(p\) nužno i prost, pa je \((p)\) prosti ideal. Uzmemo li obje strane jednakosti modulo \(p\), dobivamo polinom \(0=\bar a(x)\bar b(x)\) nad \((R/(p))[x]\), gdje su \(\bar a\) i \(\bar b\) polinomi nakon uzimanja modula. Budući da je \(R/(p)\) integralna domena, i \((R/(p))[x]\) integralna je domena, pa možemo pretpostaviti \(\bar a(x)=0\). To znači da \(p\) dijeli sve koeficijente polinoma \(\tilde a(x)\). Stoga se na objema stranama jednakosti faktor \(p\) može izravno pokratiti.

Prema definiciji faktorijalnog prstena \(r\) ima najviše konačno mnogo takvih ireducibilnih faktora, pa se nakon konačno mnogo kraćenja svodimo na slučaj u kojem je \(r\) invertibilan u \(R\). Time je lema dokazana.

Korolar

Za faktorijalni prsten \(R\) i njegovo polje razlomaka \(F\), ako je \(f(x)\in R[x]\) i svi su koeficijenti polinoma \(f(x)\) relativno prosti (tj. njihov najveći zajednički djelitelj jedinica je u \(R\)), onda je \(f(x)\) ireducibilan u \(R[x]\) ako i samo ako je \(f(x)\) ireducibilan u \(F[x]\).

Drugim riječima, ireducibilni elementi prstena polinoma s cjelobrojnim koeficijentima \(\mathbf Z[x]\) ireducibilni su elementi i u \(\mathbf Q[x]\). Učinkovita metoda za provjeru ireducibilnosti polinoma s cjelobrojnim koeficijentima jest Eisensteinov kriterij. Prema Gaussovoj lemi on daje i metodu za provjeru ireducibilnosti polinoma s racionalnim koeficijentima.

Eisensteinov kriterij

Neka je \(f(x)=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+a_nx^n\) polinom \(n\)-tog stupnja s cjelobrojnim koeficijentima. Ako postoji prost broj \(p\) takav da \(p\mid a_i\) vrijedi za sve \(i=0,1,\cdots,n-1\), \(p\) ne dijeli \(a_n\) i \(p^2\) ne dijeli \(a_0\), onda je polinom \(f(x)\) ireducibilan nad poljem racionalnih brojeva \(\mathbf Q\). Ako je \(\gcd(a_0,a_1,\cdots,a_n)=1\), polinom \(f(x)\) ireducibilan je i nad prstenom cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\).

Dokaz

Prema Gaussovoj lemi, ako je polinom \(f(x)\) reducibilan nad poljem racionalnih brojeva \(\mathbf Q\), onda je reducibilan i nad prstenom cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\). Neka je \(f(x)=b(x)c(x)\) njegov rastav u \(\mathbf Z[x]\). Uzmemo li obje strane jednakosti modulo prost broj \(p\), dobivamo rastav u \(\mathbf F_p[x]\), \(\overline{f}(x)=\overline{b}(x)\overline{c}(x)\). No uvjeti teorema kažu da je \(\overline{f}(x)=x^n\), pa nužno postoji cijeli broj \(m\) takav da je \(\overline b(x)=x^m\) i \(\overline c(x)=x^{n-m}\), gdje je \(0<m<n\). Stoga su slobodni članovi \(b_0\) i \(c_0\) faktora \(b(x)\) i \(c(x)\) višekratnici broja \(p\). Dakle, slobodni član \(a_0=b_0c_0\) polinoma \(f(x)\) nužno je višekratnik broja \(p^2\). To je u suprotnosti sa zadanim uvjetom.

Primjeri
  1. Polinom \(x^3-2\) ireducibilan je u \(\mathbf Q[x]\). Dovoljno je primijeniti Eisensteinov kriterij za \(p=2\).
  2. Polinom \(x^4+1\) ireducibilan je u \(\mathbf Q[x]\). Inače bi i \((x+1)^4+1=x^4+4x^3+6x^2+4x+2\) bio reducibilan. No primjenom Eisensteinova kriterija za \(p=2\) vidimo da potonji nije reducibilan.

Za faktorijalni prsten \(R\), budući da je prsten polinoma nad odgovarajućim poljem razlomaka \(F\) faktorijalni prsten, a Gaussova lema kaže da rastavi polinoma nad poljem razlomaka \(F\) i nad izvornom integralnom domenom \(R\) međusobno odgovaraju, i \(R[x]\) je faktorijalni prsten. Stoga vrijedi sljedeći teorem:

Teorem

Prsten polinoma \(R[x]\) faktorijalni je prsten ako i samo ako je \(R\) faktorijalni prsten.

Ovdje \(\mathbf Z[x]\) daje primjer da faktorijalni prsten ne mora biti prsten glavnih ideala. Primjerice, u \(\mathbf Z[x]\) ideal \((2,x)\) nije glavni.

Postoji mnogo načina da se prsten polinoma proširi do većeg skupa. Primjerice, prsten polinoma \(R[x]\) nad integralnom domenom može se proširiti do svojeg polja razlomaka, u oznaci \(R(x)\). To se polje razlomaka često naziva poljem racionalnih funkcija (field of rational fractions), a njegovi elementi u osnovi imaju oblik \(\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}\), gdje su \(f(x)\) i \(g(x)\) polinomi.

Prsten polinoma u više varijabli

Prsten polinoma može se poopćiti na slučaj s više neodređenica. Za komutativan prsten s jedinicom \(R\) može se definirati prsten polinoma nad \(R\), tj. prsten polinoma u jednoj varijabli \(R[x]\). Dalje se može definirati prsten polinoma nad \(R[x]\), \(R[x][y]\), koji se može promatrati kao prsten polinoma u dvije varijable \(R[x,y]\) nad \(R\). Tako se induktivno može definirati prsten polinoma u \(k\) varijabli \(R[x_1,\cdots,x_k]\) nad \(R\). Kad je \(R\) integralna domena, svaki prsten polinoma u više varijabli nad njim integralna je domena; slično se i svojstvo faktorijalnog prstena prenosi na svaki prsten polinoma u više varijabli.

Prsten formalnih redova potencija

Može se promatrati i slučaj u kojem u formalnom zbroju proizvoljno mnogo koeficijenata smije biti različito od nule. Formalni red potencija (formal power series) nad komutativnim prstenom s jedinicom \(R\) definira se kao

\[ \sum_{k=0}^\infty a_kx^k=a_0+a_1x+a_2x^2+\cdots. \]

Zbrajanje i množenje redova potencija može se definirati na isti način kao u prstenu polinoma \(R[x]\). Pritom i formalni redovi potencija čine prsten, u oznaci \(R[[x]]\). Za ove formalne redove potencija ne treba razmatrati konvergenciju, jer je zapravo svaki formalni red potencija samo svoj niz koeficijenata i nije mu pridana nikakva dodatna topološka struktura.

Struktura prstena formalnih redova potencija vrlo je zanimljiva. U prstenu polinoma nad integralnom domenom invertibilni elementi mogu biti samo konstante. No u prstenu formalnih redova potencija može vrijediti

\[ (1-x)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty x^k=1+x+x^2+\cdots. \]

Ta je pojava opća. Čim je slobodni član \(a_0\) formalnog reda potencija invertibilan u \(R\), i \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_kx^k\) nužno je invertibilan. Naime, ako stavimo

\[ \left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_kx^k\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty b_kx^k\right)=1, \]

onda se, ispišemo li sustav jednadžbi koje koeficijenti moraju zadovoljavati, izrazi za \(b_k\) mogu rekurzivno odrediti, pri čemu se pojavljuje samo inverz od \(a_0\).

Na prstenu formalnih redova potencija mogu se definirati razne operacije, poput inverza, dijeljenja, kompozicijskog inverza, formalne derivacije, elementarnih funkcija itd.; vidi Uvod u tehnike s polinomima.

Prsten formalnih Laurentovih redova

Prsten formalnih redova potencija može se dodatno proširiti tako da dopušta članove negativnog stupnja. Formalni Laurentov red (formal Laurent series) nad komutativnim prstenom s jedinicom \(R\) definira se kao

\[ \sum_{k=N}^\infty a_kx^k, \]

gdje je \(N\in\mathbf Z\). Formalni Laurentov red može, dakle, imati konačno mnogo članova negativnog stupnja. Proširimo li prethodno zbrajanje i množenje na formalne Laurentove redove, dobivamo prsten formalnih Laurentovih redova, u oznaci \(R((x))\). Ako je \(F\) polje, onda je i \(F((x))\) polje.

Prsten formalnih Laurentovih redova ima primjenu u Lagrangeovoj inverziji.

Kineski teorem o ostatcima

Povezano štivo: Kineski teorem o ostatcima

U teoriji brojeva kineski teorem o ostatcima često se upotrebljava za rješavanje sustava kongruencija. Za opće komutativne prstene s jedinicom također se može uspostaviti kineski teorem o ostatcima. Svaka kongruencija zapravo zadaje sliku nepoznanice u nekom kvocijentnom prstenu, pa kineski teorem o ostatcima u komutativnom prstenu s jedinicom odgovara određivanju elementa prstena preko njegovih slika u tim kvocijentnim prstenima.

Ta se rasprava može pretočiti u formalni jezik. Za nenul komutativan prsten s jedinicom \(R\) i njegove ideale \(I_1,\cdots,I_n\) promotrimo homomorfizam prstena \(\varphi:R\rightarrow R/I_1\times \cdots\times R/I_n\) koji \(r\) preslikava u \((r+I_1,\cdots,r+I_n)\). Pritom je \(r+I_i\) susjedna klasa, a \(\times\) označava direktni produkt prstena:

Direktni produkt

Za prstene \(R_1\) i \(R_2\) na direktnom produktu \(R_1\times R_2\) njihovih aditivnih grupa može se definirati množenje po komponentama; tada \(R_1\times R_2\) postaje prsten koji se naziva direktnim produktom (direct product) prstena \(R_1\) i \(R_2\) i i dalje označava s \(R_1\times R_2\).

Jezgra homomorfizma \(\varphi\) jest \(\ker\varphi=I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_n\). Pitanje na koje kineski teorem o ostatcima odgovara jest uz koje je uvjete takvo preslikavanje surjekcija.

U slučaju teorije brojeva za vrijeđenje teorema potrebno je da moduli budu relativno prosti. Taj se uvjet može poopćiti na slučaj teorije prstena.

Komaksimalnost

Neka prsten \(R\) ima ideale \(I\) i \(J\). Ako je \(I+J=R\), kažemo da su \(I\) i \(J\) komaksimalni (comaximal).

U slučaju prstena s jedinicom, promatramo li glavne ideale \((a)\) i \((b)\), taj je uvjet isto što i postojanje \(x,y\in R\) takvih da je \(ax+by=1\), što je slično Bézoutovu teoremu za relativno proste cijele brojeve. Pomoću te definicije može se, potpuno po uzoru na slučaj prstena cijelih brojeva, uspostaviti kineski teorem o ostatcima (Chinese remainder theorem) u komutativnom prstenu s jedinicom.

Kineski teorem o ostatcima

Neka nenul komutativan prsten s jedinicom \(R\) ima ideale \(I_1,\cdots,I_n\). Ako su oni u parovima komaksimalni, onda je gore definirani homomorfizam prstena \(\varphi\) surjekcija, a njegova je jezgra jednaka umnošku tih ideala, \(\ker\varphi=I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_n=I_1\cdots I_n\), pa je

\[ R/(I_1\cdots I_n)=R/(I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_n)\cong R/I_1\times\cdots\times R/I_n. \]
Dokaz

Sadržaj teorema vrlo je bogat, ali treba dokazati samo dvije tvrdnje: da je \(\varphi\) surjekcija i da je \(I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_n=I_1\cdots I_n\). Ključno je dobro iskoristiti uvjet komaksimalnosti.

Najprije dokazujemo slučaj \(n=2\). Budući da su ideali \(I_1\) i \(I_2\) komaksimalni, tj. \(I_1+I_2=R\), jedinica \(1\) prstena \(R\) može se zapisati u obliku \(a_1+a_2\), gdje je \(a_i\in I_i\). Budući da je \(a_1\in I_1\) i \(a_1=1-a_2\in 1+I_2\), vrijedi \(\varphi(a_1)=(I_1,1+I_2)\); analogno, \(\varphi(a_2)=(1+I_1,I_2)\). Stoga \((\varphi(a_2),\varphi(a_1))\) igra ulogu sličnu „bazi” u vektorskom prostoru. Dakle, za proizvoljnu sliku \((r_1+I_1,r_2+I_2)\) može se naći praslika \(r_1a_2+r_2a_1\) pri homomorfizmu \(\varphi\). To pokazuje da je \(\varphi\) surjekcija.

Treba još dokazati \(I_1\cap I_2=I_1I_2\). Za opći prsten uvijek vrijedi \(I_1I_2\subseteq I_1\cap I_2\); ključna je obratna inkluzija. Za proizvoljan \(r\in I_1\cap I_2\) vrijedi \(r=r(a_1+a_2)=ra_1+ra_2\in I_1I_2\). Stoga vrijedi i \(I_1\cap I_2\subseteq I_1I_2\). Time je tražena tvrdnja dokazana.

Za slučaj \(n>2\) treba upotrijebiti matematičku indukciju. Ključ koraka indukcije jest dokazati da su za u parovima komaksimalne ideale \(I_1,\cdots,I_n\) ideali \(I_1\) i \(I_2\cdots I_n\) uvijek komaksimalni. Budući da je \(I_1\) komaksimalan sa svakim od \(I_2,\cdots,I_n\), za svaki \(i=2,\cdots,n\) postoje \(a_i\in I_1\) i \(b_i\in I_i\) takvi da vrijedi \(1=a_i+b_i\). Stoga vrijedi \(1=(a_2+b_2)\cdots(a_n+b_n)\). Dakle, \(1\in (b_2\cdots b_n)+I_1\subseteq I_1+(I_2\cdots I_n)\). To pokazuje da su ideali \(I_1\) i \(I_2\cdots I_n\) komaksimalni.

Primjena: Lagrangeova interpolacijska formula

Povezano štivo: Lagrangeova interpolacija, Brza interpolacija polinoma

Problem interpolacije (interpolation) glasi: za zadani niz točaka \(\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n\) naći polinom \(f(x)\) nad poljem \(F\) takav da \(f(x_i)=y_i\) vrijedi za sve \(i=1,\cdots,n\). Naravno, pretpostavlja se da su svi \(x_i\) međusobno različiti. Lagrangeova interpolacijska formula daje opće rješenje te vrste problema.

Za polinom \(f(x)\) nad poljem \(F\) uvjet \(f(x_i)=y_i\) ekvivalentan je tome da je \(x_i\) korijen polinoma \(f(x)-y_i\), dakle ekvivalentan s \((x-x_i)\mid(f(x)-y_i)\), tj. s \(f(x)\equiv y_i\pmod{x-x_i}\). Stoga je problem interpolacije ekvivalentan rješavanju sustava kongruencija

\[ \begin{cases} f(x)\equiv y_1&\pmod{x-x_1},\\ f(x)\equiv y_2&\pmod{x-x_2},\\ \cdots\\ f(x)\equiv y_n&\pmod{x-x_n}. \end{cases} \]

Ti linearni polinomi \(\{x-x_i\}_{i=1}^n\) u parovima su relativno prosti. Prema kineskom teoremu o ostatcima rješenje problema treba imati oblik

\[ f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^ny_iM_i(x), \]

gdje je \(M_i(x)=m_i(x)\prod_{j\neq i}(x-x_j)\) i \(M_i(x)\equiv 1\pmod{x-x_i}\). Prema prije izvedenoj ekvivalenciji to je ekvivalentno s \(M_i(x_i)=1\), tj.

\[ m_i(x_i)\prod_{j\neq i}(x_i-x_j) = 1. \]

Možemo uzeti da je \(m_i(x)\) konstantan polinom, tj.

\[ m_i(x) = \frac{1}{\prod_{j\neq i}(x_i-x_j)}. \]

Odatle dobivamo Lagrangeovu interpolacijsku formulu

\[ f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\frac{\prod_{j\neq i}(x-x_j)}{\prod_{j\neq i}(x_i-x_j)}. \]

Općenito, poopćenjem te metode može se izvesti i Hermiteova interpolacijska formula, koja dopušta da se u pojedinim točkama propišu vrijednosti nekoliko derivacija polinoma.

Primjena: multiplikativna grupa klasa ostataka cijelih brojeva

Povezano štivo: Primitivni korijen, Osnovni teorem o konačno generiranim Abelovim grupama

Kao primjenu kineskog teorema o ostatcima i sadržaja iz teorije grupa ovdje raspravljamo o strukturi multiplikativne grupe cijelih brojeva modulo \(n\). U ovom odjeljku izostavljamo crtu iznad oznaka klasa ostataka.

Multiplikativna grupa cijelih brojeva modulo \(n\) (multiplicative group of integers modulo \(n\)) jest \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\), tj. multiplikativna grupa invertibilnih elemenata kvocijentnog prstena \(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\) (naziva se i grupom jedinica). Red grupe \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\) jest \(\varphi(n)\), jer je nužan i dovoljan uvjet za postojanje inverza relativna prostost s \(n\). Ovdje je \(\varphi(n)\) Eulerova funkcija. Štoviše, grupa \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\) uvijek je Abelova.

Prema osnovnom teoremu aritmetike modul \(n\) može se rastaviti na umnožak potencija različitih prostih brojeva:

\[ n=p_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots p_s^{\alpha_s}. \]

Lako se provjeri da je za ideale prstena cijelih brojeva uvjet komaksimalnosti ideala ekvivalentan relativnoj prostosti njihovih generatora. Stoga primjenom kineskog teorema o ostatcima dobivamo

\[ \mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\cong\mathbf Z/p_1^{\alpha_1}\mathbf Z\times\cdots\times\mathbf Z/p_s^{\alpha_s}\mathbf Z. \]

Izomorfizam prstena znači da su i odgovarajuće multiplikativne strukture izomorfne, pa je

\[ (\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\cong(\mathbf Z/p_1^{\alpha_1}\mathbf Z)^\times\times\cdots\times(\mathbf Z/p_s^{\alpha_s}\mathbf Z)^\times. \]

To pokazuje da je \(\varphi(n)=\varphi(p_1^{\alpha_1})\cdots\varphi(p_s^{\alpha_s})\), tj. Eulerova je funkcija multiplikativna.

Stoga je za proučavanje slučaja općeg modula dovoljno promotriti slučaj u kojem je modul potencija prostog broja \(p^k\). Za potencije prostog broja treba zasebno razmotriti dva slučaja, \(p=2\) i \(p\) neparan prost broj:

  • Za \(p=2\) izravnom provjerom dobivamo \((\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_1\) i \((\mathbf Z/4\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_2\). Za \(k\ge3\) vrijedi \((\mathbf Z/2^k\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_2\times C_{2^{k-2}}\).

    Dokaz

    Izravnim računom pomoću binomnog teorema dobivamo

    \[ \begin{aligned} 5^{2^{k-2}}=(1+2^2)^{2^{k-2}}&\equiv 1\pmod {2^k},\\ 5^{2^{k-3}}=(1+2^2)^{2^{k-3}}&\equiv 1+2^{k-1}\pmod {2^k}. \end{aligned} \]

    Stoga je \(5\) element reda \(2^{k-2}\) u \((\mathbf Z/2^k\mathbf Z)^\times\). Istodobno su \(-1\) i \(5^{2^{k-3}}\) dva različita elementa reda dva, pa je \(-1\notin\langle 5\rangle\). Dakle, presjek podgrupa \(\langle-1\rangle\) i \(\langle 5\rangle\) trivijalan je, pa prema karakterizaciji direktnog produkta grupa vrijedi

    \[ (\mathbf Z/2^k\mathbf Z)^\times\cong\langle-1\rangle\times\langle 5\rangle\cong C_2\times C_{2^{k-2}}. \]
    • Za neparan prost broj \(p\) može se dokazati da je \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\) izomorfna cikličkoj grupi \(C_{\varphi(p^k)}\).
    Dokaz

    Da bismo dokazali da je \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\) ciklička, prema osnovnom teoremu o konačnim Abelovim grupama dovoljno je dokazati da je svaka njezina Sylowljeva \(q\)‑podgrupa ciklička. Najprije, za Sylowljevu \(p\)‑podgrupu izravnim računom dobivamo

    \[ \begin{aligned} (1+p)^{p^{k-1}} &\equiv 1\pmod{p^k},\\ (1+p)^{p^{k-2}} &\equiv 1+p^{k-1}\pmod{p^k}. \end{aligned} \]

    Stoga je \((1+p)\) element reda \(p^{k-1}\). Drugim riječima, jedina Sylowljeva \(p\)‑podgrupa grupe \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\) ciklička je grupa \(\langle 1+p\rangle\).

    Ostale Sylowljeve \(q\)‑podgrupe (\(q\neq p\)) mogu se homomorfizmom grupa svesti na slučaj \(k=1\). Promotrimo homomorfizam grupa \(\varphi:(\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\rightarrow(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\) koji susjednu klasu \(r+p^k\mathbf Z\) preslikava u susjednu klasu \(r+p\mathbf Z\). Jezgra tog preslikavanja ima \(p^{k-1}\) elemenata, pa, ograničimo li preslikavanje \(\varphi\) na Sylowljevu \(q\)‑podgrupu (\(q\neq p\)) grupe \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\), jezgra ograničenog preslikavanja trivijalna je, pa je ta Sylowljeva \(q\)‑podgrupa izomorfna slici preslikavanja, tj. Sylowljevoj \(q\)‑podgrupi grupe \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\). Stoga je dovoljno dokazati da su sve Sylowljeve \(q\)‑podgrupe grupe \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\) cikličke.

    Naposljetku dokazujemo da su sve Sylowljeve \(q\)‑podgrupe grupe \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\) cikličke. Budući da je \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\) konačna Abelova grupa, može se po invarijantnim faktorima rastaviti kao

    \[ C_{n_1}\times\cdots\times C_{n_r}. \]

    Ovdje je \(n_1\mid n_2\mid \cdots \mid n_r\). Stoga u svakom faktoru direktnog produkta postoji \(n_1\) elemenata čiji red dijeli \(n_1\). Ako je \(r>1\), onda nužno strogo više od \(n_1\) elemenata zadovoljava jednadžbu \(x^{n_1}=1\). No \(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z\) je polje, a polinom \(n_1\)-og stupnja nad poljem ima najviše \(n_1\) korijena, pa je \(r=1\). Drugim riječima, \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_{p-1}\).

    Time je dokazano \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_{p^{k-1}}\times C_{p-1}\cong C_{\varphi(p^{k})}\).

Time je određena i struktura multiplikativne grupe za opći modul. Iz dobivenih rezultata znamo da je multiplikativna grupa cijelih brojeva modulo \(n\) ciklička ako i samo ako modul \(n\) poprima vrijednost

\[ 1,2,4,p^k,2p^k \]

gdje je \(p\) neparan prost broj; inače multiplikativna grupa cijelih brojeva modulo \(n\) nužno ima podgrupu \(C_2\times C_2\) i ne može biti ciklička. Kad je multiplikativna grupa ciklička, njezin generator naziva se primitivnim korijenom (primitive root) tog modula. Stoga ovdje navedeni teorem daje upravo nužan i dovoljan uvjet postojanja primitivnog korijena.

Naravno, analiza strukture multiplikativne grupe sadrži više informacija od samog uvjeta postojanja primitivnog korijena. Ona jasno odražava redove različitih elemenata multiplikativne grupe. Element \(x\) grupe \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\) koji zadovoljava \(x^k=1\), tj. rješenje kongruencije \(x^k\equiv 1\pmod n\), naziva se \(k\)-tim korijenom jedinice modulo \(n\) (\(k\)-th root of unity modulo \(n\)); element reda točno \(k\) naziva se primitivnim \(k\)-tim korijenom jedinice modulo \(n\) (primitive \(k\)-th root of unity modulo \(n\)). Pomoću strukture multiplikativne grupe postojanje i broj tih korijena jedinice mogu se točno izračunati. Naposljetku, najmanji zajednički višekratnik redova svih elemenata grupe \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\), tj. najmanji prirodan broj \(k\) takav da \(x^k=1\) vrijedi za sve \(x\in (\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\), izražen kao funkcija od \(n\), upravo je Carmichaelova funkcija. Niz njezinih svojstava može se dobiti iz strukture multiplikativne grupe.

Prerequisites: Basic concepts of abstract algebra, Group theory

Introduction

Ring theory studies rings of all kinds.

The parts of ring theory covered in this article are inseparable from the theory of divisibility in number theory. First, analogously to normal subgroups in group theory, this article introduces the kernel of a ring homomorphism, which is called an ideal of the ring; this is in fact a generalization of the concept of a number in number theory to general rings. Then, generalizing concepts from the ring of integers such as primes, the Euclidean algorithm and prime factorization to general rings yields the concepts of various types of integral domains.

Many results from number theory still hold in other common rings. One could say that part of the work of ring theory consists in discussing whether these results from number theory hold in general rings, and if not, what restrictions must be imposed on the ring for them to hold.

Notation

When there is no risk of ambiguity, this article may omit the multiplication symbol of a ring and write the ring \((R,+,\cdot)\) as the ring \(R\). In a ring \(R\), the additive identity is also called the zero element and denoted by \(0\); the multiplicative identity is also called the unity (identity element) and denoted by \(1\).

The definition of a ring in this article does not require a unity

Note that the definition of a ring in this article does not require a unity. Some texts require a unity in the definition of a ring; in that case the statements of some results in this article need slight adjustments. For example, in this article ideals can be defined in terms of subrings, whereas in other texts they may need to be defined in terms of additive subgroups.

Ideals

Analogously to the case of groups, the concepts of subrings and ring homomorphisms can be established.

Subring

For a ring \((R,+,\cdot)\) and a subset \(S\) of it, if \((S,+,\cdot)\) is also a ring, then \(S\) is called a subring of \(R\).

Example: the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\)

For any integer \(n\), the set \(n\mathbf Z=\{nk:k\in\mathbf Z\}\) is a subring of \(\mathbf Z\).

Ring homomorphism

For rings \((R,+,\cdot)\) and \((S,\oplus,\odot)\), if a map \(\pi\) preserves the addition and multiplication of the rings, that is, \(\pi(r_1+r_2)=\pi(r_1)\oplus\pi(r_2)\) and \(\pi(r_1\cdot r_2)=\pi(r_1)\odot\pi(r_2)\) hold for all \(r_1,r_2\in R\), then the map \(\pi:R\rightarrow S\) is called a homomorphism from the ring \(R\) to the ring \(S\).

The case where the definition of a ring requires a unity

If the definition of a ring requires a unity, then the definition of a ring homomorphism often also requires that the unity be mapped to the unity. For homomorphisms between nonzero rings with unity, this extra requirement merely guarantees that the homomorphism does not map the whole ring with unity to the zero element.

Example: the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\) (continued)

The map of reduction modulo any nonzero integer \(n\), that is, \(\pi:\mathbf Z\rightarrow\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\) with \(\pi(a)=\bar a\), is a ring homomorphism.

The discussion of the kernel and image of a group homomorphism can be carried over here almost verbatim. The (relative) size of the image of a homomorphism determines whether the homomorphism is surjective, while the triviality or not of the kernel of a homomorphism determines whether the homomorphism is injective. The kernel of a ring homomorphism is defined as follows:

Kernel of a homomorphism

The kernel of a homomorphism \(\pi:R\rightarrow S\) from the ring \(R\) to the ring \(S\) is \(\{r\in R:\pi(r)=0\}\), denoted by \(\ker\pi\), where \(0\) is the additive identity of \(S\).

Clearly, the kernel and image of a ring homomorphism are both subrings. Conversely, not every subring can be the kernel of some ring homomorphism. The subrings that can be kernels of ring homomorphisms are called ideals of the ring.

Ideal

For a ring \(R\) and a subring \(I\) of it, \(I\) is called, in the ring \(R\), a

  • left ideal if \(rI\subseteq I\) for all \(r\in R\), where \(rI=\{ra:a\in I\}\);
  • right ideal if \(Ir\subseteq I\) for all \(r\in R\), where \(Ir=\{ar:a\in I\}\);
  • ideal if \(I\) is both a left ideal of \(R\) and a right ideal of \(R\).

Here the ideal \(I\) is required to be closed under both left and right multiplication by elements of the ring \(R\). This condition is natural. Indeed, the elements of an ideal are mapped to the zero element under a ring homomorphism, and multiplying any element by zero, on the left or on the right, should give zero; this is precisely the closure that is required. Moreover, since the additive structure of a ring is an abelian group, every subgroup is normal; and the multiplicative structure of a ring is very primitive and imposes no additional restrictions on substructures. This shows that closure under left and right multiplication is also sufficient.

Example: the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\) (continued)

As an example, the subring \(n\mathbf Z\) mentioned earlier is in fact an ideal of \(\mathbf Z\). It is the set of all multiples of \(n\). The product of a multiple of \(n\) with any integer is again a multiple of \(n\). In fact, all ideals of \(\mathbf Z\) are of this form; such a ring is called a principal ideal domain. In a general ring, some ideals are not the set of multiples of some element; the existence of such general rings was precisely the original motivation for studying ideals (rather than simply multiples)4.

Quotient rings

As with groups, based on an ideal of a ring one can define the quotient ring on the set of all cosets (in the sense of the additive group). Consider the set

\[ R/I=\{a+I:a\in R\}, \]

where the coset \(a+I=\{a+b:b\in I\}\). It can be shown that, if and only if \(I\) is an ideal, the operations

\[ \begin{aligned} (a+I)+(b+I)&=(a+b)+I,\\ (a+I)(b+I)&=(ab)+I \end{aligned} \]

are well defined, that is, the results of these operations do not depend on the choice of representatives of the cosets. Under these operations, \(R/I\) forms a ring. Again in accordance with the case of groups, the first isomorphism theorem for rings can be established, and there is a natural homomorphism from a ring to its quotient ring. These show that ideals of rings and normal subgroups of groups play the same role in the homomorphisms of the respective structures.

First isomorphism theorem

Let \(\pi:R\rightarrow S\) be a homomorphism from the ring \(R\) to the ring \(S\). Then \(\ker\pi\) is an ideal of \(R\), and \(R/\ker\pi\cong\pi(R)\) is a subring of \(S\).

Natural homomorphism

For a ring \(R\) and an ideal \(I\) of it, the map \(\pi:R\rightarrow R/I\) given by \(\pi(r)=r+I\) is a surjective homomorphism from \(R\) to \(R/I\), called the natural homomorphism from the ring \(R\) to the quotient ring \(R/I\).

Example: the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\) (continued)

As an example, the ring \(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\) of congruence classes of integers modulo \(n\) is the quotient ring of \(\mathbf Z\) by its ideal \(n\mathbf Z\). This also explains the meaning of the notation \(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\). The map modulo \(n\) mentioned above, \(\pi:\mathbf Z\rightarrow\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\), is exactly the natural map mentioned here, and the corresponding kernel is precisely the ideal \(n\mathbf Z\).

In the case of rings, the other isomorphism theorems hold as well.

Second isomorphism theorem

Let the ring \(R\) have a subring \(A\) and an ideal \(B\). Then \(A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}\) is also a subring of \(R\), \(A\cap B\) is an ideal of \(A\), \(B\) is an ideal of \(A+B\), and \((A+B)/B\cong A/(A\cap B)\).

Third isomorphism theorem

Let the ring \(R\) have ideals \(I,J\) with \(I\subseteq J\). Then \(J/I\) is also an ideal of \(R/I\), and \((R/I)/(J/I)\cong R/J\).

Correspondence theorem

Let the ring \(R\) have an ideal \(I\). Then there is a bijection \(\varphi:\mathcal S\rightarrow\mathcal T\) between the set of all subrings of \(R\) containing \(I\), \(\mathcal S=\{S:I\subseteq S\subseteq R\}\), and the set of all subrings of the quotient ring \(R/I\), \(\mathcal T=\{T:T\text{ is a subring of }R/I\}\), which maps \(S\in\mathcal S\) to \(S/I\in\mathcal T\). This bijection preserves the inclusion relation between subrings, and ideals of \(R\) are always mapped to ideals of \(R/I\).

These theorems will play a fundamental role in the discussion of the structure of rings and ideals below.

Operations on ideals

Various operations can be defined on the ideals of a ring. This is similar to how concepts such as the greatest common divisor and the least common multiple can be defined on the divisibility structure of the integers.

Operations on ideals

Let the ring \(R\) have ideals \(I,J\). The following operations can be defined:

  • the sum of ideals: \(I+J=\{a+b:a\in I,b\in J\}\);
  • the product of ideals: \(IJ=\{\sum_{i=1}^na_ib_i:a_i\in I,b_i\in J\}\), that is, the set of all finite sums of products of the form \(ab\);
  • the intersection of ideals: \(I\cap J\).

It is easy to verify that the results of these operations are still ideals of the ring.

Example: the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\) (continued)

Consider the case of the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\). For the ideals \(n\mathbf Z\) and \(m\mathbf Z\), we get

\[ \begin{aligned} n\mathbf Z+m\mathbf Z&=\gcd(m,n)\mathbf Z,\\ (n\mathbf Z)(m\mathbf Z)&=(mn)\mathbf Z,\\ (n\mathbf Z)\cap(m\mathbf Z)&=\mathrm{lcm}(m,n)\mathbf Z. \end{aligned} \]

In general, for a ring \(R\) and its ideals \(I\) and \(J\), we always have

\[ IJ\subseteq I\cap J\subseteq I,J\subseteq I+J. \]

Using these definitions, the Chinese remainder theorem for integers can be generalized to general rings. But before that, concepts such as primes and coprimality also need to be generalized to general rings.

Maximal ideals

The properties of a ring can be understood through the structure of its ideals.

A nonzero ring \(R\) always has two trivial ideals, namely \(\{0\}\) and \(R\). If the ring \(R\) is moreover a commutative nonzero ring with unity, then a ring with only these two ideals is a field, and only a field has this property1.

Theorem

Let \(R\) be a commutative nonzero ring with unity. Then \(R\) is a field if and only if \(R\) has only the trivial ideals \(\{0\}\) and \(R\).

Proof

If \(R\) is a field, then for any nonzero ideal \(I\) we can take an arbitrary nonzero element \(a\in I\); then any element \(r\in R\) of the field satisfies \(r=(ra^{-1})a\in (ra^{-1})I\subseteq I\), hence \(I=R\). Conversely, for any \(a\in R\) with \(a\neq 0\), one can verify that \(aR=\{ar:r\in R\}\) is an ideal, which must equal \(R\), so there exists \(b\in R\) such that \(ab=1\); this shows that \(a\) has an inverse, hence \(R\) is a field.

The commutativity condition here is necessary; otherwise, one would need to require both the left and the right ideals to be trivial in order to guarantee that the ring is a division ring.

This result can be generalized to the case where the ring itself is not a field. But then one has to consider quotient rings instead and discuss the conditions under which a quotient ring of a commutative nonzero ring with unity is a field. The quotient ring \(R/I\) being a field means that the quotient ring \(R/I\) has only trivial ideals; by the correspondence theorem, this means that in the original ring \(R\) there is no ideal strictly between the ideal \(I\) being quotiented out and the original ring \(R\). Such an ideal \(I\) is called a maximal ideal.

Maximal ideal

For a ring \(R\) and an ideal \(M\) of it, if \(M\neq R\) and the only ideals of \(R\) containing \(M\) are \(M\) and \(R\), then the ideal \(M\) is called a maximal ideal.

Theorem

Let the commutative nonzero ring with unity \(R\) have an ideal \(M\). Then the quotient ring \(R/M\) is a field if and only if \(M\) is a maximal ideal.

Example: the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\) (continued)

For example, the ideal \(n\mathbf Z\) of the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\) is maximal if and only if \(n\) is a prime. For a prime \(p\), the quotient ring \(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z\) is a field, also denoted by \(\mathbf F_p\).

Not every ring has a maximal ideal, but a nonzero ring with unity always has a maximal ideal.

Theorem (Krull)

For an ideal \(I\neq R\) of a nonzero ring with unity \(R\), there always exists a maximal ideal \(M\) of \(R\) such that \(I\subseteq M\).

Proof

The idea is to use Zorn's lemma. Consider the set \(\mathcal S\) of all proper ideals of \(R\) (that is, ideals not equal to \(R\)) containing \(I\). Since \(I\in\mathcal S\), it is nonempty, and it forms a partially ordered set under inclusion. For any chain \(J_0\subseteq J_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq J_n\subseteq\cdots\) in it, let \(J\) be their union; it is easy to verify that this is also an ideal. Moreover, \(J\neq R\), for otherwise \(1\in J\), that is, there would exist \(n\) such that \(1\in J_n\), contradicting the fact that \(J_n\) is a proper ideal. Therefore, by Zorn's lemma, there exists a maximal ideal \(M\supseteq I\).

A maximal ideal is the analogue of an irreducible element in the theory of divisibility. This is because the inclusion relation between ideals corresponds to the divisibility relation between integers; having no ideal as a superset corresponds to having no factor that divides it. However, the concept of a maximal ideal is broader than that of an irreducible element, because not all ideals are principal.

Prime ideals

The condition of being a field is stricter than that of being an integral domain. Ideals that ensure the quotient ring is an integral domain are called prime ideals; they are analogous to the concept of primes in the theory of divisibility.

Prime ideal

For a commutative ring \(R\) and an ideal \(P\) of it, if \(P\neq R\) and for any elements \(a,b\in R\) of the ring, whenever \(ab\in P\) holds we always have \(a\in P\) or \(b\in P\), then the ideal \(P\) is called a prime ideal.

This definition may look somewhat abrupt, but compared with the definition of a prime, this definition of a prime ideal is natural as well.

Theorem

Let the commutative nonzero ring with unity \(R\) have an ideal \(P\). Then the quotient ring \(R/P\) is an integral domain if and only if \(P\) is a prime ideal.

Proof

For a commutative nonzero ring with unity \(R\), the quotient ring \(R/P\) is an integral domain if and only if \(R/P\) has no zero divisors. Denote the coset \(a+P\) by \(\bar a\). That the quotient ring \(R/P\) has no zero divisors is equivalent to: \(\bar a\bar b=\bar 0\) always implies \(\bar a=\bar 0\) or \(\bar b=\bar 0\). By the definition of the quotient ring, this is equivalent to: \(ab\in P\) always implies \(a\in P\) or \(b\in P\).

In the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\), \(n\mathbf Z\) is a maximal ideal if and only if \(n\) is a prime, while \(n\mathbf Z\) is a prime ideal if and only if \(n\) is a prime or \(n=0\). In a general commutative ring, a maximal ideal is always a prime ideal, but the converse need not hold, of course; this can be seen from the properties of the corresponding quotient rings.

Theorem

For a commutative nonzero ring with unity \(R\), every maximal ideal of it is necessarily a prime ideal.

We will see later that the converse holds only in rings with sufficiently good properties, sufficiently similar to the ring of integers.

Principal ideals

Analogously to the concept of subgroups, in the discussion of rings one often needs to consider the ideal generated by some subset.

Ideal generated by a subset

For a nonzero ring with unity \(R\) and a nonempty subset \(A\subseteq R\) of it, if \(I\) is the smallest (with respect to inclusion) among the ideals of \(R\) containing \(A\), then the ideal \(I\) is called the ideal generated by the subset \(A\) and denoted by \((A)\). In this case, \(A\) is called a generating set of \((A)\).

Principal ideal

An ideal generated by a single element \(a\in R\) is called a principal ideal and denoted by \((a)\). In this case, \(a\) is called a generator of \((a)\).

For a set \(A\), one can give a construction of the ideal it generates. First, define

\[ \begin{aligned} RA&=\{r_1a_1+\cdots+r_na_n:r_i\in R,a_i\in A,n\in\mathbf N_+\},\\ AR&=\{a_1r_1+\cdots+a_nr_n:r_i\in R,a_i\in A,n\in\mathbf N_+\}. \end{aligned} \]

These are in fact the left ideal and the right ideal generated by \(A\), respectively. Then the ideal generated by the subset \(A\) is exactly \(RAR\). For commutative rings, all of these structures coincide.

All ideals \(n\mathbf Z\) of the ring of integers are principal ideals; below they are often denoted by \((n)\).

Integral domains

An integral domain is a commutative nonzero ring with unity and without zero divisors. This concept is precisely the generalization of the ring of integers. However, a ring obtained this way need not have properties good enough to allow every result of the theory of divisibility of integers to be carried over verbatim. In order to generalize results from number theory, further restrictions can be imposed on integral domains. Among them, the three most common kinds of integral domains are Euclidean domains, principal ideal domains and unique factorization domains; each of these concepts is strictly contained in the next.

Divisibility relation

First, the relevant concepts of the theory of divisibility of integers are generalized here to general commutative rings.

Divisibility

Let the commutative ring \(R\) have elements \(a,b\in R\). If there exists \(x\in R\) such that \(a=bx\), then \(b\) is said to divide \(a\), written \(b\mid a\). In this case, \(b\) is called a divisor of \(a\).

Associates

Let the commutative ring \(R\) have elements \(a,b\in R\). If they differ only by an invertible element, that is, there exists an invertible element \(u\in R\) such that \(a=bu\), then \(a\) and \(b\) are said to be associates.

The divisibility relation is a partial order on the ring, and the associate relation is an equivalence relation on the ring. From the point of view of ideals, \(a\mid b\) is equivalent to \((b)\subseteq (a)\), and \(a\) and \(b\) being associates is equivalent to \((a)=(b)\). Therefore, when discussing elements of a ring, one usually does not care about the difference between associates. Similarly to the case of integers, the greatest common divisor of \(a\) and \(b\) in a commutative ring is defined as the infimum of \(\{a,b\}\).

Greatest common divisor

For a commutative ring \(R\) and its elements \(a,b\in R\), if there exists a nonzero element \(d\in R\) satisfying \(d\mid a\) and \(d\mid b\), and such that \(d'\mid d\) holds for any \(d'\) satisfying \(d'\mid a\) and \(d'\mid b\), then \(d\) is called a greatest common divisor of \(a\) and \(b\), written \(\gcd(a,b)\).

In an integral domain, the greatest common divisor is uniquely determined up to associates. The discussion below is restricted to integral domains.

In an integral domain, a concept similar to primes can also be established. In the theory of integers, there are two equivalent definitions of a prime, but in a general integral domain these two definitions correspond to different concepts:

Prime element

Let the integral domain \(R\) have a nonzero element \(p\in R\). If \((p)\) is a prime ideal, that is, \(p\) is not invertible and \(p\mid ab\) always implies \(p\mid a\) or \(p\mid b\), then \(p\) is called a prime (prime element).

Irreducible element

Let the integral domain \(R\) have a nonzero element \(r\in R\). If \(r\) is not invertible, and for any \(a,b\in R\) with \(r=ab\) either \(a\) or \(b\) is invertible, then \(r\) is called an irreducible (irreducible element), or \(r\) is said to be irreducible. Conversely, if \(r=ab\) with \(a,b\in R\) both not invertible, then \(r\) is said to be reducible.

It can be shown that the principal ideal \((r)\) corresponding to an irreducible element \(r\) is necessarily maximal among all proper principal ideals of the ring; however, in a general integral domain not all ideals are principal, so the concepts of irreducible element and maximal ideal are not equivalent.

Analogously to the proof that in a principal ideal domain every prime ideal is maximal, the following result can be proved in general:

Theorem

Let \(R\) be an integral domain. If \(a\in R\) is a prime, then \(a\) is necessarily also an irreducible.

Proof

Let \(r\in R\) be a prime, and let \(a,b\in R\) satisfy \(r=ab\). Since \(r\) is prime, we may assume \(r\mid a\) holds; then \(a=cr=cba\). Since the cancellation law holds in an integral domain, we have \(1=bc\), so \(b\) has the inverse \(c\). This shows that \(r\) is irreducible.

The converse does not hold.

Counterexample

In the quadratic integer ring \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-5}]\), \(3\) is irreducible, but \(9=3\cdot 3=(2+\sqrt{-5})(2-\sqrt{-5})\), so it is not prime.

Here we give the proof of this counterexample; readers unfamiliar with quadratic integer rings should first read the section Quadratic integer rings. Let \(N(\cdot)\) be the norm on the quadratic integer ring. For any factorization \(3=ab\) we have \(N(a)N(b)=N(3)=9\). If \(a,b\) are both not invertible, then \(N(a)\) and \(N(b)\) are both greater than \(1\), so necessarily \(N(a)=N(b)=3\). But there are no such elements in \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-5}]\), that is, \(x^2+5y^2=3\) has no integer solutions. This shows that \(3\) is irreducible. As for \(3\) not being prime, one needs to show that \(3\) does not divide \(2\pm\sqrt{-5}\), which is obvious.

Euclidean domains

Related reading: (Extended) Euclidean algorithm, Bézout's identity

A Euclidean domain is an integral domain in which successive division (that is, the Euclidean algorithm) can be performed.

Euclidean domain

For an integral domain \(R\), if there exists a map \(N:R\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbf N\) such that for any \(a,b\in R\) with \(b\neq 0\) there exist \(q,r\in R\) such that \(a=qb+r\) holds with \(r=0\) or \(N(r)<N(b)\), then the integral domain \(R\) is called a Euclidean domain (ED). The map \(N\) is called the norm of the elements of the Euclidean domain.

Other equivalent definitions

The definition adopted in this article defines the norm only at nonzero elements. Different texts may treat the definition of a Euclidean domain differently. For example, some texts may additionally define \(N(0)=0\); but the value \(N(0)\) is not used in the subsequent division with remainder, so this is immaterial. As another example, the definition on Wikipedia further requires the norm \(N\) to satisfy the property that \(N(a)\le N(ab)\) for any nonzero \(a,b\in R\). However, it is easy to verify that if a Euclidean domain \(R\) has a norm \(N(\cdot)\) satisfying the property required by the definition in this article, then one can define the norm \(N'(a)=\min_{b\in R\setminus\{0\}} N(ab)\), which satisfies the additional property \(N'(a)\le N'(ab)\). Therefore, these different definitions are all equivalent.

This definition is in fact a generalization of division with remainder for integers. The existence of the norm makes it possible to measure the relative size of the remainder and the divisor. Thus, during successive division, the norm of the corresponding remainders keeps decreasing; since the norm takes values in the natural numbers, such a process must end at \(r=0\). In this way, we obtain the Euclidean algorithm in a Euclidean domain.

Being able to perform the Euclidean algorithm means that greatest common divisors can be computed efficiently in a Euclidean domain. In complete analogy with the theory of divisibility of integers, one can prove that the result of the Euclidean algorithm is necessarily a greatest common divisor, and that Bézout's identity holds, where the coefficients can be determined by the extended Euclidean algorithm.

Theorem

For a Euclidean domain \(R\) and its elements \(a,b\in R\), the result \(d\) of applying the Euclidean algorithm to \(a\) and \(b\) is a greatest common divisor of \(a\) and \(b\), and there exist \(x,y\in R\) such that \(d=ax+by\) holds; conversely, any element of the form \(ax+by\) is a multiple of \(d\).

Note that, in the language of ring theory, all elements of the form \(ax+by\) are exactly the elements of the ideal \((a,b)\), and this theorem says that \((a,b)\) is necessarily the principal ideal \((d)\).

In fact, every ideal in a Euclidean domain is principal.

Theorem

Every ideal in a Euclidean domain is a principal ideal.

Proof

Let \(R\) be a Euclidean domain and \(I\) an ideal of it. If \(I=\{0\}\), it is obviously principal. Let \(I\) be a nonzero ideal. By definition, there is a norm \(N(\cdot)\) on the ring \(R\), so we can take a nonzero element \(d\) of \(I\) with the smallest norm. Then for any \(a\in I\) we have \(a=qd+r\) with \(r=0\) or \(N(r)< N(d)\). Since \(r=a-qd\in I\), by the way \(d\) was chosen we get \(r=0\), that is, \(a=qd\in (d)\). This shows that \(I\) is necessarily a principal ideal.

Principal ideal domains

An integral domain in which all ideals are principal is called a principal ideal domain. This is a class of integral domains with rather good properties, and also a very common one. In these integral domains, the concept of an ideal of the ring is equivalent to the concept of multiples in the integers.

Principal ideal domain

For an integral domain \(R\), if every ideal of it is principal, then it is called a principal ideal domain (PID).

Therefore, the last theorem of the previous section can be restated as follows:

Theorem

Every Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain.

In a principal ideal domain, maximal ideals are equivalent to ideals generated by irreducible elements. Just as primes and irreducibles are equivalent in the integers, in a principal ideal domain these two concepts are also equivalent, so maximal ideals and prime ideals are completely equivalent as well.

Theorem

Let the principal ideal domain \(R\) have a nonzero ideal \(I\). Then \(I\) is a prime ideal if and only if \(I\) is a maximal ideal.

Proof

It suffices to show that every prime ideal is maximal. Let the principal ideal domain \(R\) have a nonzero prime ideal \((p)\), and at the same time an ideal \((a)\) satisfying \((p)\subseteq(a)\subseteq R\). This means \(a\mid p\), so there exists \(b\in R\) such that \(p=ab\). But since \((p)\) is a prime ideal, \(ab\in(p)\) implies \(a\in(p)\) or \(b\in(p)\). If \(a\in(p)\), then \((a)\subseteq (p)\), hence \((a)=(p)\); if \(b\in(p)\), then \(b=cp\), hence \(p=acp\), and since \(p\neq 0\), we have \(1=ac\), that is, \(a\) has the inverse \(c\), so \((a)=R\). This shows that \((p)\) is a maximal ideal.

Corollary

Let the principal ideal domain \(R\) have a nonzero element \(r\). Then \(r\) is a prime if and only if \(r\) is an irreducible.

The analysis of Bézout's identity in the previous section can be transferred to principal ideal domains.

Theorem

Let \(R\) be a principal ideal domain, and let \(a,b\in R\) be nonzero elements. Let \(d\in R\) be a generator of the ideal \((a,b)\). Then the greatest common divisor of \(a\) and \(b\) is \(d\), unique up to associates; moreover, there exist \(x,y\in R\) such that \(ax+by=d\) holds.

That is, Bézout's identity still holds in a principal ideal domain. Although greatest common divisors exist in both, the biggest difference between Euclidean domains and principal ideal domains is that in the former the greatest common divisor can be computed efficiently by the Euclidean algorithm, whereas in a principal ideal domain there is generally no such efficient algorithm.

Unique factorization domains

A concept more general than principal ideal domains is that of unique factorization domains. The unique factorization theorem for integers is called the fundamental theorem of arithmetic. A similar unique factorization theorem actually still holds in some integral domains that are not principal ideal domains. Such integral domains are called unique factorization domains.

Unique factorization domain

For an integral domain \(R\), if every nonzero non-invertible element \(r\) can be written in the form \(r=p_1\cdots p_n\), where \(p_1,\cdots,p_n\) are irreducibles that may repeat, and such a factorization is unique up to associates and reordering, then the integral domain \(R\) is called a unique factorization domain (UFD).

The fundamental theorem of arithmetic says that the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\) is a unique factorization domain.

Earlier we gave a counterexample of an irreducible that is not prime; in the integral domain \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-5}]\) involved there, the unique factorization theorem no longer holds. However, in all unique factorization domains, irreducibles and primes are equivalent.

Theorem

For a unique factorization domain \(R\) and a nonzero element \(a\in R\) of it, \(a\) is a prime if and only if \(a\) is an irreducible.

Proof

It suffices to show that every irreducible is prime. For an irreducible \(r\), if \(r\mid ab\), then there exists \(c\in R\) such that \(ab=rc\) holds. Since \(R\) is a unique factorization domain, \(a,b,c\in R\) can all be factored into products of irreducibles. Comparing the two sides, by the uniqueness of factorization, \(r\) must be an associate of some irreducible factor of \(a\) or \(b\), so \(r\) divides one of \(a\) and \(b\). This shows that \(r\) is also prime.

All principal ideal domains are unique factorization domains.

Theorem

Every principal ideal domain is a unique factorization domain.

Proof

Let \(R\) be a principal ideal domain, and let \(r\in R\) be neither zero nor invertible. To show that \(r\) can be uniquely factored into a product of a sequence of irreducibles, we proceed in two steps: first prove the existence of the factorization, then prove its uniqueness.

The existence of the factorization is rather natural. If \(r\) is already irreducible, there is no need to factor further; otherwise, there necessarily exist \(r_1,r_2\) such that \(r=r_1r_2\) with \(r_1,r_2\) both not invertible. Further, if \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) are both irreducible, there is again no need to factor further; otherwise, whichever of \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) is not irreducible can be factored further, and \(r\) can be written as a product of more elements. Thus, as long as not all factors in the product are irreducible, the factorization process can be continued. The factorization necessarily terminates after finitely many steps. Otherwise, the axiom of choice guarantees that one can extract from \(R\) an infinite chain of elements \(\{r_{(i)}\}_{i=0}^\infty\) with \(r_{(0)}=r\) and \(r_{(i+1)}\mid r_{(i)}\) for all \(i\in\mathbf N\), where all these divisibility relations are strict, that is, there are no associates in the chain. In the language of ideals, this corresponds to a strictly increasing infinite sequence of ideals \(I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset \cdots\subset I_{i}\subset\cdots\subset R\), where \(I_i=(r_{(i)})\). It is easy to verify that the union of these ideals \(I=\bigcup_{i=0}^\infty I_i\) is still an ideal, hence necessarily principal. Let \(a\) be a generator of the principal ideal \(I\); then there exists \(n\in\mathbf N\) with \(a\in I_n\). Therefore, \(I=(a)\subseteq I_n\). This shows that such a strictly increasing infinite sequence of ideals does not exist, hence the above factorization process necessarily terminates within finitely many steps.

Next we prove the uniqueness of the factorization. We can use induction on the number of factors in the factorization. The key step of the induction is to verify that if \(r=p_1p_2\cdots p_n=q_1q_2\cdots q_m\) and \(n\le m\), then \(p_1\) is necessarily an associate of some \(q_j\). Here we need the earlier result: in a principal ideal domain, every irreducible is prime. We know that \(p_1\) is an irreducible in \(R\), so it is also prime, and for the product on the right-hand side one can show inductively that there necessarily exists some element \(q_j\) such that \(p_1\mid q_j\). Therefore, there exists \(c\in R\) such that \(q_j=p_1c\), and since \(q_j\) is irreducible and \(p_1\) is irreducible, by definition \(c\) can only be invertible, hence \(p_1\) is an associate of \(q_j\). Then, using the cancellation law, \(p_1\) and \(q_j\) can be cancelled on the left and right sides respectively, and the invertible element by which they differ can be multiplied into any of the remaining elements. By the induction hypothesis, \(p_2\cdots p_n\) and \(q_1\cdots q_{j-1}q_{j+1}\cdots q_m\) necessarily have the same number of irreducibles, and they are the same up to associates. This proves the theorem.

Finally, the existence of greatest common divisors still holds in unique factorization domains.

Theorem

Let the unique factorization domain \(R\) have nonzero elements \(a,b\in R\) that can be factored in the form \(a=up_1^{r_1}\cdots p_n^{r_n}\) and \(b=vp_1^{s_1}\cdots p_n^{s_n}\), where \(u,v\) are invertible, \(p_1,\cdots,p_n\) are pairwise distinct irreducibles, and \(r_i,s_i\) are natural numbers. Then one of their greatest common divisors is \(d=p_1^{\min\{r_1,s_1\}}\cdots p_n^{\min\{r_n,s_n\}}\).

This actually shows that the property of greatest common divisors existing is weaker than the unique factorization theorem holding2.

Example: quadratic integer rings

Related reading: Quadratic fields

An understanding of abstract algebra cannot do without examples. It was precisely because the research on Fermat's Last Theorem required studying the properties of a class of algebraic integers that today's ring theory gradually developed3. Here we discuss the simplest algebraic integers, namely quadratic integers. The proofs of many results in this part require sophisticated knowledge of algebraic number theory and are therefore omitted.

A quadratic integer is a complex root of a quadratic equation \(\alpha^2+b\alpha+c=0\) with integer coefficients and leading coefficient one. All quadratic integers, and only they, have the form

\[ \alpha=a+b\omega,~(a,b\in\mathbf Z) \]

where

\[ \omega=\begin{cases} \dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2},& D\equiv 1\pmod 4,\\ \sqrt D,& D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4, \end{cases} \]

in which \(D\) is squarefree.

Analysis

By the quadratic formula, we know that a root of this equation can necessarily be written as

\[ \alpha=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4c}}{2}. \]

When \(b=2k+1\) is odd, this root can be written as

\[ \alpha=-k-\frac{1\pm\sqrt{4(k^2+k-c)+1}}{2}. \]

Otherwise, when \(b=2k\) is even, this root can be written as

\[ \alpha=-k\pm\sqrt{k^2-c}. \]

From this one concludes that quadratic integers necessarily have the above form.

It is easy to verify that for such \(\omega\), the set \(\mathbf Z[\omega]=\{a+b\omega:a,b\in\mathbf Z\}\) forms a ring. This is called a quadratic integer ring, and its field of fractions is exactly the quadratic field \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt D)\). When \(D>0\), all quadratic integers are real, so it is also called a real quadratic integer ring; when \(D<0\), all quadratic integers other than the integers are complex, so it is also called an imaginary quadratic integer ring.

All quadratic integer rings \(\mathbf Z[\omega]\) are integral domains. Among them, when \(D=-1\), \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-1}]\) (also written \(\mathbf Z[\mathrm{i}]\)) is also called the ring of Gaussian integers; when \(D=-3\), \(\mathbf Z\left[\dfrac{1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}\right]\) is called the ring of Eisenstein integers.

For a quadratic integer \(a+b\omega\), its conjugate can be defined as \(a+b\bar\omega\), where

\[ \bar\omega=\begin{cases} \dfrac{1-\sqrt{D}}{2},& D\equiv 1\pmod 4,\\ -\sqrt D,& D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4, \end{cases} \]

Note that since quadratic integers are real when \(D>0\), the concept of conjugate here does not fully coincide with the concept of the conjugate of a complex number, but both are special cases of the concept of conjugates of algebraic elements in field theory. Conjugate quadratic integers are roots of the same quadratic equation with integer coefficients.

On a quadratic integer ring one can define the norm

\[ \begin{aligned} N(a+b\omega)&=(a+b\omega)(a+b\bar\omega)\\ &=\begin{cases} a^2+ab+\dfrac{1-D}{4}b^2,& D\equiv 1\pmod 4,\\ a^2-Db^2,& D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4. \end{cases} \end{aligned} \]

The norm of a quadratic integer is always an integer. In particular, when \(D<0\), the norm is always a natural number. The norm preserves the multiplicative structure, that is, \(N(ab)=N(a)N(b)\).

The invertible elements (units) in a quadratic integer ring are exactly those elements whose norm is \(\pm1\). For the case \(D>0\), this amounts to considering the solutions of the Pell equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm1\) or \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm4\). For the case \(D<0\), it is easy to verify that, apart from the two special cases where the invertible elements in the ring of Gaussian integers \(\mathbf Z[\rm{i}]\) are \(\{\pm1,\pm\rm{i}\}\) and the invertible elements in the ring of Eisenstein integers \(\mathbf Z[\omega]\) are \(\{\pm1,\pm\omega,\pm\omega^2\}\), the invertible elements in all other cases are only \(\{\pm1\}\).

The norm \(N(\alpha)\) defined on a quadratic integer ring can be used to prove that it is a Euclidean domain. For the case \(D>0\), its absolute value \(|N(\alpha)|\) has to be used as the norm in the definition of a Euclidean domain. Using the norm obtained this way, it can be shown that, for \(D<0\),

\[ D=-1,-2,-3,-7,-11 \]

or, for \(D>0\),

\[ D=2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 21, 29, 33, 37, 41, 57, 73 \]

the quadratic integer rings corresponding to these integers are Euclidean domains with respect to the norm \(|N(\cdot)|\). However, the norm in the definition of a Euclidean domain need not be the norm defined above. For example, for \(D=14,69\), the corresponding quadratic integer rings are also Euclidean domains, but a different norm has to be used. For the case \(D<0\), it can be shown that the cases listed above are all the Euclidean domains among quadratic integer rings.

Using more sophisticated methods, one can also determine whether a given quadratic integer ring is a principal ideal domain. It can be shown that for \(D<0\), only for

\[ D=-1,-2,-3,-7,-11,-19,-43,-67,-163 \]

are the corresponding quadratic integer rings principal ideal domains. Comparing with the result above, we see that cases such as \(D=-19\) provide examples of principal ideal domains that are not Euclidean domains. For \(D>0\), there is currently no complete result.

However, it can be shown that in quadratic integer rings, unique factorization domains and principal ideal domains are equivalent. The result above shows that, for example, \(\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-5}]\) is not a principal ideal domain, hence not a unique factorization domain either. We have already shown directly by an example that it does not have unique factorization, namely

\[ 9=3\times3=(2+\sqrt{-5})\times(2-\sqrt{-5}). \]

Using the same example, one can show that the ideal \((3,2+\sqrt{-5})\) is not principal either. We will see later that a simple example of a unique factorization domain that is not a principal ideal domain is the polynomial ring \(\mathbf Z[x]\).

Although many quadratic integer rings are not unique factorization domains, they are all Dedekind domains. This means that every nontrivial ideal in any quadratic integer ring can be uniquely factored into a product of a sequence of prime ideals. But if the quadratic integer ring itself is not a principal ideal domain, these prime ideal factors do not necessarily correspond to prime elements, so the unique factorization theorem (that is, the factorization of numbers into products of primes) no longer holds: this was also the original motivation for studying ideals rather than numbers.

Polynomial rings

Related reading: Introduction to polynomial techniques

In algorithmic competitions, one frequently encounters various operations on polynomials. Operations on polynomials such as multiplication, inversion and remainder can be regarded as generalizations of operations on numbers to the polynomial ring. Using the language of abstract algebra, the properties of the corresponding operations in the polynomial ring can be understood more quickly.

Polynomial

For a nonzero commutative ring with unity \(R\), a polynomial over \(R\) is a formal sum

\[ \sum_{k=0}^{n}a_kx^k = a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+a_nx^n, \]

where \(n\in\mathbf N\) and \(a_k\in R\) for each \(k\). These \(a_k\) are called the coefficients of the polynomial, and the corresponding \(a_kx^k\) are called the terms of the polynomial. The \(k\) in the term \(a_kx^k\) is called the degree of that term.

The polynomial all of whose coefficients are zero (that is, the zero element) is called the zero polynomial and denoted by \(0\). For other polynomials, we may assume \(a_n\neq 0\), that is, \(a_nx^n\) is the term of highest degree among the terms with nonzero coefficient. In this case, the natural number \(n\) is called the degree of the polynomial, the term \(a_nx^n\) it belongs to is called the leading term, and \(a_n\) is also called the leading coefficient. A polynomial whose leading coefficient equals one (that is, the unity) is called a monic polynomial. The degree of the zero polynomial is left unspecified, or defined to be \(-\infty\).

The \(x\) appearing in the notation of a polynomial is called the indeterminate of the polynomial. It has no meaning of its own and no range of values. It exists only to mark the position of the coefficients through its exponent. Therefore, a polynomial can also be written as a sequence over \(R\)

\[ (a_0,a_1,...,a_{n-1},a_n,0,0,\cdots). \]

However, such a sequence can have only finitely many nonzero terms. Two polynomials are said to be equal if their corresponding coefficient sequences are the same. This amounts to their formal sums being completely identical after padding with terms whose coefficients are zero. Below, we no longer distinguish between the formal-sum notations of equal polynomials: if necessary, the reader can fill in the missing zero coefficients.

Sometimes one needs to substitute elements of the ring for the indeterminate in a polynomial. For example, let \(f(x)\) be a polynomial over \(R\) and \(a\in R\); then the result of substituting \(a\) into the polynomial \(f(x)\) is \(f(a)\). Its meaning is: replacing \(x\) by \(a\) in the formal sum of the polynomial yields an arithmetic expression in \(R\), and \(f(a)\) is the result of evaluating this expression in \(R\).

“Polynomials” and “polynomial functions”

The reader should not confuse these two concepts. A polynomial is merely a finite sequence of coefficients; it does not automatically become a function. Although the operation of substituting ring elements for the indeterminate described here does map polynomials to polynomial functions, this map need not be injective. For example, \(f(x)=x^p-x\) as a polynomial over the field \(\mathbf F_p\) is clearly not equal to the zero polynomial; but \(f(x)\) as a polynomial function \(\mathbf F_p\rightarrow \mathbf F_p\) is identically zero (this is Fermat's little theorem). Although the two concepts are different, many concepts for polynomial functions can be generalized to polynomials; for example, in imitation of differentiation, indefinite integration and composition of polynomial functions, one can define the (formal) derivative, indefinite integral, composition, etc. of polynomials. These formal operations do not depend on any topological structure, but many rules of computation still hold.

For polynomials

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x)&=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+a_nx^n,\\ g(x)&=b_0+b_1x+\cdots+b_{n-1}x^{n-1}+b_nx^n, \end{aligned} \]

the addition of polynomials is defined as

\[ f(x)+g(x) = (a_0+b_0)+(a_1+b_1)x+\cdots+(a_{n-1}+b_{n-1})x^{n-1}+(a_n+b_n)x^n, \]

and the multiplication of polynomials is defined as

\[ f(x)g(x) = a_0b_0+(a_1b_0+a_0b_1)x+(a_2b_0+a_1b_1+a_0b_2)x^2+\cdots, \]

where the coefficient of the \(x^k\) term is \(\sum_{i=0}^ka_{k-i}b_i\). Under the addition and multiplication defined this way, it can be shown that all polynomials over \(R\) form a ring, denoted by \(R[x]\).

The degree of a polynomial \(f(x)\) is denoted by \(\deg f(x)\). The polynomials of degree zero are the constant polynomials; together with the zero polynomial, they constitute the embedding of \(R\) into \(R[x]\). Clearly, \(R\) has zero divisors if and only if \(R[x]\) has zero divisors.

Theorem

The polynomial ring \(R[x]\) is an integral domain if and only if \(R\) is an integral domain.

In the polynomial ring \(R[x]\) over an integral domain \(R\), the results of addition and multiplication satisfy

\[ \begin{aligned} \deg(f(x)+g(x)) &\le \max\{\deg f(x),\deg g(x)\},\\ \deg(f(x)g(x)) &= \deg f(x) + \deg g(x). \end{aligned} \]

Here we set \(\deg 0 = -\infty\). Therefore, the invertible elements of the polynomial ring are necessarily just the invertible elements among its constant polynomials. No polynomial of degree one or higher is invertible.

The discussion below is restricted to polynomials over integral domains.

Convention

Below, the phrases “polynomial over the ring \(R\)” and “polynomial in the polynomial ring \(R[x]\)” are used interchangeably. For example, a polynomial being irreducible over the ring \(R\) means that the polynomial is irreducible in the ring \(R[x]\). Moreover, if \(R\) is a subring of \(S\), then a polynomial over \(R\) automatically becomes a polynomial over \(S\); this is not further remarked upon either.

Polynomial rings over a field

Among polynomial rings over integral domains, the ones with the simplest properties are, of course, polynomial rings over a field. In the polynomial ring \(F[x]\) over a field \(F\), the coefficients can be divided, so division with remainder can be defined. Let the norm of a nonzero polynomial \(f(x)\) be \(N(f(x))=\deg f(x)\). Then for a polynomial \(f(x)\) and a nonzero polynomial \(g(x)\) in \(F[x]\), division with remainder can obviously be performed:

\[ f(x)=g(x)q(x)+r(x), \]

where \(q(x),r(x)\in F[x]\), and \(r(x)=0\) or \(\deg r(x)<\deg g(x)\). This shows that polynomial rings over a field are all Euclidean domains.

Theorem

The polynomial ring \(F[x]\) over a field \(F\) is a Euclidean domain, hence also a principal ideal domain and a unique factorization domain.

In algorithmic competitions, due to computational precision, one often considers the polynomial ring \(\mathbf F_p[x]=(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)[x]\), where the modulus \(p\) is required to be a prime. Such a ring admits the Euclidean algorithm and similar operations. However, the polynomial ring \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)[x]\) for an arbitrary modulus \(n\) is not even an integral domain.

Division with remainder holding means that a root of a polynomial always corresponds to a linear factor of it.

Root

A root of a polynomial \(f(x)\) is an element \(\xi\in F\) such that \(f(\xi)=0\) holds.

Theorem

For a polynomial \(f(x)\) over a field \(F\) and an element \(\xi\in F\) of the field, \(\xi\) is a root of \(f(x)\) if and only if \(f(x)\) has the linear factor \((x-\xi)\).

Proof

Division with remainder shows that there exist \(q(x),r(x)\) such that \(f(x)=q(x)(x-\xi)+r(x)\) holds with \(\deg r(x)<\deg(x-\xi)=1\). Therefore, \(r(x)\) is a constant polynomial or the zero polynomial; let \(r(x)=c\), then necessarily \(f(x)=q(x)(x-\xi)+c\). Substituting \(x=\xi\), we get \(0=f(\xi)=c\), that is, \(f(x)=q(x)(x-\xi)\).

The concept of a root can be generalized to the case of multiple roots.

Multiple root

If the polynomial \(f(x)\) has the factor \((x-\xi)^k\), and \((x-\xi)^{k+1}\) does not divide \(f(x)\), then \(\xi\) is called a root of multiplicity \(k\) of \(f(x)\). If \(k>1\), the root \(\xi\) is called a multiple root of \(f(x)\); if \(k=1\), the root \(\xi\) is called a simple root of \(f(x)\).

Theorem

If a polynomial \(f(x)\) over a field \(F\) has the (possibly repeated) roots \(\xi_1,\cdots,\xi_k\), then it necessarily has the factor \((x-\xi_1)\cdots(x-\xi_k)\). Furthermore, if a polynomial \(f(x)\) over a field \(F\) has degree \(n\), then it has at most \(n\) roots (counted with multiplicity).

Proof

It suffices to note that \(F[x]\) is a unique factorization domain.

Although the unique factorization theorem holds for polynomials over a field, there is no general method to determine whether a given polynomial is reducible. The cases of small degree are relatively easy. For example, all polynomials of degree one are irreducible. Over special fields, all irreducible polynomials are of degree one. Such fields are called algebraically closed fields. Over such a field, every polynomial of degree greater than zero has a root, so every polynomial of degree greater than one can be factored further. One such example is the field of complex numbers \(\mathbf C\). Over the field of real numbers \(\mathbf R\), on the other hand, there exist irreducible polynomials of degree two; over the field of rational numbers \(\mathbf Q\), the structure of irreducible polynomials is even more complicated. The Field theory page has more discussion of polynomials over the field of rational numbers and over finite fields.

The results above all concern polynomials over a field. Polynomials over more general integral domains can often be reduced to this case.

Now consider the polynomial ring \(R[x]\) over a unique factorization domain \(R\). When computing directly in \(R[x]\), many operations are restricted because the coefficients often cannot be divided. Let us consider extending \(R\) to its field of fractions \(F\), and then factoring a polynomial \(f(x)\) of \(R[x]\) in \(F[x]\). We know that \(F[x]\) is a unique factorization domain, so the factorization in \(R[x]\) can be deduced from the factorization of \(f(x)\) in \(F[x]\). Fortunately, this idea is always feasible.

Gauss's lemma

For a unique factorization domain \(R\) and its field of fractions \(F\), if \(f(x)\in R[x]\), then if \(f(x)=A(x)B(x)\) in \(F[x]\), there necessarily exist \(s,t\in F\) such that \(a(x)=sA(x)\in R[x]\), \(b(x)=tB(x)\in R[x]\), and \(f(x)=a(x)b(x)\). Therefore, if \(f(x)\) of degree greater than zero is irreducible in \(R[x]\), then it is irreducible in \(F[x]\).

Proof

Let \(f(x)\in R[x]\) be reducible in \(F[x]\), with \(f(x)=A(x)B(x)\). Let \(r_a\) and \(r_b\) be the least common multiples of the denominators of all coefficients of \(A(x)\) and \(B(x)\), respectively; then \(\tilde a(x)=r_aA(x)\) and \(\tilde b(x)=r_bB(x)\) are both polynomials over \(R\). Let \(r=r_ar_b\); then \(rf(x)=\tilde a(x)\tilde b(x)\). If \(r\) is invertible in \(R\), we can take the factorization \(f(x)=(r^{-1}\tilde a(x))\tilde b(x)\), which obviously satisfies the requirements of the lemma.

Otherwise, if \(r\) has an irreducible factor \(p\), we need to show that this factor can be cancelled on both sides of the equation while all coefficients remain in the integral domain \(R\). Note that \(p\) is necessarily also prime, so \((p)\) is a prime ideal. Reducing both sides of the equation modulo \(p\), we get the polynomial equation \(0=\bar a(x)\bar b(x)\) over \((R/(p))[x]\), where \(\bar a\) and \(\bar b\) are the reduced polynomials. Since \(R/(p)\) is an integral domain, \((R/(p))[x]\) is also an integral domain, so we may assume \(\bar a(x)=0\). This means that \(p\) divides all coefficients of \(\tilde a(x)\). Therefore, the factor \(p\) can be cancelled directly on both sides of the equation.

By the definition of a unique factorization domain, \(r\) has at most finitely many such irreducible factors, so after cancelling them finitely many times we are reduced to the case where \(r\) is invertible in \(R\). This proves the lemma.

Corollary

For a unique factorization domain \(R\) and its field of fractions \(F\), if \(f(x)\in R[x]\) and all coefficients of \(f(x)\) are coprime (that is, their greatest common divisor is the unity of \(R\)), then \(f(x)\) is irreducible in \(R[x]\) if and only if \(f(x)\) is irreducible in \(F[x]\).

That is, the irreducibles of the ring of integer-coefficient polynomials \(\mathbf Z[x]\) are all irreducibles of \(\mathbf Q[x]\). An effective method for determining whether an integer-coefficient polynomial is irreducible is Eisenstein's criterion. By Gauss's lemma, it also provides a method for determining whether a rational-coefficient polynomial is irreducible.

Eisenstein's criterion

Let \(f(x)=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+a_nx^n\) be an integer-coefficient polynomial of degree \(n\). If there exists a prime \(p\) such that \(p\mid a_i\) holds for all \(i=0,1,\cdots,n-1\), \(p\) does not divide \(a_n\), and \(p^2\) does not divide \(a_0\), then the polynomial \(f(x)\) is irreducible over the field of rational numbers \(\mathbf Q\). If \(\gcd(a_0,a_1,\cdots,a_n)=1\), then the polynomial \(f(x)\) is also irreducible over the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\).

Proof

By Gauss's lemma, if the polynomial \(f(x)\) is reducible over the field of rational numbers \(\mathbf Q\), then it is also reducible over the ring of integers \(\mathbf Z\). Let \(f(x)=b(x)c(x)\) be its factorization in \(\mathbf Z[x]\). Reducing both sides of the equation modulo the prime \(p\), we get a factorization in \(\mathbf F_p[x]\), \(\overline{f}(x)=\overline{b}(x)\overline{c}(x)\). But the hypotheses of the theorem say that \(\overline{f}(x)=x^n\), so there necessarily exists an integer \(m\) such that \(\overline b(x)=x^m\) and \(\overline c(x)=x^{n-m}\), where \(0<m<n\). Hence, the constant terms \(b_0\) and \(c_0\) of the factors \(b(x)\) and \(c(x)\) are both multiples of \(p\). Hence, the constant term \(a_0=b_0c_0\) of \(f(x)\) is necessarily a multiple of \(p^2\). This contradicts the given hypothesis.

Examples
  1. The polynomial \(x^3-2\) is irreducible in \(\mathbf Q[x]\). It suffices to apply Eisenstein's criterion with \(p=2\).
  2. The polynomial \(x^4+1\) is irreducible in \(\mathbf Q[x]\). Otherwise, \((x+1)^4+1=x^4+4x^3+6x^2+4x+2\) would be reducible as well. But applying Eisenstein's criterion with \(p=2\) shows that the latter is not reducible.

For a unique factorization domain \(R\), since the polynomial ring over the corresponding field of fractions \(F\) is a unique factorization domain, and Gauss's lemma shows that the factorizations of polynomials over the field of fractions \(F\) and over the original integral domain \(R\) correspond to each other, \(R[x]\) is also a unique factorization domain. Therefore, we have the following theorem:

Theorem

The polynomial ring \(R[x]\) is a unique factorization domain if and only if \(R\) is a unique factorization domain.

Here \(\mathbf Z[x]\) provides an example that a unique factorization domain need not be a principal ideal domain. For example, in \(\mathbf Z[x]\), \((2,x)\) is not a principal ideal.

There are many ways to extend a polynomial ring to a larger set. For example, for the polynomial ring \(R[x]\) over an integral domain, it can be extended to its field of fractions, denoted by \(R(x)\). This field of fractions is often called the field of rational fractions, whose elements have the basic form \(\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}\), where \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) are both polynomials.

Multivariate polynomial rings

Polynomial rings can be generalized to the case of several indeterminates. For a commutative ring with unity \(R\), one can define the polynomial ring over \(R\), namely the univariate polynomial ring \(R[x]\). Further, one can define the polynomial ring \(R[x][y]\) over \(R[x]\), which can be regarded as the bivariate polynomial ring \(R[x,y]\) over \(R\). In this way, the polynomial ring in \(k\) variables \(R[x_1,\cdots,x_k]\) over \(R\) can be defined inductively. When \(R\) is an integral domain, every multivariate polynomial ring over it is an integral domain; similarly, the property of being a unique factorization domain is also passed on to every multivariate polynomial ring.

Ring of formal power series

One can also consider the case where arbitrarily many coefficients in the formal sum may be nonzero. A formal power series over a commutative ring with unity \(R\) is defined as

\[ \sum_{k=0}^\infty a_kx^k=a_0+a_1x+a_2x^2+\cdots. \]

Addition and multiplication of power series can be defined in the same way as in the polynomial ring \(R[x]\). Moreover, formal power series then also form a ring, denoted by \(R[[x]]\). The convergence of these formal power series need not be considered, because in fact each formal power series is just its sequence of coefficients, and no further topological structure is attached.

The structure of the ring of formal power series is very interesting. In a polynomial ring over an integral domain, the invertible elements can only be constants. In the ring of formal power series, however, one can have

\[ (1-x)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty x^k=1+x+x^2+\cdots. \]

This phenomenon is general. As long as the constant term \(a_0\) of a formal power series is invertible in \(R\), \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_kx^k\) is necessarily invertible as well. This is because if we set

\[ \left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_kx^k\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty b_kx^k\right)=1, \]

then, writing out the system of equations that the coefficients must satisfy, the expressions for \(b_k\) can be found recursively, involving only the inverse of \(a_0\).

Various operations can be defined on the ring of formal power series, such as inversion, division, compositional inverse, formal derivative, elementary functions, etc.; see Introduction to polynomial techniques for details.

Ring of formal Laurent series

The ring of formal power series can be extended further so as to allow terms of negative degree. A formal Laurent series over a commutative ring with unity \(R\) is defined as

\[ \sum_{k=N}^\infty a_kx^k, \]

where \(N\in\mathbf Z\). Thus, a formal Laurent series can have finitely many terms of negative degree. Extending the previous addition and multiplication to formal Laurent series yields the ring of formal Laurent series, denoted by \(R((x))\). If \(F\) is a field, then \(F((x))\) is also a field.

The ring of formal Laurent series has applications in Lagrange inversion.

Chinese remainder theorem

Related reading: Chinese remainder theorem

In number theory, the Chinese remainder theorem is often used to solve systems of congruences. For general commutative rings with unity, a Chinese remainder theorem can be established as well. Each congruence amounts to specifying the image of the unknown in some quotient ring, so the Chinese remainder theorem in a commutative ring with unity amounts to determining an element of the ring from its images in these quotient rings.

This discussion can be turned into formal language. For a nonzero commutative ring with unity \(R\) and its ideals \(I_1,\cdots,I_n\), consider the ring homomorphism \(\varphi:R\rightarrow R/I_1\times \cdots\times R/I_n\) that maps \(r\) to \((r+I_1,\cdots,r+I_n)\). Here, \(r+I_i\) is a coset, and \(\times\) denotes the direct product of rings:

Direct product

For rings \(R_1\) and \(R_2\), multiplication on the direct product \(R_1\times R_2\) of their additive groups can be defined componentwise; then \(R_1\times R_2\) becomes a ring, called the direct product of the rings \(R_1\) and \(R_2\), still denoted by \(R_1\times R_2\).

The kernel of the homomorphism \(\varphi\) is \(\ker\varphi=I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_n\). The question the Chinese remainder theorem answers is under what conditions such a map is surjective.

In the number-theoretic case, the theorem requires the moduli to be coprime. This condition can be generalized to the ring-theoretic setting.

Comaximal ideals

Let the ring \(R\) have ideals \(I\) and \(J\). If \(I+J=R\), then \(I\) and \(J\) are said to be comaximal.

In the case of rings with unity, if we consider the principal ideals \((a)\) and \((b)\), this condition amounts to the existence of \(x,y\in R\) such that \(ax+by=1\), which is similar to Bézout's identity for coprime integers. Using this definition, one can establish the Chinese remainder theorem in commutative rings with unity, completely in imitation of the case of the ring of integers.

Chinese remainder theorem

Let the nonzero commutative ring with unity \(R\) have ideals \(I_1,\cdots,I_n\). If they are pairwise comaximal, then the ring homomorphism \(\varphi\) defined above is surjective, and its kernel equals the product of these ideals, \(\ker\varphi=I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_n=I_1\cdots I_n\); therefore,

\[ R/(I_1\cdots I_n)=R/(I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_n)\cong R/I_1\times\cdots\times R/I_n. \]
Proof

The content of the theorem is rich, but only two claims remain to be proved, namely that \(\varphi\) is surjective and that \(I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_n=I_1\cdots I_n\). The key is to make good use of the comaximality condition.

First we prove the case \(n=2\). Since the ideals \(I_1\) and \(I_2\) are comaximal, that is, \(I_1+I_2=R\), the unity \(1\) of \(R\) can be written in the form \(a_1+a_2\), where \(a_i\in I_i\). Since \(a_1\in I_1\) and \(a_1=1-a_2\in 1+I_2\), we have \(\varphi(a_1)=(I_1,1+I_2)\); similarly, \(\varphi(a_2)=(1+I_1,I_2)\). Therefore, \((\varphi(a_2),\varphi(a_1))\) plays a role similar to a “basis” in a vector space. Hence, for any image \((r_1+I_1,r_2+I_2)\), one can find the preimage \(r_1a_2+r_2a_1\) under the homomorphism \(\varphi\). This shows that \(\varphi\) is surjective.

It remains to prove \(I_1\cap I_2=I_1I_2\). For a general ring we always have \(I_1I_2\subseteq I_1\cap I_2\); the key is the reverse inclusion. For any \(r\in I_1\cap I_2\), we have \(r=r(a_1+a_2)=ra_1+ra_2\in I_1I_2\). Hence \(I_1\cap I_2\subseteq I_1I_2\) holds as well. This proves the claim.

For the case \(n>2\), mathematical induction is needed. The key of the induction step is to show that for pairwise comaximal ideals \(I_1,\cdots,I_n\), the ideals \(I_1\) and \(I_2\cdots I_n\) are always comaximal. Since \(I_1\) is comaximal with each of \(I_2,\cdots,I_n\), for each \(i=2,\cdots,n\) there exist \(a_i\in I_1\) and \(b_i\in I_i\) such that \(1=a_i+b_i\) holds. Therefore, \(1=(a_2+b_2)\cdots(a_n+b_n)\) holds. Hence, \(1\in (b_2\cdots b_n)+I_1\subseteq I_1+(I_2\cdots I_n)\). This shows that the ideals \(I_1\) and \(I_2\cdots I_n\) are comaximal.

Application: Lagrange interpolation formula

Related reading: Lagrange interpolation, Fast polynomial interpolation

The interpolation problem is: given a sequence of point values \(\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n\), find a polynomial \(f(x)\) over a field \(F\) such that \(f(x_i)=y_i\) holds for all \(i=1,\cdots,n\). Of course, all \(x_i\) are assumed to be pairwise distinct. The Lagrange interpolation formula gives the general solution to this kind of problem.

For a polynomial \(f(x)\) over a field \(F\), the condition \(f(x_i)=y_i\) is equivalent to \(x_i\) being a root of the polynomial \(f(x)-y_i\), hence equivalent to \((x-x_i)\mid(f(x)-y_i)\), that is, \(f(x)\equiv y_i\pmod{x-x_i}\). Therefore, the interpolation problem is equivalent to solving the system of congruences

\[ \begin{cases} f(x)\equiv y_1&\pmod{x-x_1},\\ f(x)\equiv y_2&\pmod{x-x_2},\\ \cdots\\ f(x)\equiv y_n&\pmod{x-x_n}. \end{cases} \]

These linear polynomials \(\{x-x_i\}_{i=1}^n\) are pairwise coprime. By the Chinese remainder theorem, the solution of the problem should have the form

\[ f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^ny_iM_i(x), \]

where \(M_i(x)=m_i(x)\prod_{j\neq i}(x-x_j)\) and \(M_i(x)\equiv 1\pmod{x-x_i}\). By the equivalence derived earlier, this is equivalent to \(M_i(x_i)=1\), that is,

\[ m_i(x_i)\prod_{j\neq i}(x_i-x_j) = 1. \]

We may take \(m_i(x)\) to be a constant polynomial, namely

\[ m_i(x) = \frac{1}{\prod_{j\neq i}(x_i-x_j)}. \]

From this we obtain the Lagrange interpolation formula

\[ f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^ny_i\frac{\prod_{j\neq i}(x-x_j)}{\prod_{j\neq i}(x_i-x_j)}. \]

In general, by generalizing this method, one can also derive the Hermite interpolation formula, which allows prescribing the values of several derivatives of the polynomial at each point.

Application: multiplicative group of integers modulo n

Related reading: Primitive roots, Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups

As an application of the Chinese remainder theorem and of material from group theory, here we discuss the structure of the multiplicative group of integers modulo \(n\). In this section, the bar notation for congruence classes is omitted.

The multiplicative group of integers modulo \(n\) is \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\), that is, the multiplicative group of the invertible elements of the quotient ring \(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\) (also called the group of units). The order of the group \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\) is \(\varphi(n)\), because the necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of an inverse is being coprime to \(n\). Here \(\varphi(n)\) is Euler's totient function. Moreover, the group \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\) is always abelian.

By the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, the modulus \(n\) can be factored into a product of powers of distinct primes:

\[ n=p_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots p_s^{\alpha_s}. \]

It is easy to verify that for ideals of the ring of integers, the condition that the ideals are comaximal is equivalent to their generators being coprime. Therefore, applying the Chinese remainder theorem yields

\[ \mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\cong\mathbf Z/p_1^{\alpha_1}\mathbf Z\times\cdots\times\mathbf Z/p_s^{\alpha_s}\mathbf Z. \]

An isomorphism of rings means that the corresponding multiplicative structures are isomorphic as well, so

\[ (\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\cong(\mathbf Z/p_1^{\alpha_1}\mathbf Z)^\times\times\cdots\times(\mathbf Z/p_s^{\alpha_s}\mathbf Z)^\times. \]

This shows that \(\varphi(n)=\varphi(p_1^{\alpha_1})\cdots\varphi(p_s^{\alpha_s})\), that is, Euler's totient function is multiplicative.

Therefore, to study the case of a general modulus, it suffices to consider the case where the modulus is a prime power \(p^k\). For prime powers, the two cases \(p=2\) and \(p\) an odd prime need to be considered separately:

  • For the case \(p=2\), direct verification gives \((\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_1\) and \((\mathbf Z/4\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_2\). For the case \(k\ge3\), we have \((\mathbf Z/2^k\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_2\times C_{2^{k-2}}\).

    Proof

    Direct computation using the binomial theorem shows that

    \[ \begin{aligned} 5^{2^{k-2}}=(1+2^2)^{2^{k-2}}&\equiv 1\pmod {2^k},\\ 5^{2^{k-3}}=(1+2^2)^{2^{k-3}}&\equiv 1+2^{k-1}\pmod {2^k}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(5\) is an element of order \(2^{k-2}\) in \((\mathbf Z/2^k\mathbf Z)^\times\). At the same time, \(-1\) and \(5^{2^{k-3}}\) are two distinct elements of order two, so \(-1\notin\langle 5\rangle\). Hence, the intersection of \(\langle-1\rangle\) and \(\langle 5\rangle\) is trivial, and by the characterization of direct products of groups we have

    \[ (\mathbf Z/2^k\mathbf Z)^\times\cong\langle-1\rangle\times\langle 5\rangle\cong C_2\times C_{2^{k-2}}. \]
    • For the case where \(p\) is an odd prime, it can be shown that \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\) is isomorphic to the cyclic group \(C_{\varphi(p^k)}\).
    Proof

    To show that \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\) is cyclic, by the fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups it suffices to show that each of its Sylow \(q\)‑subgroups is cyclic. First, for the Sylow \(p\)‑subgroup, direct computation shows that

    \[ \begin{aligned} (1+p)^{p^{k-1}} &\equiv 1\pmod{p^k},\\ (1+p)^{p^{k-2}} &\equiv 1+p^{k-1}\pmod{p^k}. \end{aligned} \]

    Hence, \((1+p)\) is an element of order \(p^{k-1}\). That is, the unique Sylow \(p\)‑subgroup of \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\) is the cyclic group \(\langle 1+p\rangle\).

    For the other Sylow \(q\)‑subgroups (\(q\neq p\)), they can be reduced to the case \(k=1\) via a group homomorphism. Consider the group homomorphism \(\varphi:(\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\rightarrow(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\) that maps the coset \(r+p^k\mathbf Z\) to the coset \(r+p\mathbf Z\). The kernel of this map has size \(p^{k-1}\), so when the map \(\varphi\) is restricted to a Sylow \(q\)‑subgroup (\(q\neq p\)) of \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\), the kernel of the restricted map is trivial, so this Sylow \(q\)‑subgroup is isomorphic to the image of the map, namely the Sylow \(q\)‑subgroup of \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\). Therefore, it suffices to show that the Sylow \(q\)‑subgroups of \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\) are all cyclic.

    Finally, we show that the Sylow \(q\)‑subgroups of \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\) are all cyclic. Since \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\) is a finite abelian group, it can be decomposed according to invariant factors as

    \[ C_{n_1}\times\cdots\times C_{n_r}. \]

    Here, \(n_1\mid n_2\mid \cdots \mid n_r\). Therefore, each direct factor has \(n_1\) elements whose order divides \(n_1\). If \(r>1\), then necessarily strictly more than \(n_1\) elements satisfy the equation \(x^{n_1}=1\). But \(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z\) is a field, and a polynomial of degree \(n_1\) over a field has at most \(n_1\) roots, so \(r=1\). That is, \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_{p-1}\).

    This proves \((\mathbf Z/p^k\mathbf Z)^\times\cong C_{p^{k-1}}\times C_{p-1}\cong C_{\varphi(p^{k})}\).

The structure of the multiplicative group for a general modulus is thereby determined as well. From the results obtained, we know that the multiplicative group of integers modulo \(n\) is cyclic if and only if the modulus \(n\) takes the values

\[ 1,2,4,p^k,2p^k \]

where \(p\) is an odd prime; otherwise, the multiplicative group of integers modulo \(n\) necessarily has a subgroup \(C_2\times C_2\) and cannot be cyclic. When the multiplicative group is cyclic, a generator of the multiplicative group is called a primitive root of that modulus. Therefore, the theorem here gives precisely the necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of primitive roots.

Of course, the analysis of the structure of the multiplicative group contains more information than the condition for the existence of primitive roots. It clearly reflects the orders of the various elements of the multiplicative group. An element \(x\) of the group \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\) satisfying \(x^k=1\), that is, a solution of the congruence \(x^k\equiv 1\pmod n\), is called a \(k\)-th root of unity modulo \(n\); an element of order exactly \(k\) is called a primitive \(k\)-th root of unity modulo \(n\). Using the structure of the multiplicative group, the existence and number of these roots of unity can be computed precisely. Finally, the least common multiple of the orders of all elements of the group \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\), that is, the smallest positive integer \(k\) such that \(x^k=1\) for all \(x\in (\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times\), expressed as a function of \(n\), is the Carmichael function. A series of its properties can all be obtained from the structure of the multiplicative group.
