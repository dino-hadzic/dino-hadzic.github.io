Ovo poglavlje kratko uvodi znanja iz apstraktne algebre. Glavni sadržaj algoritamskih natjecanja trenutačno ne ispituje izravno znanje apstraktne algebre, ali se u opisima algoritama ili u rješenjima zadataka često pojavljuju osnovni pojmovi apstraktne algebre, pa čitatelji koji vladaju osnovnim pojmovima apstraktne algebre mogu brže razumjeti neke algoritme. Stoga ovaj sadržaj nije obvezno znanje za ikojeg natjecatelja, nego je namijenjen samo onim čitateljima koje to zanima ili koji od toga mogu imati koristi. Istodobno, ovo poglavlje izbjegava preopširno i preduboko izlaganje apstraktne algebre1 te se usredotočuje na osnovne pojmove i na dijelove najtješnje povezane s ostalim gradivom OI-ja. Čitatelji koji žele sustavno naučiti apstraktnu algebru trebali bi učiti iz stručnih udžbenika apstraktne algebre.

Kako bismo čitatelju bolje pomogli razumjeti što može dobiti čitanjem ovog dijela, navodimo nekoliko primjera iz algoritamskih natjecanja u kojima se može pojaviti znanje apstraktne algebre:

  • mnogi teoremi teorije brojeva i teorije polinoma posebni su slučajevi rezultata apstraktne algebre;
  • u strukturama podataka, strukture poput segment treea mogu održavati informacije koje čine monoid, a rekurzivne relacije mnogih DP problema mogu se apstrahirati u takvu strukturu monoida;
  • u kombinatorici, stroga formulacija i dokaz Pólyina teorema prebrojavanja zahtijevaju pojmove iz teorije grupa.

Na temelju toga, ovo se poglavlje usredotočuje na osnovno znanje koje se ne može preskočiti i na dijelove izravno povezane s tim primjenama. Za početak, ovaj članak uvodi osnovne pojmove grupe, prstena i polja.

Grupa

Definicija grupe glasi ovako.

Grupa

Neka je \(G\) neprazan skup na kojem je zadana binarna operacija \(\cdot:G\times G\rightarrow G\). Ako oni zadovoljavaju sljedeća svojstva, kažemo da je \((G,\cdot)\) grupa (group):

  1. asocijativnost (associative property): za sve \(a,b,c\in G\) vrijedi \(a\cdot(b\cdot c)=(a\cdot b)\cdot c\);
  2. postojanje neutralnog elementa: postoji \(e\in G\) takav da za svaki \(a\in G\) vrijedi \(a\cdot e = e\cdot a = a\). Ovdje se \(e\) naziva neutralnim elementom (identity element) grupe \(G\), a također i jediničnim elementom;
  3. postojanje inverznih elemenata: za svaki \(a\in G\) postoji odgovarajući \(b\in G\) takav da je \(a\cdot b=b\cdot a=e\). Ovdje se \(b\) naziva inverznim elementom (inverse element) elementa \(a\).
O uvjetu zatvorenosti u definiciji

Ovdje binarna operacija već implicitno sadrži takozvani uvjet zatvorenosti, tj. da za sve \(a,b\in G\) vrijedi \(a\cdot b\in G\). Neki ga tekstovi navode zasebno.

Osnovna svojstva grupe

Za grupu \((G,\cdot)\) uvijek vrijede sljedeća svojstva:

  1. za svaki konačan niz \(\{g_i\}_{i=1}^k\subseteq G\), rezultat umnoška \(g_1\cdot g_2\cdot\cdots\cdot g_k\) ne ovisi o načinu postavljanja zagrada;
  2. neutralni element \(e\) uvijek je jedinstven;
  3. za svaki element \(a\in G\) njegov inverz \(a^{-1}\) također je jedinstven;
  4. zakon kraćenja (cancellation law): za \(a,b,c\in G\), ako je \(a\cdot c=b\cdot c\) ili \(c\cdot a=c\cdot b\), onda je \(a=b\).

Grupe su vrlo česte. Jednostavno rečeno, sve transformacije koje ne gube strukturu automatski čine grupu. Kao primjer uzmimo nekoliko uobičajenih vrsta grupa.

Primjeri grupa
  • Simetrična grupa (symmetric group): sve permutacije skupa \(M\), tj. bijekcije s \(M\) na sam \(M\), čine uz kompoziciju preslikavanja grupu \(S_M\). Neutralni element je identiteta, a inverzni element je inverzno preslikavanje (bijekcija nužno ima inverzno preslikavanje). Ako je skup \(M\) konačan, veličine \(n\), grupa se često označava i \(S_n\) i naziva simetričnom grupom stupnja \(n\).
  • Grupa simetrija (symmetry group) u prostoru: za geometrijski lik, sve transformacije koje ga preslikavaju na njega samog također čine uz kompoziciju preslikavanja grupu. Ona opisuje prostornu simetriju tog geometrijskog lika. Konkretne primjere vidi u Uobičajene grupe simetrija.
  • Aditivna grupa cijelih brojeva: skup cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) uz operaciju zbrajanja \(+\) čini grupu \((\mathbf Z,+)\). Neutralni element je \(0\), a inverzni element je suprotni broj.
  • Multiplikativna grupa cijelih brojeva modulo \(n\) (multiplicative group of integers modulo \(n\)): za modul \(n\), klase kongruencije koje odgovaraju svim cijelim brojevima relativno prostim s \(n\) čine uz operaciju množenja grupu \(((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times,\times)\). Neutralni element je \(\bar 1\), a inverzni element upravo je modularni inverz modulo \(n\) (odgovarajuća klasa kongruencije), čije postojanje jamči Bézoutov teorem. Konkretnu analizu strukture vidi u Multiplikativna grupa cijelih brojeva modulo \(n\).
  • Opća linearna grupa (general linear group): sve invertibilne kvadratne matrice reda \(n\) nad brojevnim poljem \(F\) čine uz operaciju množenja grupu \(GL_n(F)\). Neutralni element je jedinična matrica, a inverzni element je inverzna matrica.

Da bismo bolje razumjeli definiciju grupe, pogledajmo usporedno nekoliko primjera koji nisu grupe.

Primjeri koji nisu grupe
  • Sva preslikavanja s \(M\) na sebe (ne nužno bijekcije) ne čine grupu, jer preslikavanja koja nisu bijekcije nemaju inverzni element.
  • Cijeli brojevi uz množenje ne čine grupu, jer \(2\) nema multiplikativni inverz u skupu cijelih brojeva.
  • Pozitivni cijeli brojevi uz zbrajanje također ne čine grupu, jer pozitivni cijeli brojevi nemaju neutralni element za zbrajanje.
  • Sve klase kongruencije modulo \(n\) različite od nule uz množenje često ne čine grupu. Primjerice, u \((\mathbf Z/6\mathbf Z)\setminus\{\overline 0\}\) umnožak \(\overline 2\times\overline 3=\overline 0\) ne pripada tom skupu, što znači da množenje nije dobro definirana binarna operacija na tom skupu (odnosno, ne zadovoljava zatvorenost).

Katkad je potrebno razmatrati i svojstva ovakvih manje potpunih struktura. Zato se mogu definirati sljedeći pojmovi, koji su općenitiji od grupe.

Polugrupa

Za neprazan skup \(G\) i binarnu operaciju \(\cdot\) na njemu, ako je ta operacija asocijativna, kažemo da je \((G,\cdot)\) polugrupa (semigroup).

Monoid

Za polugrupu \((G,\cdot)\), ako u njoj još postoji i neutralni element, kažemo da je \((G,\cdot)\) monoid (monoid).

Primjeri monoida i polugrupa

Među gornjim primjerima, \((\mathbf N_+,+)\) je polugrupa, a \((\mathbf Z,\times)\) je monoid.

Naposljetku, mnoge poznate operacije na grupama osim asocijativnosti zadovoljavaju i komutativnost. Struktura takvih grupa relativno je jednostavna; one se nazivaju Abelovim grupama, a također i komutativnim grupama.

Abelova grupa

Za grupu \((G,\cdot)\), ako operacija \(\cdot\) zadovoljava i komutativnost (commutative property), tj. ako za sve \(a,b\in G\) vrijedi \(a\cdot b=b\cdot a\), kažemo da je \((G,\cdot)\) Abelova grupa (Abelian group) ili komutativna grupa (commutative group).

Primjeri Abelovih i ne-Abelovih grupa
  • Aditivna grupa cijelih brojeva \((\mathbf Z,+)\) jest Abelova grupa.
  • Za \(n\ge3\) simetrična grupa \(S_n\) nije Abelova grupa.

To su osnovne definicije vezane uz teoriju grupa. Više o teoriji grupa vidi u Teorija grupa ili u odgovarajućim knjigama.

Prsten

Definicija prstena glasi ovako.

Prsten

Za neprazan skup \(R\) i dvije binarne operacije na njemu, \(+:R\times R\rightarrow R\) i \(\cdot:R\times R\rightarrow R\), ako oni zadovoljavaju sljedeća svojstva, kažemo da je \((R,+,\cdot)\) prsten (ring):

  1. \((R,+)\) čini Abelovu grupu, čiji se neutralni element označava \(0\), a inverz elementa \(a\in R\) u odnosu na \(+\) označava se \(-a\).
  2. \((R,\cdot)\) čini polugrupu, tj. \(\cdot\) je asocijativna.
  3. Distributivnost (distributive property): za sve \(a,b,c\in R\) vrijedi \(a\cdot(b+c)=a\cdot b+a\cdot c\) i \((a+b)\cdot c=a\cdot c+b\cdot c\).

Radi jednostavnosti izražavanja, te se dvije binarne operacije \(+\) i \(\cdot\) obično nazivaju zbrajanjem i množenjem tog prstena; u skladu s tim, neutralni element zbrajanja naziva se nula (zero), a neutralni element množenja (ako postoji) jedinični element (identity). Treba paziti da ih ne pomiješamo sa zbrajanjem i množenjem u konkretnim skupovima brojeva te s prirodnim brojevima nula i jedan.

O tome zahtijeva li definicija neutralni element množenja

U nekim definicijama prsten nužno mora imati neutralni element množenja; nasuprot tome, oni bez neutralnog elementa množenja nazivaju se pseudoprstenima (rng ili pseudo-ring). Kad se s tim susretnete, treba prosuditi prema kontekstu. Wikipedija koristi upravo takvu definiciju3.

Aditivna struktura prstena prilično je jednostavna, ali je multiplikativna struktura vrlo primitivna. Stoga, ako po analogiji s grupama postavimo više zahtjeva na množenje, dobivamo sljedeće srodne definicije.

Prsten s jedinicom

Za prsten \((R,+,\cdot)\), ako ima jedinični element, tj. ako postoji neutralni element množenja, koji se označava \(1\), kažemo da je \((R,+,\cdot)\) prsten s jedinicom (ring with identity).

Tijelo

Za prsten s jedinicom \((R,+,\cdot)\) različit od nule, ako za svaki element \(a\in R\) različit od \(0\) postoji multiplikativni inverz (označava se \(a^{-1}\)), kažemo da je \((R,+,\cdot)\) tijelo (division ring).

Komutativni prsten

Za prsten \((R,+,\cdot)\), ako je njegovo množenje komutativno, kažemo da je \((R,+,\cdot)\) komutativni prsten (commutative ring).

Zanimljivo je da se u definiciji tijela \(0\) smatra posebnim elementom multiplikativne strukture. Razlog je to što je \(0 = 0\cdot a = a\cdot 0\)2. Drugim riječima, neutralni element zbrajanja u prstenu pomnožen bilo kojim elementom daje opet sebe. Tako on prirodno ne može imati multiplikativni inverz, osim ako je on sâm neutralni element množenja. Takav je prsten jedino nul-prsten (vidi primjere dolje).

Pouka je da pri razumijevanju multiplikativne strukture općeg prstena treba ukloniti utjecaj neutralnog elementa zbrajanja i promatrati \(R\setminus\{0\}\). Na temelju te zamisli imamo sljedeće definicije.

Djelitelj nule

Za prsten \((R,+,\cdot)\), ako postoji \(b\in R\), \(b\ne 0\), takav da vrijedi \(a\cdot b=0\) ili \(b\cdot a=0\), kažemo da je element \(a\) različit od nule djelitelj nule (zero divisor).

Invertibilni element (jedinica)

Za prsten \((R,+,\cdot)\), ako element \(a\) ima multiplikativni inverz, tj. ako postoji \(b\in R\) takav da vrijedi \(a\cdot b=b\cdot a=1\), kažemo da je element \(a\in R\) invertibilni element ili jedinica (unit).

„Jedinica” i „neutralni element”

Nemojte miješati ta dva pojma. Kako bi se izbjegla zabuna, u dijelu o apstraktnoj algebri upotrebljavat će se naziv „invertibilni element” umjesto „jedinica”.

Djelitelj nule ne može biti invertibilni element, a invertibilni element ne može biti djelitelj nule. Međutim, element različit od nule može biti ni djelitelj nule ni invertibilni element.

Ako prsten nema djelitelja nule, to znači da je skup svih elemenata različitih od nule zatvoren na množenje, tj. \((R\setminus\{0\},\cdot)\) čini polugrupu. Nadalje, ako još zahtijevamo da to bude komutativni monoid, dobivamo definiciju integralne domene.

Integralna domena

Za prsten \((R,+,\cdot)\) različit od nule, ako je komutativan, ima neutralni element množenja i nema djelitelja nule, nazivamo ga integralnom domenom (integral domain).

Iako elementi integralne domene ne moraju imati inverz, svojstvo da nema djelitelja nule već je dovoljno da se u integralnoj domeni uspostavi zakon kraćenja.

Zakon kraćenja u integralnoj domeni

Neka integralna domena \(R\) ima elemente \(a,b,c\in R\) s \(a\neq 0\). Ako je \(ab=ac\), onda nužno vrijedi \(b=c\).

Za opći prsten s jedinicom, ako promatramo samo sve njegove invertibilne elemente, također dobivamo strukturu grupe. Ona se naziva multiplikativnom grupom prstena ili grupom jedinica.

Multiplikativna grupa (grupa jedinica)

Za prsten s jedinicom \((R,+,\cdot)\), neka je \(R^\times\) skup svih invertibilnih elemenata u \(R\). Tada \((R^\times,\cdot)\) čini grupu, koja se naziva multiplikativnom grupom (multiplicative group) prstena s jedinicom \(R\), ili grupom jedinica (unit group).

Neki od najjednostavnijih primjera prstena su sljedeći.

Primjeri prstena
  • Nul-prsten (zero ring): skup \(\{0\}\) uz uobičajeno zbrajanje \(+\) i množenje \(\times\) čini prsten, koji se naziva nul-prstenom. To je jedini prsten sa samo jednim elementom, a također i jedini prsten u kojem su neutralni element zbrajanja i neutralni element množenja jednaki.
  • Prsten cijelih brojeva: skup cijelih brojeva \(\mathbf Z\) s uobičajeno definiranim zbrajanjem \(+\) i množenjem \(\times\) čini prsten \((\mathbf Z,+,\times)\). Zapravo, to je integralna domena, ali nije tijelo.
  • Prsten polinoma: za prsten \(R\) može se nad njim definirati prsten polinoma \(R[x]\). Ako je \(R\) integralna domena, onda je i taj prsten polinoma nužno integralna domena.

  • Kvaternioni (quaternion): po analogiji s kompleksnim brojevima može se promatrati skup \(\mathbf H=\{a+b\mathrm{i}+c\mathrm{j}+d\mathrm{k}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbf R\}\) i na njemu definirati zbrajanje i množenje, pri čemu množenje elemenata \(\mathrm{i},\mathrm{j},\mathrm{k}\) zadovoljava

    \[ \mathrm{i}^2=\mathrm{j}^2=\mathrm{k}^2=-1,\ \mathrm{i}\mathrm{j}=-\mathrm{j}\mathrm{i}=\mathrm{k},\ \mathrm{j}\mathrm{k}=-\mathrm{k}\mathrm{j}=\mathrm{i},\ \mathrm{k}\mathrm{i}=-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{k}=\mathrm{j}. \]

    Tada se može provjeriti da \(\mathbf H\) čini prsten, i to nekomutativno tijelo.

  • Podskup \(2\mathbf Z\) skupa cijelih brojeva uz uobičajeno zbrajanje i množenje čini prsten; on je komutativan prsten bez djelitelja nule, ali nema jedinični element.

  • Klase kongruencije cijelih brojeva modulo \(n\), \(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\), uz zbrajanje i množenje klasa kongruencije čine prsten; on je komutativan prsten s jedinicom (tj. \(\bar 1\)). Takav prsten ima djelitelje nule ako i samo ako je \(n\) složen broj. Stoga je za prost \(n\) prsten \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z, +,\times)\) integralna domena; štoviše, tada je on i tijelo, pa zapravo čini polje. Njegova multiplikativna grupa \(((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times,\times)\) upravo je multiplikativna grupa cijelih brojeva modulo \(n\).
  • Prsten matrica: sve kvadratne matrice reda \(n\) nad prstenom \(R\) uz zbrajanje i množenje matrica čine prsten \(M_n(R)\). Općenito, taj prsten ima djelitelje nule i nije komutativan.
  • Za skup svih podskupova \(\mathcal P(A)\) nekog skupa \(A\), ako simetričnu razliku skupova \(\triangle\) definiramo kao zbrajanje, a presjek \(\cap\) kao množenje, onda \((\mathcal P(A),\triangle,\cap)\) čini prsten. Općenito, taj prsten ima jedinični element, ima djelitelje nule i komutativan je.

Naravno, rasprava o strukturi prstena daleko nadilazi ovo; za više sadržaja vidi Teorija prstena ili odgovarajuće knjige.

Polje

Polje je algebarska struktura s jačim svojstvima od prstena. Konkretno, polje je komutativno tijelo. Naravno, može se napisati i njegova potpuna definicija.

Polje

Za neprazan skup \(F\) i dvije binarne operacije na njemu, \(+:F\times F\rightarrow F\) i \(\cdot:F\times F\rightarrow F\), ako oni zadovoljavaju sljedeća svojstva, kažemo da je \((F,+,\cdot)\) polje (field):

  1. \((F,+)\) čini Abelovu grupu, čiji se neutralni element označava \(0\), a inverz elementa \(a\in F\) u odnosu na \(+\) označava se \(-a\).
  2. \((F\setminus\{0\},\cdot)\) čini Abelovu grupu, čiji se neutralni element označava \(1\), a inverz elementa \(a\in F\setminus\{0\}\) u odnosu na \(\cdot\) označava se \(a^{-1}\).
  3. Distributivnost: za sve \(a,b,c\in F\) vrijedi \(a\cdot(b+c)=a\cdot b+a\cdot c\).

Drugim riječima, polje je algebarska struktura zatvorena na sve četiri osnovne računske operacije: zbrajanje, oduzimanje, množenje i dijeljenje.

Uobičajeni primjeri polja su sljedeći.

Primjeri polja
  • Brojevna polja: skup racionalnih brojeva \(\mathbf Q\), skup realnih brojeva \(\mathbf R\) i skup kompleksnih brojeva \(\mathbf C\) uz uobičajeno zbrajanje i množenje čine polja.
  • Konačno polje (finite field): skup klasa kongruencije cijelih brojeva modulo prost broj \(p\), \(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z\), uz zbrajanje i množenje klasa kongruencije čini polje. Naravno, osim njih postoje i druga konačna polja; njihova je struktura jednoznačno određena njihovom veličinom, a veličina je nužno oblika potencije prostog broja.

  • Polje razlomaka (fraction field): neka je \((R,+,\cdot)\) integralna domena. Može se promatrati skup \(Q\) elemenata oblika \(ab^{-1}\). Strogo govoreći, na skupu \(R\times(R\setminus\{0\})\) definiramo relaciju ekvivalencije: \((a_1,b_1)\sim(a_2,b_2)\) ako i samo ako je \(a_1b_2=a_2b_1\). Tada je skup \(Q\) skup klasa ekvivalencije te relacije, \(R\times(R\setminus\{0\})/\sim\), pri čemu se klasa ekvivalencije koja sadrži \((a,b)\) označava \(ab^{-1}\). Ako na njemu definiramo operacije

    \[ \begin{aligned} a_1b_1^{-1}+a_2b_2^{-1} &= (a_1\cdot b_2+a_2\cdot b_1)(b_1\cdot b_2)^{-1},\\ (a_1b_1^{-1})\cdot(a_2b_2^{-1}) &= (a_1\cdot a_2)(b_1\cdot b_2)^{-1} \end{aligned} \]

    onda \((Q,+,\cdot)\) čini polje, koje se naziva poljem razlomaka integralne domene \(R\). Primjerice, polje racionalnih brojeva \((\mathbf Q,+,\times)\) polje je razlomaka prstena cijelih brojeva \((\mathbf Z,+,\times)\).

  • Kvadratno polje (quadratic field): nastaje proširenjem polja racionalnih brojeva \(\mathbf Q\) dodavanjem \(\sqrt d\), gdje je \(d\neq 0,1\) bez kvadratnih faktora. Više o tome vidi u Kvadratno polje.

U usporedbi s prstenima, polja imaju vrlo jednostavnu aditivnu i multiplikativnu strukturu. Stoga je struktura samog polja često vrlo jednostavna. Zbog toga se proučavanje polja uvelike razlikuje od proučavanja prstena: obično se umjesto toga proučavaju proširenja polja i pripadna Galoisova teorija. Na algoritamskim natjecanjima katkad je potrebno računati u proširenjima polja racionalnih brojeva ili konačnih polja. Sadržaj iz teorije polja vidi u Teorija polja ili u odgovarajućim knjigama.

Primjene

Na kraju, na primjeru sljedećeg zadatka pokazujemo kako apstraktni algebarski objekti pomažu u analizi konkretnih problema.

【模板】„动态 DP”& 动态树分治（加强版）(Predložak: „dinamički DP” i dinamička dekompozicija stabla, pojačana verzija)

Zadano je stablo veličine \(n\) s težinama vrhova; izvodi se \(m\) izmjena težina vrhova. Nakon svake izmjene treba ispisati zbroj težina najvećeg težinskog nezavisnog skupa na stablu. Zadatak je prisilno online.

Analiza ideje

Ovaj je zadatak predložak za dinamički DP; implementacija ispravne složenosti zahtijeva globalno balansirano binarno stablo, a konkretan primjer koda nalazi se na odgovarajućoj stranici. Ovdje samo u kontekstu tog zadatka analiziramo postupak modeliranja.

Da bismo istaknuli bitno, ovdje zasad ne razmatramo obradu strukture stabla globalnim balansiranim binarnim stablom, nego promatramo DP problem najvećeg težinskog nezavisnog skupa na lancu. Redom promatramo svaki vrh na lancu \([1,n]\); vrh \(i\) može se odabrati (\(1\)) ili ne odabrati (\(0\)). Neka je optimalno rješenje potproblema na \([1,i]\) u tim dvama slučajevima redom \(f_{i,1}\) i \(f_{i,0}\). Tako se može napisati DP jednadžba

\[ \begin{aligned} f_{i,1}&=w_{i}+f_{i-1,0},\\ f_{i,0}&=\max\{f_{i-1,1},f_{i-1,0}\}. \end{aligned} \]

Početne su vrijednosti \((f_{0,1},f_{0,0})=(0,0)\), a konačni je odgovor \(\max\{f_{n,1},f_{n,0}\}\). Da bismo prikazali utjecaj vrha \(i\) na konačni rezultat, dovoljno je primijetiti da se ta rekurzivna relacija može zapisati kao

\[ (f_{i,1},f_{i,0})=g(f_{i-1,1},f_{i-1,0};w_i). \]

To je niz preslikavanja s \(\mathbf R^2\) u \(\mathbf R^2\) koja \((f_{i-1,1},f_{i-1,0})\) preslikavaju u \((f_{i,1},f_{i,0})\); jezikom grupa, te transformacije uz kompoziciju preslikavanja čine monoid. Upravo to segment tree može održavati.

Međutim, ako takve parametrizirane transformacije \(g(\cdot;w_i)\) nemaju posebnu strukturu, opće preslikavanje s \(\mathbf R^2\) u \(\mathbf R^2\) nemoguće je opisati konačnodimenzionalnim podacima. Ovdje je potrebno još jedno opažanje: ako na \(\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}\) definiramo \(\max\) kao zbrajanje, a \(+\) kao množenje, onda \(\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}\) čini strukturu nalik prstenu, pri čemu je \(-\infty\) neutralni element zbrajanja, a \(0\) neutralni element množenja. No to nije prsten, jer njegovi elementi nemaju svi aditivni inverz. Takva se struktura naziva poluprstenom4, a poluprsten \((\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\},\max,+)\) koji se ovdje pojavljuje naziva se tropskim poluprstenom (tropical semiring).

Na temelju tropskog poluprstena \((R,\oplus,\otimes)\) može se definirati množenje matrica nad njim. Naime, za matricu \(A=(a_{ij})\) dimenzija \(m\times n\) i matricu \(B=(b_{jk})\) dimenzija \(n\times p\) može se definirati njihov umnožak \(AB\) kao \((c_{ik})\), čiji je svaki element jednak

\[ c_{ik} = \bigoplus_{j=1}^n(a_{ij}\otimes b_{jk}) = \max_{1\le j\le n}\;(a_{ij}+b_{jk}). \]

S tim oznakama gornja se rekurzivna relacija može shvatiti kao linearna transformacija nad tropskim poluprstenom i matričnim jezikom zapisati kao

\[ \left(\begin{matrix}f_{i,1}\\f_{i,0}\end{matrix}\right) =\left(\begin{matrix}-\infty&w_i\\0&0\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}f_{i-1,1}\\f_{i-1,0}\end{matrix}\right). \]

Stoga, dovoljno je segment treeom održavati umnožak tih matrica nad tropskim poluprstenom da bi se odgovorilo na problem dinamičkog DP-a na lancu s više izmjena.

Vratimo se sada na verziju problema na stablu. Za vrh \(i\) stabla, skup njegove djece označimo \(S(i)\); tada DP jednadžba na tom mjestu glasi

\[ \begin{aligned} f_{i,1}&=w_i+\sum_{j\in S(i)}f_{j,0},\\ f_{i,0}&=\sum_{j\in S(i)}\max\{f_{j,1},f_{j,0}\}. \end{aligned} \]

Najprije heavy-light dekompozicijom problem svodimo na verziju na lancu. Neka je \(h\) teško dijete vrha \(i\); tada se gornja rekurzivna jednadžba može zapisati kao

\[ \begin{aligned} f_{i,1}&=w_i+f_{h,0}+g_{i,1},\\ f_{i,0}&=\max\{f_{h,0},f_{h,1}\}+g_{i,0}, \end{aligned} \]

gdje

\[ \begin{aligned} g_{i,1}&=\sum_{j\in S(i),\ j\neq h}f_{j,0},\\ g_{i,0}&=\sum_{j\in S(i),\ j\neq h}\max\{f_{j,1},f_{j,0}\} \end{aligned} \]

sažimaju doprinos lake djece. Prema gore opisanom, sve se te transformacije mogu zapisati u matričnom obliku nad tropskim poluprstenom, pa se i cijeli problem može održavati segment treeom nad heavy-light dekompozicijom. Međutim, jedna izmjena izravno s heavy-light dekompozicijom i segment treeom košta \(O(\log^2n)\), pa je potrebno gore spomenuto globalno balansirano binarno stablo kojim se to optimizira na \(O(\log n)\); naravno, može se održavati i LCT-om.

Ovdje spomenuti tropski poluprsten i gornje matrične operacije zapravo nisu rijetki. Ako u gornjem tekstu \(\max\) zamijenimo s \(\min\), odgovarajući se tropski poluprsten često koristi u problemima najkraćih putova. Ako kvadratna matrica \(A\) reda \(n\) daje (najmanje) težine bridova između parova vrhova nekog grafa s \(n\) vrhova, onda je element na mjestu \((i,j)\) matrice \(A^k\) najkraća udaljenost od vrha \(i\) do vrha \(j\) koristeći najviše \(k\) bridova; posebno, \(A^n\) upravo je matrica udaljenosti tog grafa. Naravno, u stvarnoj implementaciji ta se potencija matrice ne računa doslovno grubom silom, nego se koristi Floydov algoritam složenosti \(O(n^3)\).

This chapter gives a brief introduction to abstract algebra. At present, the main content of algorithm competitions does not directly test knowledge of abstract algebra, but descriptions of algorithms and solutions of problems often involve basic concepts of abstract algebra, so readers who know the basic concepts of abstract algebra can understand some algorithms more quickly. Therefore, this material is not required knowledge for any contestant; it is only provided for readers who are interested or who may benefit from it. At the same time, this chapter avoids an overly complete and deep treatment of abstract algebra1 and focuses on the basic concepts and on the parts most closely related to the rest of the OI material. Readers who want to study abstract algebra systematically should learn from professional textbooks on abstract algebra.

To help readers better understand what they may gain from reading this part, here are some examples from algorithm competitions in which knowledge of abstract algebra may be involved:

  • many theorems in number theory and on polynomials are special cases of results in abstract algebra;
  • in data structures, structures such as the segment tree can maintain information forming a monoid, and the recurrence relations of many DP problems can be abstracted into such a monoid structure;
  • in combinatorics, the rigorous statement and proof of the Pólya enumeration theorem require concepts from group theory.

Based on this, this chapter focuses on the basic knowledge that cannot be skipped and on the parts directly related to these applications. To begin with, this article introduces the basic concepts of groups, rings and fields.

Groups

The definition of a group is as follows.

Group

Let \(G\) be a nonempty set with a binary operation \(\cdot:G\times G\rightarrow G\) on it. If they satisfy the following properties, then \((G,\cdot)\) is called a group:

  1. associative property: for all \(a,b,c\in G\), \(a\cdot(b\cdot c)=(a\cdot b)\cdot c\) holds;
  2. existence of an identity element: there exists \(e\in G\) such that for every \(a\in G\), \(a\cdot e = e\cdot a = a\) holds. Here, \(e\) is called the identity element of \(G\), also called the unit element;
  3. existence of inverse elements: for every \(a\in G\) there exists a corresponding \(b\in G\) such that \(a\cdot b=b\cdot a=e\). Here, \(b\) is called the inverse element of \(a\).
On the closure condition in the definition

The binary operation here already implies the so-called closure condition, namely that for all \(a,b\in G\), \(a\cdot b\in G\). Some texts list it separately.

Basic properties of groups

For a group \((G,\cdot)\), the following properties always hold:

  1. for any finite sequence \(\{g_i\}_{i=1}^k\subseteq G\), the result of the product \(g_1\cdot g_2\cdot\cdots\cdot g_k\) does not depend on how the parentheses are placed;
  2. the identity element \(e\) is always unique;
  3. for every element \(a\in G\), its inverse \(a^{-1}\) is also unique;
  4. cancellation law: for \(a,b,c\in G\), if \(a\cdot c=b\cdot c\) or \(c\cdot a=c\cdot b\), then \(a=b\).

Groups are quite common. Informally, all transformations that do not lose structure automatically form a group. Let us take several common types of groups as examples.

Examples of groups
  • Symmetric group: all permutations of a set \(M\), i.e. bijections from \(M\) to \(M\) itself, form a group \(S_M\) under composition of maps. The identity element is the identity transformation, and the inverse element is the inverse map (a bijection necessarily has an inverse map). If the set \(M\) is finite of size \(n\), the group is also often denoted \(S_n\) and called the symmetric group of degree \(n\).
  • Symmetry group in space: for a geometric figure, all transformations that map it onto itself also form a group under composition of maps. This describes the spatial symmetry of the geometric figure. For concrete examples, see Common symmetry groups.
  • Additive group of the integers: the set of integers \(\mathbf Z\) forms a group \((\mathbf Z,+)\) under the addition operation \(+\). The identity element is \(0\), and the inverse element is the opposite number.
  • Multiplicative group of integers modulo \(n\): for a modulus \(n\), the congruence classes corresponding to all integers coprime to \(n\) form a group \(((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times,\times)\) under multiplication. The identity element is \(\bar 1\), and the inverse element is exactly the modular inverse modulo \(n\) (the corresponding congruence class), whose existence is guaranteed by Bézout's identity. For a concrete structural analysis, see Multiplicative group of integers modulo \(n\).
  • General linear group: all invertible square matrices of dimension \(n\) over a number field \(F\) form a group \(GL_n(F)\) under multiplication. The identity element is the identity matrix, and the inverse element is the inverse matrix.

To better understand the definition of a group, it helps to look at a few examples that are not groups for comparison.

Examples that are not groups
  • All maps from \(M\) to itself (not necessarily bijections) do not form a group, because the maps that are not bijections have no inverse element.
  • The integers under multiplication do not form a group, because \(2\) has no multiplicative inverse within the integers.
  • The positive integers under addition do not form a group either, because the positive integers have no additive identity element.
  • All nonzero congruence classes modulo \(n\) often do not form a group under multiplication. For example, in \((\mathbf Z/6\mathbf Z)\setminus\{\overline 0\}\), the product \(\overline 2\times\overline 3=\overline 0\) does not belong to this set, which means that multiplication is not a well-defined binary operation on this set (in other words, it does not satisfy closure).

Sometimes it is also necessary to discuss the properties of these less complete structures. Therefore, the following concepts, which are broader than groups, can be defined.

Semigroup

For a nonempty set \(G\) and a binary operation \(\cdot\) on it, if the operation is associative, then \((G,\cdot)\) is called a semigroup.

Monoid

For a semigroup \((G,\cdot)\), if it also has an identity element, then \((G,\cdot)\) is called a monoid.

Examples of monoids and semigroups

Among the examples above, \((\mathbf N_+,+)\) is a semigroup, and \((\mathbf Z,\times)\) is a monoid.

Finally, many familiar operations on groups satisfy not only the associative property but also the commutative property. The structure of such groups is relatively simple; they are called Abelian groups, also called commutative groups.

Abelian group

For a group \((G,\cdot)\), if the operation \(\cdot\) also satisfies the commutative property, i.e. for all \(a,b\in G\), \(a\cdot b=b\cdot a\) holds, then \((G,\cdot)\) is called an Abelian group or a commutative group.

Examples of Abelian and non-Abelian groups
  • The additive group of the integers \((\mathbf Z,+)\) is an Abelian group.
  • For \(n\ge3\), the symmetric group \(S_n\) is not an Abelian group.

These are the basic definitions related to group theory. For more on group theory, see Group theory or related books.

Rings

The definition of a ring is as follows.

Ring

For a nonempty set \(R\) and two binary operations on it, \(+:R\times R\rightarrow R\) and \(\cdot:R\times R\rightarrow R\), if they satisfy the following properties, then \((R,+,\cdot)\) is called a ring:

  1. \((R,+)\) forms an Abelian group, whose identity element is denoted \(0\), and the inverse of an element \(a\in R\) under \(+\) is denoted \(-a\).
  2. \((R,\cdot)\) forms a semigroup, i.e. \(\cdot\) is associative.
  3. Distributive property: for all \(a,b,c\in R\), \(a\cdot(b+c)=a\cdot b+a\cdot c\) and \((a+b)\cdot c=a\cdot c+b\cdot c\) hold.

For convenience of exposition, these two binary operations \(+\) and \(\cdot\) are usually called the addition and multiplication of the ring; correspondingly, the additive identity element is called the zero, and the multiplicative identity element (if it exists) is called the identity. One should avoid confusing them with the addition and multiplication of concrete sets of numbers, and with the natural numbers zero and one.

On whether the definition requires a multiplicative identity

In some definitions, a ring must have a multiplicative identity element; correspondingly, those without a multiplicative identity are called rngs (or pseudo-rings). When encountered, this must be judged from the context. Wikipedia uses this definition3.

The additive structure of a ring is quite simple, but the multiplicative structure is very primitive. Therefore, by analogy with groups, imposing more requirements on multiplication yields the following related definitions.

Ring with identity

For a ring \((R,+,\cdot)\), if it has an identity, i.e. a multiplicative identity element exists, denoted \(1\), then \((R,+,\cdot)\) is called a ring with identity.

Division ring

For a nonzero ring with identity \((R,+,\cdot)\), if every element \(a\in R\) other than \(0\) has a multiplicative inverse (denoted \(a^{-1}\)), then \((R,+,\cdot)\) is called a division ring.

Commutative ring

For a ring \((R,+,\cdot)\), if its multiplication satisfies the commutative property, then \((R,+,\cdot)\) is called a commutative ring.

An interesting point in the definition of a division ring is that it treats \(0\) as a special element of the multiplicative structure. This is because \(0 = 0\cdot a = a\cdot 0\)2. In other words, the additive identity of a ring multiplied by any element gives itself again. Thus it naturally cannot have a multiplicative inverse, unless it is itself the multiplicative identity. The only such ring is the zero ring (see the examples below).

The lesson here is that, when understanding the multiplicative structure of a general ring, one should remove the influence of the additive identity and examine \(R\setminus\{0\}\). Based on this idea, we have the following definitions.

Zero divisor

For a ring \((R,+,\cdot)\), if there exists \(b\in R\) with \(b\ne 0\) such that \(a\cdot b=0\) or \(b\cdot a=0\) holds, then the nonzero element \(a\) is called a zero divisor.

Invertible element (unit)

For a ring \((R,+,\cdot)\), if an element \(a\) has a multiplicative inverse, i.e. there exists \(b\in R\) such that \(a\cdot b=b\cdot a=1\) holds, then the element \(a\in R\) is called an invertible element, or a unit.

“Unit” and “identity element”

Please do not confuse these two concepts. To avoid confusion, the abstract algebra section will use the name “invertible element” instead of “unit”.

A zero divisor cannot be an invertible element, and an invertible element cannot be a zero divisor. However, a nonzero element can be neither a zero divisor nor an invertible element.

If a ring has no zero divisors, this means that the set of all nonzero elements is closed under multiplication, i.e. \((R\setminus\{0\},\cdot)\) forms a semigroup. Further, if we also require it to be a commutative monoid, we obtain the definition of an integral domain.

Integral domain

For a nonzero ring \((R,+,\cdot)\), if it is a commutative ring, has a multiplicative identity element, and has no zero divisors, then it is called an integral domain.

Although elements of an integral domain do not necessarily have inverses, the property of having no zero divisors is already enough to establish the cancellation law in an integral domain.

Cancellation law in an integral domain

Let an integral domain \(R\) have elements \(a,b,c\in R\) with \(a\neq 0\). If \(ab=ac\), then necessarily \(b=c\).

For a general ring with identity, if we consider only all of its invertible elements, we likewise obtain a group structure. This is called the multiplicative group or the unit group of the ring.

Multiplicative group (unit group)

For a ring with identity \((R,+,\cdot)\), let \(R^\times\) be the set of all invertible elements in \(R\). Then \((R^\times,\cdot)\) forms a group, called the multiplicative group of the ring with identity \(R\), or its unit group.

Some of the simplest examples of rings are as follows.

Examples of rings
  • Zero ring: the set \(\{0\}\) forms a ring under the usual addition \(+\) and multiplication \(\times\), called the zero ring. It is the only ring with just one element, and also the only ring in which the additive identity and the multiplicative identity are equal.
  • Ring of integers: the set of integers \(\mathbf Z\) with the usual addition \(+\) and multiplication \(\times\) defined on it forms the ring \((\mathbf Z,+,\times)\). In fact, this is an integral domain, but it is not a division ring.
  • Polynomial ring: for a ring \(R\), one can define the polynomial ring \(R[x]\) over it. If \(R\) is an integral domain, then this polynomial ring is necessarily an integral domain.

  • Quaternions: by analogy with the complex numbers, one can consider the set \(\mathbf H=\{a+b\mathrm{i}+c\mathrm{j}+d\mathrm{k}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbf R\}\) and define addition and multiplication on it, where the multiplication of \(\mathrm{i},\mathrm{j},\mathrm{k}\) satisfies

    \[ \mathrm{i}^2=\mathrm{j}^2=\mathrm{k}^2=-1,\ \mathrm{i}\mathrm{j}=-\mathrm{j}\mathrm{i}=\mathrm{k},\ \mathrm{j}\mathrm{k}=-\mathrm{k}\mathrm{j}=\mathrm{i},\ \mathrm{k}\mathrm{i}=-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{k}=\mathrm{j}. \]

    Then one can verify that \(\mathbf H\) forms a ring, and moreover it is a noncommutative division ring.

  • The subset \(2\mathbf Z\) of the integers forms a ring under the usual addition and multiplication; it is a commutative ring with no zero divisors, but it has no identity.

  • The congruence classes of integers modulo \(n\), \(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\), form a ring under the addition and multiplication of congruence classes; it is a commutative ring with identity (namely \(\bar 1\)). Such a ring has zero divisors if and only if \(n\) is composite. Hence, when \(n\) is prime, the ring \((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z, +,\times)\) is an integral domain; moreover, it is then also a division ring, so it actually forms a field. Its multiplicative group \(((\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z)^\times,\times)\) is exactly the multiplicative group of integers modulo \(n\).
  • Matrix ring: all square matrices of dimension \(n\) over a ring \(R\) form a ring \(M_n(R)\) under matrix addition and multiplication. In general, this ring has zero divisors and is not commutative.
  • For the set of all subsets \(\mathcal P(A)\) of a set \(A\), if we define the symmetric difference of sets \(\triangle\) and the intersection \(\cap\) as its addition and multiplication respectively, then \((\mathcal P(A),\triangle,\cap)\) forms a ring. In general, this ring has an identity, has zero divisors, and is commutative.

Of course, the discussion of the structure of rings goes far beyond this; to learn more, see Ring theory or related books.

Fields

A field is an algebraic structure with stronger properties than a ring. Specifically, a field is a commutative division ring. Of course, its complete definition can also be written out.

Field

For a nonempty set \(F\) and two binary operations on it, \(+:F\times F\rightarrow F\) and \(\cdot:F\times F\rightarrow F\), if they satisfy the following properties, then \((F,+,\cdot)\) is called a field:

  1. \((F,+)\) forms an Abelian group, whose identity element is denoted \(0\), and the inverse of an element \(a\in F\) under \(+\) is denoted \(-a\).
  2. \((F\setminus\{0\},\cdot)\) forms an Abelian group, whose identity element is denoted \(1\), and the inverse of an element \(a\in F\setminus\{0\}\) under \(\cdot\) is denoted \(a^{-1}\).
  3. Distributive property: for all \(a,b,c\in F\), \(a\cdot(b+c)=a\cdot b+a\cdot c\) holds.

In other words, a field is an algebraic structure closed under all four arithmetic operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication and division.

Common examples of fields are as follows.

Examples of fields
  • Number fields: the set of rational numbers \(\mathbf Q\), the set of real numbers \(\mathbf R\) and the set of complex numbers \(\mathbf C\) all form fields under the usual addition and multiplication.
  • Finite field: the set of congruence classes of integers modulo a prime \(p\), \(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z\), forms a field under the addition and multiplication of congruence classes. Of course, there are other finite fields besides these; their structure is uniquely determined by their size, and the size is necessarily a power of a prime.

  • Fraction field: let \((R,+,\cdot)\) be an integral domain. One can consider the set \(Q\) of elements of the form \(ab^{-1}\). Strictly speaking, define an equivalence relation on the set \(R\times(R\setminus\{0\})\): \((a_1,b_1)\sim(a_2,b_2)\) if and only if \(a_1b_2=a_2b_1\). Then the set \(Q\) is the set of equivalence classes under this relation, \(R\times(R\setminus\{0\})/\sim\), where the equivalence class containing \((a,b)\) is denoted \(ab^{-1}\). If we define the operations on it as

    \[ \begin{aligned} a_1b_1^{-1}+a_2b_2^{-1} &= (a_1\cdot b_2+a_2\cdot b_1)(b_1\cdot b_2)^{-1},\\ (a_1b_1^{-1})\cdot(a_2b_2^{-1}) &= (a_1\cdot a_2)(b_1\cdot b_2)^{-1} \end{aligned} \]

    then \((Q,+,\cdot)\) forms a field, called the fraction field of \(R\). For example, the field of rational numbers \((\mathbf Q,+,\times)\) is the fraction field of the ring of integers \((\mathbf Z,+,\times)\).

  • Quadratic field: it is obtained by extending the field of rational numbers \(\mathbf Q\) by adjoining \(\sqrt d\), where \(d\neq 0,1\) is squarefree. For related content, see Quadratic field.

Compared with rings, fields have a very simple additive and multiplicative structure. Hence the structure of a field itself is often very simple. This makes the study of fields very different from the study of rings: one usually turns instead to studying field extensions and the corresponding Galois theory. In algorithm competitions, it is sometimes necessary to compute in extensions of the field of rational numbers or of finite fields. For content on field theory, see Field theory or related books.

Applications

Finally, we use the following problem as an example to show how abstract algebraic objects help analyze concrete problems.

【模板】“动态 DP”& 动态树分治（加强版）(Template: “dynamic DP” and dynamic tree decomposition, enhanced version)

Given a tree of size \(n\) with vertex weights, perform \(m\) modifications of vertex weights. After each modification, output the total weight of a maximum weighted independent set of the tree. The problem is forced online.

Analysis

This problem is the template for dynamic DP; an implementation with the correct complexity requires the global balanced binary tree, and the concrete sample code is on the corresponding page. Here we only analyze the modeling process in the setting of this problem.

To highlight the key points, for now we do not consider how the global balanced binary tree handles the tree structure, and instead consider the DP problem of the maximum weighted independent set on a chain. Consider each vertex on the chain \([1,n]\) in order; vertex \(i\) can be chosen (\(1\)) or not chosen (\(0\)). Let the optimal solutions of the subproblem on \([1,i]\) in these two cases be \(f_{i,1}\) and \(f_{i,0}\) respectively. Thus the DP equation can be written as

\[ \begin{aligned} f_{i,1}&=w_{i}+f_{i-1,0},\\ f_{i,0}&=\max\{f_{i-1,1},f_{i-1,0}\}. \end{aligned} \]

Its initial values are \((f_{0,1},f_{0,0})=(0,0)\), and the final answer is \(\max\{f_{n,1},f_{n,0}\}\). To express the influence of vertex \(i\) on the final result, it suffices to note that this recurrence relation can be written as

\[ (f_{i,1},f_{i,0})=g(f_{i-1,1},f_{i-1,0};w_i). \]

This is a sequence of maps from \(\mathbf R^2\) to \(\mathbf R^2\) that map \((f_{i-1,1},f_{i-1,0})\) to \((f_{i,1},f_{i,0})\); in the language of groups, these transformations form a monoid under composition of maps. This is exactly what a segment tree can maintain.

However, if such parametrized transformations \(g(\cdot;w_i)\) have no special structure, a general map from \(\mathbf R^2\) to \(\mathbf R^2\) cannot be described by finite-dimensional data. Here another observation is needed: if on \(\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}\) we define \(\max\) as addition and \(+\) as multiplication, then \(\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\}\) forms a ring-like structure, where \(-\infty\) is the additive identity and \(0\) is the multiplicative identity. But it is not a ring, because not all of its elements have additive inverses. Such a structure is called a semiring4, and the semiring formed here by \((\mathbf R\cup\{-\infty\},\max,+)\) is called the tropical semiring.

Based on the tropical semiring \((R,\oplus,\otimes)\), one can define matrix multiplication over it. Namely, for an \(m\times n\) matrix \(A=(a_{ij})\) and an \(n\times p\) matrix \(B=(b_{jk})\), their product \(AB\) can be defined as \((c_{ik})\), each of whose entries equals

\[ c_{ik} = \bigoplus_{j=1}^n(a_{ij}\otimes b_{jk}) = \max_{1\le j\le n}\;(a_{ij}+b_{jk}). \]

With this notation, the recurrence relation above can be viewed as a linear transformation over the tropical semiring and written in matrix language as

\[ \left(\begin{matrix}f_{i,1}\\f_{i,0}\end{matrix}\right) =\left(\begin{matrix}-\infty&w_i\\0&0\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}f_{i-1,1}\\f_{i-1,0}\end{matrix}\right). \]

Hence, it suffices to maintain the product of these matrices over the tropical semiring with a segment tree to answer the dynamic DP problem on a chain with multiple modifications.

Now let us return to the tree version of the problem. For a vertex \(i\) of the tree, denote the set of its children by \(S(i)\); then the DP equation there is

\[ \begin{aligned} f_{i,1}&=w_i+\sum_{j\in S(i)}f_{j,0},\\ f_{i,0}&=\sum_{j\in S(i)}\max\{f_{j,1},f_{j,0}\}. \end{aligned} \]

First, heavy-light decomposition reduces the problem to the chain version. Let \(h\) be the heavy child of \(i\); then the recurrence above can be written as

\[ \begin{aligned} f_{i,1}&=w_i+f_{h,0}+g_{i,1},\\ f_{i,0}&=\max\{f_{h,0},f_{h,1}\}+g_{i,0}, \end{aligned} \]

where

\[ \begin{aligned} g_{i,1}&=\sum_{j\in S(i),\ j\neq h}f_{j,0},\\ g_{i,0}&=\sum_{j\in S(i),\ j\neq h}\max\{f_{j,1},f_{j,0}\} \end{aligned} \]

summarize the contributions of the light children. As described above, all these transformations can be written in matrix form over the tropical semiring, so the whole problem can be maintained on a segment tree over the heavy-light decomposition. However, a single modification using heavy-light decomposition plus a segment tree directly costs \(O(\log^2n)\), so the global balanced binary tree mentioned above is needed to optimize it to \(O(\log n)\); of course, it can also be maintained with an LCT.

The tropical semiring mentioned here and the matrix operations above are actually not rare. If \(\max\) above is replaced by \(\min\), the corresponding tropical semiring is commonly used in shortest path problems. If a square matrix \(A\) of dimension \(n\) gives the (minimum) edge weights between pairs of vertices of some graph with \(n\) vertices, then the entry at \((i,j)\) of \(A^k\) is the shortest distance from vertex \(i\) to vertex \(j\) using at most \(k\) edges; in particular, \(A^n\) is the distance matrix of the graph. Of course, in an actual implementation one does not really compute this matrix power by brute force, but uses the Floyd algorithm with complexity \(O(n^3)\).
