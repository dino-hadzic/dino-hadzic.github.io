Definicija

Dirichletov princip naziva se i princip pretinaca ili princip golubinjaka (the pigeonhole principle).

Često se koristi u dokazima egzistencije i pri određivanju rješenja u najgorem slučaju.

Jednostavan slučaj

Ako \(n+1\) predmeta podijelimo u \(n\) skupina, tada barem jedna skupina sadrži dva (ili više) predmeta.

Taj se teorem čini prilično očitim; dokazuje se kontradikcijom: kad bi svaka skupina sadržavala najviše \(1\) predmet, ukupno bi bilo najviše \(1\times n\) predmeta, a zapravo ih ima \(n+1\) – kontradikcija.

Poopćenje

Ako \(n\) predmeta podijelimo u \(k\) skupina, tada postoji barem jedna skupina koja sadrži barem \(\left \lceil \dfrac{n}{k} \right \rceil\) predmeta.

I poopćeni oblik može se dokazati kontradikcijom: kad bi svaka skupina sadržavala manje od \(\left \lceil \dfrac{n}{k} \right \rceil\) predmeta, njihov bi ukupan broj bio \(S\leq (\left \lceil \dfrac{n}{k} \right \rceil -1 ) \times k=k\left\lceil \dfrac{n}{k} \right\rceil-k < k(\dfrac{n}{k}+1)-k=n\) – kontradikcija.

Osim toga, particiju je moguće oslabiti u pokrivač, a zaključak ostaje isti.
Zadan je skup \(S\) i familija \(\{A_1,A_2\ldots A_k\}\) nepraznih podskupova skupa \(S\).

  • Ako vrijedi \(\bigcup_{i=1}^k A_i\), familija se naziva pokrivačem (cover) skupa \(S\).
  • Ako pokrivač dodatno zadovoljava \(i\neq j\to A_i\cap A_j=\varnothing\), naziva se particijom skupa \(S\).

Dirichletov princip tada se može iskazati ovako: za pokrivač \(\{A_1,A_2\ldots A_k\}\) skupa \(S\) postoji barem jedan skup \(A_i\) za koji vrijedi \(\left\vert A_i \right\vert \geq \left\lceil \dfrac{\left\vert S \right\vert}{k} \right\rceil\).

Definition

The drawer principle is also known as the pigeonhole principle (or Dirichlet's box principle).

It is often used in existence proofs and for finding the solution in the worst case.

Simple case

If \(n+1\) objects are divided into \(n\) groups, then at least one group contains two (or more) objects.

This theorem looks fairly obvious; it is proved by contradiction: if every group contained at most \(1\) object, there would be at most \(1\times n\) objects in total, but there are actually \(n+1\) objects, a contradiction.

Generalization

If \(n\) objects are divided into \(k\) groups, then there exists at least one group containing at least \(\left \lceil \dfrac{n}{k} \right \rceil\) objects.

The generalized form can also be proved by contradiction: if every group contained fewer than \(\left \lceil \dfrac{n}{k} \right \rceil\) objects, their total would be \(S\leq (\left \lceil \dfrac{n}{k} \right \rceil -1 ) \times k=k\left\lceil \dfrac{n}{k} \right\rceil-k < k(\dfrac{n}{k}+1)-k=n\), a contradiction.

Moreover, the partition can be weakened to a cover and the conclusion still holds.
Given a set \(S\) and a family \(\{A_1,A_2\ldots A_k\}\) of nonempty subsets of \(S\):

  • If \(\bigcup_{i=1}^k A_i\) holds, the family is called a cover of \(S\).
  • If a cover additionally satisfies \(i\neq j\to A_i\cap A_j=\varnothing\), it is called a partition of \(S\).

The pigeonhole principle can then be stated as follows: for a cover \(\{A_1,A_2\ldots A_k\}\) of \(S\), there is at least one set \(A_i\) satisfying \(\left\vert A_i \right\vert \geq \left\lceil \dfrac{\left\vert S \right\vert}{k} \right\rceil\).
