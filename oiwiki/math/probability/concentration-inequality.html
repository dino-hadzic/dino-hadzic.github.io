Na algoritamskim natjecanjima ponekad se koriste randomizirani algoritmi, čija ispravnost te vremenska i prostorna složenost obično ovise o pretpostavci da je „vjerojatnost nekih slučajnih događaja vrlo mala”. Primjerice, složenost quick sorta ovisi o tome da se događaj „odabrani pivot element gotovo je najmanji ili najveći element” rijetko dogodi.

U ovom se članku kratko predstavljaju neki alati za analizu randomiziranih algoritama i daje nekoliko primjera jednostavnih primjena.

Union Bound

Neka su \(A_1, \cdots, A_m\) slučajni događaji. Tada je

\[ P\left\{ \bigcup_{i=1}^m A_i \right\} \leq \sum_{i=1}^m P\{A_i\} \]

tj. vjerojatnost da se dogodi barem jedan od skupine događaja ne premašuje zbroj vjerojatnosti pojedinih događaja.

Zapravo, ovaj se rezultat može još malo pojačati:

  • Vjerojatnost da se dogodi barem jedan od skupine događaja nije manja od zbroja vjerojatnosti pojedinih događaja umanjenog za zbroj vjerojatnosti da se istodobno dogode svaka dva.
  • Vjerojatnost da se dogodi barem jedan od skupine događaja ne premašuje zbroj vjerojatnosti pojedinih događaja, umanjen za zbroj vjerojatnosti da se istodobno dogode svaka dva i uvećan za zbroj vjerojatnosti da se istodobno dogode svaka tri.
  • …

Sa sve više razina naizmjenične gornje i donje ograde postaju sve oštrije. Ovaj je niz rezultata po obliku sličan formuli uključivanja-isključivanja, a i dokaz je sličan dokazu formule uključivanja-isključivanja, pa ga ovdje izostavljamo.

Markovljeva nejednakost

Neka je \(X\) slučajna varijabla koja poprima nenegativne vrijednosti. Tada za svaki pozitivan realan broj \(a\) vrijedi

\[ P\{ X \geq a \} \leq \frac{EX}{a} \]

Zapravo, budući da Markovljeva nejednakost sama ne koristi nikakvu informaciju o razdiobi slučajne varijable osim očekivanja, ograda dobivena izravnom primjenom te nejednakosti obično je vrlo labava.

Dokaz

Neka je \(I\) indikatorska funkcija događaja \(X \geq a\). Tada je

\[ I \leq \frac{X}{a} \]

pa slijedi

\[ P\{ X \geq a \} = EI \leq E \left[ \frac{X}{a} \right] = \frac{EX}{a} \]

Čebiševljeva nejednakost

Neka je \(X\) slučajna varijabla. Tada za svaki \(a > 0\) vrijedi

\[ P \{ |X - EX| \geq a \} \leq \frac{DX}{a^2} \]

Posebno, za \(a\) jednako \(k\sigma\) vrijedi

\[ P \{ |X - EX| \geq k\sigma \} \leq \frac{1}{k^2} \]

gdje je \(\sigma\) standardna devijacija varijable \(X\).

Dokaz

Iz pretpostavki vrijedi

\[ P \{ |X - EX| \geq a \} = P \{ (X - EX)^2 \geq a^2 \} \]

Primijetimo da je \((X - EX)^2\) nenegativno, pa iz Markovljeve nejednakosti slijedi

\[ P \{ (X - EX)^2 \geq a^2 \} \leq \frac{E(X - EX)^2}{a^2} = \frac{DX}{a^2} \]

Chernoffova nejednakost

Opća Chernoffova nejednakost može se dobiti izravnom primjenom Markovljeve nejednakosti na slučajnu varijablu \(\mathrm{e}^{tX}\):

Neka je \(X\) slučajna varijabla. Tada za svaki \(t > 0\) vrijedi

\[ P\{ X \geq a \} = P\{ \mathrm{e}^{tX} > \mathrm{e}^{ta} \} \leq \frac{E \mathrm{e}^{tX}}{\mathrm{e}^{ta}} \]

Analogno, za \(t < 0\) vrijedi

\[ P\{ X \leq a \} = P\{ \mathrm{e}^{tX} > \mathrm{e}^{ta} \} \leq \frac{E \mathrm{e}^{tX}}{\mathrm{e}^{ta}} \]

Chernoffova nejednakost za zbroj Poissonovih pokusa

Slučajne varijable koje se pojavljuju na algoritamskim natjecanjima obično nisu tako „opće”; možemo ih opisati Poissonovim pokusima iz teorije vjerojatnosti.

Poissonov pokus (Poisson trial) slučajni je pokus sa samo dva moguća ishoda.

Ishod jednog Poissonova pokusa može se opisati slučajnom varijablom \(X\) koja poprima vrijednost \(0\) ili \(1\), s razdiobom

\[ P\{ X = i \} = \begin{cases} p_i, & i = 1 \\ 1 - p_1, & i = 0 \end{cases} \]

Za Poissonove pokuse vrijedi sljedeći rezultat:

Za \(n\) nezavisnih Poissonovih pokusa \(X_1, X_2, \cdots, X_n\) označimo \(X = \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\) i \(\mu = EX\). Tada za svaki \(0 < \epsilon < 1\) vrijedi

\[ P\left\{ |X - \mu| \geq \epsilon \mu \right\} \leq 2 \exp\left( - \frac{1}{3} \mu \epsilon^2 \right) \]

Hoeffdingova nejednakost

Ako su \(X_1, \cdots, X_n\) međusobno nezavisne realne slučajne varijable takve da je \(X_i\in [a_i,b_i]\), i označimo slučajnu varijablu \(X=\sum\limits_{i=1}^n X_i\), tada

\[ P\{ |X - EX| \geq \epsilon \} \leq 2\exp \left( \frac {-2\epsilon^2}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^n (b_i-a_i)^2} \right) \]

I Chernoffova i Hoeffdingova nejednakost ograničavaju koliko slučajna varijabla odstupa od svog očekivanja. Dokazi tih dviju nejednakosti prilično su dugi; zainteresirani mogu pogledati odgovarajuća poglavlja knjige Probability and Computing.

Iskustveno, ako \(EX\) nije previše blizu \(a_1+\cdots+a_n\), ograda koju daje ova nejednakost obično je relativno oštra; ako je pak vrlo blizu (primjerice u UOJ #72 全新做法 (Potpuno novi pristup)), dobivena je ograda obično vrlo labava, pa je tada bolje upotrijebiti Chernoffovu nejednakost.

Primjeri primjene

Primjer: procjena broja π nasumičnim bacanjem točaka

Promotrimo sljedeći algoritam za procjenu točne vrijednosti broja \(\pi\):

U kvadratu \([-1, 1]^2\) nasumično generiramo \(n\) točaka i označimo s \(m\) broj točaka koje padnu u jedinični krug \(x^2 + y^2 \leq 1\); tada se \(\dfrac{4m}{n}\) može uzeti kao približna vrijednost broja \(\pi\).

Pitanje: kako odabrati \(n\) da bi gornji algoritam s vjerojatnošću barem \((1 - \delta)\) vratio rezultat s relativnom pogreškom najviše \(\epsilon\)?

Rješenje

Neka \(X_i\) označava događaj „nasumično generirana \(i\)-ta točka leži u jediničnom krugu”; tada je ukupan broj točaka u krugu \(X = \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\). Trebamo pronaći prikladan \(n\) takav da

\[ P\left\{ \left| \frac{4X}{n} - \pi \right| \geq \epsilon \pi \right\} \leq \delta \]

Gornji je izraz ekvivalentan s

\[ P\left\{ \left| X - \frac{\pi}{4}n \right| \geq \epsilon \cdot \frac{\pi}{4}n \right\} \leq \delta \]

Prema Chernoffovoj nejednakosti dovoljno je zahtijevati

\[ 2 \exp\left( - \frac{1}{3} \epsilon^2 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4}n \right) \leq \delta \]

odakle se dobiva

\[ n \geq \frac{12}{\pi} \epsilon^{-2} \ln \frac{2}{\delta} \]

tj. za \(n = \Omega(\epsilon^{-2} \ln \frac{1}{\delta})\) postiže se tražena točnost.

Primjer: problem nagradne igre

U kutiji je \(n\) kuglica, od kojih točno \(k\) odgovara glavnim nagradama. Izvodite niz nezavisnih, jednako vjerojatnih nasumičnih izvlačenja, a nakon svakog izvlačenja kuglicu vraćate u kutiju. Koliko puta treba izvlačiti da bi s vjerojatnošću barem \((1 - \epsilon)\) svaka nagradna kuglica bila izvučena barem jednom?

Rješenje

Kad bi postojala samo jedna nagradna kuglica, dovoljno bi bilo \(M=n\log\epsilon^{-1}\) izvlačenja, jer je vjerojatnost da je u svih \(M\) izvlačenja promašimo

\[ \Big(1-\dfrac 1n\Big)^{n\log\epsilon^{-1}}\leq e^{\log\epsilon}=\epsilon \]

Sad imamo \(k>1\) nagradnih kuglica, pa je prema Union Boundu dovoljno osigurati da vjerojatnost propuštanja svake pojedine nagradne kuglice ne premašuje \(\dfrac \epsilon k\). Odgovor je stoga \(n \log \dfrac{k}{\epsilon}\).

Primjer: nasumičan odabir polovice elemenata

Zadajte algoritam koji iz \(n\) elemenata jednako vjerojatno nasumično bira podskup veličine \(\dfrac{n}{2}\); zajamčeno je da je \(n\) paran. Jedini izvor slučajnosti koji smijete koristiti je pošten novčić, a broj bacanja novčića pokušajte što više smanjiti (ne traži se minimum).

Rješenje

Prvo se može zamisliti ovakav algoritam:

  • Bacanjem novčića \(n\) puta možemo jednako vjerojatno nasumično odabrati jedan od svih podskupova.
  • Postupak ponavljamo dok veličina odabranog podskupa ne bude točno \(\dfrac n2\).
    • Primijetimo da podskupovi veličine \(\dfrac n2\) čine barem \(\dfrac 1n\) svih podskupova, pa je očekivani broj ponavljanja \(\leq n\).

Ovaj algoritam u očekivanju treba \(n^2\) bacanja novčića.

Drugi algoritam:

  • Nasumičan odabir 1 od \(n\) možemo ostvariti s očekivano \(2\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) bacanja novčića.
    • Konkretno: nasumično generiramo binarni broj s \(\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) znamenki; ako je veći ili jednak \(n\), ponavljamo, a inače biramo element s odgovarajućim indeksom (indeksi počinju od 0) i završavamo.
  • Zatim iz svih elemenata biramo jedan, pa iz preostalih još jedan, i tako dalje, dok ne odaberemo \(\dfrac n2\) elemenata.

Ovaj algoritam u očekivanju treba \(n\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) bacanja novčića.

Spojimo oba algoritma:

  • Prvo prvim algoritmom nasumično dobijemo jedan podskup.
  • Ako je veličina tog podskupa manja od \(\dfrac n2\), drugim algoritmom dodajemo elemente dok veličinu ne dopunimo do \(\dfrac n2\).
  • Ako veličina tog podskupa premašuje \(\dfrac n2\), drugim algoritmom uklanjamo elemente dok veličinu ne smanjimo na \(\dfrac n2\).

Pokušajmo analizirati broj operacija potrebnih u drugom i trećem koraku (tj. broj dodavanja/uklanjanja elemenata):

  • Neka 01-slučajna varijabla \(X_i\) označava je li \(i\) odabran u početni podskup i neka \(X:=X_1+\cdots+X_n\) označava veličinu podskupa; tada je broj operacija u drugom i trećem koraku jednak \(\big|X-\mathrm{E}[X]\big|\). Uzmemo li u Hoeffdingovoj nejednakosti \(t=c\cdot\sqrt n\) (gdje je \(c\) proizvoljna konstanta), dobivamo \(\mathrm{Pr}\Big[\big|X-\mathrm{E}[X]\big|\geq t\Big]\leq 2\mathrm{e}^{-c^2}\). Drugim riječima, dopuštajući odstupanje reda \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) možemo dobiti po volji malu konstantnu vjerojatnost neuspjeha.

Time smo pokazali: ovaj algoritam s velikom vjerojatnošću jamči da broj bacanja novčića ne premašuje \(n+\Theta(\sqrt n\log n)\).

  • Pri tome \(n\) dolazi od bacanja novčića za dobivanje početnog podskupa, a \(\Theta(\sqrt n\log n)\) je ukupni trošak \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) dodavanja/uklanjanja elemenata.
Računanje očekivane složenosti

Analizirajmo još iz drugog kuta i pokušajmo izračunati očekivani broj bacanja novčića ovog algoritma.

Hoeffdingovom nejednakošću odredimo gornju ogradu očekivanog broja operacija u drugom i trećem koraku:

\[ E|X - EX| = \int_0^\infty P\{ |X - E[X]| \geq t \} \mathrm{d}t \leq 2 \int_0^\infty \exp \left(-\frac {t^2}{n}\right) \mathrm{d}t=\sqrt{\pi n} \]

Stoga je očekivani broj bacanja novčića potrebnih u drugom i trećem koraku \(\sqrt{\pi n}\cdot2\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\).

Ukupno, ovaj algoritam u očekivanju treba \(n+2\sqrt{\pi n}\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) bacanja novčića.

Vježba: Balls and Bins

\(n\) kuglica nezavisno i nasumično bacamo u \(n\) kutija. Dokažite: broj kuglica u kutiji s najviše kuglica s vjerojatnošću \(1 - \dfrac{1}{n}\) nije manji od \(\Omega \left( \dfrac{\log n}{\log \log n} \right)\).

Randomized algorithms are sometimes used in competitive programming, and the correctness and time/space complexity of these algorithms usually rely on the premise that "the probability of certain random events is very small". For example, the complexity of quicksort relies on the event "the chosen pivot element is almost the smallest or the largest element" happening rarely.

This article briefly introduces some tools for analyzing randomized algorithms and gives several examples of simple applications.

Union Bound

Let \(A_1, \cdots, A_m\) be random events. Then

\[ P\left\{ \bigcup_{i=1}^m A_i \right\} \leq \sum_{i=1}^m P\{A_i\} \]

that is, the probability that at least one of a group of events occurs does not exceed the sum of the probabilities of the individual events.

In fact, this result can be strengthened slightly:

  • The probability that at least one of a group of events occurs is at least the sum of the probabilities of the individual events, minus the sum of the probabilities that each pair occurs simultaneously.
  • The probability that at least one of a group of events occurs is at most the sum of the probabilities of the individual events, minus the sum of the probabilities that each pair occurs simultaneously, plus the sum of the probabilities that each triple occurs simultaneously.
  • …

As the number of levels grows, the alternating upper and lower bounds become tighter and tighter. This series of results resembles the inclusion–exclusion principle in form, and the proof is also similar to that of inclusion–exclusion, so it is omitted here.

Markov's inequality

Let \(X\) be a random variable taking nonnegative values. Then for any positive real number \(a\),

\[ P\{ X \geq a \} \leq \frac{EX}{a} \]

In fact, since Markov's inequality itself does not use any information about the distribution of the random variable other than its expectation, the bound obtained by applying this inequality directly is usually very loose.

Proof

Let \(I\) be the indicator function of the event \(X \geq a\). Then

\[ I \leq \frac{X}{a} \]

and hence

\[ P\{ X \geq a \} = EI \leq E \left[ \frac{X}{a} \right] = \frac{EX}{a} \]

Chebyshev's inequality

Let \(X\) be a random variable. Then for any \(a > 0\),

\[ P \{ |X - EX| \geq a \} \leq \frac{DX}{a^2} \]

In particular, when \(a\) is taken to be \(k\sigma\),

\[ P \{ |X - EX| \geq k\sigma \} \leq \frac{1}{k^2} \]

where \(\sigma\) is the standard deviation of \(X\).

Proof

By assumption,

\[ P \{ |X - EX| \geq a \} = P \{ (X - EX)^2 \geq a^2 \} \]

Note that \((X - EX)^2\) is nonnegative, so by Markov's inequality

\[ P \{ (X - EX)^2 \geq a^2 \} \leq \frac{E(X - EX)^2}{a^2} = \frac{DX}{a^2} \]

Chernoff bound

The general Chernoff bound can be derived by applying Markov's inequality directly to the random variable \(\mathrm{e}^{tX}\):

Let \(X\) be a random variable. Then for any \(t > 0\),

\[ P\{ X \geq a \} = P\{ \mathrm{e}^{tX} > \mathrm{e}^{ta} \} \leq \frac{E \mathrm{e}^{tX}}{\mathrm{e}^{ta}} \]

Similarly, when \(t < 0\),

\[ P\{ X \leq a \} = P\{ \mathrm{e}^{tX} > \mathrm{e}^{ta} \} \leq \frac{E \mathrm{e}^{tX}}{\mathrm{e}^{ta}} \]

Chernoff bound for sums of Poisson trials

The random variables encountered in competitive programming are usually not that "general"; we can describe them using Poisson trials from probability theory.

A Poisson trial is a random experiment with only two possible outcomes.

The outcome of a single Poisson trial can be described by a random variable \(X\) taking the value \(0\) or \(1\), with distribution

\[ P\{ X = i \} = \begin{cases} p_i, & i = 1 \\ 1 - p_1, & i = 0 \end{cases} \]

For Poisson trials, we have the following result:

For \(n\) independent Poisson trials \(X_1, X_2, \cdots, X_n\), let \(X = \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\) and \(\mu = EX\). Then for any \(0 < \epsilon < 1\),

\[ P\left\{ |X - \mu| \geq \epsilon \mu \right\} \leq 2 \exp\left( - \frac{1}{3} \mu \epsilon^2 \right) \]

Hoeffding's inequality

If \(X_1, \cdots, X_n\) are mutually independent real random variables with \(X_i\in [a_i,b_i]\), and we define the random variable \(X=\sum\limits_{i=1}^n X_i\), then

\[ P\{ |X - EX| \geq \epsilon \} \leq 2\exp \left( \frac {-2\epsilon^2}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^n (b_i-a_i)^2} \right) \]

Both the Chernoff bound and Hoeffding's inequality bound the extent to which a random variable deviates from its expected value. The proofs of these two inequalities are rather lengthy; interested readers can consult the relevant chapters of the book Probability and Computing.

Empirically, if \(EX\) is not too close to \(a_1+\cdots+a_n\), the bound given by this inequality is often relatively tight; if it is very close (for example in UOJ #72 全新做法 (A completely new approach)), the bound given is often very loose, and in that case the Chernoff bound is the better choice.

Examples of applications

Example: estimating π by random sampling of points

Consider the following algorithm for estimating the exact value of \(\pi\):

Generate \(n\) points at random in the square region \([-1, 1]^2\), and let \(m\) be the number of points that fall into the unit disk \(x^2 + y^2 \leq 1\); then \(\dfrac{4m}{n}\) can be taken as an approximation of \(\pi\).

Question: how should \(n\) be chosen so that the above algorithm returns a result with relative error at most \(\epsilon\) with probability at least \((1 - \delta)\)?

Solution

Let \(X_i\) denote the event "the \(i\)-th randomly generated point lies in the unit disk"; then the total number of points in the disk is \(X = \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\). We need to find a suitable \(n\) such that

\[ P\left\{ \left| \frac{4X}{n} - \pi \right| \geq \epsilon \pi \right\} \leq \delta \]

This is equivalent to

\[ P\left\{ \left| X - \frac{\pi}{4}n \right| \geq \epsilon \cdot \frac{\pi}{4}n \right\} \leq \delta \]

By the Chernoff bound, it suffices to require

\[ 2 \exp\left( - \frac{1}{3} \epsilon^2 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4}n \right) \leq \delta \]

from which we obtain

\[ n \geq \frac{12}{\pi} \epsilon^{-2} \ln \frac{2}{\delta} \]

that is, the required accuracy is achieved when \(n = \Omega(\epsilon^{-2} \ln \frac{1}{\delta})\).

Example: the lottery problem

A box contains \(n\) balls, exactly \(k\) of which correspond to grand prizes. You perform a number of independent, equiprobable random draws, putting the ball back into the box after each draw. How many draws are needed to guarantee, with probability at least \((1 - \epsilon)\), that every prize ball has been drawn at least once?

Solution

If there were only one prize ball, then \(M=n\log\epsilon^{-1}\) draws would suffice, because the probability of missing it in all \(M\) draws is

\[ \Big(1-\dfrac 1n\Big)^{n\log\epsilon^{-1}}\leq e^{\log\epsilon}=\epsilon \]

Now there are \(k>1\) prize balls, so by the Union Bound it suffices to ensure that the probability of missing each prize ball is at most \(\dfrac \epsilon k\). The answer is therefore \(n \log \dfrac{k}{\epsilon}\).

Example: randomly selecting half of the elements

Give an algorithm that selects, uniformly at random, a subset of size \(\dfrac{n}{2}\) from \(n\) elements; it is guaranteed that \(n\) is even. The only source of randomness you may use is a fair coin, and you should try to minimize the number of coin flips (the minimum is not required).

Solution

First, one can think of the following algorithm:

  • By flipping the coin \(n\) times, we can choose one of all subsets uniformly at random.
  • Repeat this process until the size of the chosen subset is exactly \(\dfrac n2\).
    • Note that subsets of size \(\dfrac n2\) make up at least \(\dfrac 1n\) of all subsets, so the expected number of repetitions is \(\leq n\).

This algorithm requires \(n^2\) coin flips in expectation.

Another algorithm:

  • We can select 1 out of \(n\) at random using \(2\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) coin flips in expectation.
    • Concretely: generate a random binary number with \(\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) digits; if it is greater than or equal to \(n\), try again, otherwise select the element with the corresponding index (indices start from 0) and stop.
  • Then we select one element from all the elements, then another one from the remaining elements, and so on, until \(\dfrac n2\) elements have been selected.

This algorithm requires \(n\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) coin flips in expectation.

Stitching the two algorithms together:

  • First, use the first algorithm to obtain a random subset.
  • If the size of this subset is less than \(\dfrac n2\), use the second algorithm to keep adding elements until the size is filled up to \(\dfrac n2\).
  • If the size of this subset exceeds \(\dfrac n2\), use the second algorithm to keep removing elements until the size is cut down to \(\dfrac n2\).

Let us try to analyze the number of operations required in the second and third steps (i.e. the number of additions/removals of elements):

  • Let the 0-1 random variable \(X_i\) indicate whether \(i\) is selected into the initial subset, and let \(X:=X_1+\cdots+X_n\) denote the size of the subset; then the number of operations required in the second and third steps equals \(\big|X-\mathrm{E}[X]\big|\). Taking \(t=c\cdot\sqrt n\) in Hoeffding's inequality (where \(c\) is an arbitrary constant), we get \(\mathrm{Pr}\Big[\big|X-\mathrm{E}[X]\big|\geq t\Big]\leq 2\mathrm{e}^{-c^2}\). In other words, by allowing a deviation of order \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\), we can obtain an arbitrarily small constant failure probability.

So far we have shown that this algorithm guarantees, with high probability, that the number of coin flips is within \(n+\Theta(\sqrt n\log n)\).

  • Here \(n\) comes from the coin flips used to obtain the initial subset, and \(\Theta(\sqrt n\log n)\) is the total cost of \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) additions/removals of elements.
Computing the expected complexity

Let us analyze from another angle and try to compute the expected number of coin flips of this algorithm.

Use Hoeffding's inequality to bound the expected number of operations in the second and third steps from above:

\[ E|X - EX| = \int_0^\infty P\{ |X - E[X]| \geq t \} \mathrm{d}t \leq 2 \int_0^\infty \exp \left(-\frac {t^2}{n}\right) \mathrm{d}t=\sqrt{\pi n} \]

Hence the expected number of coin flips required in the second and third steps is \(\sqrt{\pi n}\cdot2\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\).

In total, this algorithm requires \(n+2\sqrt{\pi n}\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) coin flips in expectation.
