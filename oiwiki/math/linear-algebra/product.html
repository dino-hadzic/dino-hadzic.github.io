U ovom se članku uvode jednostavne operacije među vektorima.

Prije samog teksta posebno napominjemo nešto o prijevodu. Iz povijesnih razloga u matematici i fizici postoje najrazličitiji kineski prijevodi dvaju pojmova „inner product” i „outer product”.

U fizici se obično prevode kao „标积” i „矢积” („skalarni produkt” i „vektorski produkt”), čime se izražava da je rezultat operacije skalar odnosno vektor. Nazivi „数量积” i „向量积” („količinski produkt” i „vektorski produkt”) iz srednjoškolskih udžbenika matematike također slijede taj način prevođenja po smislu.

U matematici se obično prevode i kao „内积” i „外积” („unutarnji produkt” i „vanjski produkt”), što je doslovan prijevod tih dvaju naziva. „点乘” i „叉乘” („množenje točkom” i „množenje križićem”) kolokvijalni su nazivi izvedeni iz znaka operacije i također su vrlo česti.

U operaciji „množenja točkom” znak točke često se izostavlja, a u linearnoj se algebri ta operacija čak izravno promatra kao množenje matrica, pa se točka ne piše.

Skalarni produkt

Pojam skalarnog produkta primjenjiv je na vektore bilo koje dimenzije.

Definicija

Skalarni produkt ima više različitih, ali ekvivalentnih definicija; u nastavku navodimo neke od njih.

Geometrijska definicija

U \(n\)-dimenzionalnom euklidskom prostoru \(\mathbf{R}^n\) neka su zadana dva vektora \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}\) i neka je kut među njima \(\theta\). Tada je

\[ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} = |\boldsymbol{a}| |\boldsymbol{b}| \cos \theta \]

skalarni produkt tih dvaju vektora, koji se zove i unutarnji produkt (inner product) ili točkasti produkt (dot product). Pritom se \(|\boldsymbol{b}|\cos \theta\) zove projekcija vektora \(\boldsymbol{b}\) na smjer vektora \(\boldsymbol{a}\). Geometrijsko značenje skalarnog produkta jest: skalarni produkt \(\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}\) jednak je umnošku modula vektora \(\boldsymbol{a}\) i projekcije vektora \(\boldsymbol{b}\) na smjer vektora \(\boldsymbol{a}\).

Algebarska definicija

U \(n\)-dimenzionalnom euklidskom prostoru \(\mathbf{R}^n\) neka su zadana dva vektora \(\boldsymbol{a} = (a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n), \boldsymbol{b} = (b_1, b_2, \dots, b_n)\). Tada je

\[ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} = \sum_{i = 1}^{n} a_i b_i \]

skalarni produkt tih dvaju vektora, koji se zove i unutarnji produkt ili točkasti produkt. Geometrijska i algebarska definicija skalarnog produkta ekvivalentne su u euklidskom prostoru, a potonja je praktičnija za uporabu.

Kad ne može doći do zabune, točka skalarnog produkta može se izostaviti. Ako vektor ima gornji indeks \(2\), to je skraćeni zapis skalarnog produkta vektora sa samim sobom, tj. kvadrat modula vektora, pri čemu se oznaka modula izostavlja. Taj gornji indeks \(2\) ne smije se shvatiti kao kvadrat vektora, jer je rezultat skalarnog produkta vektora skalar, pa skalarni produkt bilo kojeg broja vektora različitog od \(2\) ne postoji. Isto tako, kvadrat kvadrata modula vektora ne može se skraćeno zapisati gornjim indeksom \(4\), nego se rezultat s gornjim indeksom \(2\) mora promatrati kao cjelina, i tako dalje.

Svojstva

Vidimo da je rezultat skalarnog produkta skalar; njegova je posebnost to što je riječ o bilinearnoj operaciji, linearnoj u svakom od dvaju vektora zasebno. Konkretno, skalarni produkt zadovoljava:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol{a} + \boldsymbol{b}) \cdot \boldsymbol{c} &= \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{c} + \boldsymbol{b} \cdot \boldsymbol{c} \\ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot (\boldsymbol{b} + \boldsymbol{c}) &= \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} + \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{c} \\ (\lambda \boldsymbol{a}) \cdot \boldsymbol{b} &= \lambda (\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}) \\ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot (\lambda \boldsymbol{b}) &= \lambda (\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}) \end{aligned} \]

Skalarni produkt zadovoljava i komutativnost, tj.:

\[ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} = \boldsymbol{b} \cdot \boldsymbol{a} \]

Primjene

U nastavku navodimo neke uobičajene primjene skalarnog produkta.

  1. Provjera okomitosti dvaju vektora:

    \[ \boldsymbol{a} \perp \boldsymbol{b} \iff \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} = 0 \]

    Dakle, skalarni produkt dvaju međusobno okomitih vektora jednak je \(0\); skalarni produkt vektora i nulvektora jednak je \(0\). Ako se kao definicija okomitosti uzme da je skalarni produkt jednak nuli, slijedi da je nulvektor okomit na svaki vektor.

  2. Provjera kolinearnosti dvaju vektora:

    \[ \exists\lambda \in \mathbf{R} (\boldsymbol{a} = \lambda \boldsymbol{b}) \iff |\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}| = |\boldsymbol{a}| |\boldsymbol{b}| \]

  3. Računanje modula vektora:

    \[ |\boldsymbol a| = \sqrt{\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{a}} \]

  4. Računanje kuta između dvaju vektora:

    \[ \theta = \arccos \frac{\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}}{|\boldsymbol a| |\boldsymbol b|} \]

Determinante drugog i trećeg reda

Determinante drugog i trećeg reda mogu se posebno definirati kao jednostavniji posebni slučajevi determinante. U posljednjem dijelu matematičke analize, teoriji polja, Greenov teorem koristi determinantu drugog reda, Gaussov teorem (teorem o divergenciji) koristi skalarni produkt, a Stokesov teorem koristi determinantu trećeg reda.

Determinanta drugog reda može se promatrati kao funkcija četiriju varijabli, a definirana je kao:

\[ \begin{vmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{vmatrix}=ad-bc \]

Determinanta trećeg reda može se promatrati kao funkcija devet varijabli, a definirana je kao:

\[ \begin{vmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & f \\ g & h & i \end{vmatrix}=aei+dhc+gbf-ahf-dbi-gec \]

Poseban način pamćenja jest „pravilo dijagonala” (Sarrusovo pravilo); pravilo dijagonala vrijedi samo za determinante drugog i trećeg reda.

Posebno napominjemo: razvoj determinante četvrtog reda ima ukupno 24 člana, a član na sporednoj dijagonali ima pozitivan predznak. Primijenimo li nasilno „pravilo dijagonala” za determinante trećeg reda, ne samo da ne dobivamo dovoljno članova nego je i predznak člana na sporednoj dijagonali pogrešan; stoga „pravilo dijagonala” za determinante trećeg reda ne vrijedi za determinante višeg reda, a determinante višeg reda nije prikladno računati ni izravnim razvojem.

Vektorski produkt

Vektorski produkt operacija je svojstvena trodimenzionalnim vektorima.

U fizici se trodimenzionalni vektori podrazumijevano odnose na prostorni položaj i uvijek se pišu podebljano. Međutim, četverodimenzionalni vektori u teoriji relativnosti ne pišu se podebljano, nego posebnim oznakama i indeksima.

U linearnoj algebri svi se vektori pišu podebljano, a budući da je to zamorno i budući da su u linearnoj algebri operacije uglavnom među vektorima i matricama, pa teško može doći do dvosmislenosti, pri pisanju rukom oznaka vektora može se izostaviti.

Definicija

Vektorski produkt ima više različitih, ali ekvivalentnih definicija; u nastavku navodimo neke od njih.

Geometrijska definicija

U trodimenzionalnom euklidskom prostoru \(\mathbf{R}^3\) vektorski produkt vektora \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}\) definira se kao vektor, označen \(\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\), čiji su modul i smjer definirani ovako:

  1. \(|\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}| = |\boldsymbol{a}| |\boldsymbol{b}| \sin \langle \boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b} \rangle\);
  2. \(\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\) okomit je na oba vektora \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}\), a smjerovi vektora \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\) zadovoljavaju pravilo desne ruke.

Promotrimo li modul vektorskog produkta i prisjetimo se formule za površinu trokuta \(S=\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C\), vidimo da je geometrijsko značenje vektorskog produkta: \(|\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}|\) je površina paralelograma kojemu su \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}\) susjedne stranice.

Algebarska definicija

U trodimenzionalnom euklidskom prostoru \(\mathbf{R}^3\) vektorski produkt vektora \(\boldsymbol{a} = (x_1, y_1, z_1), \boldsymbol{b} = (x_2, y_2, z_2)\) definira se kao vektor \(\boldsymbol{c}\), označen \(\boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\), a rezultat se može prikazati determinantom trećeg reda:

\[ \begin{vmatrix} \boldsymbol{i} & \boldsymbol{j} & \boldsymbol{k} \\ x_1 & y_1 & z_1 \\ x_2 & y_2 & z_2 \end{vmatrix} \]

pri čemu \(\boldsymbol{i}, \boldsymbol{j}, \boldsymbol{k}\) označavaju jedinične vektore u smjeru koordinatnih osi \(x, y, z\) i pišu se na mjestima odgovarajućih koordinata. Razvojem dobivamo

\[ \begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{c} &= \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b} \\ &= (y_1z_2 - y_2z_1)\boldsymbol{i} + (z_1x_2 - z_2x_1)\boldsymbol{j} + (x_1y_2 - x_2y_1)\boldsymbol{k} \\ &= (y_1z_2 - y_2z_1, z_1x_2 - z_2x_1, x_1y_2 - x_2y_1) \end{aligned} \]

Svojstva

  1. Vektorski produkt bilinearna je operacija, linearna u svakom od dvaju vektora zasebno. Konkretno, vektorski produkt zadovoljava:

    \[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol{a} + \boldsymbol{b}) \times \boldsymbol{c} &= \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{c} + \boldsymbol{b} \times \boldsymbol{c} \\ \boldsymbol{a} \times (\boldsymbol{b} + \boldsymbol{c}) &= \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b} + \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{c} \\ (\lambda \boldsymbol{a}) \times \boldsymbol{b} &= \lambda (\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}) \\ \boldsymbol{a} \times (\lambda \boldsymbol{b}) &= \lambda (\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}) \end{aligned} \]

    Prva dva svojstva mogu se nazvati i distributivnost, tj. vektorski produkt distributivan je prema zbrajanju vektora.

  2. Vektorski produkt zadovoljava antikomutativnost, tj.:

    \[ \boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b=-\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol a \]

  3. Prema gornjim geometrijskim definicijama skalarnog i vektorskog produkta:

    \[ \begin{aligned} |\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b| &= |\boldsymbol a| |\boldsymbol b| \sin \langle \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b \rangle \\ \boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b &= |\boldsymbol a| |\boldsymbol b| \cos \theta \\ &= |\boldsymbol a| |\boldsymbol b| \cos \langle \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b\rangle \end{aligned} \]

    može se napisati identitet:

    \[ (\boldsymbol a\times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol a\times \boldsymbol b) = |\boldsymbol a|^2 |\boldsymbol b|^2-{(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)}^2 \]

  4. Vektorski produkt zadovoljava Jacobijev identitet:

    \[ \boldsymbol a \times (\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol c) + \boldsymbol b \times (\boldsymbol c \times \boldsymbol a) + \boldsymbol c \times (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) = \boldsymbol 0 \]

Primjene

U nastavku navodimo neke uobičajene primjene vektorskog produkta.

  1. Provjera jesu li dva vektora kolinearna:

    \[ \exists\lambda \in \mathbf{R} (\boldsymbol{a} = \lambda \boldsymbol{b}) \iff \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b} = \boldsymbol{0} \]

    Dakle, vektorski produkt dvaju kolinearnih trodimenzionalnih vektora jednak je \(\boldsymbol 0\); vektorski produkt trodimenzionalnog vektora sa samim sobom jednak je \(\boldsymbol 0\); vektorski produkt trodimenzionalnog vektora i nulvektora jednak je \(\boldsymbol 0\). Ako se kao definicija kolinearnosti dvaju vektora uzme da je vektorski produkt jednak nuli, slijedi da je nulvektor kolinearan sa svakim vektorom.

  2. Računanje površine paralelograma razapetog dvama vektorima:

    \[ S \langle \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b \rangle = |\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b| \]

Slučaj dvodimenzionalnih vektora

Za dvodimenzionalne vektore vektorski produkt nije moguće izračunati, ali se ipak može izračunati površina paralelograma razapetog dvama vektorima:

Označimo \(\boldsymbol{a} = (m, n), \boldsymbol{b} = (p, q)\) i proširimo pravokutni koordinatni sustav u ravnini do pravokutnog koordinatnog sustava u prostoru tako da izvorna ravnina bude ravnina \(xOy\) novog koordinatnog sustava; izvorne koordinate \((m, n)\) i \((p, q)\) postaju \((m, n, 0)\) i \((p, q, 0)\).

Tada je vektorski produkt tih dvaju vektora \((0, 0, mq - np)\), pa je površina paralelograma \(|mq - np|\), što se može promatrati kao apsolutna vrijednost determinante drugog reda.

Pritom se prema pravilu desne ruke i predznaku koordinate \(z\) može zaključiti kako je \(\boldsymbol b\) usmjeren u odnosu na \(\boldsymbol a\): ako je u smjeru suprotnom od kazaljke na satu, koordinata \(z\) je pozitivna, a u protivnom negativna; skraćeno: u smjeru kazaljke negativno, suprotno od kazaljke pozitivno.

Mješoviti produkt

Kao i vektorski produkt, mješoviti produkt vektora operacija je svojstvena trodimenzionalnim vektorima.

Definicija

Neka su \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) tri vektora u trodimenzionalnom prostoru. Tada se \((\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot \boldsymbol c\) zove mješoviti produkt triju vektora \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) i označava \([\boldsymbol a \boldsymbol b \boldsymbol c]\) ili \((\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c)\) ili \((\boldsymbol a \boldsymbol b \boldsymbol c)\) ili \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c)\). Geometrijsko značenje apsolutne vrijednosti mješovitog produkta \(|(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot \boldsymbol c|\) jest volumen paralelepipeda s bridovima \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\).

Mješoviti produkt vektora može se prikazati determinantom trećeg reda:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot \boldsymbol c &= \det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c) \\ &= \begin{vmatrix} a_x & b_x & c_x \\ a_y & b_y & c_y \\ a_z & b_z & c_z \end{vmatrix} \\ &= a_x b_y c_z + a_y b_z c_x + a_z b_x c_y - a_z b_y c_x -a _y b_x c_z - a_x b_z c_y \end{aligned} \]

Svojstva

  1. Mješoviti produkt linearan je u svakom od triju vektora zasebno; konkretno, vrijedi:

    \[ \begin{aligned} \det(\lambda\boldsymbol{u} + \mu\boldsymbol{v}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{c}) &= \lambda\det(\boldsymbol{u}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{c}) + \mu\det(\boldsymbol{v}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{c}) \\ \det(\boldsymbol{a}, \lambda\boldsymbol{u} + \mu\boldsymbol{v}, \boldsymbol{c}) &= \lambda\det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{u}, \boldsymbol{c}) + \mu\det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{v}, \boldsymbol{c}) \\ \det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}, \lambda\boldsymbol{u} + \mu\boldsymbol{v}) &= \lambda\det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{u}) + \mu\det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{v}) \end{aligned} \]

  2. Mješoviti produkt je antisimetričan: zamjena mjesta dvaju vektora mijenja predznak mješovitog produkta, pa vrijedi:

    \[ \det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c) = \det(\boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol a) = \det(\boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b) = -\det(\boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol c) = -\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol b)= -\det(\boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol a) \]

    Iz toga se može dobiti i sljedeća veza skalarnog i vektorskog produkta:

    \[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot \boldsymbol c = \boldsymbol a \cdot (\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol c) \]

Primjene

Mješoviti produkt vektora ima sljedeće uobičajene primjene.

  1. Računanje volumena tetraedra \(ABCD\):

    \[ V=\frac{1}{6}\left|\det(\overrightarrow{AB}, \overrightarrow{AC}, \overrightarrow{AD})\right| \]

  2. Provjera jesu li \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) komplanarni;

    Tri trodimenzionalna vektora \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) komplanarna su ako i samo ako je \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c)=0\).

  3. Određivanje orijentacije (kiralnosti) koordinatnog sustava koji čine \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\);

    Je li predznak mješovitog produkta \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c)\) pozitivan ili negativan ovisi o tome je li kut između \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\) i \(\boldsymbol c\) šiljast ili tup, tj. pokazuju li na istu ili na suprotnu stranu ravnine razapete vektorima \(\boldsymbol a\) i \(\boldsymbol b\), što je isto kao pitanje čine li vektori \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) u tom redoslijedu desni ili lijevi sustav. Konkretno:

    • \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c) < 0\) ekvivalentno je tome da \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) u tom redoslijedu čine lijevi sustav;
    • \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c) > 0\) ekvivalentno je tome da \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) u tom redoslijedu čine desni sustav.

Dvostruki vektorski produkt

Mješoviti produkt trodimenzionalnih vektora kombinacija je skalarnog i vektorskog produkta i ima cikličku simetriju. Vektorski produkt dvaju trodimenzionalnih vektora opet je trodimenzionalni vektor; postoje li onda slični rezultati za vektorski produkt vektorskog produkta?

Najprije dokažimo jednu lemu.

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol a = (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a) \boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) \boldsymbol a \]

Dokaz: prema pravilu desne ruke, \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\) okomit je i na \(\boldsymbol a\) i na \(\boldsymbol b\), a lijeva strana jednakosti koju dokazujemo okomita je na \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\), pa je lijeva strana jednakosti komplanarna s \(\boldsymbol a\) i \(\boldsymbol b\).

Stoga možemo pretpostaviti:

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol a = \lambda \boldsymbol a + \mu \boldsymbol b \]

Prema rezultatima o mješovitom produktu, pomnožimo li obje strane gornje jednakosti skalarno s \(\boldsymbol a\) odnosno s \(\boldsymbol b\), dobivamo:

\[ \begin{aligned} \lambda (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a)+\mu (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) &= 0 \\ \lambda (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) + \mu (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol b) &= \det(\boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol a) \\ &= (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \end{aligned} \]

Prema ranije izvedenom identitetu:

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) = |\boldsymbol a|^2|\boldsymbol b|^2-(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)^2 \]

možemo riješiti:

\[ \begin{aligned} \lambda &= -\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b \\ \mu &= \boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a \end{aligned} \]

Time je dokaz završen.

U gornjem je dokazu spomenuto da je vektorski produkt vektora \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\) s bilo kojim vektorom komplanaran s \(\boldsymbol a\) i \(\boldsymbol b\). Dokažimo sada rezultat o dvostrukom vektorskom produktu:

\[ (\boldsymbol a\times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol c=(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol a \]

Spomenuta komplanarnost pomaže pri pamćenju formule za dvostruki vektorski produkt. Vidimo da je gornja lema poseban slučaj dvostrukog vektorskog produkta.

Dokaz: dovoljno je razmotriti slučaj kad su sva tri vektora nenul i nekolinearni; ostali su posebni slučajevi očiti.

Trodimenzionalni vektori \(\boldsymbol a\), \(\boldsymbol b\) i \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\) nisu komplanarni, pa možemo pretpostaviti:

\[ \boldsymbol c = \alpha \boldsymbol a + \beta \boldsymbol b + \gamma(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \]

Stoga je:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol c &= (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times (\alpha \boldsymbol a + \beta \boldsymbol b + \gamma(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b)) \\ &= \alpha(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol a + \beta(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol b \end{aligned} \]

Prema gornjoj lemi vrijedi:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol a &=(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a) \boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) \boldsymbol a \\ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol b &= -(\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol a) \times \boldsymbol b \\ &= -(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol a+(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) \boldsymbol b \end{aligned} \]

Dakle:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol c &= \alpha((\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol a) + \beta((\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol a) \\ &=(\alpha(-\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) + \beta(-\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol b))\boldsymbol a + (\alpha \boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a + \beta \boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol b \\ &= (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c) \boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c) \boldsymbol a \end{aligned} \]

Time je dokaz završen.

Iz antikomutativnosti vektorskog produkta dobivaju se dvije formule za dvostruki vektorski produkt:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a\times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol c &=(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol a \\ \boldsymbol a \times(\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol c) &= (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol c \end{aligned} \]

Vidimo da dvostruki vektorski produkt ima stroge zahtjeve na redoslijed operacija.

Pomoću mješovitog i dvostrukog vektorskog produkta može se dokazati i Lagrangeov identitet.

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol c \times \boldsymbol d)=(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol d)-(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol d)(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c) \]

Dokaz:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol c \times \boldsymbol d) &= \det(\boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol d, \boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \\ &= \det(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol d) \\ &= ((\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol c)\cdot \boldsymbol d \\ &= (\boldsymbol b(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)- \boldsymbol a(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c))\cdot \boldsymbol d \\ &= (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol d) - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol d)(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c) \end{aligned} \]

Vidimo da je raniji identitet

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) = |\boldsymbol a|^2|\boldsymbol b|^2 - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)^2 \]

poseban slučaj Lagrangeova identiteta.

This article introduces simple operations between vectors.

Before the main text, a special note on translation. For historical reasons, mathematics and physics have all sorts of Chinese translations for the two terms "inner product" and "outer product".

In physics they are usually translated as "标积" and "矢积" ("scalar product" and "vector product"), expressing that the result of the operation is a scalar or a vector, respectively. The terms "数量积" and "向量积" ("quantity product" and "vector product") in high-school mathematics textbooks also follow this way of translating by meaning.

In mathematics they can also usually be translated as "内积" and "外积" ("inner product" and "outer product"), which is a literal translation of the two terms. "点乘" and "叉乘" ("dot multiplication" and "cross multiplication") are colloquial names derived from the operation symbols, and these colloquial names are also very common.

In the "dot multiplication" operation the dot symbol is often omitted, and in linear algebra it is even regarded directly as matrix multiplication, without writing the dot.

Dot product

The concept of the dot product applies to vectors of any dimension.

Definition

The dot product has several different but equivalent definitions; some of them are introduced below.

Geometric definition

In the \(n\)-dimensional Euclidean space \(\mathbf{R}^n\), given two vectors \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}\) with angle \(\theta\) between them,

\[ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} = |\boldsymbol{a}| |\boldsymbol{b}| \cos \theta \]

is the inner product of these two vectors, also called the dot product or scalar product. Here \(|\boldsymbol{b}|\cos \theta\) is called the projection of \(\boldsymbol{b}\) onto the direction of \(\boldsymbol{a}\). The geometric meaning of the dot product is: the dot product \(\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}\) equals the product of the magnitude of \(\boldsymbol{a}\) and the projection of \(\boldsymbol{b}\) onto the direction of \(\boldsymbol{a}\).

Algebraic definition

In the \(n\)-dimensional Euclidean space \(\mathbf{R}^n\), given two vectors \(\boldsymbol{a} = (a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n), \boldsymbol{b} = (b_1, b_2, \dots, b_n)\),

\[ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} = \sum_{i = 1}^{n} a_i b_i \]

is the inner product of these two vectors, also called the dot product or scalar product. The geometric and algebraic definitions of the dot product are equivalent in Euclidean space, and the latter is more convenient to use.

When no confusion can arise, the dot of the dot product may be omitted. A superscript \(2\) on a vector is shorthand for the dot product of the vector with itself, i.e. the square of the magnitude of the vector, with the magnitude bars omitted. This superscript \(2\) must not be understood as the square of a vector, because the result of a dot product of vectors is a scalar, so there is no dot product of any number of vectors other than \(2\). Likewise, the square of the squared magnitude of a vector cannot be abbreviated as a superscript \(4\); instead, the result with superscript \(2\) must be treated as a whole, and so on.

Properties

Note that the result of the dot product is a scalar; what is special about it is that it is a bilinear operation, linear in each of the two vectors separately. Specifically, the dot product satisfies:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol{a} + \boldsymbol{b}) \cdot \boldsymbol{c} &= \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{c} + \boldsymbol{b} \cdot \boldsymbol{c} \\ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot (\boldsymbol{b} + \boldsymbol{c}) &= \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} + \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{c} \\ (\lambda \boldsymbol{a}) \cdot \boldsymbol{b} &= \lambda (\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}) \\ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot (\lambda \boldsymbol{b}) &= \lambda (\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}) \end{aligned} \]

The dot product is also commutative, i.e.:

\[ \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} = \boldsymbol{b} \cdot \boldsymbol{a} \]

Applications

Some common applications of the dot product are introduced below.

  1. Testing whether two vectors are perpendicular:

    \[ \boldsymbol{a} \perp \boldsymbol{b} \iff \boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b} = 0 \]

    That is, the dot product of two mutually perpendicular vectors is \(0\); the dot product of a vector and the zero vector is \(0\). If a zero dot product is used as the definition of perpendicularity, it follows that the zero vector is perpendicular to every vector.

  2. Testing whether two vectors are collinear:

    \[ \exists\lambda \in \mathbf{R} (\boldsymbol{a} = \lambda \boldsymbol{b}) \iff |\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}| = |\boldsymbol{a}| |\boldsymbol{b}| \]

  3. Computing the magnitude of a vector:

    \[ |\boldsymbol a| = \sqrt{\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{a}} \]

  4. Computing the angle between two vectors:

    \[ \theta = \arccos \frac{\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}}{|\boldsymbol a| |\boldsymbol b|} \]

Determinants of order 2 and 3

Determinants of order 2 and 3 can be defined specially as simpler special cases of the determinant. In the last part of calculus, field theory, Green's theorem uses a determinant of order 2, Gauss's theorem (the divergence theorem) uses the dot product, and Stokes' theorem uses a determinant of order 3.

A determinant of order 2 can be regarded as a function of four variables, defined as:

\[ \begin{vmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{vmatrix}=ad-bc \]

A determinant of order 3 can be regarded as a function of nine variables, defined as:

\[ \begin{vmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & f \\ g & h & i \end{vmatrix}=aei+dhc+gbf-ahf-dbi-gec \]

A special mnemonic is the "rule of diagonals" (rule of Sarrus); the rule of diagonals applies only to determinants of order 2 and 3.

Special note: the expansion of a determinant of order 4 has 24 terms in total, and the term on the anti-diagonal has a positive sign. If the "rule of diagonals" for determinants of order 3 is forcibly applied, not only are there too few terms, but the sign of the anti-diagonal term is also wrong; therefore the "rule of diagonals" for determinants of order 3 does not apply to determinants of higher order, and determinants of higher order are also not suitable for computation by direct expansion.

Cross product

The cross product is an operation specific to three-dimensional vectors.

In physics, three-dimensional vectors are by default vectors related to spatial position and are always written in bold. However, the four-dimensional vectors related to relativity in physics are not written in bold, but use special notation and indices.

In linear algebra, all vectors are written in bold, and since this is tedious, and since most operations in linear algebra are between vectors and matrices, so that ambiguity is unlikely, the vector notation may be omitted when writing by hand.

Definition

The cross product has several different but equivalent definitions; some of them are introduced below.

Geometric definition

In the three-dimensional Euclidean space \(\mathbf{R}^3\), the cross product of vectors \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}\) is defined as a vector, denoted by \(\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\), whose magnitude and direction are defined as follows:

  1. \(|\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}| = |\boldsymbol{a}| |\boldsymbol{b}| \sin \langle \boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b} \rangle\);
  2. \(\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\) is perpendicular to both \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}\), and the directions of \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\) obey the right-hand rule.

Looking at the magnitude of the cross product and recalling the formula for the area of a triangle \(S=\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C\), we see that the geometric meaning of the cross product is: \(|\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}|\) is the area of the parallelogram with \(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}\) as adjacent sides.

Algebraic definition

In the three-dimensional Euclidean space \(\mathbf{R}^3\), the cross product of vectors \(\boldsymbol{a} = (x_1, y_1, z_1), \boldsymbol{b} = (x_2, y_2, z_2)\) is defined as a vector \(\boldsymbol{c}\), denoted by \(\boldsymbol{c} = \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\), and the result can be expressed by a determinant of order 3:

\[ \begin{vmatrix} \boldsymbol{i} & \boldsymbol{j} & \boldsymbol{k} \\ x_1 & y_1 & z_1 \\ x_2 & y_2 & z_2 \end{vmatrix} \]

where \(\boldsymbol{i}, \boldsymbol{j}, \boldsymbol{k}\) denote the unit vectors pointing along the coordinate axes \(x, y, z\) and are written in the places of the corresponding coordinates. Expanding gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{c} &= \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b} \\ &= (y_1z_2 - y_2z_1)\boldsymbol{i} + (z_1x_2 - z_2x_1)\boldsymbol{j} + (x_1y_2 - x_2y_1)\boldsymbol{k} \\ &= (y_1z_2 - y_2z_1, z_1x_2 - z_2x_1, x_1y_2 - x_2y_1) \end{aligned} \]

Properties

  1. The cross product is a bilinear operation, linear in each of the two vectors separately. Specifically, the cross product satisfies:

    \[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol{a} + \boldsymbol{b}) \times \boldsymbol{c} &= \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{c} + \boldsymbol{b} \times \boldsymbol{c} \\ \boldsymbol{a} \times (\boldsymbol{b} + \boldsymbol{c}) &= \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b} + \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{c} \\ (\lambda \boldsymbol{a}) \times \boldsymbol{b} &= \lambda (\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}) \\ \boldsymbol{a} \times (\lambda \boldsymbol{b}) &= \lambda (\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}) \end{aligned} \]

    The first two properties may also be called distributivity, i.e. the cross product is distributive over vector addition.

  2. The cross product is anticommutative, i.e.:

    \[ \boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b=-\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol a \]

  3. By the geometric definitions of the dot product and the cross product above:

    \[ \begin{aligned} |\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b| &= |\boldsymbol a| |\boldsymbol b| \sin \langle \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b \rangle \\ \boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b &= |\boldsymbol a| |\boldsymbol b| \cos \theta \\ &= |\boldsymbol a| |\boldsymbol b| \cos \langle \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b\rangle \end{aligned} \]

    we can write the identity:

    \[ (\boldsymbol a\times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol a\times \boldsymbol b) = |\boldsymbol a|^2 |\boldsymbol b|^2-{(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)}^2 \]

  4. The cross product satisfies the Jacobi identity:

    \[ \boldsymbol a \times (\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol c) + \boldsymbol b \times (\boldsymbol c \times \boldsymbol a) + \boldsymbol c \times (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) = \boldsymbol 0 \]

Applications

Some common applications of the cross product are introduced below.

  1. Testing whether two vectors are collinear:

    \[ \exists\lambda \in \mathbf{R} (\boldsymbol{a} = \lambda \boldsymbol{b}) \iff \boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b} = \boldsymbol{0} \]

    That is, the cross product of two collinear three-dimensional vectors is \(\boldsymbol 0\); the cross product of a three-dimensional vector with itself is \(\boldsymbol 0\); the cross product of a three-dimensional vector and the zero vector is \(\boldsymbol 0\). If a zero cross product is used as the definition of collinearity of two vectors, it follows that the zero vector is collinear with every vector.

  2. Computing the area of the parallelogram spanned by two vectors:

    \[ S \langle \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b \rangle = |\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b| \]

The case of two-dimensional vectors

For two-dimensional vectors the cross product cannot be computed, but the area of the parallelogram spanned by two vectors can still be computed:

Let \(\boldsymbol{a} = (m, n), \boldsymbol{b} = (p, q)\), and extend the rectangular coordinate system in the plane to a rectangular coordinate system in space, with the original plane lying in the \(xOy\) plane of the new coordinate system; the original coordinates \((m, n)\) and \((p, q)\) become \((m, n, 0)\) and \((p, q, 0)\).

Then the cross product of the two vectors is \((0, 0, mq - np)\), so the area of the parallelogram is \(|mq - np|\), which can be regarded as the absolute value of a determinant of order 2.

Here, by the right-hand rule and the sign of the \(z\) coordinate, one can infer the direction of \(\boldsymbol b\) relative to \(\boldsymbol a\): if it is in the counterclockwise direction the \(z\) coordinate is positive, otherwise it is negative; in short, clockwise negative, counterclockwise positive.

Scalar triple product

Like the cross product, the scalar triple product of vectors is an operation specific to three-dimensional vectors.

Definition

Let \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) be three vectors in three-dimensional space. Then \((\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot \boldsymbol c\) is called the scalar triple product of the three vectors \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\), denoted by \([\boldsymbol a \boldsymbol b \boldsymbol c]\) or \((\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c)\) or \((\boldsymbol a \boldsymbol b \boldsymbol c)\) or \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c)\). The geometric meaning of the absolute value of the scalar triple product, \(|(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot \boldsymbol c|\), is the volume of the parallelepiped with edges \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\).

The scalar triple product of vectors can be expressed by a determinant of order 3:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot \boldsymbol c &= \det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c) \\ &= \begin{vmatrix} a_x & b_x & c_x \\ a_y & b_y & c_y \\ a_z & b_z & c_z \end{vmatrix} \\ &= a_x b_y c_z + a_y b_z c_x + a_z b_x c_y - a_z b_y c_x -a _y b_x c_z - a_x b_z c_y \end{aligned} \]

Properties

  1. The scalar triple product is linear in each of the three vectors separately; specifically:

    \[ \begin{aligned} \det(\lambda\boldsymbol{u} + \mu\boldsymbol{v}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{c}) &= \lambda\det(\boldsymbol{u}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{c}) + \mu\det(\boldsymbol{v}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{c}) \\ \det(\boldsymbol{a}, \lambda\boldsymbol{u} + \mu\boldsymbol{v}, \boldsymbol{c}) &= \lambda\det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{u}, \boldsymbol{c}) + \mu\det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{v}, \boldsymbol{c}) \\ \det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}, \lambda\boldsymbol{u} + \mu\boldsymbol{v}) &= \lambda\det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{u}) + \mu\det(\boldsymbol{a}, \boldsymbol{b}, \boldsymbol{v}) \end{aligned} \]

  2. The scalar triple product is antisymmetric: swapping the positions of two vectors turns the scalar triple product into its negative, hence:

    \[ \det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c) = \det(\boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol a) = \det(\boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b) = -\det(\boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol c) = -\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol b)= -\det(\boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol a) \]

    From this we also obtain the following relation between the dot product and the cross product:

    \[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot \boldsymbol c = \boldsymbol a \cdot (\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol c) \]

Applications

The scalar triple product of vectors has the following common applications.

  1. Computing the volume of the tetrahedron \(ABCD\):

    \[ V=\frac{1}{6}\left|\det(\overrightarrow{AB}, \overrightarrow{AC}, \overrightarrow{AD})\right| \]

  2. Testing whether \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) are coplanar;

    Three three-dimensional vectors \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\) are coplanar if and only if \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c)=0\).

  3. Determining the handedness of the coordinate system formed by \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\);

    Whether the sign of the scalar triple product \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c)\) is positive or negative depends on whether the angle between \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\) and \(\boldsymbol c\) is acute or obtuse, i.e. whether they point to the same side or to opposite sides of the plane spanned by \(\boldsymbol a\) and \(\boldsymbol b\), which is equivalent to whether the three vectors \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\), in this order, form a right-handed or a left-handed system. Specifically:

    • \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c) < 0\) is equivalent to \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\), in this order, forming a left-handed system;
    • \(\det(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c) > 0\) is equivalent to \(\boldsymbol a, \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c\), in this order, forming a right-handed system.

Vector triple product

The scalar triple product of three-dimensional vectors is a mix of the dot product and the cross product and has cyclic symmetry. The cross product of two three-dimensional vectors is again a three-dimensional vector; so are there related results for the cross product of a cross product?

First we prove a lemma.

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol a = (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a) \boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) \boldsymbol a \]

Proof: by the right-hand rule, \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\) is perpendicular to both \(\boldsymbol a\) and \(\boldsymbol b\), and the left-hand side of the identity to be proved is perpendicular to \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\), so the left-hand side is coplanar with \(\boldsymbol a\) and \(\boldsymbol b\).

Therefore we may assume:

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol a = \lambda \boldsymbol a + \mu \boldsymbol b \]

By the results on the scalar triple product, taking the dot product of both sides of the above equation with \(\boldsymbol a\) and with \(\boldsymbol b\) respectively gives:

\[ \begin{aligned} \lambda (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a)+\mu (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) &= 0 \\ \lambda (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) + \mu (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol b) &= \det(\boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol a) \\ &= (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \end{aligned} \]

By the identity derived earlier:

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) = |\boldsymbol a|^2|\boldsymbol b|^2-(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)^2 \]

we can solve:

\[ \begin{aligned} \lambda &= -\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b \\ \mu &= \boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a \end{aligned} \]

This completes the proof.

In the proof above it was mentioned that the cross product of \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\) with any vector is coplanar with \(\boldsymbol a\) and \(\boldsymbol b\). Next we prove the result on the vector triple product:

\[ (\boldsymbol a\times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol c=(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol a \]

The coplanarity above helps in memorizing the vector triple product formula. Clearly, the lemma above is a special case of the vector triple product.

Proof: it suffices to consider the case where all three vectors are nonzero and non-collinear; the other special cases are obvious.

The three-dimensional vectors \(\boldsymbol a\), \(\boldsymbol b\) and \(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b\) are not coplanar, so we may assume:

\[ \boldsymbol c = \alpha \boldsymbol a + \beta \boldsymbol b + \gamma(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \]

Hence:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol c &= (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times (\alpha \boldsymbol a + \beta \boldsymbol b + \gamma(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b)) \\ &= \alpha(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol a + \beta(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol b \end{aligned} \]

By the lemma above:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol a &=(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a) \boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) \boldsymbol a \\ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol b &= -(\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol a) \times \boldsymbol b \\ &= -(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol a+(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) \boldsymbol b \end{aligned} \]

Therefore:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \times \boldsymbol c &= \alpha((\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol a) + \beta((\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol a) \\ &=(\alpha(-\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b) + \beta(-\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol b))\boldsymbol a + (\alpha \boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol a + \beta \boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol b \\ &= (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c) \boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c) \boldsymbol a \end{aligned} \]

This completes the proof.

By the anticommutativity of the cross product, we obtain the two formulas for the vector triple product:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a\times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol c &=(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol a \\ \boldsymbol a \times(\boldsymbol b \times \boldsymbol c) &= (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)\boldsymbol b - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)\boldsymbol c \end{aligned} \]

Clearly, the vector triple product has strict requirements on the order of operations.

With the help of the scalar triple product and the vector triple product, Lagrange's identity can also be proved.

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol c \times \boldsymbol d)=(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol d)-(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol d)(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c) \]

Proof:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol c \times \boldsymbol d) &= \det(\boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol d, \boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \\ &= \det(\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b, \boldsymbol c, \boldsymbol d) \\ &= ((\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b)\times \boldsymbol c)\cdot \boldsymbol d \\ &= (\boldsymbol b(\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)- \boldsymbol a(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c))\cdot \boldsymbol d \\ &= (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol c)(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol d) - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol d)(\boldsymbol b \cdot \boldsymbol c) \end{aligned} \]

Clearly, the earlier identity

\[ (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) \cdot (\boldsymbol a \times \boldsymbol b) = |\boldsymbol a|^2|\boldsymbol b|^2 - (\boldsymbol a \cdot \boldsymbol b)^2 \]

is a special case of Lagrange's identity.
