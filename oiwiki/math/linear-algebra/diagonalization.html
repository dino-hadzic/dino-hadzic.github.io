Svojstveni potprostor

Svi svojstveni vektori matrice \(A\) koji pripadaju svojstvenoj vrijednosti \(\lambda_0\), zajedno s nul-vektorom, tvore linearni prostor koji zovemo svojstvenim potprostorom matrice \(A\) i označavamo \(E(\lambda_0)\). To je prostor rješenja homogenog linearnog sustava:

\[ (\lambda_0 I-A)X=0 \]

tj. skup svih rješenja tog sustava.

Za svojstveni potprostor \(E(\lambda_i)=N(\lambda_i I-A)\) iz teorema o rangu i defektu slijedi:

\[ r(\lambda_i I-A)+\operatorname{dim} N(\lambda_i I-A)=n \]

Stoga je dimenzija svojstvenog potprostora \(E(\lambda_i)\) jednaka:

\[ \operatorname{dim} E(\lambda_i)=n-r(\lambda_i I-A) \]

Ona se zove i geometrijska kratnost od \(\lambda_i\).

Invarijantni potprostor

Pri proučavanju linearnog operatora \(T\) često želimo odabrati bazu prostora \(V\) u kojoj matrica linearnog operatora \(T\) ima što jednostavniji oblik.

Neka je \(V\) linearni prostor nad poljem \(F\), \(W\) potprostor od \(V\), a \(T\) linearni operator na \(V\). Ako za svaki vektor \(x\) iz \(W\) i \(T(x)\) leži u \(W\) (kažemo i da je potprostor invarijantan ili stabilan pod operatorom), kažemo da je \(W\) invarijantni potprostor od \(T\).

Da je prostor invarijantan pod operatorom ne znači da koordinate doista ostaju „nepromijenjene”; moguće je da se provodi, primjerice, rastezanje ili neka druga deformacija, samo što rezultat i dalje ostaje u tom prostoru.

  • Svaki potprostor linearnog prostora \(V\) invarijantni je potprostor množenja skalarom.
  • Za svaki linearni operator \(T\) na \(V\), prostor \(V\) i nul-potprostor invarijantni su potprostori od \(T\); zovemo ih trivijalnim invarijantnim potprostorima.
  • Presjek i zbroj invarijantnih potprostora također su invarijantni potprostori.

Neka je \(W\) invarijantni potprostor linearnog operatora \(T\). Promatramo li samo djelovanje operatora \(T\) na invarijantnom potprostoru \(W\), dobivamo linearni operator na samom potprostoru \(W\); zovemo ga restrikcijom operatora \(T\) na potprostor \(W\) i označavamo \({T|}_W\).

Za svaki linearni operator \(T\) na \(V\), slika \(R(T)\) i jezgra \(N(T)\) invarijantni su potprostori od \(T\). Značenje tih dvaju slučajeva jest da se prostor \(V\) pod operatorom sažima u sebe (slika) ili sažima u \(0\) (jezgra).

Za svaki linearni operator \(T\) na \(V\), svojstveni potprostori od \(T\) invarijantni su potprostori od \(T\).

Primarna dekompozicija

Prema osnovnom teoremu algebre minimalni polinom može se faktorizirati kao:

\[ m_A(\lambda)={(\lambda-\lambda_1)}^{r_1}\cdots{(\lambda-\lambda_S)}^{r_S} \]

Uvrstimo li u minimalni polinom umjesto varijable \(\lambda\) matricu \(A\), jezgre pojedinih faktora tvore niz invarijantnih potprostora matrice \(A\):

\[ W_i=N({(\lambda_i I-A)}^{r_i}) \]

Teorem: Dimenzija tog invarijantnog potprostora \(W_i\) jednaka je upravo algebarskoj kratnosti svojstvene vrijednosti \(\lambda_i\).

Podsjetimo se: algebarska kratnost je stupanj odgovarajućeg faktora karakterističnog polinoma, a geometrijska kratnost je dimenzija svojstvenog potprostora \(E(\lambda_i)=N(\lambda_i I-A)\). Taj invarijantni potprostor \(W_i\) i svojstveni potprostor \(E(\lambda_i)\) oba su jezgre matrica, a te dvije matrice odnose se kao matrica i njezina \(r_i\)-ta potencija iz minimalnog polinoma. Drugim riječima, dimenzija svojstvenog potprostora je geometrijska kratnost, a „svojstveni potprostor” nakon podizanja na \(r_i\)-tu potenciju iz minimalnog polinoma postaje „invarijantni potprostor” čija dimenzija doseže algebarsku kratnost iz karakterističnog polinoma.

Taj je teorem zapravo korolar sljedećeg teorema o primarnoj dekompoziciji.

Označimo s \(T\) linearni operator koji odgovara matrici \(A\), a s \(T_i={T|}_{W_i}\) njegovu restrikciju na svaki potprostor \(W_i\). Tada je minimalni polinom od \(T_i\) jednak \((x-\lambda_i)^{r_i}\).

Teorem: Neka je \(V\) linearni prostor nad poljem \(F\), a \(T\) linearni operator na \(V\). Tada se prostor \(V\) može primarno dekomponirati u odnosu na linearni operator \(T\), tj. rastaviti na direktnu sumu nekoliko invarijantnih potprostora \(W_i\).

\[ V=W_1\oplus W_2\oplus\cdots\oplus W_S \]

To znači da je matrica od \(T\) u nekoj bazi blok-dijagonalna (kvazidijagonalna):

\[ \operatorname{diag}\{A_1,A_2,\cdots,A_S\} \]

gdje je \(A_i\) matrica od \(T_i\) u odgovarajućoj bazi.

Teorem pokazuje da se invarijantnim potprostorima može pojednostavniti matrica linearnog operatora.

Dijagonalizabilne matrice

Ako je kvadratna matrica \(A\) reda \(n\) slična nekoj dijagonalnoj matrici, kažemo da je \(A\) dijagonalizabilna matrica ili da je jednostavna (semisimple) matrica.

  • Zbroj, produkt i inverz dijagonalnih matrica, ako postoje, opet su dijagonalne matrice, a elementi na dijagonali upravo su njihove svojstvene vrijednosti.
  • Matrica linearnog operatora \(T\) dijagonalizabilna je ako i samo ako je matrica od \(T\) u nekoj bazi dijagonalna.

Teorem: Neka su \(\lambda_1,\cdots,\lambda_m\) sve međusobno različite svojstvene vrijednosti matrice \(A\). Tada su sljedeće tvrdnje ekvivalentne:

  • Matrica \(A\) je dijagonalizabilna.
  • Matrica \(A\) ima \(n\) linearno nezavisnih svojstvenih vektora.
  • Vrijedi sljedeća formula:
\[ \operatorname{dim} E(\lambda_1)+\cdots+\operatorname{dim} E(\lambda_m)=n \]

Već smo spomenuli da se stupanj svojstvene vrijednosti u faktorizaciji karakterističnog polinoma zove algebarska kratnost, a dimenzija svojstvenog potprostora geometrijska kratnost. Ovaj teorem pokazuje i da je matrica \(A\) dijagonalizabilna ako i samo ako je za svaku svojstvenu vrijednost \(\lambda\) od \(A\) algebarska kratnost jednaka geometrijskoj kratnosti.

Korolar: Ako kvadratna matrica \(A\) reda \(n\) ima točno \(n\) međusobno različitih svojstvenih vrijednosti, onda je nužno dijagonalizabilna. Obrat ne mora vrijediti.

Teorem: Matrica \(A\) dijagonalizabilna je ako i samo ako minimalni polinom od \(A\) nema višestrukih korijena.

Sličnost matrica također čuva odnose linearne zavisnosti među svojstvenim vektorima.

Svojstveni vektori sasvim lako mogu biti nerealni, a sasvim je moguće i da se ne može naći \(n\) linearno nezavisnih svojstvenih vektora.

Za višestruku svojstvenu vrijednost svojstveni vektori razapinju prostor. Da bismo opisali taj prostor, iz njega treba odabrati predstavnike. Obično se biraju linearno nezavisni predstavnici, a broj predstavnika jednak je dimenziji prostora.

Pri odabiru predstavnika često ih ortogonaliziramo i normiramo. Na kraju dobivamo skup ortonormiranih predstavnika.

Svojstveni vektori ne moraju biti ortogonalni; svojstvene vektore različitih svojstvenih vrijednosti možda nije moguće ortogonalizirati. Zato se ortogonalizacija može provesti samo na svojstvenim vektorima višestruke svojstvene vrijednosti. Normirati se, međutim, može svaki svojstveni vektor.

Nilpotentne matrice

Neka je \(T\) linearni operator na prostoru \(V\). Ako postoji prirodan broj \(r\) takav da je \(T^r\) nul-operator, kažemo da je \(T\) nilpotentan operator na prostoru \(V\).

Matrica koja za neki prirodan broj \(r\) zadovoljava uvjet \(N^r=0\) zove se nilpotentna matrica.

Obično se dodatno može pretpostaviti da je \(r\) najmanji prirodan broj za koji je \(T^r\) nul-operator; tada je minimalni polinom od \(T\) jednak \(x^r\). Stoga postoji vektor \(\xi_0\) takav da:

  • \[ T^r(\xi_0)=0 \]
  • \[ T^{r-1}(\xi_0)\neq 0 \]

Ciklički potprostor

Teorem: Neka je \(T\) linearni operator na prostoru \(V\), a \(\xi\) vektor iz prostora \(V\). Ako postoji prirodan broj \(s\) takav da:

  • \[ T^s(\xi)=0 \]
  • \[ T^{s-1}(\xi)\neq 0 \]

onda su vektori \(\xi,T(\xi),\cdots,T^{s-1}(\xi)\) linearno nezavisni.

Na temelju tog teorema možemo dati definiciju:

Neka je \(T\) linearni operator na prostoru \(V\), a \(W\) potprostor od \(V\). Ako postoje vektor \(\xi_0\) i prirodan broj \(r\) takvi da:

  • vektori \(\xi_0,T(\xi_0),\cdots,T^{r-1}(\xi_0)\) tvore bazu od \(W\),

  • vrijedi jednakost:

    \[ T^r(\xi_0)=0 \]

onda potprostor \(W\) zovemo cikličkim potprostorom u odnosu na \(T\), kraće \(T\)-cikličkim potprostorom. Pritom \(\xi_0\) zovemo generirajućim vektorom cikličkog potprostora \(W\), a vektore \(\xi_0,T(\xi_0),\cdots,T^{r-1}(\xi_0)\) cikličkom bazom od \(W\).

Očito je \(T\)-ciklički potprostor \(W\) invarijantan pod \(T\), a za svaki vektor \(\xi\) iz cikličkog potprostora \(W\) vrijedi \(T^r(\xi)=0\), gdje je \(r\) dimenzija cikličkog potprostora.

Nilpotentni Jordanov blok

Ako je prostor \(W\) ciklički potprostor operatora \(T\), onda je restrikcija \({T|}_W\) operatora \(T\) na \(W\) nilpotentan operator na \(W\), a matrica od \({T|}_W\) u obrnuto poredanoj cikličkoj bazi \(T^{r-1}(\xi_0),T^{r-2}(\xi_0),\cdots,\xi_0\) od \(W\) gornjotrokutasta je matrica reda \(r\) sljedećeg oblika:

\[ N_r=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots& \vdots\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 1\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ \end{pmatrix} \]

Matrica \(N_r\) zove se nilpotentna Jordanova matrica reda \(r\) ili nilpotentni Jordanov blok reda \(r\).

Neka je \(T\) nilpotentan operator na \(n\)-dimenzionalnom prostoru \(V\). Jednoznačno određen niz prirodnih brojeva \(r_1\geq\cdots\geq r_S\) koji se pojavljuje u rastavu prostora \(V\) na \(T\)-cikličke potprostore zovemo invarijantnim eksponentima (indeksima) od \(T\).

Za nilpotentnu matricu \(A\) reda \(n\), \(A\) je slična matrici \(N\) gornjeg oblika i također jednoznačno određuje niz prirodnih brojeva \(r_1\geq\cdots\geq r_S\), koji zovemo invarijantnim eksponentima matrice \(A\).

Iako nilpotentna matrica ne može biti slična dijagonalnoj matrici, slična je takvom standardnom obliku. Jordanova normalna forma objedinjuje dijagonalizaciju sličnošću i standardni oblik nilpotentnih matrica te daje standardni oblik koji se za opću matricu može postići transformacijom sličnosti.

Neki teoremi

  1. Neka je \(T\) nilpotentan operator na prostoru \(V\), a

    \[ h(x)=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_mx^m \]

    polinom. Tada linearni operator \(h(T)\) ima inverz ako i samo ako je \(a_0\neq 0\). Kad je \(h(T)\) invertibilan, inverz od \(h(T)\) također je polinom u \(T\).

  2. Neka je \(T\) nilpotentan operator na prostoru \(V\), \(W\) \(r\)-dimenzionalni \(T\)-ciklički potprostor, a \(\xi\) vektor iz \(W\). Ako postoji cijeli broj \(k\) takav da je

    \[ T^{r-k}(\xi)=0 \]

    onda postoji vektor \(\eta\) iz \(W\) takav da je

    \[ \xi=T^k(\eta) \]

  3. Neka je \(T\) nilpotentan operator na \(n\)-dimenzionalnom prostoru \(V\), \(x^r\) minimalni polinom od \(T\), a \(W_1\) \(r\)-dimenzionalni \(T\)-ciklički potprostor. Tada postoji komplement \(W_2\) potprostora \(W_1\) takav da je:

    \[ V=W_1\oplus W_2 \]

    i \(W_2\) je također invarijantan pod \(T\).

  4. Neka je \(T\) nilpotentan operator na \(n\)-dimenzionalnom prostoru \(V\). Tada se \(V\) može rastaviti na direktnu sumu \(T\)-cikličkih potprostora:

    \[ V=W_1\oplus W_2\oplus\cdots\oplus W_S \]

  5. Svaka nilpotentna matrica reda \(n\) slična je matrici oblika:

    \[ N=\begin{pmatrix} N_{r_1} & & & 0\\ & N_{r_2} & & \\ & & \cdots & \\ 0 & & & N_{r_S}\\ \end{pmatrix} \]

    gdje je svaki \(N_{r_i}\) nilpotentni Jordanov blok reda \(r_i\).

  6. Ako dogovorimo da se \(T\)-ciklički potprostori \(W_i\) poredaju po dimenzijama \(r_i\) silazno, \(r_1\geq\cdots\geq r_S\), onda je rastav prostora \(V\) na \(T\)-cikličke potprostore jednoznačno određen operatorom \(T\).

Eigenspace

All eigenvectors of the matrix \(A\) belonging to the eigenvalue \(\lambda_0\), together with the zero vector, form a linear space called an eigenspace of the matrix \(A\), denoted \(E(\lambda_0)\). It is the solution space of the homogeneous linear system:

\[ (\lambda_0 I-A)X=0 \]

that is, the set of all solutions of that system.

For the eigenspace \(E(\lambda_i)=N(\lambda_i I-A)\), the rank–nullity theorem gives:

\[ r(\lambda_i I-A)+\operatorname{dim} N(\lambda_i I-A)=n \]

Therefore, the dimension of the eigenspace \(E(\lambda_i)\) is:

\[ \operatorname{dim} E(\lambda_i)=n-r(\lambda_i I-A) \]

It is also called the geometric multiplicity of \(\lambda_i\).

Invariant subspaces

When studying a linear operator \(T\), we often want to choose a basis of the space \(V\) in which the matrix of the linear operator \(T\) has as simple a shape as possible.

Let \(V\) be a linear space over a field \(F\), \(W\) a subspace of \(V\), and \(T\) a linear operator on \(V\). If for every vector \(x\) in \(W\), \(T(x)\) also lies in \(W\) (we also say that the subspace is invariant or stable under the operator), we say that \(W\) is an invariant subspace of \(T\).

That the space is invariant under the operator does not mean that the coordinates really stay "unchanged"; there may be a stretch or some other deformation, but the result still lands in the space.

  • Every subspace of the linear space \(V\) is an invariant subspace of scalar multiplication.
  • For every linear operator \(T\) on \(V\), the space \(V\) and the zero subspace are invariant subspaces of \(T\); they are called the trivial invariant subspaces.
  • The intersection and the sum of invariant subspaces are also invariant subspaces.

Let \(W\) be an invariant subspace of the linear operator \(T\). Considering only the action of \(T\) on the invariant subspace \(W\), we obtain a linear operator on the subspace \(W\) itself, called the restriction of \(T\) to the subspace \(W\) and denoted \({T|}_W\).

For every linear operator \(T\) on \(V\), the image \(R(T)\) and the kernel \(N(T)\) are invariant subspaces of \(T\). The meaning of these two cases is that, under the operator, the space \(V\) is compressed into itself (the image) or compressed to \(0\) (the kernel).

For every linear operator \(T\) on \(V\), the eigenspaces of \(T\) are invariant subspaces of \(T\).

Primary decomposition

By the fundamental theorem of algebra, the minimal polynomial can be factored as:

\[ m_A(\lambda)={(\lambda-\lambda_1)}^{r_1}\cdots{(\lambda-\lambda_S)}^{r_S} \]

Substituting the matrix \(A\) for the variable \(\lambda\) in the minimal polynomial, the kernels of the individual factors form a series of invariant subspaces of the matrix \(A\):

\[ W_i=N({(\lambda_i I-A)}^{r_i}) \]

Theorem: The dimension of this invariant subspace \(W_i\) is exactly the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue \(\lambda_i\).

Recall that the algebraic multiplicity is the degree of the corresponding factor of the characteristic polynomial, and the geometric multiplicity is the dimension of the eigenspace \(E(\lambda_i)=N(\lambda_i I-A)\). This invariant subspace \(W_i\) and the eigenspace \(E(\lambda_i)\) are both kernels of matrices, and the two matrices are related as a matrix and its \(r_i\)-th power from the minimal polynomial. In other words, the dimension of the eigenspace is the geometric multiplicity, and after raising to the \(r_i\)-th power from the minimal polynomial the "eigenspace" becomes an "invariant subspace" whose dimension reaches the algebraic multiplicity from the characteristic polynomial.

This theorem is in fact a corollary of the primary decomposition theorem below.

Denote by \(T\) the linear operator corresponding to the matrix \(A\), and by \(T_i={T|}_{W_i}\) its restriction to each subspace \(W_i\). Then the minimal polynomial of \(T_i\) is \((x-\lambda_i)^{r_i}\).

Theorem: Let \(V\) be a linear space over a field \(F\), and \(T\) a linear operator on \(V\). Then the space \(V\) admits a primary decomposition with respect to the linear operator \(T\), i.e., it splits into a direct sum of several invariant subspaces \(W_i\).

\[ V=W_1\oplus W_2\oplus\cdots\oplus W_S \]

This means that the matrix of \(T\) in some basis is block diagonal (quasi-diagonal):

\[ \operatorname{diag}\{A_1,A_2,\cdots,A_S\} \]

where \(A_i\) is the matrix of \(T_i\) in the corresponding basis.

The theorem shows that invariant subspaces can be used to simplify the matrix of a linear operator.

Diagonalizable matrices

If a square matrix \(A\) of order \(n\) is similar to a diagonal matrix, we say that \(A\) is a diagonalizable matrix, or a simple (semisimple) matrix.

  • The sum, product, and inverse of diagonal matrices, if they exist, are again diagonal matrices, and the entries on their diagonal are exactly their eigenvalues.
  • The matrix of a linear operator \(T\) is diagonalizable if and only if the matrix of \(T\) in some basis is diagonal.

Theorem: Let \(\lambda_1,\cdots,\lambda_m\) be all the distinct eigenvalues of the matrix \(A\). Then the following statements are equivalent:

  • The matrix \(A\) is diagonalizable.
  • The matrix \(A\) has \(n\) linearly independent eigenvectors.
  • The following formula holds:
\[ \operatorname{dim} E(\lambda_1)+\cdots+\operatorname{dim} E(\lambda_m)=n \]

We pointed out earlier that the exponent of an eigenvalue in the factorization of the characteristic polynomial is called the algebraic multiplicity, and the dimension of the eigenspace is called the geometric multiplicity. This theorem also shows that the matrix \(A\) is diagonalizable if and only if, for every eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(A\), the algebraic multiplicity equals the geometric multiplicity.

Corollary: If a square matrix \(A\) of order \(n\) has exactly \(n\) distinct eigenvalues, then it is necessarily diagonalizable. The converse need not hold.

Theorem: The matrix \(A\) is diagonalizable if and only if the minimal polynomial of \(A\) has no repeated roots.

Matrix similarity also preserves the linear dependence relations among eigenvectors.

Eigenvectors may very well fail to be real, and it may very well be impossible to find \(n\) linearly independent eigenvectors.

For a repeated eigenvalue, the eigenvectors span a space. To describe this space, representatives must be chosen from it. Usually linearly independent representatives are chosen, and the number of representatives equals the dimension of the space.

When choosing representatives, they are often orthogonalized and normalized. What we finally obtain is a set of orthonormal representatives.

Eigenvectors are not necessarily orthogonal; eigenvectors of different eigenvalues may not be orthogonalizable. Therefore, orthogonalization can only be performed on eigenvectors of a repeated eigenvalue. Normalization, however, can be performed on any eigenvector.

Nilpotent matrices

Let \(T\) be a linear operator on the space \(V\). If there is a positive integer \(r\) such that \(T^r\) is the zero operator, we say that \(T\) is a nilpotent operator on the space \(V\).

A matrix satisfying the condition \(N^r=0\) for some positive integer \(r\) is called a nilpotent matrix.

Usually one may further assume that \(r\) is the smallest positive integer for which \(T^r\) is the zero operator; then the minimal polynomial of \(T\) is \(x^r\). Hence there is a vector \(\xi_0\) such that:

  • \[ T^r(\xi_0)=0 \]
  • \[ T^{r-1}(\xi_0)\neq 0 \]

Cyclic subspaces

Theorem: Let \(T\) be a linear operator on the space \(V\), and \(\xi\) a vector of the space \(V\). If there is a positive integer \(s\) such that:

  • \[ T^s(\xi)=0 \]
  • \[ T^{s-1}(\xi)\neq 0 \]

then the vectors \(\xi,T(\xi),\cdots,T^{s-1}(\xi)\) are linearly independent.

This theorem allows us to give a definition:

Let \(T\) be a linear operator on the space \(V\), and \(W\) a subspace of \(V\). If there are a vector \(\xi_0\) and a positive integer \(r\) such that:

  • the vectors \(\xi_0,T(\xi_0),\cdots,T^{r-1}(\xi_0)\) form a basis of \(W\),

  • the following equation holds:

    \[ T^r(\xi_0)=0 \]

then the subspace \(W\) is called a cyclic subspace with respect to \(T\), or a \(T\)-cyclic subspace for short. In this case \(\xi_0\) is called a generating vector of the cyclic subspace \(W\), and the vectors \(\xi_0,T(\xi_0),\cdots,T^{r-1}(\xi_0)\) are called a cyclic basis of \(W\).

Clearly, a \(T\)-cyclic subspace \(W\) is invariant under \(T\), and for every vector \(\xi\) in the cyclic subspace \(W\) we have \(T^r(\xi)=0\), where \(r\) is the dimension of the cyclic subspace.

Nilpotent Jordan blocks

If the space \(W\) is a cyclic subspace of the operator \(T\), then the restriction \({T|}_W\) of \(T\) to \(W\) is a nilpotent operator on \(W\), and the matrix of \({T|}_W\) with respect to the reversed cyclic basis \(T^{r-1}(\xi_0),T^{r-2}(\xi_0),\cdots,\xi_0\) of \(W\) is an upper triangular matrix of order \(r\) of the following shape:

\[ N_r=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots& \vdots\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 1\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ \end{pmatrix} \]

The matrix \(N_r\) is called a nilpotent Jordan matrix of order \(r\), or a nilpotent Jordan block of order \(r\).

Let \(T\) be a nilpotent operator on the \(n\)-dimensional space \(V\). The uniquely determined sequence of positive integers \(r_1\geq\cdots\geq r_S\) appearing in the decomposition of \(V\) into \(T\)-cyclic subspaces is called the invariant exponents (indices) of \(T\).

For a nilpotent matrix \(A\) of order \(n\), \(A\) is similar to a matrix \(N\) of the above shape and also uniquely determines a sequence of positive integers \(r_1\geq\cdots\geq r_S\), called the invariant exponents of the matrix \(A\).

Although a nilpotent matrix cannot be similar to a diagonal matrix, it is similar to such a standard form. The Jordan normal form combines diagonalization by similarity with the standard form of nilpotent matrices, and gives the standard form that a general matrix can reach by a similarity transformation.

Some theorems

  1. Let \(T\) be a nilpotent operator on the space \(V\), and let

    \[ h(x)=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_mx^m \]

    be a polynomial. Then the linear operator \(h(T)\) has an inverse if and only if \(a_0\neq 0\). When \(h(T)\) is invertible, the inverse of \(h(T)\) is also a polynomial in \(T\).

  2. Let \(T\) be a nilpotent operator on the space \(V\), \(W\) an \(r\)-dimensional \(T\)-cyclic subspace, and \(\xi\) a vector in \(W\). If there is an integer \(k\) such that

    \[ T^{r-k}(\xi)=0 \]

    then there is a vector \(\eta\) in \(W\) such that

    \[ \xi=T^k(\eta) \]

  3. Let \(T\) be a nilpotent operator on the \(n\)-dimensional space \(V\), \(x^r\) the minimal polynomial of \(T\), and \(W_1\) an \(r\)-dimensional \(T\)-cyclic subspace. Then there is a complement \(W_2\) of \(W_1\) such that:

    \[ V=W_1\oplus W_2 \]

    and \(W_2\) is also invariant under \(T\).

  4. Let \(T\) be a nilpotent operator on the \(n\)-dimensional space \(V\). Then \(V\) can be decomposed into a direct sum of \(T\)-cyclic subspaces:

    \[ V=W_1\oplus W_2\oplus\cdots\oplus W_S \]

  5. Every nilpotent matrix of order \(n\) is similar to a matrix of the form:

    \[ N=\begin{pmatrix} N_{r_1} & & & 0\\ & N_{r_2} & & \\ & & \cdots & \\ 0 & & & N_{r_S}\\ \end{pmatrix} \]

    where each \(N_{r_i}\) is a nilpotent Jordan block of order \(r_i\).

  6. If we stipulate that the \(T\)-cyclic subspaces \(W_i\) are arranged in decreasing order of their dimensions \(r_i\), \(r_1\geq\cdots\geq r_S\), then the decomposition of \(V\) into \(T\)-cyclic subspaces is uniquely determined by \(T\).
