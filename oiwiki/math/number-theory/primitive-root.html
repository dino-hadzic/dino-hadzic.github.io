Predznanje: mali Fermatov teorem, Eulerov teorem, Lagrangeov teorem

Red elementa i primitivni korijen važni su alati za razumijevanje multiplikativne strukture reduciranog sustava ostataka \(\mathbf Z_m^*\) modulo \(m\). Na njima se temelje pojmovi poput diskretnog logaritma. Općenitija rasprava može se naći u odgovarajućim poglavljima stranica o teoriji grupa i teoriji prstena u dijelu o apstraktnoj algebri.

Red

U ovom odjeljku uvijek pretpostavljamo da su modul \(m\in\mathbf N_+\) i baza \(a\in\mathbf Z\) relativno prosti, tj. \((a,m)=1\), što zapisujemo i kao \(a\perp m\).

Za \(n\in\mathbf Z\) potencije \(a^n\bmod m\) pokazuju cikličku strukturu. Najmanja duljina tog ciklusa upravo je red elementa \(a\) modulo \(m\). Red se definira kao eksponent pri kojem se potencija \(a^n \bmod m\) prvi put vraća na početak \(a^0\bmod m = 1\):

Red

Za \(a\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) takve da je \(a\perp m\), najmanji pozitivni cijeli broj \(n\) koji zadovoljava kongruenciju \(a^n \equiv 1 \pmod m\) naziva se red elementa \(a\) modulo \(m\) (engl. the order of \(a\) modulo \(m\)) i označava se \(\delta_m(a)\) ili \(\operatorname{ord}_m(a)\).

Napomena

U apstraktnoj algebri ovaj je „red” upravo red elementa \(a\) u grupi koju reducirani sustav ostataka modulo \(m\) tvori s obzirom na množenje. Oznaka \(\delta\) za red koristi se samo za tu posebnu grupu. Mnoga od svojstava u nastavku mogu se izravno poopćiti na svojstva reda elementa grupe u apstraktnoj algebri.

Postoji i pojam „polureda” (half-order), koji se u teoriji brojeva označava \(\delta^-\). To je najmanji pozitivni cijeli broj koji zadovoljava kongruenciju \(a^n \equiv -1 \pmod m\). Polured nije pojam iz teorije grupa. Red uvijek postoji, a polured ne mora postojati.

Ciklička struktura potencija

Pomoću reda može se opisati ciklička struktura potencija. Za potenciju \(a^n\bmod m\) eksponent \(n\) možemo podijeliti s ostatkom redom \(\delta_m(a)\):

\[ n = \delta_m(a)q + r, ~ 0\le r < \delta_m(a). \]

Zatim, prema pravilima potenciranja, dobivamo

\[ a^n = a^{\delta_m(a)q + r} = (a^{\delta_m(a)})^q \cdot a^r \equiv a^r \pmod m. \]

To pokazuje da se potencija s bilo kojim eksponentom može pomaknuti u prvi nenegativni ciklus. Iz toga se dobiva niz svojstava reda.

Svojstvo 1

Za \(a\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) takve da je \(a\perp m\), potencije \(a^0(=1),a,a^2,\cdots,a^{\delta_m(a)-1}\) međusobno su nekongruentne modulo \(m\).

Dokaz

Dokazujemo kontradikcijom. Pretpostavimo da postoje dva broja \(0\le i< j<\delta_m(a)\) takva da je \(a^i\equiv a^j\pmod m\); tada vrijedi \(a^{j - i}\equiv 1\pmod m\). No \(0 < j - i < \delta_m(a)\). To je u suprotnosti s minimalnošću reda, pa tvrdnja vrijedi.

Svojstvo 2

Za \(a,n\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) takve da je \(a\perp m\), kongruencija \(a^n \equiv 1 \pmod m\) vrijedi ako i samo ako \(\delta_m(a)\mid n\).

Dokaz

Kao što je gore rečeno, \(a^{n}\equiv a^{n\bmod\delta_m(a)}\pmod m\). Prema svojstvu 1, jedini \(r\) u rasponu \(0\le r < \delta_m(a)\) za koji vrijedi \(a^r\equiv 1\pmod m\) jest \(r=0\). Stoga \(a^n \equiv 1 \pmod m\) vrijedi ako i samo ako je \(n\bmod \delta_m(a) = 0\), tj. \(\delta_m(a)\mid n\).

Prema Eulerovu teoremu kongruencija \(a^{\varphi(m)}\equiv 1\pmod m\) vrijedi za sve \(a\perp m\). U kombinaciji sa svojstvom 2 to znači da za sve \(a\perp m\) vrijedi \(\delta_m(a)\mid\varphi(m)\). Drugim riječima, \(\varphi(m)\) je zajednički višekratnik redova svih \(a\perp m\). Za pozitivni cijeli broj \(m\) najmanji zajednički višekratnik redova \(\delta_m(a)\) svih \(a\perp m\), označen \(\lambda(m)\), upravo je Carmichaelova funkcija broja \(m\). Njezina se svojstva detaljno razmatraju u nastavku.

Slično kao i kod drugih cikličkih struktura, iz reda elementa \(a\) može se izračunati red elementa \(a^k\).

Svojstvo 3

Za \(k,a\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) takve da je \(a\perp m\) vrijedi

\[ \delta_m(a^k) = \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{(\delta_m(a),k)}. \]
Dokaz

Prema svojstvu 2, kongruencija \((a^k)^n = a^{kn} \equiv 1\pmod m\) vrijedi ako i samo ako \(\delta_m(a) \mid kn\). Taj je uvjet ekvivalentan uvjetu

\[ \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{\left(\delta_m(a),k\right)} \mid n. \]

Najmanji pozitivni cijeli broj koji zadovoljava taj uvjet upravo je

\[ \delta_m(a^k)=\dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{\left(\delta_m(a),k\right)}. \]

Red umnoška

Neka su \(a,b\) različiti cijeli brojevi relativno prosti s \(m\). Ako su poznati redovi \(\delta_m(a)\) i \(\delta_m(b)\), tada se također mogu dobiti neke informacije o redu \(\delta_{m}(ab)\) njihova umnoška \(ab\).

Svojstvo 4

Za \(a,b\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) takve da je \(a,b\perp m\) vrijedi

\[ \dfrac{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))} \mid \delta_m(ab) \mid [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]
Dokaz

Budući da je \([\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]\) višekratnik i od \(\delta_m(a)\) i od \(\delta_m(b)\), prema svojstvu 2 vrijedi

\[ (ab)^{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]} = a^{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]} b^{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]} \equiv 1 \pmod m. \]

Ponovnom primjenom svojstva 2 dobivamo

\[ \delta_m(ab) \mid [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]

Time je dobivena desna relacija djeljivosti.

Obrnuto, budući da je

\[ 1 \equiv (ab)^{\delta_m(ab)\delta_m(b)} \equiv a^{\delta_m(ab)\delta_m(b)} \pmod m, \]

primjenom svojstva 2 dobivamo \(\delta_m(a)\mid\delta_m(ab)\delta_m(b)\). Kraćenjem obiju strana s \((\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))\) dobivamo

\[ \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))}\mid\delta_m(ab)\dfrac{\delta_m(b)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))}. \]

Nakon kraćenja zajedničkog faktora ta su dva razlomka relativno prosta, pa dobivamo

\[ \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))}\mid\delta_m(ab). \]

Analogno vrijedi i

\[ \dfrac{\delta_m(b)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))}\mid\delta_m(ab). \]

Budući da su lijeve strane tih dviju relacija djeljivosti relativno proste, vrijedi

\[ \dfrac{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))} =\dfrac{\delta_m(a)\delta_m(b)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))^2}\mid\delta_m(ab). \]

Time je dobivena lijeva relacija djeljivosti.

U slučaju kada su redovi elemenata \(a\) i \(b\) relativno prosti, ovaj zaključak ima jednostavniji oblik.

Svojstvo 4'

Za \(a,b\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) takve da je \(a,b\perp m\) vrijedi

\[ \delta_m(ab) = \delta_m(a)\delta_m(b) \iff \delta_m(a)\perp\delta_m(b). \]
Dokaz

Ako je \(\delta_m(a)\perp\delta_m(b)\), tada su sve relacije djeljivosti u svojstvu 4 jednakosti, pa vrijedi

\[ \delta_m(ab) = [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)] = \delta_m(a)\delta_m(b). \]

Obrnuto, ako je \(\delta_m(ab)=\delta_m(a)\delta_m(b)\), tada prema svojstvu 4 vrijedi

\[ \delta_m(a)\delta_m(b) = \delta_m(ab) \mid [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]

Iz toga odmah slijedi \((\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))=1\), tj. \(\delta_m(a)\perp\delta_m(b)\).

U općem slučaju ograda dobivena u svojstvu 4 već je najbolja moguća. Slučaj u kojem red umnoška postiže donju ogradu lako je konstruirati: na primjer, za \((a,b,m)=(3,5,7)\) vrijedi \(\delta_m(a)=\delta_m(b)=6\), ali red njihova umnoška je \(\delta_m(ab)=1\).

Iako u općem slučaju red umnoška \(ab\) ne mora biti najmanji zajednički višekratnik njihovih redova, uvijek se može pronaći element čiji je red jednak tom najmanjem zajedničkom višekratniku.

Svojstvo 5

Za \(a,b\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) takve da je \(a,b\perp m\) uvijek postoji \(c\in\mathbf Z\) takav da je \(c\perp m\) i

\[ \delta_m(c) = [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]
Dokaz

Promotrimo rastave na proste faktore:

\[ \delta_m(a) = \prod_p p^{\alpha_p},~ \delta_m(b) = \prod_p p^{\beta_p}. \]

Prema odnosu veličina \(\alpha_p\) i \(\beta_p\) svi se prosti faktori mogu podijeliti u dvije klase:

\[ A = \{p : \alpha_p \ge \beta_p\}, ~ B = \{p : \alpha_p < \beta_p\}. \]

U skladu s tim označimo redom

\[ \gamma_A = \prod_{p\in A}p^{\alpha_p},~\gamma_B = \prod_{p\in B}p^{\alpha_p},~\eta_A = \prod_{p\in A}p^{\beta_p},~\eta_B = \prod_{p\in B}p^{\beta_p}, \]

pa vrijedi \(\delta_m(a) = \gamma_A\gamma_B\) i \(\delta_m(b)=\eta_A\eta_B\). Prema svojstvu 3 vrijedi

\[ \begin{aligned} \delta_m(a^{\gamma_B}) &= \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{(\delta_m(a),\gamma_B)} = \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{\gamma_B} = \gamma_A,\\ \delta_m(b^{\eta_A}) &= \dfrac{\delta_m(b)}{(\delta_m(b),\eta_A)} = \dfrac{\delta_m(b)}{\eta_A} = \eta_B. \end{aligned} \]

Budući da je \(\gamma_A\perp\eta_B\), prema svojstvu 4' vrijedi

\[ \delta_m(a^{\gamma_B}b^{\eta_A}) = \gamma_A\eta_B = \prod_p p^{\max\{\alpha_p,\beta_p\}} = [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]

Dakle, \(c=a^{\gamma_B}b^{\eta_A}\) upravo je element reda \([\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]\).

Ovaj se zaključak često koristi za konstrukciju elemenata zadanog reda.

Primitivni korijen

Primitivni korijeni posebni su elementi – njihov je red jednak broju elemenata reduciranog sustava ostataka modulo \(m\).

Primitivni korijen

Za \(m\in\mathbf N_+\), ako postoji \(g\in\mathbf Z\) takav da je \(g\perp m\) i \(\delta_m(g)=|\mathbf Z_m^*|=\varphi(m)\), tada se \(g\) naziva primitivni korijen modulo \(m\) (engl. primitive root modulo \(m\)). Pritom je \(\varphi(m)\) Eulerova funkcija.

Primitivni korijen modulo \(m\) ne postoji za svaki pozitivni cijeli broj \(m\). Prema gornjem svojstvu 1, ako primitivni korijen \(g\) modulo \(m\) postoji, tada su klase kongruencije kojima pripadaju \(g,g^2,\cdots,g^{\varphi(m)}\) međusobno različite i tvore reducirani sustav ostataka modulo \(m\). Posebno, za prost broj \(p\) ostatci \(g^i\bmod p\) za \(i=1,2,\cdots,p-1\) međusobno su različiti.

Napomena

U apstraktnoj algebri primitivni korijen upravo je generator cikličke grupe. Taj pojam nosi naziv „primitivni korijen” samo u grupi koju reducirani sustav ostataka modulo \(m\) tvori s obzirom na množenje; u općim cikličkim grupama govori se o „generatoru”. Nije svaka grupa koju reducirani sustav ostataka modulo \(m\) tvori s obzirom na množenje ciklička: postojanje primitivnog korijena znači da je ona izomorfna cikličkoj grupi, a nepostojanje da nije.

Kada je modul \(1\), multiplikativna grupa cijelih brojeva modulo \(1\) jest \(\{0\}\). Ona je očito ciklička, pa je primitivni korijen \(0\).

Kriterij za primitivni korijen

Ako su poznati svi prosti faktori broja \(\varphi(m)\), lako je provjeriti je li zadani \(g\) primitivni korijen modulo \(m\).

Teorem

Za cijeli broj \(m\ge 3\) i \(g\perp m\), \(g\) je primitivni korijen modulo \(m\) ako i samo ako za svaki prosti faktor \(p\) broja \(\varphi(m)\) vrijedi

\[ g^{\frac{\varphi(m)}{p}}\not\equiv 1 \pmod m. \]
Dokaz

Nužnost je očita. Za dokaz dovoljnosti koristimo kontradikciju. Ako \(g\) nije primitivni korijen modulo \(m\), tada nužno vrijedi \(\delta_m(g)< \varphi(m)\). Prema svojstvu 2 i Eulerovu teoremu, \(\delta_m(g)\mid\varphi(m)\). Neka je stoga \(p\) neki prosti faktor broja \(\dfrac{\varphi(m)}{\delta_m(g)}\); tada vrijedi \(\delta_m(g)\mid\dfrac{\varphi(m)}{p}\). Ponovnom primjenom svojstva 2 dobivamo

\[ g^{\frac{\varphi(m)}{p}} \equiv 1 \pmod m. \]

No \(p\) je ujedno prosti faktor broja \(\varphi(m)\), što je u suprotnosti s pretpostavkom teorema. Time je dokazana dovoljnost.

Broj primitivnih korijena

Primitivni korijen, ako postoji, ne mora biti jedinstven. Općenito, o mogućim redovima svih elemenata reduciranog sustava ostataka modulo \(m\) i o broju elemenata određenog reda vrijedi sljedeći zaključak:

Teorem

Ako pozitivni cijeli broj \(m\) ima primitivni korijen \(g\), tada element reda \(d\) modulo \(m\) postoji ako i samo ako \(d\mid\varphi(m)\), i tada takvih elemenata ima točno \(\varphi(d)\). Posebno, broj primitivnih korijena modulo \(m\) jest \(\varphi(\varphi(m))\).

Dokaz

Prema definiciji primitivnog korijena, sve reducirane klase kongruencije modulo \(m\) mogu se zapisati u obliku \(g^k\bmod m\), pri čemu je \(k\) jedan od brojeva \(1,2,\cdots,\varphi(m)\). Prema svojstvu 3 red tih elemenata jednak je

\[ \delta_m(g^k) = \dfrac{\varphi(m)}{(\varphi(m),k)}. \]

Dakle, element reda \(d\) postoji ako i samo ako \(d\mid\varphi(m)\). Štoviše, za \(d\mid\varphi(m)\), uz oznaku \(d'=\varphi(m)/d\), skup tih elemenata jest

\[ \begin{aligned} A &= \{g^k : (\varphi(m),k)=d',~1\le k \le\varphi(m)\} \\ &= \{g^k : d'\mid k,~ (d, k/d') = 1,~ 1 \le k/d' \le d\}. \end{aligned} \]

Brojevi \(k'=k/d'\) koji odgovaraju tim elementima upravo su pozitivni cijeli brojevi koji ne premašuju \(d\) i relativno su prosti s \(d\). Prema definiciji Eulerove funkcije, njih je \(\varphi(d)\).

Teorem o postojanju primitivnog korijena

U ovom odjeljku dokazujemo sljedeći teorem o postojanju primitivnog korijena:

Teorem

Primitivni korijen modulo \(m\) postoji ako i samo ako je \(m=1,2,4,p^e,2p^e\), gdje je \(p\) neparan prost broj i \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

Za dokaz ove tvrdnje treba zasebno razmotriti sljedeća četiri slučaja:

  1. \(m=1,2,4\): primitivni korijeni su redom \(g=0,1,3\) i očito postoje.

  2. \(m=p^{e}\) je potencija neparnog prostog broja, gdje je \(p\) neparan prost broj, \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

    Lema 1

    Za neparan prost broj \(p\) postoji primitivni korijen modulo \(p\).

    Dokaz

    Dokaz se sastoji od dva koraka.

    Prvi korak: za \(d\mid(p-1)\) kongruencija \(x^d\equiv 1\pmod p\) ima točno \(d\) međusobno različitih rješenja.

    Neka je \(p-1=kd\) i promotrimo polinom

    \[ f(x) = x^{d(k-1)} + x^{d(k-2)} + \cdots + x^d + 1. \]

    Prema Eulerovu teoremu kongruencija \((x^d-1)f(x)=x^{p-1}-1\equiv 0\pmod{p}\) ima točno \(p-1\) međusobno različitih rješenja. Ta su rješenja redom nultočke polinoma \(x^d-1\) odnosno \(f(x)\). Prema Lagrangeovu teoremu oni mogu imati najviše \(d\) odnosno \(d(k-1)\) međusobno različitih nultočaka. Budući da je \(d+d(k-1)=p-1\), prvi od njih mora imati točno \(d\) međusobno različitih nultočaka. To pokazuje da kongruencija \(x^d\equiv 1\pmod p\) ima točno \(d\) međusobno različitih rješenja.

    Drugi korak: za \(d\mid(p-1)\) postoji točno \(\varphi(d)\) elemenata reda \(d\).

    Poredamo sve djelitelje broja \(p-1\) i primijenimo indukciju. Budući da element reda \(1\) može biti samo \(1\), takav je element samo jedan, pa baza indukcije vrijedi. Za \(d\mid(p-1)\), prema gornjem svojstvu 2, rješenja kongruencije \(x^d\equiv 1\pmod p\) nužno zadovoljavaju \(\delta_p(x)\mid d\). Stoga je broj elemenata reda \(d\) među njima jednak

    \[ N(d) = d - \sum_{e\mid d,~e\neq d} N(e) = d - \sum_{e\mid d,~e\neq d} \varphi(e) = \varphi(d). \]

    Druga jednakost je pretpostavka indukcije, a treća je svojstvo Eulerove funkcije. Matematičkom indukcijom zaključujemo da za sve \(d\mid(p-1)\) postoji točno \(\varphi(d)\) elemenata reda \(d\).

    Posebno, za \(d=p-1\) postoji točno \(\varphi(p-1)\) elemenata reda \((p-1)\). Dakle, primitivni korijen modulo \(p\) postoji.

    Lema 2

    Za neparan prost broj \(p\) i \(e \in \mathbf{N}_+\) postoji primitivni korijen modulo \(p^e\).

    Dokaz

    Dokaz se sastoji od tri koraka.

    Prvi korak: postoji primitivni korijen \(g\) modulo \(p\) takav da je \(g^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^2}\).

    Uzmimo bilo koji primitivni korijen \(g\) modulo \(p\). Ako on ne zadovoljava uvjet, tj. ako je \(g^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p^2}\), tada se može pokazati da \(g+p\) zadovoljava uvjet: \(g+p\) je također primitivni korijen modulo \(p\) i vrijedi

    \[ \begin{aligned} (g+p)^{p-1} &\equiv \binom{p-1}{0}g^{p-1} + \binom{p-1}{1}g^{p-2}p \\ &= g^{p-1} + g^{p-2}p(p-1) \\ &\equiv 1 - pg^{p-2} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}. \end{aligned} \]

    Drugi korak: za gore odabrani \(g\) i svaki \(e\ge 1\) vrijedi \(g^{\varphi(p^e)}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{e+1}}\).

    Izbor \(g\) jamči da tvrdnja vrijedi za \(e=1\). Pretpostavimo da tvrdnja vrijedi za \(e\); dokazujemo da vrijedi i za \(e+1\). Za svaki \(e \ge 1\), prema Eulerovu teoremu, postoji cijeli broj \(t\) takav da vrijedi

    \[ g^{\varphi(p^e)} = 1 + t p^e \]

    Prema pretpostavci indukcije, \(t\perp p\). Budući da je \(\varphi(p^{e+1})=p\varphi(p^e)\), vrijedi

    \[ g^{\varphi(p^{e+1})} = \left(g^{\varphi(p^{e})}\right)^p = (1 + t p^e)^p \equiv 1 + t p^{e+1} \pmod{p^{e+2}}. \]

    Zajedno s \(t\perp p\) zaključujemo da je \(g^{\varphi(p^{e+1})}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{e+2}}\). Matematičkom indukcijom tvrdnja vrijedi.

    Treći korak: gore odabrani \(g\) primitivni je korijen modulo \(p^e\) za svaki \(e\ge 1\).

    Izbor \(g\) jamči da tvrdnja vrijedi za \(e=1\). Pretpostavimo da tvrdnja vrijedi za \(e\); dokazujemo da vrijedi i za \(e+1\). Označimo kraće \(\delta_{p^{e+1}}(g)\) s \(\delta\). Budući da je \(g^\delta\equiv 1\pmod{p^{e+1}}\), nužno vrijedi i \(g^\delta\equiv 1\pmod{p^e}\). Prema pretpostavci indukcije \(\delta_{p^e}(g) = \varphi(p^e)\). Stoga prema gornjem svojstvu 2 reda vrijedi \(\varphi(p^e)\mid\delta\). Prema Eulerovu teoremu vrijedi \(\delta\mid\varphi(p^{e+1})\). No \(\varphi(p^{e+1})=p\varphi(p^e)\). Stoga postoje samo dvije mogućnosti: \(\delta=\varphi(p^e)\) ili \(\delta=\varphi(p^{e+1})\). Međutim, zaključak drugog koraka pokazuje da je \(g^{\varphi(p^e)}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{e+1}}\). Dakle, mogućnost \(\delta=\varphi(p^e)\) otpada. Jedina je mogućnost \(\delta=\varphi(p^{e+1})\). To pokazuje da je \(g\) primitivni korijen modulo \(p^{e+1}\). Matematičkom indukcijom tvrdnja vrijedi za sve \(e\ge 1\).

  3. \(m=2p^{e}\), gdje je \(p\) neparan prost broj, \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

    Lema 3

    Za neparan prost broj \(p\) i \(e \in \mathbf{N}_+\) postoji primitivni korijen modulo \(2p^e\).

    Dokaz

    Neka je \(g\) primitivni korijen modulo \(p^{e}\); tada je i \(g+p^e\) primitivni korijen modulo \(p^{e}\). Jedan od njih nužno je neparan; bez smanjenja općenitosti neka je to \(g\). Očito je \((g,2p^e)=1\). Neka je \(\delta=\delta_{2p^e}(g)\); treba dokazati da je \(\delta=\varphi(2p^e)\). Prema Eulerovu teoremu, \(\delta\mid\varphi(2p^e)\). Istodobno, po definiciji je \(g^\delta\equiv 1\pmod{2p^e}\), pa je \(g^\delta\equiv 1\pmod{p^e}\); stoga prema svojstvu 2 reda i izboru \(g\) vrijedi \(\delta_{p^e}(g)=\varphi(p^e)\mid \delta\). Iz izraza za Eulerovu funkciju slijedi \(\varphi(2p^e) = \varphi(p^e)\). Dakle, \(\delta=\delta_{2p^e}(g)=\varphi(p^e)=\varphi(2p^e)\). To pokazuje da je \(g\) primitivni korijen modulo \(2p^e\).

  4. \(m\ne 1,2,4,p^{e},2p^{e}\), gdje je \(p\) neparan prost broj, \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

    Lema 4

    Pretpostavimo da je \(m\neq 1,2,4\) i da ne postoje neparan prost broj \(p\) i pozitivni cijeli broj \(e\) takvi da je \(m=p^e\) ili \(m=2p^e\). Tada primitivni korijen modulo \(m\) ne postoji.

    Dokaz

    Za \(m=2^e\) uz \(e\ge 3\) pretpostavimo da primitivni korijen \(g\) modulo \(m\) postoji. Budući da je \(g\perp m\), on je nužno neparan. Neka je \(g=2k+1\) uz \(k\in\mathbf N\); tada vrijedi

    \[ \begin{aligned} g^{2^{e-2}} &=(2k+1)^{2^{e-2}} \\ &\equiv 1 + \binom{2^{e-2}}{1}(2k) + \binom{{2^{e-2}}}{2}(2k)^2 \\ &= 1 + 2^{e-1}k + 2^{e-1}(2^{e-2}-1)k^2 \\ &= 1 + 2^{e-1}(k + (2^{e-2}-1)k^2) \\ &\equiv 1 \pmod{2^{e}}. \end{aligned} \]

    U pretposljednjem retku, budući da \(k\) i \((2^{e-2}-1)k^2\) imaju istu parnost, njihov je zbroj paran. Prema definiciji reda, \(\delta_{2^{e}}(g)\le 2^{e-2}< \varphi(2^{e}) = 2^{e-1}\). To je u suprotnosti s pretpostavkom da je \(g\) primitivni korijen. Kontradikcijom zaključujemo da takav primitivni korijen ne postoji.

    Pretpostavimo da \(m\) zadovoljava navedene uvjete i da nije potencija broja \(2\); tada nužno postoje \(2 < m_1 < m_2\) takvi da je \(m_1\perp m_2\) i \(m=m_1m_2\). Pretpostavimo da primitivni korijen \(g\) modulo \(m\) postoji. Budući da je \(g\perp m\), za \(i=1,2\) vrijedi \(g\perp m_i\). Prema Eulerovu teoremu,

    \[ g^{\varphi(m_i)} \equiv 1 \pmod{m_i}. \]

    Budući da je \(m_i > 2\), broj \(\varphi(m_i)\) je paran, pa za \(i=1,2\) vrijedi

    \[ g^{\frac{1}{2}\varphi(m_1)\varphi(m_2)} \equiv 1 \pmod{m_i}. \]

    Prema kineskom teoremu o ostatcima vrijedi

    \[ g^{\frac{1}{2}\varphi(m_1)\varphi(m_2)} \equiv 1 \pmod{m}. \]

    Budući da je još \(\varphi(m)=\varphi(m_1)\varphi(m_2)\), prema definiciji reda vrijedi

    \[ \delta_m(g) \le \frac{1}{2}\varphi(m_1)\varphi(m_2) = \dfrac{1}{2}\varphi(m) < \varphi(m). \]

    To je u suprotnosti s pretpostavkom da je \(g\) primitivni korijen modulo \(m\). Stoga, kontradikcijom, primitivni korijen modulo \(m\) ne postoji.

Objedinjujući ova četiri slučaja, dobili smo nužan i dovoljan uvjet da broj ima primitivni korijen.

Algoritam za pronalaženje primitivnog korijena

Za svaki modul \(m\) za koji primitivni korijen postoji, da bismo našli njegov primitivni korijen \(g\), dovoljno je nabrajati moguće pozitivne cijele brojeve i za svaki redom provjeriti je li primitivni korijen. Pri nabrajanju obično postoje dva pristupa: nabrajanje redom od manjih prema većima ili slučajno generiranje pozitivnih cijelih brojeva. Stvarna učinkovitost tih dvaju pristupa podjednaka je.

Pri nabrajanju redom od manjih prema većima dobiva se najmanji primitivni korijen \(g_m\) modulo \(m\), pa složenost dijela s nabrajanjem ovisi o veličini \(g_m\). O tome postoje sljedeće ocjene:

  • Ocjena gornje ograde: Wang Yuan5 i Burgess6 dokazali su da za najmanji primitivni korijen prostog broja \(p\) vrijedi \(g_p=O\left(p^{0.25+\epsilon}\right)\), gdje je \(\epsilon>0\). Cohen, Odoni i Stothers7 te Elliott i Murata8 dokazali su da ta ocjena vrijedi i za module \(p^2\) odnosno \(2p^2\), gdje je \(p\) neparan prost broj. Budući da je za \(e>2\) primitivni korijen modulo \(p^2\) (odnosno \(2p^2\)) ujedno primitivni korijen modulo \(p^e\) (odnosno \(2p^e\)), gornja ograda \(O\left(p^{0.25+\epsilon}\right)\) za najmanji primitivni korijen vrijedi u svim slučajevima.
  • Ocjena donje ograde: Fridlender9 i Salié10 dokazali su da postoji \(C>0\) takav da za beskonačno mnogo prostih brojeva \(p\) najmanji primitivni korijen zadovoljava \(g_p > C\log p\).
  • Ocjena prosječnog slučaja: Burgess i Elliott11 dokazali su da je u prosječnom slučaju najmanji primitivni korijen prostog broja \(p\) jednak \(g_p=O((\log p)^2(\log\log p)^4)\). Elliott i Murata12 nadalje su postavili slutnju da je prosječna vrijednost najmanjeg primitivnog korijena prostog broja \(p\) konstanta te su numeričkom provjerom13 dobili da iznosi približno \(4.926\). Potom su Elliott i Murata8 tu slutnju proširili na slučaj modula \(2p^2\).

Prema tim analizama, pri pronalaženju najmanjeg primitivnog korijena grubom silom složenost dijela s nabrajanjem \(O(g_m(\log m)^2)\) prihvatljiva je.

Osim nabrajanja od manjih prema većima, primitivni korijen može se tražiti i slučajnim generiranjem pozitivnih cijelih brojeva i njihovom provjerom. Gustoća primitivnih korijena nije mala:1

\[ \dfrac{\varphi(\varphi(m))}{m} = \Omega\left(\dfrac{1}{\log\log m}\right). \]

Stoga je pri traženju primitivnog korijena slučajnom metodom očekivana složenost dijela s nabrajanjem \(O((\log m)^2\log\log m)\).

Treba napomenuti da je za provjeru primitivnog korijena potrebno poznavati rastav broja \(\varphi(m)\) na proste faktore. Među uobičajenim algoritmima za faktorizaciju u algoritamskim natjecanjima i Pollardov rho algoritam, koji ima najbolju složenost, treba \(O(m^{1/4+\epsilon})\) vremena. Stoga, dok god rastav broja \(\varphi(m)\) na proste faktore nije poznat, bez obzira na način nabrajanja usko grlo složenosti pronalaženja primitivnog korijena jest korak faktorizacije, a ne dio s nabrajanjem i provjerom.

Carmichaelova funkcija

Za razliku od reda elementa modulo \(m\), koji je lokalan pojam, Carmichaelova funkcija globalan je pojam. Ona je najmanji zajednički period potencija svih cijelih brojeva relativno prostih s \(m\).

Carmichaelova funkcija

Za \(m\in\mathbf N_+\) definiramo \(\lambda(m)\) kao najmanji pozitivni cijeli broj \(n\) za koji kongruencija \(a^n\equiv 1\pmod m\) vrijedi za sve \(a\perp m\). Funkcija \(\lambda:\mathbf N_+\to\mathbf N_+\) naziva se Carmichaelova funkcija.

Prema svojstvu 2, to da \(a^n\equiv 1\pmod m\) vrijedi za sve \(a\perp m\) znači da \(\delta_m(a)\mid n\) vrijedi za sve \(a\perp m\). Drugim riječima, pozitivni cijeli broj \(n\) koji zadovoljava taj uvjet nužno je zajednički višekratnik svih \(\delta_m(a)\). Stoga je najmanji takav \(n\) upravo njihov najmanji zajednički višekratnik:

\[ \lambda(m) = \operatorname{lcm}\{\delta_m(a) : a\perp m\}. \]

To se često koristi i kao ekvivalentna definicija Carmichaelove funkcije.

Uzastopnom primjenom svojstva 5 zaključujemo da nužno postoji neki element \(a\perp m\) takav da je \(\delta_m(a)=\lambda(m)\). Stoga se gornji izraz može zapisati i kao

\[ \lambda(m) = \max\{\delta_m(a) : a\perp m\}. \]

Element \(a\perp m\) koji postiže taj maksimum naziva se i \(\lambda\)‑primitivni korijen modulo \(m\). On postoji za svaki modul \(m\).

Rekurzivna formula

Carmichaelova funkcija jest aritmetička funkcija. U ovom odjeljku razmatramo jednu njezinu rekurzivnu formulu i iz nje izvodimo još jedan dokaz teorema o postojanju primitivnog korijena.

Iako nije multiplikativna funkcija, pri računanju Carmichaelove funkcije relativno prosti faktori također se mogu obraditi zasebno.

Lema

Za relativno proste pozitivne cijele brojeve \(m_1,m_2\) vrijedi \(\lambda(m_1m_2)=[\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]\).

Dokaz

Neka su \(a_1\) i \(a_2\) redom \(\lambda\)‑primitivni korijeni modulo \(m_1\) i modulo \(m_2\). Neka je \(m=m_1m_2\); prema kineskom teoremu o ostatcima postoji \(a\perp m\) takav da \(a\equiv a_i\pmod{m_i}\) vrijedi za \(i=1,2\). Budući da je \(a^{\lambda(m)}\equiv 1\pmod m\), za \(i=1,2\) vrijedi \(a_i^{\lambda(m)} \equiv 1\pmod{m_i}\), pa prema svojstvu 2 i izboru \(a_i\) vrijedi \(\lambda(m_i)=\delta_{m_i}(a_i)\mid \lambda(m)\). To pokazuje da \([\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]\mid\lambda(m)\).

Obrnuto, za svaki \(a\perp m\) i \(i=1,2\) vrijedi \(a^{[\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]} \equiv 1 \pmod{m_i}\). Primjenom kineskog teorema o ostatcima dobivamo da \(a^{[\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]} \equiv 1 \pmod{m}\) vrijedi za sve \(a\perp m\). Prema definiciji Carmichaelove funkcije, \(\lambda(m)\mid [\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]\).

Time vrijedi jednakost iz tvrdnje.

Stoga u nastavku treba samo izračunati vrijednosti Carmichaelove funkcije u potencijama prostih brojeva. Najprije obrađujemo slučaj potencija broja \(2\).

Lema

Za \(m=2^e\) uz \(e\in\mathbf N_+\) vrijedi \(\lambda(2)=1\), \(\lambda(4)=2\), a za \(e\ge 3\) vrijedi \(\lambda(m)=2^{e-2}\).

Dokaz

Slučajeve \(m=2,4\) dovoljno je provjeriti zasebno. Za \(m=2^e\) uz \(e\ge 3\) najprije ponovimo prvi dio dokaza gornje leme 4, čime dobivamo \(\lambda(m)\le 2^{e-2}\). Zatim je dovoljno dokazati da postoji element reda \(2^{e-2}\). U tu svrhu vrijedi

\[ 5^{2^{e-3}} = (1 + 2^2)^{2^{e-3}} \equiv 1 + 2^2\times 2^{e-3} = 1 + 2^{e-1} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{2^e}. \]

To pokazuje da \(\delta_m(5)\nmid 2^{e-3}\), a budući da \(\delta_m(5) \mid 2^{e-2}\), \(5\) može biti jedino element reda \(2^{e-2}\). To pokazuje da je \(\lambda(m)=2^{e-2}\).

U dokazu ove leme zapravo smo dobili opis strukture reduciranog sustava ostataka modulo \(2^e\):

Korolar

Neka je modul \(2^e\) uz \(e \ge 2\). Tada je svaki neparan broj kongruentan točno jednom cijelom broju oblika \(\pm 5^k\), gdje je \(k\in\mathbf N\) i \(k < 2^{e-2}\). Drugim riječima, brojevi \(\pm 1,\pm 5,\cdots,\pm 5^{2^{e-2}-1}\) međusobno su nekongruentni i tvore reducirani sustav ostataka.

Dokaz

Lako se provjerava da tvrdnja vrijedi za \(e=2\). Za \(e \ge 3\), budući da smo u prethodnom dokazu već dobili da je red broja \(5\) modulo \(2^e\) jednak \(2^{e-2}\), brojevi \(1,5,\cdots,5^{2^{e-2}-1}\) međusobno su nekongruentni. Budući da svi ti cijeli brojevi daju ostatak \(1\) modulo \(4\), a njihovi suprotni brojevi daju ostatak \(3\) modulo \(4\), brojevi \(\pm 1,\pm 5,\cdots,\pm 5^{2^{e-2}-1}\) međusobno su nekongruentni modulo \(2^e\). Budući da ih ima ukupno \(2^{e-1}\), što je upravo veličina reduciranog sustava ostataka modulo \(2^{e}\), oni tvore sam reducirani sustav ostataka.

Zatim obrađujemo slučaj potencija neparnih prostih brojeva.

Lema

Za \(m=p^e\), gdje je \(p\) neparan prost broj i \(e\in\mathbf N_+\), vrijedi \(\lambda(m)=p^{e-1}(p-1)\).

Dokaz

Najprije dokazujemo da tvrdnja vrijedi za \(e=1\), tj. kada je \(m=p\) neparan prost broj. U tu svrhu, prema definiciji Carmichaelove funkcije, svi cijeli brojevi \(a\) relativno prosti s \(p\) rješenja su kongruencije \(x^{\lambda(p)}\equiv 1\pmod{p}\). Modulo \(p\) ta kongruencija ima ukupno \(p-1\) međusobno različitih rješenja. Prema Lagrangeovu teoremu vrijedi \(p-1\le\lambda(p)\). Istodobno, Eulerov teorem zahtijeva \(\lambda(p)\mid\varphi(p)=p-1\). Dakle, \(\lambda(p)=p-1\).

Za \(m=p^e\) uz \(e> 1\) možemo početi dokazom da je \(1+p\) element reda \(p^{e-1}\). U tu svrhu vrijedi

\[ (1+p)^{p^{e-1}} \equiv 1,\quad (1+p)^{p^{e-2}} \equiv 1 + p^{e-1} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p^e}. \]

Dakle, \(\delta_m(1+p)=p^{e-1}\). Nadalje, neka je \(g\) primitivni korijen modulo \(p\); tada, budući da je \(g^{\delta_m(g)}\equiv 1 \pmod{p}\), prema svojstvu 2 reda vrijedi \(p-1\mid\delta_m(g)\). Prema definiciji Carmichaelove funkcije i Eulerovu teoremu vrijedi

\[ p^{e-1}(p-1) \mid [\delta_m(1+p),\delta_m(g)] \mid \lambda(m) \mid \varphi(m) = p^{e-1}(p-1). \]

Dakle, \(\lambda(m)=p^{e-1}(p-1)\).

Jednostavnim sažimanjem rezultata ovog odjeljka dobivamo rekurzivnu formulu za Carmichaelovu funkciju:

Teorem

Za svaki pozitivni cijeli broj \(m\) vrijedi

\[ \lambda(m) = \begin{cases} \varphi(m), & \text{if }m=1,2,4,p^e\text{ for odd prime }p\text{ and }e \ge 1,\\ \frac{1}{2}\varphi(m), &\text{if }m=2^e,~e\ge 3,\\ \operatorname{lcm}\{\lambda(p_1^{e_1}),\lambda(p_2^{e_2}),\cdots,\lambda(p_s^{e_s})\}, &\text{if }m = p_1^{e_1}p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_s^{e_s}\text{ for distinct }p_1,p_2,\cdots,p_s. \end{cases} \]

Pomoću te rekurzivne formule može se pojačati prethodni rezultat:

Korolar

Za pozitivne cijele brojeve \(m_1,m_2\) vrijedi \(\lambda([m_1,m_2])=[\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]\).

Usporedbom definicija primitivnog korijena i Carmichaelove funkcije vidimo da primitivni korijen modulo \(m\) postoji ako i samo ako je \(\lambda(m)=\varphi(m)\). Iz rekurzivne formule za Carmichaelovu funkciju lako se indukcijom izvodi sljedeći rezultat:

Korolar

Primitivni korijen modulo \(m\) postoji ako i samo ako je \(m=1,2,4,p^e,2p^e\), gdje je \(p\) neparan prost broj i \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

Budući da se u dokazu rekurzivne formule u ovom odjeljku nije koristio teorem o postojanju primitivnog korijena, ovo predstavlja još jedan dokaz tog teorema.

Carmichaelovi brojevi

Pomoću Carmichaelove funkcije mogu se razmatrati svojstva i raspodjela Carmichaelovih brojeva (OEIS: A002997). To su složeni brojevi koje Fermatov test prostosti nikada ne može ispravno isključiti.

Carmichaelov broj

Za složeni broj \(n\), ako za sve cijele brojeve \(a\perp n\) vrijedi kongruencija \(a^{n-1} \equiv 1 \pmod n\), tada se \(n\) naziva Carmichaelov broj.

Najmanji Carmichaelov broj jest \(561 = 3 \times 11 \times 17\).

Prema definiciji Carmichaelove funkcije, složeni broj \(n\) Carmichaelov je broj ako i samo ako \(\lambda(n)\mid n-1\), gdje je \(\lambda(n)\) Carmichaelova funkcija. Nadalje, dobiva se sljedeći način provjere je li složeni broj \(n\) Carmichaelov broj:

Korseltov kriterij14

Složeni broj \(n\) Carmichaelov je broj ako i samo ako \(n\) nema kvadratnih faktora i za svaki prosti faktor \(p\) broja \(n\) vrijedi \((p-1) \mid (n-1)\).

Dokaz

Najprije dokazujemo nužnost uvjeta. Pretpostavimo da \(\lambda(n)\mid (n-1)\). Pregledom rekurzivne formule za Carmichaelovu funkciju vidimo da, ako \(n\) ima kvadratni faktor \(p^2\), nužno vrijedi \(p\mid \lambda(n)\). No \(p\nmid (n-1)\), što je kontradikcija. Slično, za svaki prosti faktor \(p\) broja \(n\) rekurzivna formula za Carmichaelovu funkciju pokazuje da \((p-1)\mid \lambda(n)\), pa vrijedi i \((p-1) \mid (n-1)\).

Zatim dokazujemo dovoljnost uvjeta. Budući da je \(n\) složen broj bez kvadratnih faktora, ima barem dva različita prosta faktora, među kojima je nužno i neki neparan prosti faktor \(p\); iz \((p-1)\mid(n-1)\) slijedi da je \(n-1\) paran, pa je \(n\) nužno neparan. Za neparan složen broj \(n\) bez kvadratnih faktora, prema rekurzivnoj formuli za Carmichaelovu funkciju, vrijedi \(\lambda(n)=\operatorname{lcm}\{p-1:p\mid n\}\). Stoga, čim \((p-1) \mid (n-1)\) vrijedi za sve proste faktore \(p\), nužno vrijedi \(\lambda(n)\mid (n-1)\).

Polazeći od tog kriterija, mogu se izvesti neka jednostavna svojstva Carmichaelovih brojeva:

Korolar

Carmichaelovi brojevi neparni su, nemaju kvadratnih faktora i imaju barem \(3\) različita prosta faktora.

Dokaz

Prva dva svojstva izravno slijede iz Korseltova kriterija i njegova dokaza. Za treće svojstvo dovoljno je još dokazati da umnožak \(n=p_1p_2\) dvaju različitih prostih brojeva \(p_1,p_2\) nikada nije Carmichaelov broj. Pretpostavimo da je \(n=p_1p_2\) Carmichaelov broj. Prema Korseltovu kriteriju, \((p_i-1)\mid (n-1)\). No vrijedi

\[ n-1=p_1p_2-1\equiv p_2-1 \pmod{p_1-1}. \]

Dakle, \((p_1-1)\mid(p_2-1)\). Analogno, \((p_2-1)\mid(p_1-1)\). Drugim riječima, \(p_1=p_2\). To je u suprotnosti s pretpostavkom. Stoga Carmichaelov broj \(n\) ima barem \(3\) različita prosta faktora.

Pomoću analitičke teorije brojeva mogu se dobiti i neka svojstva raspodjele Carmichaelovih brojeva. Neka je \(C(n)\) broj Carmichaelovih brojeva manjih ili jednakih \(n\). Alford, Granville i Pomerance2 dokazali su da za dovoljno velik \(n\) vrijedi \(C(n)>n^{2/7}\). Iz toga slijedi da Carmichaelovih brojeva ima beskonačno mnogo. Prije toga je Erdős3 već dokazao da je \(C(n) < n\exp\left(-c\dfrac{\ln n\ln\ln\ln n}{\ln\ln n}\right)\), gdje je \(c\) konstanta. Stoga je raspodjela Carmichaelovih brojeva (u usporedbi s prostim brojevima) vrlo rijetka. Zapravo vrijedi4 \(C(10^9)=646\), \(C(10^{18})=1~401~644\).

Prerequisites: Fermat's little theorem, Euler's theorem, Lagrange's theorem

The multiplicative order and the primitive root are important tools for understanding the multiplicative structure of the reduced residue system \(\mathbf Z_m^*\) modulo \(m\). Concepts such as the discrete logarithm are defined on top of them. A more general discussion can be found in the relevant sections of the group theory and ring theory pages of the abstract algebra part.

Order

In this section we always assume that the modulus \(m\in\mathbf N_+\) and the base \(a\in\mathbf Z\) are coprime, i.e. \((a,m)=1\), which is also written as \(a\perp m\).

For \(n\in\mathbf Z\), the powers \(a^n\bmod m\) exhibit a cyclic structure. The minimum length of this cycle is the order of \(a\) modulo \(m\). The order is defined as the exponent at which the power \(a^n \bmod m\) returns to the starting point \(a^0\bmod m = 1\) for the first time:

Order

For \(a\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) with \(a\perp m\), the smallest positive integer \(n\) satisfying the congruence \(a^n \equiv 1 \pmod m\) is called the order of \(a\) modulo \(m\), denoted by \(\delta_m(a)\) or \(\operatorname{ord}_m(a)\).

Note

In abstract algebra, the "order" here is exactly the order of the element \(a\) in the group formed by the reduced residue system modulo \(m\) under multiplication. The notation \(\delta\) for the order is used only for this particular group. Many of the properties below generalize directly to properties of the order of a group element in abstract algebra.

There is also the concept of the "half-order", denoted by \(\delta^-\) in number theory. It is the smallest positive integer satisfying the congruence \(a^n \equiv -1 \pmod m\). The half-order is not a concept of group theory. The order always exists, while the half-order does not necessarily exist.

Cyclic structure of powers

Using the order, the cyclic structure of powers can be described. For the power \(a^n\bmod m\), divide the exponent \(n\) by the order \(\delta_m(a)\) with remainder:

\[ n = \delta_m(a)q + r, ~ 0\le r < \delta_m(a). \]

Then, by the laws of exponents, we obtain

\[ a^n = a^{\delta_m(a)q + r} = (a^{\delta_m(a)})^q \cdot a^r \equiv a^r \pmod m. \]

This shows that a power with an arbitrary exponent can be shifted into the first non-negative cycle. From this, a series of properties of the order can be derived.

Property 1

For \(a\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) with \(a\perp m\), the powers \(a^0(=1),a,a^2,\cdots,a^{\delta_m(a)-1}\) are pairwise incongruent modulo \(m\).

Proof

We argue by contradiction. Suppose there exist two numbers \(0\le i< j<\delta_m(a)\) with \(a^i\equiv a^j\pmod m\); then \(a^{j - i}\equiv 1\pmod m\). But \(0 < j - i < \delta_m(a)\). This contradicts the minimality of the order, so the statement holds.

Property 2

For \(a,n\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) with \(a\perp m\), the congruence \(a^n \equiv 1 \pmod m\) holds if and only if \(\delta_m(a)\mid n\).

Proof

As stated above, \(a^{n}\equiv a^{n\bmod\delta_m(a)}\pmod m\). By Property 1, the only \(r\) in the range \(0\le r < \delta_m(a)\) for which \(a^r\equiv 1\pmod m\) holds is \(r=0\). Therefore \(a^n \equiv 1 \pmod m\) holds if and only if \(n\bmod \delta_m(a) = 0\), that is, \(\delta_m(a)\mid n\).

By Euler's theorem, the congruence \(a^{\varphi(m)}\equiv 1\pmod m\) holds for all \(a\perp m\). Combined with Property 2, this means that \(\delta_m(a)\mid\varphi(m)\) for all \(a\perp m\). In other words, \(\varphi(m)\) is a common multiple of the orders of all \(a\perp m\). For a positive integer \(m\), the least common multiple of the orders \(\delta_m(a)\) of all \(a\perp m\), denoted by \(\lambda(m)\), is the Carmichael function of \(m\). Its properties are discussed in detail later.

Similarly to other cyclic structures, the order of \(a^k\) can be computed from the order of \(a\).

Property 3

For \(k,a\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) with \(a\perp m\), we have

\[ \delta_m(a^k) = \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{(\delta_m(a),k)}. \]
Proof

By Property 2, the congruence \((a^k)^n = a^{kn} \equiv 1\pmod m\) holds if and only if \(\delta_m(a) \mid kn\). This condition is equivalent to

\[ \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{\left(\delta_m(a),k\right)} \mid n. \]

The smallest positive integer satisfying this condition is

\[ \delta_m(a^k)=\dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{\left(\delta_m(a),k\right)}. \]

Order of a product

Let \(a,b\) be distinct integers coprime to \(m\). If the orders \(\delta_m(a)\) and \(\delta_m(b)\) are known, then some information about the order \(\delta_{m}(ab)\) of their product \(ab\) can also be obtained.

Property 4

For \(a,b\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) with \(a,b\perp m\), we have

\[ \dfrac{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))} \mid \delta_m(ab) \mid [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]
Proof

Since \([\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]\) is a multiple of both \(\delta_m(a)\) and \(\delta_m(b)\), by Property 2 we have

\[ (ab)^{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]} = a^{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]} b^{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]} \equiv 1 \pmod m. \]

Applying Property 2 again, we obtain

\[ \delta_m(ab) \mid [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]

This gives the divisibility relation on the right.

Conversely, since

\[ 1 \equiv (ab)^{\delta_m(ab)\delta_m(b)} \equiv a^{\delta_m(ab)\delta_m(b)} \pmod m, \]

applying Property 2 we obtain \(\delta_m(a)\mid\delta_m(ab)\delta_m(b)\). Canceling \((\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))\) on both sides, we obtain

\[ \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))}\mid\delta_m(ab)\dfrac{\delta_m(b)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))}. \]

After canceling the common factor, the two fractions are coprime, which gives

\[ \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))}\mid\delta_m(ab). \]

Similarly, we also have

\[ \dfrac{\delta_m(b)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))}\mid\delta_m(ab). \]

Since the left-hand sides of the two divisibility relations are coprime, we have

\[ \dfrac{[\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))} =\dfrac{\delta_m(a)\delta_m(b)}{(\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))^2}\mid\delta_m(ab). \]

This gives the divisibility relation on the left.

In the case where the orders of \(a\) and \(b\) are coprime, this conclusion has a simpler form.

Property 4'

For \(a,b\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) with \(a,b\perp m\), we have

\[ \delta_m(ab) = \delta_m(a)\delta_m(b) \iff \delta_m(a)\perp\delta_m(b). \]
Proof

If \(\delta_m(a)\perp\delta_m(b)\), then all divisibility relations in Property 4 are equalities, so

\[ \delta_m(ab) = [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)] = \delta_m(a)\delta_m(b). \]

Conversely, if \(\delta_m(ab)=\delta_m(a)\delta_m(b)\), then by Property 4 we have

\[ \delta_m(a)\delta_m(b) = \delta_m(ab) \mid [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]

This immediately shows \((\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b))=1\), i.e. \(\delta_m(a)\perp\delta_m(b)\).

In the general case, the bound obtained in Property 4 is already tight. A case in which the order of the product attains the lower bound is easy to construct: for example, for \((a,b,m)=(3,5,7)\) we have \(\delta_m(a)=\delta_m(b)=6\), but the order of their product is \(\delta_m(ab)=1\).

Although in the general case the order of the product \(ab\) is not necessarily the least common multiple of their orders, one can always find an element whose order equals this least common multiple.

Property 5

For \(a,b\in\mathbf Z,m\in\mathbf N_+\) with \(a,b\perp m\), there always exists \(c\in\mathbf Z\) with \(c\perp m\) such that

\[ \delta_m(c) = [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]
Proof

Consider the prime factorizations:

\[ \delta_m(a) = \prod_p p^{\alpha_p},~ \delta_m(b) = \prod_p p^{\beta_p}. \]

According to the relative sizes of \(\alpha_p\) and \(\beta_p\), all prime factors can be divided into two classes:

\[ A = \{p : \alpha_p \ge \beta_p\}, ~ B = \{p : \alpha_p < \beta_p\}. \]

Accordingly, set

\[ \gamma_A = \prod_{p\in A}p^{\alpha_p},~\gamma_B = \prod_{p\in B}p^{\alpha_p},~\eta_A = \prod_{p\in A}p^{\beta_p},~\eta_B = \prod_{p\in B}p^{\beta_p}, \]

so that \(\delta_m(a) = \gamma_A\gamma_B\) and \(\delta_m(b)=\eta_A\eta_B\). By Property 3, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \delta_m(a^{\gamma_B}) &= \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{(\delta_m(a),\gamma_B)} = \dfrac{\delta_m(a)}{\gamma_B} = \gamma_A,\\ \delta_m(b^{\eta_A}) &= \dfrac{\delta_m(b)}{(\delta_m(b),\eta_A)} = \dfrac{\delta_m(b)}{\eta_A} = \eta_B. \end{aligned} \]

Since \(\gamma_A\perp\eta_B\), by Property 4' we have

\[ \delta_m(a^{\gamma_B}b^{\eta_A}) = \gamma_A\eta_B = \prod_p p^{\max\{\alpha_p,\beta_p\}} = [\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]. \]

Therefore, \(c=a^{\gamma_B}b^{\eta_A}\) is an element of order \([\delta_m(a),\delta_m(b)]\).

This conclusion is often used to construct elements of a prescribed order.

Primitive root

Primitive roots are special elements – their order equals the number of elements of the reduced residue system modulo \(m\).

Primitive root

For \(m\in\mathbf N_+\), if there exists \(g\in\mathbf Z\) with \(g\perp m\) such that \(\delta_m(g)=|\mathbf Z_m^*|=\varphi(m)\), then \(g\) is called a primitive root modulo \(m\). Here \(\varphi(m)\) is Euler's totient function.

Not every positive integer \(m\) has a primitive root modulo \(m\). By Property 1 above, if a primitive root \(g\) modulo \(m\) exists, then the congruence classes of \(g,g^2,\cdots,g^{\varphi(m)}\) are pairwise distinct and form the reduced residue system modulo \(m\). In particular, for a prime \(p\), the residues \(g^i\bmod p\) for \(i=1,2,\cdots,p-1\) are pairwise distinct.

Note

In abstract algebra, a primitive root is a generator of a cyclic group. This concept bears the name "primitive root" only in the group formed by the reduced residue system modulo \(m\) under multiplication; in general cyclic groups it is called a "generator". Not every group formed by the reduced residue system modulo \(m\) under multiplication is cyclic: the existence of a primitive root indicates that it is isomorphic to a cyclic group, and the non-existence indicates that it is not.

When the modulus is \(1\), the multiplicative group of integers modulo \(1\) is \(\{0\}\). It is obviously cyclic, so the primitive root is \(0\).

Primitive root criterion

If all prime factors of \(\varphi(m)\) are known, it is easy to decide whether a given \(g\) is a primitive root modulo \(m\).

Theorem

For an integer \(m\ge 3\) and \(g\perp m\), \(g\) is a primitive root modulo \(m\) if and only if for every prime factor \(p\) of \(\varphi(m)\),

\[ g^{\frac{\varphi(m)}{p}}\not\equiv 1 \pmod m. \]
Proof

Necessity is obvious. To prove sufficiency, we argue by contradiction. If \(g\) is not a primitive root modulo \(m\), then necessarily \(\delta_m(g)< \varphi(m)\). By Property 2 and Euler's theorem, \(\delta_m(g)\mid\varphi(m)\). Hence, let \(p\) be a prime factor of \(\dfrac{\varphi(m)}{\delta_m(g)}\); then \(\delta_m(g)\mid\dfrac{\varphi(m)}{p}\). Applying Property 2 again, we obtain

\[ g^{\frac{\varphi(m)}{p}} \equiv 1 \pmod m. \]

But \(p\) is also a prime factor of \(\varphi(m)\), which contradicts the hypothesis of the theorem. Hence sufficiency holds.

Number of primitive roots

A primitive root, if it exists, is not necessarily unique. In general, regarding the possible orders of all elements of the reduced residue system modulo \(m\) and the number of elements of a given order, we have the following conclusion:

Theorem

If a positive integer \(m\) has a primitive root \(g\), then an element of order \(d\) modulo \(m\) exists if and only if \(d\mid\varphi(m)\), and in that case there are exactly \(\varphi(d)\) of them. In particular, the number of primitive roots modulo \(m\) is \(\varphi(\varphi(m))\).

Proof

By the definition of a primitive root, all reduced congruence classes modulo \(m\) can be written in the form \(g^k\bmod m\), where \(k\) is one of \(1,2,\cdots,\varphi(m)\). By Property 3, the orders of these elements equal

\[ \delta_m(g^k) = \dfrac{\varphi(m)}{(\varphi(m),k)}. \]

Therefore, an element of order \(d\) exists if and only if \(d\mid\varphi(m)\). Moreover, for \(d\mid\varphi(m)\), letting \(d'=\varphi(m)/d\), the set of these elements is

\[ \begin{aligned} A &= \{g^k : (\varphi(m),k)=d',~1\le k \le\varphi(m)\} \\ &= \{g^k : d'\mid k,~ (d, k/d') = 1,~ 1 \le k/d' \le d\}. \end{aligned} \]

The numbers \(k'=k/d'\) corresponding to these elements are exactly the positive integers not exceeding \(d\) and coprime to \(d\). By the definition of Euler's totient function, there are \(\varphi(d)\) of them.

Primitive root existence theorem

In this section we establish the following primitive root existence theorem:

Theorem

A primitive root modulo \(m\) exists if and only if \(m=1,2,4,p^e,2p^e\), where \(p\) is an odd prime and \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

To prove this statement, the following four cases need to be discussed separately:

  1. \(m=1,2,4\): the primitive roots are \(g=0,1,3\) respectively, and they obviously exist.

  2. \(m=p^{e}\) is a power of an odd prime, where \(p\) is an odd prime and \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

    Lemma 1

    For an odd prime \(p\), a primitive root modulo \(p\) exists.

    Proof

    The proof consists of two steps.

    Step 1: for \(d\mid(p-1)\), the congruence \(x^d\equiv 1\pmod p\) has exactly \(d\) pairwise distinct solutions.

    Let \(p-1=kd\) and consider the polynomial

    \[ f(x) = x^{d(k-1)} + x^{d(k-2)} + \cdots + x^d + 1. \]

    By Euler's theorem, the congruence \((x^d-1)f(x)=x^{p-1}-1\equiv 0\pmod{p}\) has exactly \(p-1\) pairwise distinct solutions. These solutions are the zeros of \(x^d-1\) and of \(f(x)\) respectively. By Lagrange's theorem, they can have at most \(d\) and \(d(k-1)\) pairwise distinct zeros respectively. Since \(d+d(k-1)=p-1\), the former must have exactly \(d\) pairwise distinct zeros. This shows that the congruence \(x^d\equiv 1\pmod p\) has exactly \(d\) pairwise distinct solutions.

    Step 2: for \(d\mid(p-1)\), there are exactly \(\varphi(d)\) elements of order \(d\).

    Sort all divisors of \(p-1\) and apply induction. Since an element of order \(1\) can only be \(1\), there is exactly one such element, so the base case holds. For \(d\mid(p-1)\), by Property 2 above, the solutions of the congruence \(x^d\equiv 1\pmod p\) must satisfy \(\delta_p(x)\mid d\). Therefore, the number of elements of order \(d\) among them is

    \[ N(d) = d - \sum_{e\mid d,~e\neq d} N(e) = d - \sum_{e\mid d,~e\neq d} \varphi(e) = \varphi(d). \]

    The second equality is the induction hypothesis, and the third is a property of Euler's totient function. By mathematical induction, for all \(d\mid(p-1)\) there are exactly \(\varphi(d)\) elements of order \(d\).

    In particular, for \(d=p-1\) there are exactly \(\varphi(p-1)\) elements of order \((p-1)\). Therefore, a primitive root modulo \(p\) exists.

    Lemma 2

    For an odd prime \(p\) and \(e \in \mathbf{N}_+\), a primitive root modulo \(p^e\) exists.

    Proof

    The proof consists of three steps.

    Step 1: there exists a primitive root \(g\) modulo \(p\) such that \(g^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^2}\).

    Take any primitive root \(g\) modulo \(p\). If it does not satisfy the condition, i.e. \(g^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p^2}\), then it can be shown that \(g+p\) satisfies the condition: \(g+p\) is also a primitive root modulo \(p\), and

    \[ \begin{aligned} (g+p)^{p-1} &\equiv \binom{p-1}{0}g^{p-1} + \binom{p-1}{1}g^{p-2}p \\ &= g^{p-1} + g^{p-2}p(p-1) \\ &\equiv 1 - pg^{p-2} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}. \end{aligned} \]

    Step 2: for the \(g\) chosen above and every \(e\ge 1\), we have \(g^{\varphi(p^e)}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{e+1}}\).

    The choice of \(g\) guarantees that the statement holds for \(e=1\). Suppose the statement holds for \(e\); we now prove that it also holds for \(e+1\). For any \(e \ge 1\), by Euler's theorem there exists an integer \(t\) such that

    \[ g^{\varphi(p^e)} = 1 + t p^e \]

    holds. By the induction hypothesis, \(t\perp p\). Since \(\varphi(p^{e+1})=p\varphi(p^e)\), we have

    \[ g^{\varphi(p^{e+1})} = \left(g^{\varphi(p^{e})}\right)^p = (1 + t p^e)^p \equiv 1 + t p^{e+1} \pmod{p^{e+2}}. \]

    Combined with \(t\perp p\), this shows \(g^{\varphi(p^{e+1})}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{e+2}}\). By mathematical induction, the statement holds.

    Step 3: the \(g\) chosen above is a primitive root modulo \(p^e\) for every \(e\ge 1\).

    The choice of \(g\) guarantees that the statement holds for \(e=1\). Suppose the statement holds for \(e\); we now prove that it also holds for \(e+1\). Write \(\delta_{p^{e+1}}(g)\) briefly as \(\delta\). Since \(g^\delta\equiv 1\pmod{p^{e+1}}\), necessarily also \(g^\delta\equiv 1\pmod{p^e}\). By the induction hypothesis, \(\delta_{p^e}(g) = \varphi(p^e)\). Therefore, by Property 2 of the order above, \(\varphi(p^e)\mid\delta\). Also, by Euler's theorem, \(\delta\mid\varphi(p^{e+1})\). But \(\varphi(p^{e+1})=p\varphi(p^e)\). Therefore, there are only two possibilities: \(\delta=\varphi(p^e)\) or \(\delta=\varphi(p^{e+1})\). However, the conclusion of Step 2 shows that \(g^{\varphi(p^e)}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{e+1}}\). Therefore, the possibility \(\delta=\varphi(p^e)\) is ruled out. The only possibility is \(\delta=\varphi(p^{e+1})\). This shows that \(g\) is a primitive root modulo \(p^{e+1}\). By mathematical induction, the statement holds for all \(e\ge 1\).

  3. \(m=2p^{e}\), where \(p\) is an odd prime and \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

    Lemma 3

    For an odd prime \(p\) and \(e \in \mathbf{N}_+\), a primitive root modulo \(2p^e\) exists.

    Proof

    Let \(g\) be a primitive root modulo \(p^{e}\); then \(g+p^e\) is also a primitive root modulo \(p^{e}\). One of the two is necessarily odd; without loss of generality, let it be \(g\). Clearly \((g,2p^e)=1\). Let \(\delta=\delta_{2p^e}(g)\); we need to prove \(\delta=\varphi(2p^e)\). By Euler's theorem, \(\delta\mid\varphi(2p^e)\). At the same time, by definition \(g^\delta\equiv 1\pmod{2p^e}\), so \(g^\delta\equiv 1\pmod{p^e}\); therefore, by Property 2 of the order and the choice of \(g\), \(\delta_{p^e}(g)=\varphi(p^e)\mid \delta\). By the formula for Euler's totient function, \(\varphi(2p^e) = \varphi(p^e)\). Therefore, \(\delta=\delta_{2p^e}(g)=\varphi(p^e)=\varphi(2p^e)\). This shows that \(g\) is a primitive root modulo \(2p^e\).

  4. \(m\ne 1,2,4,p^{e},2p^{e}\), where \(p\) is an odd prime and \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

    Lemma 4

    Suppose \(m\neq 1,2,4\) and there is no odd prime \(p\) and positive integer \(e\) such that \(m=p^e\) or \(m=2p^e\). Then no primitive root modulo \(m\) exists.

    Proof

    For \(m=2^e\) with \(e\ge 3\), suppose a primitive root \(g\) modulo \(m\) exists. Since \(g\perp m\), it must be odd. Suppose \(g=2k+1\) with \(k\in\mathbf N\); then

    \[ \begin{aligned} g^{2^{e-2}} &=(2k+1)^{2^{e-2}} \\ &\equiv 1 + \binom{2^{e-2}}{1}(2k) + \binom{{2^{e-2}}}{2}(2k)^2 \\ &= 1 + 2^{e-1}k + 2^{e-1}(2^{e-2}-1)k^2 \\ &= 1 + 2^{e-1}(k + (2^{e-2}-1)k^2) \\ &\equiv 1 \pmod{2^{e}}. \end{aligned} \]

    In the second-to-last line, since \(k\) and \((2^{e-2}-1)k^2\) have the same parity, their sum is even. By the definition of the order, \(\delta_{2^{e}}(g)\le 2^{e-2}< \varphi(2^{e}) = 2^{e-1}\). This contradicts the assumption that \(g\) is a primitive root. By contradiction, such a primitive root does not exist.

    Suppose \(m\) satisfies the stated conditions and is not a power of \(2\); then there necessarily exist \(2 < m_1 < m_2\) with \(m_1\perp m_2\) such that \(m=m_1m_2\). Suppose a primitive root \(g\) modulo \(m\) exists. Since \(g\perp m\), we have \(g\perp m_i\) for \(i=1,2\). By Euler's theorem,

    \[ g^{\varphi(m_i)} \equiv 1 \pmod{m_i}. \]

    Since \(m_i > 2\), \(\varphi(m_i)\) is even, so for \(i=1,2\) we have

    \[ g^{\frac{1}{2}\varphi(m_1)\varphi(m_2)} \equiv 1 \pmod{m_i}. \]

    By the Chinese remainder theorem,

    \[ g^{\frac{1}{2}\varphi(m_1)\varphi(m_2)} \equiv 1 \pmod{m}. \]

    Since moreover \(\varphi(m)=\varphi(m_1)\varphi(m_2)\), by the definition of the order we have

    \[ \delta_m(g) \le \frac{1}{2}\varphi(m_1)\varphi(m_2) = \dfrac{1}{2}\varphi(m) < \varphi(m). \]

    This contradicts the assumption that \(g\) is a primitive root modulo \(m\). Hence, by contradiction, no primitive root modulo \(m\) exists.

Combining the four cases above, we have obtained a necessary and sufficient condition for a number to have a primitive root.

Algorithm for finding a primitive root

For any modulus \(m\) for which a primitive root exists, to find its primitive root \(g\) it suffices to enumerate candidate positive integers and check one by one whether each is a primitive root. When enumerating, there are usually two approaches: enumerating one by one from small to large, or randomly generating some positive integers. The practical efficiency of these two approaches is comparable.

When enumerating one by one from small to large, one obtains the smallest primitive root \(g_m\) modulo \(m\), so the complexity of the enumeration part depends on the size of \(g_m\). The following estimates are known:

  • Upper bound estimates: Wang Yuan5 and Burgess6 proved that the least primitive root of a prime \(p\) satisfies \(g_p=O\left(p^{0.25+\epsilon}\right)\), where \(\epsilon>0\). Cohen, Odoni, and Stothers7 and Elliott and Murata8 proved that this estimate also holds for the moduli \(p^2\) and \(2p^2\) respectively, where \(p\) is an odd prime. Since for \(e>2\) a primitive root modulo \(p^2\) (or \(2p^2\)) is also a primitive root modulo \(p^e\) (or \(2p^e\)), the upper bound \(O\left(p^{0.25+\epsilon}\right)\) for the least primitive root holds in all cases.
  • Lower bound estimates: Fridlender9 and Salié10 proved that there exists \(C>0\) such that for infinitely many primes \(p\), the least primitive root satisfies \(g_p > C\log p\).
  • Average-case estimates: Burgess and Elliott11 proved that on average the least primitive root of a prime \(p\) is \(g_p=O((\log p)^2(\log\log p)^4)\). Elliott and Murata12 further conjectured that the average value of the least primitive root of a prime \(p\) is a constant, and by numerical verification13 obtained that it is approximately \(4.926\). Subsequently, Elliott and Murata8 extended this conjecture to the case of the modulus \(2p^2\).

Based on these analyses, when searching for the smallest primitive root by brute force, the complexity \(O(g_m(\log m)^2)\) of the enumeration part is acceptable.

Besides enumerating from small to large, a primitive root can also be found by randomly generating positive integers and verifying them. The density of primitive roots is not low:1

\[ \dfrac{\varphi(\varphi(m))}{m} = \Omega\left(\dfrac{1}{\log\log m}\right). \]

Therefore, when searching for a primitive root by the random method, the expected complexity of the enumeration part is \(O((\log m)^2\log\log m)\).

Note that testing a primitive root requires knowing the prime factorization of \(\varphi(m)\). Among the commonly used factorization algorithms in competitive programming, even Pollard's rho algorithm, which has the best complexity, needs \(O(m^{1/4+\epsilon})\) time. Therefore, as long as the prime factorization of \(\varphi(m)\) is unknown, whichever enumeration method is used, the complexity bottleneck of finding a primitive root lies in the factorization step rather than in the enumeration and verification part.

Carmichael function

In contrast to the order of an element modulo \(m\), which is a local concept, the Carmichael function is a global concept. It is the smallest common period of the powers of all integers coprime to \(m\).

Carmichael function

For \(m\in\mathbf N_+\), define \(\lambda(m)\) as the smallest positive integer \(n\) such that the congruence \(a^n\equiv 1\pmod m\) holds for all \(a\perp m\). The function \(\lambda:\mathbf N_+\to\mathbf N_+\) is called the Carmichael function.

By Property 2, \(a^n\equiv 1\pmod m\) holding for all \(a\perp m\) means that \(\delta_m(a)\mid n\) holds for all \(a\perp m\). That is, a positive integer \(n\) satisfying this condition must be a common multiple of all \(\delta_m(a)\). Therefore, the smallest such \(n\) is their least common multiple:

\[ \lambda(m) = \operatorname{lcm}\{\delta_m(a) : a\perp m\}. \]

This is also often used as an equivalent definition of the Carmichael function.

Applying Property 5 repeatedly, there must exist some element \(a\perp m\) such that \(\delta_m(a)=\lambda(m)\). Therefore, the above formula can also be written as

\[ \lambda(m) = \max\{\delta_m(a) : a\perp m\}. \]

An element \(a\perp m\) attaining this maximum is also called a \(\lambda\)‑primitive root modulo \(m\). It exists for every modulus \(m\).

Recursive formula

The Carmichael function is an arithmetic function. This section discusses a recursive formula for it and thereby gives another proof of the primitive root existence theorem.

Although it is not a multiplicative function, when computing the Carmichael function, coprime factors can likewise be handled separately.

Lemma

For coprime positive integers \(m_1,m_2\), we have \(\lambda(m_1m_2)=[\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]\).

Proof

Let \(a_1\) and \(a_2\) be \(\lambda\)‑primitive roots modulo \(m_1\) and modulo \(m_2\) respectively. Let \(m=m_1m_2\); by the Chinese remainder theorem, there exists \(a\perp m\) such that \(a\equiv a_i\pmod{m_i}\) holds for \(i=1,2\). Since \(a^{\lambda(m)}\equiv 1\pmod m\), for \(i=1,2\) we have \(a_i^{\lambda(m)} \equiv 1\pmod{m_i}\), and then by Property 2 and the choice of \(a_i\), \(\lambda(m_i)=\delta_{m_i}(a_i)\mid \lambda(m)\). This shows \([\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]\mid\lambda(m)\).

Conversely, for any \(a\perp m\) and \(i=1,2\), we have \(a^{[\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]} \equiv 1 \pmod{m_i}\). Applying the Chinese remainder theorem, we obtain that \(a^{[\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]} \equiv 1 \pmod{m}\) holds for all \(a\perp m\). By the definition of the Carmichael function, \(\lambda(m)\mid [\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]\).

Hence the equality in the statement holds.

Therefore, it remains only to compute the values of the Carmichael function at prime powers. First, we handle the case of powers of \(2\).

Lemma

For \(m=2^e\) with \(e\in\mathbf N_+\), we have \(\lambda(2)=1\), \(\lambda(4)=2\), and \(\lambda(m)=2^{e-2}\) for all \(e\ge 3\).

Proof

The cases \(m=2,4\) can be checked separately. For \(m=2^e\) with \(e\ge 3\), first repeat the first part of the proof of Lemma 4 above to obtain \(\lambda(m)\le 2^{e-2}\). Then it suffices to prove that an element of order \(2^{e-2}\) exists. For this, we have

\[ 5^{2^{e-3}} = (1 + 2^2)^{2^{e-3}} \equiv 1 + 2^2\times 2^{e-3} = 1 + 2^{e-1} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{2^e}. \]

This shows \(\delta_m(5)\nmid 2^{e-3}\), and since \(\delta_m(5) \mid 2^{e-2}\), \(5\) can only be an element of order \(2^{e-2}\). This shows that \(\lambda(m)=2^{e-2}\).

In the course of proving this lemma, we have actually obtained a description of the structure of the reduced residue system modulo \(2^e\):

Corollary

Let the modulus be \(2^e\) with \(e \ge 2\). Then every odd number is congruent to a unique integer of the form \(\pm 5^k\), where \(k\in\mathbf N\) and \(k < 2^{e-2}\). That is, \(\pm 1,\pm 5,\cdots,\pm 5^{2^{e-2}-1}\) are pairwise incongruent and form a reduced residue system.

Proof

It is easy to verify that the case \(e=2\) holds. For \(e \ge 3\), since the preceding proof already showed that the order of \(5\) modulo \(2^e\) is \(2^{e-2}\), the numbers \(1,5,\cdots,5^{2^{e-2}-1}\) are pairwise incongruent. Since all these integers are congruent to \(1\) modulo \(4\) and their negatives are congruent to \(3\) modulo \(4\), the numbers \(\pm 1,\pm 5,\cdots,\pm 5^{2^{e-2}-1}\) are pairwise incongruent modulo \(2^e\). Since there are \(2^{e-1}\) of them in total, which is exactly the size of the reduced residue system modulo \(2^{e}\), they form the reduced residue system itself.

Next, we handle the case of odd prime powers.

Lemma

For \(m=p^e\), where \(p\) is an odd prime and \(e\in\mathbf N_+\), we have \(\lambda(m)=p^{e-1}(p-1)\).

Proof

First we prove that the statement holds for \(e=1\), i.e. when \(m=p\) is an odd prime. For this, by the definition of the Carmichael function, all integers \(a\) coprime to \(p\) are solutions of the congruence \(x^{\lambda(p)}\equiv 1\pmod{p}\). Modulo \(p\), this congruence has \(p-1\) pairwise distinct solutions in total. By Lagrange's theorem, \(p-1\le\lambda(p)\). At the same time, Euler's theorem requires \(\lambda(p)\mid\varphi(p)=p-1\). Therefore, \(\lambda(p)=p-1\).

For \(m=p^e\) with \(e> 1\), we can start by proving that \(1+p\) is an element of order \(p^{e-1}\). For this, we have

\[ (1+p)^{p^{e-1}} \equiv 1,\quad (1+p)^{p^{e-2}} \equiv 1 + p^{e-1} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{p^e}. \]

Therefore, \(\delta_m(1+p)=p^{e-1}\). Moreover, let \(g\) be a primitive root modulo \(p\); then, since \(g^{\delta_m(g)}\equiv 1 \pmod{p}\), by Property 2 of the order we have \(p-1\mid\delta_m(g)\). By the definition of the Carmichael function and Euler's theorem,

\[ p^{e-1}(p-1) \mid [\delta_m(1+p),\delta_m(g)] \mid \lambda(m) \mid \varphi(m) = p^{e-1}(p-1). \]

Therefore, \(\lambda(m)=p^{e-1}(p-1)\).

Summarizing the results of this section, we obtain the recursive formula for the Carmichael function:

Theorem

For any positive integer \(m\), we have

\[ \lambda(m) = \begin{cases} \varphi(m), & \text{if }m=1,2,4,p^e\text{ for odd prime }p\text{ and }e \ge 1,\\ \frac{1}{2}\varphi(m), &\text{if }m=2^e,~e\ge 3,\\ \operatorname{lcm}\{\lambda(p_1^{e_1}),\lambda(p_2^{e_2}),\cdots,\lambda(p_s^{e_s})\}, &\text{if }m = p_1^{e_1}p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_s^{e_s}\text{ for distinct }p_1,p_2,\cdots,p_s. \end{cases} \]

Using this recursive formula, the earlier result can be strengthened:

Corollary

For positive integers \(m_1,m_2\), we have \(\lambda([m_1,m_2])=[\lambda(m_1),\lambda(m_2)]\).

Comparing the definitions of the primitive root and the Carmichael function, a primitive root modulo \(m\) exists if and only if \(\lambda(m)=\varphi(m)\). From the recursive formula for the Carmichael function, the following result is easily obtained by induction:

Corollary

A primitive root modulo \(m\) exists if and only if \(m=1,2,4,p^e,2p^e\), where \(p\) is an odd prime and \(e\in\mathbf N_+\).

Since the proof of the recursive formula in this section did not use the primitive root existence theorem, this constitutes another proof of that theorem.

Carmichael numbers

Using the Carmichael function, one can discuss the properties and distribution of Carmichael numbers (OEIS: A002997). These are the composite numbers that the Fermat primality test can never correctly rule out.

Carmichael number

For a composite number \(n\), if the congruence \(a^{n-1} \equiv 1 \pmod n\) holds for all integers \(a\perp n\), then \(n\) is called a Carmichael number.

The smallest Carmichael number is \(561 = 3 \times 11 \times 17\).

By the definition of the Carmichael function, a composite number \(n\) is a Carmichael number if and only if \(\lambda(n)\mid n-1\), where \(\lambda(n)\) is the Carmichael function. Furthermore, the following method for deciding whether a composite number \(n\) is a Carmichael number can be obtained:

Korselt's criterion14

A composite number \(n\) is a Carmichael number if and only if \(n\) is squarefree and \((p-1) \mid (n-1)\) for every prime factor \(p\) of \(n\).

Proof

First we prove the necessity of the condition. Suppose \(\lambda(n)\mid (n-1)\). Inspecting the recursive formula for the Carmichael function, if \(n\) has a square factor \(p^2\), then necessarily \(p\mid \lambda(n)\). But \(p\nmid (n-1)\), a contradiction. Similarly, for any prime factor \(p\) of \(n\), the recursive formula for the Carmichael function shows that \((p-1)\mid \lambda(n)\), so \((p-1) \mid (n-1)\) as well.

Then we prove the sufficiency of the condition. Since \(n\) is a squarefree composite number, it has at least two distinct prime factors, among which there must be an odd prime factor \(p\); from \((p-1)\mid(n-1)\) it follows that \(n-1\) is even, so \(n\) must be odd. For a squarefree odd composite number \(n\), by the recursive formula for the Carmichael function, \(\lambda(n)=\operatorname{lcm}\{p-1:p\mid n\}\). Therefore, as long as \((p-1) \mid (n-1)\) holds for all prime factors \(p\), we must have \(\lambda(n)\mid (n-1)\).

Starting from this criterion, some simple properties of Carmichael numbers can be established:

Corollary

Carmichael numbers are odd, squarefree, and have at least \(3\) distinct prime factors.

Proof

The first two properties follow directly from Korselt's criterion and its proof. To obtain the third property, it suffices to further prove that the product \(n=p_1p_2\) of distinct primes \(p_1,p_2\) is never a Carmichael number. Suppose \(n=p_1p_2\) is a Carmichael number. By Korselt's criterion, \((p_i-1)\mid (n-1)\). But we have

\[ n-1=p_1p_2-1\equiv p_2-1 \pmod{p_1-1}. \]

Therefore, \((p_1-1)\mid(p_2-1)\). Similarly, \((p_2-1)\mid(p_1-1)\). That is, \(p_1=p_2\). This contradicts the assumption. Therefore, a Carmichael number \(n\) has at least \(3\) distinct prime factors.

Using analytic number theory, some properties of the distribution of Carmichael numbers can also be obtained. Let \(C(n)\) be the number of Carmichael numbers less than or equal to \(n\). Alford, Granville, and Pomerance2 proved that for sufficiently large \(n\), \(C(n)>n^{2/7}\). Hence there are infinitely many Carmichael numbers. Before that, Erdős3 had already proved that \(C(n) < n\exp\left(-c\dfrac{\ln n\ln\ln\ln n}{\ln\ln n}\right)\), where \(c\) is a constant. Therefore, the distribution of Carmichael numbers is very sparse (compared to primes). In fact,4 \(C(10^9)=646\) and \(C(10^{18})=1~401~644\).
