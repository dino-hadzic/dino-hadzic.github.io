Uvod

U ovom članku razmatramo rješavanje (poopćene) Pellove jednadžbe. Poopćena Pellova jednadžba je diofantska jednadžba po \(x\) i \(y\) oblika

\[ x^2-Dy^2=N, \]

gdje je \(D\) pozitivan cijeli broj koji nije potpun kvadrat1, a \(N\) je cijeli broj različit od nule. Pellova jednadžba u užem smislu odnosi se na poseban slučaj \(N=1\) ili \(N=\pm 1\), a ponekad obuhvaća i slučaj \(N=\pm 4\). Poopćena Pellova jednadžba usko je povezana s traženjem kvadratnih cijelih brojeva norme \(N\) u prstenu realnih kvadratnih cijelih brojeva, a slučajevi koji se obično nazivaju Pellovom jednadžbom (u užem smislu) mogu se shvatiti kao traženje jedinica u prstenu realnih kvadratnih cijelih brojeva.

Kad se u ovom članku spominje Pellova jednadžba, misli se upravo na slučaj \(N=1\). Slučaj \(N=-1\) naziva se u skladu s time negativna Pellova jednadžba2 (negative Pell's equation).

Struktura rješenja

Cjelobrojna rješenja \((x,y)\) poopćene Pellove jednadžbe usko su povezana s kvadratnim cijelim brojem \(x+y\sqrt{D}\), pa se u literaturi rješenja Pellove jednadžbe često zapisuju u obliku \(x+y\sqrt{D}\). Budući da je norma kvadratnog cijelog broja

\[ N(x+y\sqrt{D}) = x^2-Dy^2, \]

poopćena Pellova jednadžba otprilike odgovara traženju kvadratnih cijelih brojeva norme \(N\). Ipak, između tih dvaju pojmova postoji suptilna razlika. Kad su \(x\) i \(y\) oba cijeli brojevi, \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) je sigurno kvadratni cijeli broj; obratno, kvadratni cijeli broj ne zahtijeva nužno da \(x\) i \(y\) budu cijeli brojevi — u slučaju \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), \(x\) i \(y\) mogu istodobno biti i polucijeli brojevi3.

Ta je razlika osobito važna pri traženju fundamentalne jedinice. Naime, jedinice u prstenu kvadratnih cijelih brojeva su kvadratni cijeli brojevi norme \(\pm 1\). Za \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\), da bismo našli takve jedinice, dovoljno je riješiti poopćenu Pellovu jednadžbu za \(N=\pm 1\); no za \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\) treba razmotriti i slučaj \(N=\pm 4\). Postupak traženja jedinica razmatra se u nastavku.

Da bismo razumjeli strukturu rješenja poopćene Pellove jednadžbe, krećemo od Brahmaguptina identiteta:

\[ (x_1^2-Dy_1^2)(x_2^2-Dy_2^2)=(x_1x_2+Dy_1y_2)^2-D(x_1y_2+x_2y_1)^2. \]

On odgovara činjenici da norma kvadratnih cijelih brojeva čuva množenje, tj.

\[ \begin{aligned} N\left(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\right)N\left(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}\right) &= N\left((x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D})\right) \\ &= N\left((x_1x_2+Dy_1y_2)+(x_1y_2+x_2y_1)\sqrt{D}\right). \end{aligned} \]

Pomoću tog identiteta iz cjelobrojnih rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N_1\) i jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N_2\) možemo složiti cjelobrojno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N_1N_2\). Naravno, gledano iz perspektive kvadratnih cijelih brojeva, slaganje rješenja upravo je množenje kvadratnih cijelih brojeva, što pokazuje koliko je praktično zapisivati rješenja Pellove jednadžbe u obliku kvadratnog cijelog broja. Posebno, uzmemo li \(N_1=N\) i \(N_2=1\), vidimo da iz jednog poznatog rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) i svih rješenja pripadne Pellove jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\) možemo dobiti još rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\). Naravno, tim postupkom ne moramo nužno dobiti sva rješenja. No to barem pokazuje da je razumijevanje strukture rješenja Pellove jednadžbe važno za razumijevanje strukture rješenja poopćene Pellove jednadžbe.

Pellova jednadžba

Geometrijsko značenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\) jest hiperbola kojoj je realna os na osi \(x\), a imaginarna os na osi \(y\). Svakoj točki hiperbole jednoznačno odgovara jedna vrijednost \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) različita od nule: lijeva grana hiperbole odgovara negativnim vrijednostima \(x+y\sqrt{D}\), a desna pozitivnima. Štoviše, na svakoj grani vrijednosti \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) strogo rastu kako se po hiperboli krećemo odozdo prema gore. Vrijednost kvadratnog cijelog broja tako daje rješenjima Pellove jednadžbe prirodan uređaj.

Hiperbola je simetrična i s obzirom na os \(x\) i s obzirom na os \(y\), pa je pri razmatranju rješenja Pellove jednadžbe dovoljno promatrati samo dio u prvom kvadrantu; ostala rješenja dobivaju se simetrijom. To odgovara promatranju samo rješenja s \(x+y\sqrt{D}>1\). Ako jednadžba osim \((\pm 1,0)\) ima i netrivijalna rješenja, onda u prvom kvadrantu sigurno postoji rješenje \((x_1,y_1)\) s najmanjom vrijednošću \(x+y\sqrt{D}\); to je ujedno cjelobrojna točka u prvom kvadrantu (bez koordinatnih osi) s najmanjom apscisom i ordinatom, i naziva se fundamentalno rješenje (fundamental solution) Pellove jednadžbe4. Prema prethodnom razmatranju, svi parovi cijelih brojeva \((x_k,y_k)\) za koje je \(x_k+y_k\sqrt{D}=(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^k\) rješenja su Pellove jednadžbe i svi leže u prvom kvadrantu. Obratno, to su zaista sva rješenja Pellove jednadžbe u prvom kvadrantu. Iskoristimo li još simetriju, dobivamo sljedeći zaključak:

Teorem

Neka je \((x_1,y_1)\) fundamentalno rješenje Pellove jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\). Tada je skup svih njezinih rješenja

\[ \{(x,y):x+y\sqrt{D}=\pm(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^k,k\in\mathbf Z\}. \]
Dokaz

Najprije dokazujemo da u prvom kvadrantu nema drugih rješenja. Pretpostavimo suprotno, da postoji neko drugo rješenje \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) i da za neki \(k\ge 0\) vrijedi

\[ x_k+y_k\sqrt{D}< x+y\sqrt{D}< x_{k+1}+y_{k+1}\sqrt{D}. \]

Geometrijski to znači da cjelobrojna točka \((x,y)\) pada na hiperbolu između \((x_k,y_k)\) i \((x_{k+1},y_{k+1})\) (bez krajnjih točaka). Pomnožimo li nejednakost s \(x_k-y_k\sqrt{D}=(x_k+y_k\sqrt{D})^{-1}\), dobivamo

\[ 1< (x+y\sqrt{D})(x_k-y_k\sqrt{D})=(xx_k-Dyy_k)+(x_ky-xy_k)\sqrt{D} < x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}. \]

Prema već spomenutoj monotonosti, ta nejednakost znači da je \((xx_k-Dyy_k,x_ky-xy_k)\) cjelobrojna točka koja leži između \((1,0)\) i \((x_1,y_1)\). To je u suprotnosti s izborom \((x_1,y_1)\).

Pri proširenju rješenja iz prvog kvadranta na cijelu ravninu, uzimanje suprotnog eksponenta \(k\) (tj. recipročne vrijednosti cijelog izraza) odgovara simetriji s obzirom na os \(x\), a uzimanje suprotne vrijednosti cijelog izraza odgovara simetriji s obzirom na ishodište. Dodamo li još trivijalno rješenje za \(k=0\), dobivamo sva rješenja Pellove jednadžbe.

Dosadašnje razmatranje samo je pretpostavljalo postojanje fundamentalnog rješenja. Sada treba pokazati da Pellova jednadžba uvijek ima netrivijalno rješenje.

Teorem

Pellova jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=1\) uvijek ima cjelobrojno rješenje različito od \((\pm 1,0)\).

Dokaz

Najprije, Dirichletov teorem pokazuje da postoji beskonačno mnogo parova pozitivnih cijelih brojeva \((x,y)\) za koje vrijedi

\[ \left|\dfrac{x}{y}-\sqrt{D}\right| \le \dfrac{1}{y^2} \]

a svi oni zadovoljavaju nejednakost

\[ |x^2-Dy^2|=y^2\left|\dfrac{x}{y}-\sqrt{D}\right|\left|\dfrac{x}{y}+\sqrt{D}\right| \le \dfrac{1}{y^2}+2\sqrt{D}<1+2\sqrt{D}. \]

Stoga nužno postoji cijeli broj \(m\in(-1-2\sqrt{D},1+2\sqrt{D})\) takav da beskonačno mnogo parova \((x,y)\) zadovoljava \(x^2-Dy^2 = m\). Razvrstamo li te parove \((x,y)\) prema ostatcima modulo \(m\), zaključujemo da za neki par cijelih brojeva \((x_0,y_0)\) sigurno postoji beskonačno mnogo parova \((x,y)\) za koje vrijedi \(x\equiv x_0\pmod m\) i \(y\equiv y_0\pmod m\). Odaberemo li bilo koja dva različita para \((x_1,y_1)\) i \((x_2,y_2)\) koja zadovoljavaju te uvjete, vrijedi

\[ \dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}}=\dfrac{x_1x_2-Dy_1y_2}{m}+\dfrac{x_2y_1-x_1y_2}{m}\sqrt{D}. \]

Budući da prema kongruencijama vrijedi

\[ \begin{aligned} x_1x_2-Dy_1y_2 &\equiv x_0^2-Dy_0^2 = m \equiv 0 \pmod{|m|},\\ x_2y_1-x_1y_2 &\equiv x_0y_0-x_0y_0 = 0 \pmod{|m|}, \end{aligned} \]

to znači da je desna strana gornje jednakosti cjelobrojno rješenje. Štoviše, budući da je \((x_1,y_1)\neq(x_2,y_2)\), ono nije trivijalno. Time je pokazano da Pellova jednadžba zaista ima netrivijalno rješenje.

Naravno, dokaz u ovom odjeljku nije konstruktivan; kad u nastavku budemo razmatrali metodu rješavanja Pellove jednadžbe, rješenje Pellove jednadžbe izravno ćemo konstruirati pomoću konvergenti verižnog razlomka, čime ćemo dobiti još jedan dokaz da Pellova jednadžba ima netrivijalno rješenje. Osim toga, iako se struktura rješenja Pellove jednadžbe dobivena u ovom odjeljku podudara sa strukturom jedinica prstena realnih kvadratnih cijelih brojeva, za slučaj \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\) ovaj odjeljak nije u potpunosti riješio pitanje strukture jedinica pripadnog prstena kvadratnih cijelih brojeva; o tome će još biti riječi u nastavku.

Poopćena Pellova jednadžba

Graf poopćene Pellove jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) također je hiperbola u ravnini, također s osima \(x\) i \(y\) kao osima simetrije. Već smo istaknuli da se neka rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) mogu razlikovati samo za faktor koji je rješenje Pellove jednadžbe, što znači da rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) možemo podijeliti u klase ekvivalencije. Za dva rješenja \((x_1,y_1)\) i \((x_2,y_2)\) jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) ako postoji rješenje \((u,v)\) Pellove jednadžbe takvo da vrijedi \(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}=(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})(u+v\sqrt{D})\), kažemo da su rješenja \((x_1,y_1)\) i \((x_2,y_2)\) ekvivalentna. Nužan i dovoljan uvjet da dva rješenja budu ekvivalentna jest

\[ N\mid (x_1x_2-Dy_1y_2),\ N\mid (x_2y_1-x_1y_2). \]

Budući da je rješenja Pellove jednadžbe razmjerno lako naći, prirodna je zamisao u svakoj od navedenih klasa ekvivalencije naći po jedno rješenje. Čim znamo ta rješenja, pomoću rješenja pripadne Pellove jednadžbe možemo dobiti sva tražena rješenja poopćene Pellove jednadžbe. Zbog simetrije u svakoj klasi ekvivalencije rješenja poopćene Pellove jednadžbe postoji rješenje s nenegativnom, ali što manjom ordinatom \(y\): ako je takvo rješenje jedinstveno, nazivamo ga fundamentalnim rješenjem te klase ekvivalencije; inače ta klasa nužno ima dva rješenja s nenegativnim i najmanjim \(y\), i to simetrična s obzirom na os \(y\), pa tada kao fundamentalno rješenje biramo ono s \(x>0\). Prema tome, riješiti poopćenu Pellovu jednadžbu \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) znači naći skup \(U\) njezinih fundamentalnih rješenja. Ako je \((r,s)\) fundamentalno rješenje pripadne Pellove jednadžbe, skup svih rješenja poopćene Pellove jednadžbe je

\[ \{(x,y):x+y\sqrt{D}=\pm(r+s\sqrt{D})^k(u+v\sqrt{D}),k\in\mathbf Z,u+v\sqrt{D}\in U\}. \]

Fundamentalnih rješenja poopćene Pellove jednadžbe nužno ima konačno mnogo. Naime, iz gornjeg izraza za opće rješenje vidi se da apsolutna vrijednost \(|u+v\sqrt{D}|\) nužno leži između \(r-s\sqrt{D}\) i \(r+s\sqrt{D}\). U literaturi navedenoj na kraju članka dane su strože ocjene raspona koordinata fundamentalnih rješenja. Naravno, za razliku od Pellove jednadžbe, poopćena Pellova jednadžba ne mora imati rješenja.

Da bismo iz jednog rješenja \((u,v)\) poopćene Pellove jednadžbe i fundamentalnog rješenja \((r,s)\) Pellove jednadžbe dobili sva rješenja iz iste klase ekvivalencije, osim slaganja rješenja možemo iskoristiti i sljedeću rekurziju

\[ x_{k} = 2rx_{k-1} - x_{k-2},\ y_{k} = 2ry_{k-1} - y_{k-2}, \]

gdje je \(x_k+y_k\sqrt{D}=(r+s\sqrt{D})^k(u+v\sqrt{D})\). Naime, i \(x_k\) i \(y_k\) mogu se za neki par realnih brojeva \((A,B)\) zapisati u obliku \(A(r+s\sqrt{D})^k+B(r-s\sqrt{D})^k\), a prema Vièteovim formulama \(r\pm s\sqrt{D}\) dva su realna korijena jednadžbe \(x^2-2rx+1=0\), pa \(x_k\) i \(y_k\) zadovoljavaju gornju linearnu rekurziju drugog reda s konstantnim koeficijentima. U usporedbi sa slaganjem rješenja ta rekurzija zahtijeva manje množenja.

Metode rješavanja

I Pellova i poopćena Pellova jednadžba mogu se rješavati pomoću verižnih razlomaka.

PQa algoritam

Svi algoritmi u ovom članku temelje se na PQa algoritmu, kojim se može izračunati razvoj određene kvadratne iracionalnosti u verižni razlomak.

Neka cijeli brojevi \(P_0,Q_0,D\) zadovoljavaju \(Q_0\neq 0\), \(D>0\) i nije potpun kvadrat, te \(P_0^2\equiv D\pmod{Q_0}\). Tada se razvoj kvadratne iracionalnosti

\[ \omega=\dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0} \]

u verižni razlomak \([a_0,a_1,\cdots]\) može dobiti sljedećim rekurzivnim formulama:

\[ a_k = \left\lfloor\dfrac{P_k+\sqrt{D}}{Q_k}\right\rfloor,\ P_{k+1} = a_kQ_k - P_k,\ Q_{k+1} = \dfrac{D-P_{k+1}^2}{Q_k}. \]

Nadalje, brojnik \(A_k\) i nazivnik \(B_k\) \(k\)-te konvergente od \(\omega\) dani su sljedećim rekurzivnim formulama:

\[ A_k = a_kA_{k-1} + A_{k-2},\ B_k = a_kB_{k-1} + B_{k-2} \]

uz \(A_{-1} = 1\), \(A_{-2}=0\), \(B_{-1}=0\), \(B_{-2}=1\).

Točnost tih formula već je dokazana u članku o verižnim razlomcima. Ondje je također objašnjeno da je kvadratna iracionalnost periodički verižni razlomak, pa trojke \((P_k,Q_k,a_k)\) na kraju ulaze u ciklus i algoritam uvijek završava u konačno mnogo koraka. Neka je najmanja duljina perioda \(\ell\), a najraniji početak perioda na položaju \(k_0\); tada se razvoj kvadratne iracionalnosti u verižni razlomak može zapisati kao

\[ \omega=[a_0,\cdots,a_{k_0-1},\overline{a_{k_0},\cdots,a_{k_0+\ell-1}}]. \]

Da bismo PQa algoritmom riješili Pellovu jednadžbu, trebamo sljedeći rezultat:

Teorem

Nastavljamo s gornjim oznakama. Neka je \(G_k=Q_0A_k-P_0B_k\). Tada par cijelih brojeva \((G_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) zadovoljava relaciju

\[ G_{k-1}^2-DB_{k-1}^2=(-1)^{k}Q_0Q_{k}, \]

a njihov najveći zajednički djelitelj \(\gcd(G_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) dijeli \(Q_{k}\).

Dokaz

Neka je u razvoju \(\omega\) u verižni razlomak \(k\)-ti ostatak (potpuni kvocijent) \(\omega_{k}\), tj.

\[ \omega = [a_0,a_1,\cdots,a_{k-1},\omega_k] = \dfrac{\omega_k A_{k-1}+A_{k-2}}{\omega_k B_{k-1}+B_{k-2}}. \]

Uvrstimo li \(\omega=(P_0+\sqrt{D})/Q_0\) i \(\omega_k=(P_k+\sqrt{D})/Q_k\) u gornju jednakost, dobivamo

\[ \dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0} = \dfrac{(P_k+\sqrt{D})A_{k-1}+Q_kA_{k-2}}{(P_k+\sqrt{D})B_{k-1}+Q_kB_{k-2}}. \]

Uklonimo li nazivnike s obiju strana i usporedimo koeficijente racionalnog i iracionalnog dijela, a zatim uvrstimo izraz za \(G_k\), dobivamo sljedeće jednakosti:

\[ \begin{aligned} G_{k-1} &= P_kB_{k-1} + Q_kB_{k-2},\\ DB_{k-1} &= P_kG_{k-1} + Q_kG_{k-2}. \end{aligned} \]

Stoga, pomnožimo li prvu jednakost s \(G_{k-1}\) i oduzmemo drugu pomnoženu s \(B_{k-1}\), dobivamo

\[ \begin{aligned} G_{k-1}^2-DB_{k-1}^2 &= (B_{k-2}G_{k-1}-B_{k-1}G_{k-2})Q_k \\ &= (A_{k-1}B_{k-2}-B_{k-1}A_{k-2})Q_0Q_k \\ &= (-1)^kQ_0Q_k. \end{aligned} \]

U posljednjem koraku iskorištena je formula za razliku konvergenti. Time je dokazana prva tvrdnja.

Da bismo dokazali drugu tvrdnju, uvrstimo izraz za \(G_k\) u prvu tvrdnju:

\[ (Q_0A_{k-1}-P_0B_{k-1})^2 - DB_{k-1}^2 = (-1)^kQ_0Q_k. \]

Stoga, koristeći \(Q_0\mid(P_0^2-D)\), imamo

\[ Q_0A_{k-1}^2 +\left(\dfrac{P_0^2-D}{Q_0}B_{k-1}- 2P_0A_{k-1}\right)B_{k-1} = (-1)^kQ_k. \]

Dakle, \(\gcd(G_{k-1},B_{k-1}) = \gcd(Q_0A_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) dijeli \(Q_k\).

Taj rezultat daje način traženja rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\). Ako prikladno odaberemo \(Q_0>0\) i za \(P_0\) odaberemo rješenje kongruencije \(P_0^2\equiv D\pmod{Q_0}\), onda izvođenjem PQa algoritma na \((P_0+\sqrt{D})/Q_0\) sve dok ne nađemo \((-1)^kQ_0Q_{k}=N\) dobivamo da je \((G_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) rješenje polazne jednadžbe. Štoviše, ako je \(Q_k=\pm 1\), tako dobiveno rješenje sigurno je primitivno, tj. \(G_{k-1}\) i \(B_{k-1}\) sigurno su relativno prosti.

Ta je zamisao srž rješavanja Pellove i poopćene Pellove jednadžbe. Nakon što smo je razumjeli, u nastavku se bavimo pojedinostima algoritma i dokazujemo da se sva rješenja mogu dobiti na taj način.

Pellova jednadžba

Da bismo riješili Pellovu jednadžbu \(x^2-Dy^2=1\), dovoljno je izvesti PQa algoritam za \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(0,1,D)\) sve dok se ne pojavi \((-1)^kQ_k=1\); tada je \((A_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) rješenje Pellove jednadžbe (jer je \(G_{k-1}\) u tom slučaju upravo \(A_{k-1}\)). Naravno, za Pellovu jednadžbu taj postupak možemo opisati i preciznije.

Prvo, rješenje se sigurno pojavljuje na kraju perioda. Gornji postupak odgovara razvoju \(\sqrt{D}\) u verižni razlomak. Za njega već imamo rezultat:

\[ \sqrt{D} = [\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor,\overline{a_1,\cdots,a_{\ell-1},2\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor}]. \]

Ovdje je duljina perioda \(\ell\), a period počinje od \(1\). člana (indeksi počinju od \(0\)). Štoviše, njegov \(\ell\)-ti ostatak jednak je \(\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor+\sqrt{D}\), što znači da je \(Q_{\ell}=1\). Stoga, ako je \(\ell\) paran, \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) je netrivijalno rješenje Pellove jednadžbe; ako je \(\ell\) neparan, \((A_{2\ell-1},B_{2\ell-1})\) je netrivijalno rješenje Pellove jednadžbe.

Sljedeće treba pokazati da je upravo dobiveno rješenje sigurno fundamentalno rješenje. Taj se zaključak temelji na dvama razlozima: prvo, razlomci \(x/y\) koji odgovaraju svim pozitivnim cjelobrojnim rješenjima \((x,y)\) Pellove jednadžbe pojavljuju se među konvergentama od \(\sqrt{D}\), što jamči da je \((x,y)\) nužno neki \((A_k,B_k)\) iz tijeka PQa algoritma; drugo, osim na kraju perioda, \(Q_k=1\) ne pojavljuje se ni na jednom drugom mjestu, a budući da rekurzija za \(A_k\) i \(B_k\) jamči da oni rastu s porastom indeksa, najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje (tj. fundamentalno rješenje) nužno se pojavljuje na upravo navedenom mjestu. Ta dva razloga slijede redom iz sljedećih dvaju teorema:

Teorem

Neka jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) ima pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje \((x,y)\). Ako je \(|N|<\sqrt{D}\), onda je \(\dfrac{x}{y}\) sigurno jednak nekoj konvergenti od \(\sqrt{D}\).

Dokaz

Kad je \(N>0\), iz \(x^2-Dy^2>0\) slijedi \(x>y\sqrt{D}\). Stoga vrijedi

\[ \left|\dfrac{x}{y}-\sqrt{D}\right| = \dfrac{N}{y(x+y\sqrt{D})}<\dfrac{N}{2y^2\sqrt{D}}<\dfrac{1}{2y^2}. \]

Prema Legendreovu kriteriju, \(\dfrac{x}{y}\) je konvergenta od \(\sqrt{D}\).

Kad je \(N<0\), \(x>y\sqrt{D}\) više ne vrijedi. Zato umjesto toga promatramo rješenja jednadžbe \(y^2-\dfrac{1}{D}x^2=-\dfrac{N}{D}\). Budući da je \(\dfrac{|N|}{D}<\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{D}}\), gornje rasuđivanje i dalje vrijedi. To znači da je \(\dfrac{y}{x}\) konvergenta od \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\). Prema teoremu o recipročnoj vrijednosti, \(\dfrac{x}{y}\) je također konvergenta od \(\sqrt{D}\).

Teorem

Tijekom izvođenja gornjeg PQa algoritma za \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(0,1,D)\), iz \(Q_k=1\) nužno slijedi \(\ell\mid k\).

Dokaz

U razvoju \(\sqrt{D}\) u verižni razlomak svi su ostatci osim \(0\). ostatka čisto periodički verižni razlomci. Neka je \(Q_k=1\). Prema Galoisovu rezultatu, ostatak \(\omega_k=P_k+\sqrt{D}>1\) nužno ima konjugat \(-1<P_k-\sqrt{D}<0\), što znači da je \(P_k=\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor\). Stoga je ostatak \(\omega_k\) jednak \(\omega_\ell\). No ponavljanje ostatka znači periodičnost verižnog razlomka, pa ako \(k\) nije višekratnik od \(\ell\), dolazimo u proturječje s time da je \(\ell\) najmanji pozitivni period. Dakle, nužno je \(\ell\mid k\).

Objedinimo li razmatranja u ovom odjeljku, zaključujemo: dovoljno je razviti \(\sqrt{D}\) u verižni razlomak, tj. izvesti PQa algoritam s početkom \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(0,1,D)\), i kad prvi put dobijemo \(Q_\ell=1\), stigli smo do kraja prvog perioda. Tada je, ako je \(\ell\) paran, \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) fundamentalno rješenje Pellove jednadžbe; inače je fundamentalno rješenje Pellove jednadžbe \((A_{2\ell-1},B_{2\ell-1})\). Za slučaj neparne duljine perioda \(\ell\) nije potrebno nastavljati PQa algoritam do dvostrukog perioda; uskoro ćemo pokazati da je \(A_{2\ell-1}+B_{2\ell-1}\sqrt{D}=(A_{\ell-1}+B_{\ell-1}\sqrt{D})^2\), pa se fundamentalno rješenje Pellove jednadžbe može izračunati izravno iz \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\). Sva ostala rješenja Pellove jednadžbe mogu se izračunati iz fundamentalnog rješenja Pellove jednadžbe.

Negativna Pellova jednadžba

Prema razmatranju iz prethodnog odjeljka, rješenja negativne Pellove jednadžbe također nužno odgovaraju konvergentama od \(\sqrt{D}\) i mogu se pojaviti samo ondje gdje je \((-1)^kQ_k=-1\). To se može dogoditi samo na kraju perioda. Stoga negativna Pellova jednadžba ima rješenje ako i samo ako je duljina perioda \(\ell\) neparna. Kad rješenje postoji, \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) je fundamentalno rješenje negativne Pellove jednadžbe. Postupak rješavanja isti je kao u prethodnom odjeljku.

Rasuđivanjem sličnim ranijem dokazu o strukturi rješenja Pellove jednadžbe može se dokazati sljedeći rezultat:

Teorem

Neka jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) ima rješenje i neka je njezino fundamentalno rješenje \((x_1,y_1)\). Tada sva cjelobrojna rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) pripadaju skupu

\[ \{(x,y):x+y\sqrt{D}=\pm(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^k,k\in\mathbf Z\}. \]

Posebno, cjelobrojno rješenje \((x_2,y_2)\) koje zadovoljava \(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}=(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^2\) upravo je fundamentalno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\).

Dokaz

Zbog simetrije dovoljno je promatrati pozitivna cjelobrojna rješenja, tj. slučaj \(x+y\sqrt{D}>1\). No budući da je \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) par hiperbola, \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) se ne može bijektivno pridružiti parovima \((x,y)\). Da bismo riješili tu poteškoću, najprije dokazujemo da je gore navedeni \((x_2,y_2)\) fundamentalno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\).

Očito je \((x_2,y_2)\) rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\). Ako je \((z,w)\) fundamentalno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\), nužno vrijedi \(1<z+w\sqrt{D}\le x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}\). Ako u desnoj nejednakosti ne vrijedi jednakost, dijeljenjem nejednakosti s \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) dobivamo \(-x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}<(z+w\sqrt{D})(-x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})<x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). Raspišemo li izraz u sredini te nejednakosti, dobivamo oblik \(x'+y'\sqrt{D}\), čija je norma \(-1\) i \((x',y')\) je također cjelobrojno rješenje. Uzmemo li recipročne vrijednosti u toj nejednakosti, vidimo da i \(-x'+y'\sqrt{D}\) pada između \(-x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) i \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). Kvadratni cijeli brojevi \(\pm x'+y'\sqrt{D}\) međusobno su recipročni, pa je jedan od njih nužno veći od \(1\). No između \(1\) i \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) ne bi se smio pojaviti nijedan drugi kvadratni cijeli broj norme \(-1\), što je u suprotnosti s minimalnošću \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). Dakle, nužno vrijedi \(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}=z+w\sqrt{D}\), tj. \((x_2,y_2)\) je fundamentalno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\).

Na temelju toga, ako bi se pojavilo rješenje \((x,y)\) jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) koje ne odgovara nijednom \((x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^k\), nužno bi postojao \(k\) takav da je \((x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^{2k}<x+y\sqrt{D}<(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^{2k+2}\); uklanjanjem faktora \((x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^{2k+1}\) dobivamo da postoji kvadratni cijeli broj \(x'+y'\sqrt{D}\neq 1\) norme \(\pm 1\) koji leži između \(-x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) i \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). Ponovimo li rasuđivanje s recipročnim vrijednostima iz prethodnog odlomka, to je u suprotnosti s minimalnošću \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). Time je polazna tvrdnja dokazana.

Budući da je \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje negativne Pellove jednadžbe, a sva pozitivna cjelobrojna rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) pojavljuju se u skupu

\[ \{(x,y):x+y\sqrt{D}=(A_{\ell-1}+B_{\ell-1}\sqrt{D})^k,k\in\mathbf N_+\} \]

i budući da ta pozitivna cjelobrojna rješenja nužno odgovaraju konvergentama od \(\sqrt{D}\) na kraju perioda (na mjestu neposredno prije njega), a brojnici i nazivnici konvergenti strogo rastu, za sve \(k\in\mathbf N_+\) uvijek vrijedi

\[ (A_{\ell-1}+B_{\ell-1}\sqrt{D})^k = A_{k\ell-1}+B_{k\ell-1}\sqrt{D}. \]

Među svim tim pozitivnim cjelobrojnim rješenjima, ona s neparnim \(k\) rješenja su negativne Pellove jednadžbe, a ona s parnim \(k\) rješenja su Pellove jednadžbe; ona se pojavljuju naizmjence.

Da bismo utvrdili ima li negativna Pellova jednadžba rješenje, treba izračunati duljinu perioda razvoja \(\sqrt{D}\) u verižni razlomak, što nije lako izračunati, pa bismo željeli naći jednostavniji kriterij. No trenutačno ne postoji kriterij s jednostavnim i lako izračunljivim uvjetima5. Ovdje navodimo samo jedan jednostavan rezultat.

Teorem

Ako jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) ima rješenje, onda \(4\) ne dijeli \(D\) i \(D\) nema prostih faktora oblika \(4k+3\). Obratno, ako je \(D=2\) ili je \(D\) prost broj oblika \(4k+1\), jednadžba sigurno ima rješenje.

Dokaz

Najprije, ako negativna Pellova jednadžba ima rješenje, to znači da je \(-1\) kvadratni ostatak modulo \(D\), pa je \(-1\) kvadratni ostatak i modulo svaki djelitelj \(d\) od \(D\); stoga je \(d\neq 4\) i \(d\) nije prost broj oblika \(4k+3\). Obratno, jednadžba \(x^2-2y^2=-1\) ima netrivijalno rješenje \((1,1)\). Preostaje slučaj kad je \(D\) prost broj oblika \(4k+1\).

Neka je \(D\) prost broj oblika \(4k+1\); treba dokazati da jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) ima rješenje. Zamisao je krenuti od fundamentalnog rješenja \((u,v)\) Pellove jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\) i konstruirati rješenje \((\alpha,\beta)\) jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\). Ako je \(u\) paran, uzimanjem obiju strana jednakosti \(u^2-Dv^2=1\) modulo \(4\) dobivamo \(v^2\equiv -1\pmod 4\), ali \(-1\) nije kvadratni ostatak modulo \(4\). To proturječje pokazuje da je \(u\) neparan. Promotrimo jednakost \(Dv^2=u^2-1=(u+1)(u-1)\). Budući da je \(u\) neparan, \(\gcd(u+1,u-1)=\gcd(u+1,2)=2\). Na temelju te činjenice, raspodijelimo li faktore od \(Dv^2\) na \(u+1\) i \(u-1\), jedan od njih nužno je \(2\alpha^2\), a drugi \(2D\beta^2\), gdje su \(\alpha\) i \(\beta\) relativno prosti pozitivni cijeli brojevi i \(v=2\alpha\beta\). Uvrstimo li \(u=\alpha^2+D\beta^2\) i \(v=2\alpha\beta\) u \(u^2-Dv^2=1\), dobivamo \(\alpha^2-D\beta^2=\pm 1\). Budući da je \((u,v)\) fundamentalno rješenje Pellove jednadžbe, a \((\alpha,\beta)\) je par pozitivnih cijelih brojeva manji od \((u,v)\), desna strana te jednakosti ne može biti \(+1\), pa može biti samo \(-1\). Time je dokazano da jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) ima rješenje \((\alpha,\beta)\).

Ako je \(D\) složen, to što nema prostih faktora oblika \(4k+3\) i što nema kvadratnih faktora ne jamči da jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) ima rješenje; primjerice, \(x^2-34y^2=-1\) nema rješenja.

Primjer

Iz rezultata izračuna u gornjem primjeru vidi se da jednadžba \(x^2-14y^2=-1\) nema rješenja, a najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-41y^2=-1\) je \((G_2,B_2)=(32,5)\).

Slučaj norme ±4

Sada razmatramo rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Ovdje ponašanje rješenja ovisi o vrijednosti \(D\bmod 4\).

Neki su slučajevi laki. Ako je \(D\equiv 0\pmod 4\), onda je \(x\) paran, pa je \((x/2,y)\) rješenje jednadžbe \(u^2-(D/4)v^2=\pm 1\). U ostalim slučajevima \(x,y\) nužno su oba neparna ili oba parna. Ako su \(x,y\) oba neparna, uzimanjem obiju strana jednadžbe modulo \(4\) dobivamo \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\). Stoga, ako je \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\), \(x,y\) mogu biti samo oba parna, pa je \((x/2,y/2)\) rješenje jednadžbe \(u^2-Dv^2=\pm 1\). Prema tome, osim u slučaju \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) mogu se dobiti iz rješenja pripadne (negativne) Pellove jednadžbe.

Sada razmatramo slučaj \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), koji se ne može jednostavno svesti na već riješene slučajeve. Da bismo našli fundamentalno rješenje, možemo primijeniti PQa algoritam na \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(1,2,D)\); kad prvi put dobijemo \(Q_\ell=2\), stigli smo do kraja prvog perioda. Ako je duljina perioda \(\ell\) parna, \((G_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) je fundamentalno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=4\); inače je \((G_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) fundamentalno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\). Polazeći od \((G_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) možemo dobiti sva rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\):

\[ \left\{(x,y):\dfrac{x+y\sqrt{D}}{2}=\pm\left(\dfrac{G_{\ell-1}+B_{\ell-1}\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^k,k\in\mathbf Z\right\}. \]

Ako je duljina perioda \(\ell\) parna, sva su to rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=4\); inače je za neparan \(k\) \((x,y)\) rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\), a za paran \(k\) \((x,y)\) je rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=4\).

Točnost tog algoritma oslanja se na sljedeće činjenice:

Teorem

Neka jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) ima pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje \((x,y)\). Ako je \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), onda je \(\dfrac{(x+y)/2}{y}\) sigurno konvergenta od \(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\).

Dokaz

Najprije uočimo da \(x,y\) u tom slučaju nužno imaju istu parnost, pa je \((x+y)/2\) cijeli broj. Ako je \((x,y)\) rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=4\), onda je \(x>y\sqrt{D}>2y\), pa je

\[ \left|\dfrac{(x+y)/2}{y}-\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\right| = \dfrac{2}{y(x+y\sqrt{D})}<\dfrac{1}{2y^2}. \]

Prema Legendreovu kriteriju, \(\dfrac{(x+y)/2}{y}\) je konvergenta od \(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\).

Ako je \((x,y)\) rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\), da bismo dobili gornju nejednakost, dovoljno je dokazati \(4y<x+y\sqrt{D}\). To vrijedi barem u svim slučajevima osim \(D=5,13\). Za slučaj \(D=5,13\), uvrstimo li \(x=\sqrt{Dy^2-4}\) u tu nejednakost, vidimo da je ona ekvivalentna s \(2(\sqrt{D}-2)y^2>1\). Osim za \((D,y)=(5,1)\), ta nejednakost vrijedi za sve \(D=5,13\) i pozitivne cijele brojeve \(y\). Preostaje provjeriti slučaj \((D,y)=(5,1)\); tada je rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-5y^2=-4\) jednako \((x,y)=(1,1)\), a treba provjeriti da je \(\dfrac{1}{1}\) konvergenta od \(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}=[\overline{1}]\), što očito vrijedi.

Teorem

Neka je \(D\) pozitivan cijeli broj koji nije potpun kvadrat. Razvoj kvadratne iracionalnosti \(\omega=\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\) u verižni razlomak ima oblik

\[ \omega = [\lfloor\omega\rfloor,\overline{a_1,\cdots,a_{\ell-1},2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1}], \]

gdje je \(\ell\) duljina perioda, a \(a_k=a_{\ell-k}\) vrijedi za svaki \(1\le k<\ell\).

Dokaz

Budući da je \(\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1+\omega>1\), a njegov konjugat jednak je \(\lfloor\omega\rfloor - \omega\) i leži između \(-1\) i \(0\), prema Galoisovu rezultatu \(\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1+\omega\) je čisto periodički verižni razlomak i može se zapisati kao

\[ \lfloor\omega\rfloor-1+\omega = [\overline{2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1,a_1,\cdots,a_{\ell-1}}]. \]

A Galoisov rezultat o recipročnoj vrijednosti negativnog konjugata kaže

\[ \dfrac{1}{\omega-\lfloor\omega\rfloor} = [\overline{a_{\ell-1},\cdots,a_1,2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1}]. \]

Stoga, prema definiciji verižnog razlomka, imamo

\[ \lfloor\omega\rfloor-1+\omega = 2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1 + \dfrac{1}{\dfrac{1}{\omega-\lfloor\omega\rfloor}} = [2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1,\overline{a_{\ell-1},\cdots,a_1,2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1}]. \]

Jedinstvenost razvoja u verižni razlomak pokazuje da \(a_k=a_{\ell-k}\) vrijedi za sve \(1\le k<\ell\), pa vrijedi i razvoj koji je trebalo dokazati.

Teorem

Neka je \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\). Tijekom izvođenja gornjeg PQa algoritma za \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(1,2,D)\), iz \(Q_k=2\) nužno slijedi \(\ell\mid k\).

Dokaz

U razvoju \(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\) u verižni razlomak svi su ostatci osim \(0\). ostatka čisto periodički verižni razlomci. Neka je \(Q_k=2\). Prema Galoisovu rezultatu, konjugat ostatka \(\omega_k=\dfrac{P_k+\sqrt{D}}{2}\) nužno zadovoljava \(-1<\dfrac{P_k-\sqrt{D}}{2}<0\), tj. \(\sqrt{D}-2<P_k<\sqrt{D}\). Budući da u PQa algoritmu uvijek vrijedi \(Q_k\mid P_k^2-D\) (vidi dokaz točnosti algoritma), \(P_k\) je sigurno neparan, što znači da je vrijednost \(P_k\) jedinstvena, tj. \(P_k=P_0+2(\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1)\), odnosno ostatak \(\omega_k=\omega_\ell\). No ponavljanje ostatka znači periodičnost verižnog razlomka, pa ako \(k\) nije višekratnik od \(\ell\), dolazimo u proturječje s time da je \(\ell\) najmanji pozitivni period. Dakle, nužno je \(\ell\mid k\).

Teorem

Neka je \((x_1,y_1)\) najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Tada je skup svih njezinih rješenja

\[ \left\{(x,y):\dfrac{x+y\sqrt{D}}{2}=\pm\left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^k,k\in\mathbf Z\right\}. \]
Dokaz

Zbog simetrije dovoljno je promatrati pozitivna cjelobrojna rješenja \((x,y)\). Ovdje treba dokazati samo da su parovi realnih brojeva \((x,y)\) iz tog skupa zaista cjelobrojna rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Za ostalo je dovoljno ponoviti dokaz o strukturi rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\).

Zapravo treba dokazati da je za bilo koja dva cjelobrojna rješenja \((x_1,y_1)\) i \((x_2,y_2)\) jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) par pozitivnih realnih brojeva \((x_3,y_3)\) definiran s

\[ \dfrac{x_3+y_3\sqrt{D}}{2} = \dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\dfrac{x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}}{2}. \]

također cjelobrojno rješenje. Raspišemo li desnu stranu i usporedimo koeficijente racionalnog i iracionalnog člana, dobivamo

\[ x_3=\dfrac{x_1x_2+Dy_1y_2}{2},\ y_3=\dfrac{x_1y_2+x_2y_1}{2}. \]

Budući da za \(i=1,2\) vrijedi \(x_i\equiv x_i^2\equiv Dy_i^2\equiv Dy_i\pmod 2\), imamo

\[ \begin{aligned} 2x_3 &= x_1x_2+Dy_1y_2 \equiv D^2y_1y_2+Dy_1y_2=D(D+1)y_1y_2 \equiv 0 \pmod 2,\\ 2y_3 &= x_1y_2+x_2y_1 \equiv Dy_1y_2+Dy_2y_1 = 2Dy_1y_2 \equiv 0 \pmod 2. \end{aligned} \]

To pokazuje da su \(x_3\) i \(y_3\) cijeli brojevi. Iskoristimo li još svojstvo da norma čuva množenje, zaključujemo da je \((x_3,y_3)\) rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\).

Objedinimo li te činjenice i ponovimo rasuđivanje iz prethodnih odjeljaka, možemo dokazati točnost gornjeg algoritma za rješavanje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Ti rezultati pokazuju da jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) ima jednostavnu strukturu rješenja sličnu onoj jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\): sva se njezina rješenja mogu izraziti pomoću njezina najmanjeg pozitivnog cjelobrojnog rješenja, bez rješavanja drugih jednadžbi.

Zapravo se sva rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) mogu naći među rješenjima jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\), pa je s tog gledišta jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) temeljnija. Očito je \((x,y)\) rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) ako i samo ako je \((2x,2y)\) rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Ranija analiza pokazala je da su za \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\) sva rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) nužno oba parna, pa odgovaraju rješenjima jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\).

Kad je \(D\equiv 0\pmod 4\), u rješenju \((x,y)\) jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) \(x\) je sigurno paran, ali \(y\) može biti neparan. Ako je u najmanjem pozitivnom cjelobrojnom rješenju \((x_1,y_1)\) jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) \(y_1\) paran, onda je i u svim rješenjima \(y\) sigurno paran i tada ta cjelobrojna rješenja bijektivno odgovaraju cjelobrojnim rješenjima jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\); no ako je u najmanjem cjelobrojnom rješenju \((x_1,y_1)\) \(y_1\) neparan, onda se parnost od \(y_k\) podudara s parnošću od \(k\) i naizmjence se mijenja, pa rješenju jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) odgovara samo kad je \(k\) paran. Ako je u najmanjem pozitivnom cjelobrojnom rješenju jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) \(y_1\) neparan, a norma od \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) je \(-4\), onda za takav \(D\) jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\) ima rješenje, ali jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) nema.

Kad je \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), rješenje \((x,y)\) jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) može biti i oba neparna i oba parna. Ako je najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje \((x_1,y_1)\) već oba parna, onda su i sva njezina cjelobrojna rješenja sigurno oba parna, pa uvijek odgovaraju cjelobrojnim rješenjima jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\). Ako je najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje \((x_1,y_1)\) oba neparna, vrijedi sljedeći rezultat:

Teorem

Neka je \((x_1,y_1)\) najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Ako su \(x_1\) i \(y_1\) oba neparna, onda je \(D\equiv 5\pmod 8\), a cjelobrojno rješenje \((x,y)\) te jednadžbe oba su parna ako i samo ako je

\[ \dfrac{x+y\sqrt{D}}{2} = \pm\left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^{3k},k\in\mathbf Z. \]
Dokaz

Uzimanjem obiju strana jednakosti \(x_1^2-Dy_1^2=\pm 4\) modulo \(8\) dobivamo \(D\equiv 5\pmod 8\). Da bismo dokazali drugu tvrdnju, najprije dokazujemo da su \((x_3,y_3)\) oba parna; budući da je

\[ \dfrac{x_3+y_3\sqrt{D}}{2} = \left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^{3} = \dfrac{x_1^3+3Dx_1y_1^2}{8}+\dfrac{3x_1^2y_1+Dy_1^3}{8}\sqrt{D}, \]

dovoljno je dokazati da je desna strana cjelobrojna. Budući da kvadrat neparnog broja daje ostatak \(1\) modulo \(8\), imamo

\[ \begin{aligned} &x_1^3+3Dx_1y_1^2 = x_1(x_1^2+3Dy_1^2) \equiv x_1(1+3\times 5\times 1) = 16x_1 = 0 \pmod 8,\\ &3x_1^2y_1+Dy_1^3 = y_1(3x_1^2+Dy_1^2) \equiv y_1(3\times 1+5\times 1) = 8y_1 = 0 \pmod 8. \end{aligned} \]

To pokazuje da su \(x_3,y_3\) oba parna. Nadalje, za sve \(k\in\mathbf Z\) vrijedi

\[ \dfrac{x+y\sqrt{D}}{2} = \pm\left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^{3k} = \pm\left(\dfrac{x_3+y_3\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^k \in \mathbf Z[\sqrt{D}], \]

pa su tada \((x,y)\) oba parna. Obratno, za \(r=1,2\) uvijek vrijedi

\[ \pm\left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^{3k+r} = \pm\left(\dfrac{x_3+y_3\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^k\left(\dfrac{x_r+y_r\sqrt{D}}{2}\right). \]

Da bismo dokazali da pripadni \((x,y)\) nisu cijeli brojevi, dovoljno je dokazati da taj izraz nije cjelobrojan, tj. da drugi faktor u umnošku na desnoj strani nije cjelobrojan. Za \(r=1\) to je upravo pretpostavka; za \(r=2\), budući da je

\[ \dfrac{x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}}{2} = \left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{x_1^2+Dy_1^2}{4} + \dfrac{x_1y_1}{2}\sqrt{D}, \]

a \(x_1^2+Dy_1^2\equiv 1+1\times 1=2\pmod 4\) i \(x_1y_1\equiv 1\pmod 2\), ni taj izraz nije cjelobrojan. Time je dokazano da su pripadna rješenja oba parna samo kad je eksponent višekratnik od \(3\).

Drugim riječima, od svaka tri rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) jedno je oba parno i ono odgovara cjelobrojnom rješenju jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\). To također pokazuje da za \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\) jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\) ima rješenje ako i samo ako jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) ima rješenje.

Dosadašnje razmatranje već je dovoljno za izračun fundamentalne jedinice prstena realnih kvadratnih cijelih brojeva. Neka je \(D\) pozitivan cijeli broj bez kvadratnih faktora. Za slučaj \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\) dovoljno je naći najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\); a za slučaj \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\) dovoljno je naći najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Kad dobijemo najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje \((x,y)\), za \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\) fundamentalna jedinica je \(\pm x\pm y\sqrt{D}\); za \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\) fundamentalna jedinica je \(\dfrac{\pm x\pm y\sqrt{D}}{2}\).

Opći slučaj

Na kraju razmatramo rješavanje poopćene Pellove jednadžbe.

Za slučaj \(|N|<\sqrt{D}\) postoji jednostavna metoda. Raniji rezultat pokazuje da rješenje \((x,y)\) jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) nužno zadovoljava da je \(\dfrac{x}{y}\) jednak nekoj konvergenti od \(\sqrt{D}\). Štoviše, prema ranije razmotrenoj strukturi rješenja, svako fundamentalno rješenje \((x,y)\) zadovoljava da je \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) manji ili jednak fundamentalnom rješenju \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) pripadne Pellove jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=1\). Iz monotonosti niza nazivnika \(B_k\) u PQa algoritmu slijedi da se ta fundamentalna rješenja poopćene Pellove jednadžbe sigurno pojavljuju prije pojave fundamentalnog rješenja pripadne Pellove jednadžbe. Stoga je dovoljno izvesti PQa algoritam za \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(0,1,D)\) sve dok ne bude \(Q_{\ell'}=1\) uz paran \(\ell'\), a usput za svaki dobiveni \((A_k,B_k)\) provjeriti postoji li cijeli broj \(f\) takav da vrijedi

\[ A_k^2-DB_k^2 = (-1)^{k+1}Q_{k+1} = N/f^2 \]

ako vrijedi, bilježimo da je \((fA_{k},fB_{k})\) jedno najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje. Svi \((fA_k,fB_k)\) zabilježeni tim postupkom upravo su sva najmanja pozitivna cjelobrojna rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\). Pomoću \((A_{\ell'-1},B_{\ell'-1})\), tj. fundamentalnog rješenja pripadne Pellove jednadžbe, iz tih nađenih najmanjih pozitivnih cjelobrojnih rješenja možemo generirati sva rješenja poopćene Pellove jednadžbe. Uočimo da, ovisno o tome je li duljina perioda \(\ell\) parna ili neparna, gornji \(\ell'\) može biti \(\ell\) ili \(2\ell\).

Za općenitiji \(N\) gornja metoda više ne vrijedi. Najprije nabrajamo sve kvadratne faktore \(f^2\) od \(N\), stavljamo \(m=N/f^2\) i nabrajamo sva rješenja \(z\) kongruencije \(z^2\equiv D\pmod{|m|}\) koja zadovoljavaju \(-|m|/2<z \le |m|/2\). Zatim izvodimo PQa algoritam za \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(z,|m|,D)\) sve dok ne bude \(Q_k=\pm 1\) ili dok ne završi jedan period. U drugom slučaju rješenje jednadžbe povezano s tim parom \((f,z)\) ne postoji. U prvom slučaju treba dodatno provjeriti vrijedi li \((-1)^kQ_k=N/|N|\). Ako se predznaci podudaraju, \((fG_{k-1},fB_{k-1})\) je rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\). Inače je ono rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=-N\), i rješenje jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) iz njega možemo dobiti slaganjem s fundamentalnim rješenjem pripadne negativne Pellove jednadžbe ako i samo ako rješenje te negativne Pellove jednadžbe postoji. Kad prođemo sve parove \((f,z)\), dobivamo u svakoj klasi ekvivalencije rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) po točno jedno rješenje, i to fundamentalno rješenje ili najmanje pozitivno cjelobrojno rješenje te klase ekvivalencije. Pomoću njih i fundamentalnog rješenja pripadne Pellove jednadžbe mogu se generirati sva cjelobrojna rješenja te jednadžbe. Taj se algoritam naziva Lagrange–Matthews–Mollinov algoritam.

Točnost tog algoritma jamči sljedeći teorem:

Teorem

Neka jednadžba \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) ima cjelobrojno rješenje \((x,y)\) uz \(x\ge 0, y>0,\gcd(x,y)=1\). Stavimo \(Q_0=|N|\); tada je \(\gcd(Q_0,y)=1\). Neka je \(P_0\) rješenje kongruencije \(x\equiv -P_0y\pmod{Q_0}\) uz \(-Q_0/2<P_0\le Q_0/2\), i neka je \(X\) cijeli broj za koji vrijedi \(x=Q_0X-P_0y\). Tada je \(P_0^2\equiv D\pmod{Q_0}\), \(\dfrac{X}{y}\) je neka konvergenta \(\dfrac{A_{k-1}}{B_{k-1}}\) od \(\omega=\dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0}\), i \(Q_k=(-1)^k\dfrac{N}{|N|}\).

Dokaz

Iz \(x\equiv -P_0y\pmod{Q_0}\) i \(x^2-Dy^2=N\equiv 0\pmod{Q_0}\) očito slijedi \(P_0^2\equiv D\pmod{Q_0}\). Stoga je

\[ P_0x+Dy\equiv -P_0^2y+Dy = (D-P_0^2)y\equiv 0\pmod{Q_0}. \]

Na temelju toga možemo promatrati cjelobrojnu matricu

\[ \begin{pmatrix}P & R \\ Q & S\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}X & \dfrac{P_0x+Dy}{Q_0} \\ y & x\end{pmatrix}. \]

Njezina je determinanta

\[ PS-QR = \dfrac{x(x+P_0y)-y(P_0x+Dy)}{Q_0} = \dfrac{x^2-Dy^2}{Q_0} = \pm 1. \]

Štoviše, stavimo li \(\zeta =\sqrt{D} > 1\), imamo

\[ \dfrac{P\zeta+R}{Q\zeta+S} = \dfrac{(x+P_0y)\sqrt{D}+(P_0x+Dy)}{(x+y\sqrt{D})Q_0} = \dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0} = \omega. \]

Sada treba dokazati da je \(\dfrac{P}{Q}\) konvergenta od \(\omega\). Neka \(\dfrac{P}{Q}\) ima razvoj u verižni razlomak

\[ \dfrac{P}{Q} = [a_0,a_1,\cdots,a_k] \]

takav da je \(PS-QR = (-1)^{k-1}\). Ako je \(\dfrac{p_k}{q_k}\) njegova \(k\)-ta konvergenta, onda je \((p_k,q_k)=(P,Q)\), a prema formuli za razliku konvergenti vrijedi \(p_kq_{k-1}-q_kp_{k-1}=(-1)^{k-1}\). To znači

\[ p_k(S-q_{k-1}) = q_k(R-p_{k-1}). \]

Razlikujemo slučajeve:

  • Ako je \(S=0\), lako se provjeri da je \(Q=R=1\), pa je \(\omega=P+\zeta^{-1}=[P,\zeta]\) i stoga je \(\dfrac{P}{Q}=P\) \(0\). konvergenta od \(\omega\);
  • Ako je \(Q=S>0\), onda je \(Q=S=1\) i \(P-R=\pm 1\). Tada,
    • ako je \(P=R+1\), onda je \(\omega=R+\dfrac{1}{1+\zeta^{-1}}=[R,1,\zeta]\), pa je \(\dfrac{P}{Q}=\dfrac{R+1}{1}=[R,1]\) \(1\). konvergenta od \(\omega\);
    • ako je \(P=R-1\), onda je \(\omega=R-1+\dfrac{1}{1+\zeta}=[R-1,\zeta-1]\), pa je \(\dfrac{P}{Q}=R-1\) \(0\). konvergenta od \(\omega\);
  • Ako je \(Q\neq S>0\), onda zbog \(Q=q_k\mid(S-q_{k-1})\) uvijek postoji cijeli broj \(\kappa\) takav da vrijedi \(S=\kappa q_k+q_{k-1}\) i \(R=\kappa p_k+p_{k-1}\). Budući da je \(q_k\ge q_{k-1}\) i \(S>0\), vrijedi \(\kappa\ge 0\). Stoga je \(\omega=\dfrac{(\kappa+\zeta)p_k+p_{k-1}}{(\kappa+\zeta)q_k+q_{k-1}}=[a_0,a_1,\cdots,a_k,\kappa+\zeta]\), pa je \(\dfrac{P}{Q}\) njegova \(k\)-ta konvergenta.

Sve u svemu, \(\dfrac{X}{y}\) je uvijek konvergenta od \(\omega=\dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0}\) i u oznakama PQa algoritma zapisujemo je kao \(\dfrac{A_{k-1}}{B_{k-1}}\). Budući da je \(A_{k-1}^2-DB_{k-1}^2=(-1)^kQ_0Q_k\), vrijedi \(Q_k=(-1)^k\dfrac{N}{|N|}\).

Taj teorem jamči da se sva pozitivna rješenja jednadžbe nalaze među konvergentama pripadnih kvadratnih iracionalnosti. Budući da pri računanju konvergenti PQa algoritmom, čim uđemo u period, konvergente sigurno postaju pozitivne, dovoljno je nabrojiti sve kvadratne iracionalnosti dopuštene uvjetima teorema i izračunati njihove konvergente do kraja jednog perioda da bismo našli rješenje. Budući da su dva rješenja koja se pojave među konvergentama iste kvadratne iracionalnosti nužno ekvivalentna, čim dobijemo prvo rješenje koje zadovoljava \((-1)^kQ_k=N/|N|\), možemo prekinuti daljnji račun. Za razliku od svih prethodnih algoritama, ovdje se odgovarajući \(k\) može pojaviti i prije ulaska u period.

Introduction

This article discusses solving the (generalized) Pell equation. The generalized Pell equation is the Diophantine equation in \(x\) and \(y\)

\[ x^2-Dy^2=N, \]

where \(D\) is a positive integer that is not a perfect square1 and \(N\) is a nonzero integer. Pell's equation in the narrow sense refers to the special case \(N=1\) or \(N=\pm 1\), and sometimes also includes the case \(N=\pm 4\). The generalized Pell equation is closely related to finding quadratic integers of norm \(N\) in a ring of real quadratic integers, and the cases usually called Pell's equation (in the narrow sense) can be viewed as finding the units of a ring of real quadratic integers.

When this article mentions Pell's equation, it refers specifically to the case \(N=1\). Correspondingly, the case \(N=-1\) is called the negative Pell equation2 (negative Pell's equation).

Structure of the solutions

The integer solutions \((x,y)\) of the generalized Pell equation are closely related to the quadratic integer \(x+y\sqrt{D}\), so in the literature the solutions of Pell's equation are often written in the form \(x+y\sqrt{D}\). Since the norm of a quadratic integer is

\[ N(x+y\sqrt{D}) = x^2-Dy^2, \]

the generalized Pell equation roughly amounts to finding quadratic integers of norm \(N\). However, there is indeed a subtle difference between the two. When \(x\) and \(y\) are both integers, \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) is certainly a quadratic integer; conversely, a quadratic integer does not necessarily require \(x\) and \(y\) to both be integers — in the case \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), \(x\) and \(y\) can also both be half-integers3.

This difference is especially important when finding the fundamental unit. The units of a ring of quadratic integers are the quadratic integers of norm \(\pm 1\). For \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\), to find such units it suffices to solve the generalized Pell equation in the case \(N=\pm 1\); but for \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), the case \(N=\pm 4\) must also be considered. The method for finding units is discussed below.

To understand the structure of the solutions of the generalized Pell equation, we start from Brahmagupta's identity:

\[ (x_1^2-Dy_1^2)(x_2^2-Dy_2^2)=(x_1x_2+Dy_1y_2)^2-D(x_1y_2+x_2y_1)^2. \]

It amounts to the fact that the norm of quadratic integers preserves multiplication, i.e.

\[ \begin{aligned} N\left(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\right)N\left(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}\right) &= N\left((x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D})\right) \\ &= N\left((x_1x_2+Dy_1y_2)+(x_1y_2+x_2y_1)\sqrt{D}\right). \end{aligned} \]

Using this identity, integer solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N_1\) and the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N_2\) can be composed into an integer solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N_1N_2\). Of course, from the viewpoint of quadratic integers, composing solutions is just multiplying quadratic integers, which shows the convenience of writing the solutions of Pell's equation as quadratic integers. In particular, taking \(N_1=N\) and \(N_2=1\), we see that if we know one solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) and all solutions of the corresponding Pell equation \(x^2-Dy^2=1\), we can obtain more solutions of \(x^2-Dy^2=N\). Of course, this method does not necessarily generate all solutions. But it at least shows that understanding the structure of the solutions of Pell's equation plays an important role in understanding the structure of the solutions of the generalized Pell equation.

Pell's equation

Geometrically, the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=1\) is a hyperbola whose real axis is the \(x\)-axis and whose imaginary axis is the \(y\)-axis. Every point of the hyperbola corresponds uniquely to a nonzero value of \(x+y\sqrt{D}\): the left branch of the hyperbola corresponds to negative values of \(x+y\sqrt{D}\), and the right branch to positive values. Moreover, on each branch the values of \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) strictly increase as we move along the hyperbola from bottom to top. The value of the quadratic integer thus gives the solutions of Pell's equation a natural order.

The hyperbola is symmetric with respect to both the \(x\)-axis and the \(y\)-axis, so when discussing the solutions of Pell's equation it suffices to consider the part in the first quadrant; the remaining solutions can be obtained by symmetry. This amounts to considering only the solutions with \(x+y\sqrt{D}>1\). If the equation has nontrivial solutions other than \((\pm 1,0)\), then there certainly exists a solution \((x_1,y_1)\) in the first quadrant with the smallest value of \(x+y\sqrt{D}\); it is also the lattice point in the first quadrant (excluding the coordinate axes) with both the smallest abscissa and the smallest ordinate, and it is called the fundamental solution of Pell's equation4. By the preceding discussion, all integer pairs \((x_k,y_k)\) satisfying \(x_k+y_k\sqrt{D}=(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^k\) are solutions of Pell's equation, and all of them lie in the first quadrant. Conversely, these are indeed all the solutions of Pell's equation in the first quadrant. Using symmetry again, we obtain the following conclusion:

Theorem

Let \((x_1,y_1)\) be the fundamental solution of Pell's equation \(x^2-Dy^2=1\). Then the set of all its solutions is

\[ \{(x,y):x+y\sqrt{D}=\pm(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^k,k\in\mathbf Z\}. \]
Proof

We first prove that there are no other solutions in the first quadrant. Suppose, to the contrary, that there is another solution \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) and that for some \(k\ge 0\) we have

\[ x_k+y_k\sqrt{D}< x+y\sqrt{D}< x_{k+1}+y_{k+1}\sqrt{D}. \]

Geometrically, this says that the lattice point \((x,y)\) falls on the hyperbola between \((x_k,y_k)\) and \((x_{k+1},y_{k+1})\) (endpoints excluded). Multiplying the inequality by \(x_k-y_k\sqrt{D}=(x_k+y_k\sqrt{D})^{-1}\), we get

\[ 1< (x+y\sqrt{D})(x_k-y_k\sqrt{D})=(xx_k-Dyy_k)+(x_ky-xy_k)\sqrt{D} < x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}. \]

By the monotonicity mentioned above, this inequality says that \((xx_k-Dyy_k,x_ky-xy_k)\) is a lattice point lying between \((1,0)\) and \((x_1,y_1)\). This contradicts the choice of \((x_1,y_1)\).

When extending the solutions in the first quadrant to the whole plane, negating the exponent \(k\) (i.e. taking the reciprocal of the whole expression) is the reflection in the \(x\)-axis, and negating the whole expression is the reflection in the origin. Adding the trivial solution for \(k=0\), we obtain all solutions of Pell's equation.

The preceding discussion only assumed the existence of the fundamental solution. What we now need to show is that Pell's equation always has a nontrivial solution.

Theorem

Pell's equation \(x^2-Dy^2=1\) always has an integer solution other than \((\pm 1,0)\).

Proof

First, Dirichlet's theorem shows that there are infinitely many pairs of positive integers \((x,y)\) such that

\[ \left|\dfrac{x}{y}-\sqrt{D}\right| \le \dfrac{1}{y^2} \]

holds, and they all satisfy the inequality

\[ |x^2-Dy^2|=y^2\left|\dfrac{x}{y}-\sqrt{D}\right|\left|\dfrac{x}{y}+\sqrt{D}\right| \le \dfrac{1}{y^2}+2\sqrt{D}<1+2\sqrt{D}. \]

Hence there must exist an integer \(m\in(-1-2\sqrt{D},1+2\sqrt{D})\) such that infinitely many pairs \((x,y)\) satisfy \(x^2-Dy^2 = m\). Classifying these \((x,y)\) by their residues modulo \(m\), we see that for some pair of integers \((x_0,y_0)\) there must exist infinitely many pairs \((x,y)\) such that \(x\equiv x_0\pmod m\) and \(y\equiv y_0\pmod m\) hold. Taking any two distinct pairs \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) satisfying these conditions, we have

\[ \dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}}=\dfrac{x_1x_2-Dy_1y_2}{m}+\dfrac{x_2y_1-x_1y_2}{m}\sqrt{D}. \]

Since by the congruences

\[ \begin{aligned} x_1x_2-Dy_1y_2 &\equiv x_0^2-Dy_0^2 = m \equiv 0 \pmod{|m|},\\ x_2y_1-x_1y_2 &\equiv x_0y_0-x_0y_0 = 0 \pmod{|m|}, \end{aligned} \]

this shows that the right-hand side above is an integer solution. Moreover, since \((x_1,y_1)\neq(x_2,y_2)\), it is not trivial. This shows that Pell's equation indeed has a nontrivial solution.

Of course, the proof given in this section is non-constructive; when we discuss the method of solving Pell's equation below, we will directly construct a solution of Pell's equation from the convergents of a continued fraction, thereby providing another proof that Pell's equation has a nontrivial solution. In addition, although the structure of the solutions of Pell's equation obtained in this section agrees with the structure of the units of the ring of real quadratic integers, for the case \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\) this section has not completely settled the structure of the units of the corresponding ring of quadratic integers; this will be discussed further below.

Generalized Pell equation

The graph of the generalized Pell equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) is also a hyperbola in the plane, also with the \(x\)-axis and the \(y\)-axis as axes of symmetry. It was pointed out above that some solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) may differ only by a factor that is a solution of Pell's equation, which means the solutions of \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) can be partitioned into equivalence classes. For two solutions \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\), if there is a solution \((u,v)\) of Pell's equation such that \(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}=(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})(u+v\sqrt{D})\) holds, then the solutions \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) are called equivalent. A necessary and sufficient condition for two solutions to be equivalent is

\[ N\mid (x_1x_2-Dy_1y_2),\ N\mid (x_2y_1-x_1y_2). \]

Since the solutions of Pell's equation are relatively easy to find, a natural idea is to find one solution in each of the equivalence classes above. Once these solutions are known, all the required solutions of the generalized Pell equation can be obtained using the solutions of the corresponding Pell equation. Among the equivalence classes of solutions of the generalized Pell equation, by symmetry, every equivalence class has a solution whose ordinate \(y\) is nonnegative and as small as possible: if such a solution is unique, it is called the fundamental solution of that equivalence class; otherwise, the equivalence class necessarily has two solutions with nonnegative and smallest \(y\), and they are symmetric with respect to the \(y\)-axis; in this case the one with \(x>0\) is chosen as the fundamental solution. Thus, solving the generalized Pell equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) amounts to finding its set \(U\) of fundamental solutions. Let \((r,s)\) be the fundamental solution of the corresponding Pell equation; then the set of all solutions of the generalized Pell equation is

\[ \{(x,y):x+y\sqrt{D}=\pm(r+s\sqrt{D})^k(u+v\sqrt{D}),k\in\mathbf Z,u+v\sqrt{D}\in U\}. \]

The generalized Pell equation necessarily has finitely many fundamental solutions. This is because from the expression for the general solution above, the absolute value \(|u+v\sqrt{D}|\) must lie between \(r-s\sqrt{D}\) and \(r+s\sqrt{D}\). The references at the end of the article give stricter estimates of the range of the coordinates of the fundamental solutions. Of course, unlike Pell's equation, the generalized Pell equation may have no solutions.

To obtain all solutions in the same equivalence class from one solution \((u,v)\) of the generalized Pell equation and the fundamental solution \((r,s)\) of Pell's equation, besides composing solutions one can also use the following recurrence

\[ x_{k} = 2rx_{k-1} - x_{k-2},\ y_{k} = 2ry_{k-1} - y_{k-2}, \]

where \(x_k+y_k\sqrt{D}=(r+s\sqrt{D})^k(u+v\sqrt{D})\). This is because both \(x_k\) and \(y_k\) can be written, for some pair of real numbers \((A,B)\), in the form \(A(r+s\sqrt{D})^k+B(r-s\sqrt{D})^k\), and by Vieta's formulas \(r\pm s\sqrt{D}\) are the two real roots of the equation \(x^2-2rx+1=0\), so \(x_k\) and \(y_k\) both satisfy the second-order linear recurrence with constant coefficients above. Compared with composing solutions, this recurrence requires fewer multiplications.

Solution methods

Both Pell's equation and the generalized Pell equation can be solved using continued fractions.

PQa algorithm

All the algorithms discussed in this article are based on the PQa algorithm, which can be used to compute the continued fraction expansion of a particular quadratic irrational.

Let integers \(P_0,Q_0,D\) satisfy \(Q_0\neq 0\), \(D>0\) and not a perfect square, and \(P_0^2\equiv D\pmod{Q_0}\). Then the continued fraction expansion \([a_0,a_1,\cdots]\) of the quadratic irrational

\[ \omega=\dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0} \]

can be obtained by the following recurrence formulas:

\[ a_k = \left\lfloor\dfrac{P_k+\sqrt{D}}{Q_k}\right\rfloor,\ P_{k+1} = a_kQ_k - P_k,\ Q_{k+1} = \dfrac{D-P_{k+1}^2}{Q_k}. \]

Furthermore, the numerator \(A_k\) and the denominator \(B_k\) of the \(k\)-th convergent of \(\omega\) are given by the following recurrence formulas:

\[ A_k = a_kA_{k-1} + A_{k-2},\ B_k = a_kB_{k-1} + B_{k-2} \]

with \(A_{-1} = 1\), \(A_{-2}=0\), \(B_{-1}=0\), \(B_{-2}=1\).

The correctness of these formulas has already been proved in the article on continued fractions. It is also explained there that, since a quadratic irrational is a periodic continued fraction, the triples \((P_k,Q_k,a_k)\) eventually enter a cycle, and the algorithm always terminates in finitely many steps. Let the minimal length of the period be \(\ell\) and the earliest starting position of the period be \(k_0\); then the continued fraction expansion of the quadratic irrational can be written as

\[ \omega=[a_0,\cdots,a_{k_0-1},\overline{a_{k_0},\cdots,a_{k_0+\ell-1}}]. \]

To solve Pell's equation with the PQa algorithm, we need to establish the following result:

Theorem

Continue with the notation above. Let \(G_k=Q_0A_k-P_0B_k\). Then the integer pair \((G_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) satisfies the relation

\[ G_{k-1}^2-DB_{k-1}^2=(-1)^{k}Q_0Q_{k}, \]

and their greatest common divisor \(\gcd(G_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) divides \(Q_{k}\).

Proof

In the continued fraction expansion of \(\omega\), let the \(k\)-th remainder (complete quotient) be \(\omega_{k}\), i.e.

\[ \omega = [a_0,a_1,\cdots,a_{k-1},\omega_k] = \dfrac{\omega_k A_{k-1}+A_{k-2}}{\omega_k B_{k-1}+B_{k-2}}. \]

Substituting \(\omega=(P_0+\sqrt{D})/Q_0\) and \(\omega_k=(P_k+\sqrt{D})/Q_k\) into the equation above, we get

\[ \dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0} = \dfrac{(P_k+\sqrt{D})A_{k-1}+Q_kA_{k-2}}{(P_k+\sqrt{D})B_{k-1}+Q_kB_{k-2}}. \]

Clearing the denominators on both sides, comparing the coefficients of the rational and irrational parts, and substituting the expression for \(G_k\), we obtain the following equations:

\[ \begin{aligned} G_{k-1} &= P_kB_{k-1} + Q_kB_{k-2},\\ DB_{k-1} &= P_kG_{k-1} + Q_kG_{k-2}. \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, multiplying the first equation by \(G_{k-1}\) and subtracting the second multiplied by \(B_{k-1}\), we have

\[ \begin{aligned} G_{k-1}^2-DB_{k-1}^2 &= (B_{k-2}G_{k-1}-B_{k-1}G_{k-2})Q_k \\ &= (A_{k-1}B_{k-2}-B_{k-1}A_{k-2})Q_0Q_k \\ &= (-1)^kQ_0Q_k. \end{aligned} \]

The last step uses the difference formula for convergents. This proves the first claim.

To prove the second claim, substitute the expression for \(G_k\) into the first claim:

\[ (Q_0A_{k-1}-P_0B_{k-1})^2 - DB_{k-1}^2 = (-1)^kQ_0Q_k. \]

So, using \(Q_0\mid(P_0^2-D)\), we have

\[ Q_0A_{k-1}^2 +\left(\dfrac{P_0^2-D}{Q_0}B_{k-1}- 2P_0A_{k-1}\right)B_{k-1} = (-1)^kQ_k. \]

Hence \(\gcd(G_{k-1},B_{k-1}) = \gcd(Q_0A_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) divides \(Q_k\).

This result provides a way of finding solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\). If we choose \(Q_0>0\) appropriately and choose \(P_0\) to be a solution of the congruence \(P_0^2\equiv D\pmod{Q_0}\), then by running the PQa algorithm on \((P_0+\sqrt{D})/Q_0\) until we find \((-1)^kQ_0Q_{k}=N\), the pair \((G_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) becomes a solution of the original equation. Moreover, if \(Q_k=\pm 1\), then the solution obtained in this way is certainly primitive, that is, \(G_{k-1}\) and \(B_{k-1}\) are certainly coprime.

This idea is the core of solving Pell's equation and the generalized Pell equation. Having understood this idea, we now deal with some details of the algorithm and prove that all solutions can be obtained in this way.

Pell's equation

To solve Pell's equation \(x^2-Dy^2=1\), it suffices to run the PQa algorithm on \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(0,1,D)\) until \((-1)^kQ_k=1\) appears; then \((A_{k-1},B_{k-1})\) is a solution of Pell's equation (since \(G_{k-1}\) is just \(A_{k-1}\) in this case). Of course, for Pell's equation this process can be described more precisely.

First, the solution must appear at the end of the period. The process above amounts to expanding \(\sqrt{D}\) as a continued fraction. For this, we already have the result:

\[ \sqrt{D} = [\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor,\overline{a_1,\cdots,a_{\ell-1},2\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor}]. \]

Here the period length is \(\ell\), and the period starts at the \(1\)st term (indices start from \(0\)). Moreover, its \(\ell\)-th remainder equals \(\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor+\sqrt{D}\), which shows that \(Q_{\ell}=1\). Therefore, if \(\ell\) is even, then \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) is a nontrivial solution of Pell's equation; if \(\ell\) is odd, then \((A_{2\ell-1},B_{2\ell-1})\) is a nontrivial solution of Pell's equation.

Next we need to show that the solution just obtained is certainly the fundamental solution. This conclusion rests on two reasons: first, the fractions \(x/y\) corresponding to all positive integer solutions \((x,y)\) of Pell's equation appear among the convergents of \(\sqrt{D}\), which guarantees that \((x,y)\) must be some \((A_k,B_k)\) produced during the PQa algorithm; second, apart from the end of the period, \(Q_k=1\) does not appear at any other position, and since the recurrences for \(A_k\) and \(B_k\) guarantee that they grow as the index increases, the smallest positive integer solution (i.e. the fundamental solution) must appear at the position just indicated. These two reasons follow respectively from the following two theorems:

Theorem

Let the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) have a positive integer solution \((x,y)\). If \(|N|<\sqrt{D}\), then \(\dfrac{x}{y}\) must equal a convergent of \(\sqrt{D}\).

Proof

When \(N>0\), since \(x^2-Dy^2>0\), we have \(x>y\sqrt{D}\). Hence

\[ \left|\dfrac{x}{y}-\sqrt{D}\right| = \dfrac{N}{y(x+y\sqrt{D})}<\dfrac{N}{2y^2\sqrt{D}}<\dfrac{1}{2y^2}. \]

By Legendre's criterion, \(\dfrac{x}{y}\) is a convergent of \(\sqrt{D}\).

When \(N<0\), \(x>y\sqrt{D}\) no longer holds. So instead we consider the solutions of the equation \(y^2-\dfrac{1}{D}x^2=-\dfrac{N}{D}\). Since \(\dfrac{|N|}{D}<\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{D}}\), the argument above still holds. This shows that \(\dfrac{y}{x}\) is a convergent of \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\). By the reciprocal theorem, \(\dfrac{x}{y}\) is also a convergent of \(\sqrt{D}\).

Theorem

While running the PQa algorithm above on \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(0,1,D)\), \(Q_k=1\) necessarily implies \(\ell\mid k\).

Proof

In the continued fraction expansion of \(\sqrt{D}\), all remainders except the \(0\)-th are purely periodic continued fractions. Let \(Q_k=1\). By Galois's result, the remainder \(\omega_k=P_k+\sqrt{D}>1\) must have conjugate \(-1<P_k-\sqrt{D}<0\), which shows that \(P_k=\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor\). Therefore the remainder \(\omega_k\) equals \(\omega_\ell\). But a repeated remainder means the continued fraction is periodic, so if \(k\) is not a multiple of \(\ell\), this contradicts \(\ell\) being the smallest positive period. Hence necessarily \(\ell\mid k\).

Summarizing the discussion of this section: it suffices to expand \(\sqrt{D}\) as a continued fraction, i.e. run the PQa algorithm starting from \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(0,1,D)\), and when \(Q_\ell=1\) is obtained for the first time, we have reached the end of the first period. At this point, if \(\ell\) is even, then \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) is the fundamental solution of Pell's equation; otherwise, \((A_{2\ell-1},B_{2\ell-1})\) is the fundamental solution of Pell's equation. For the case where the period length \(\ell\) is odd, there is no need to continue the PQa algorithm to twice the period; we will show shortly that \(A_{2\ell-1}+B_{2\ell-1}\sqrt{D}=(A_{\ell-1}+B_{\ell-1}\sqrt{D})^2\), so the fundamental solution of Pell's equation can be computed directly from \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\). All other solutions of Pell's equation can be computed from the fundamental solution of Pell's equation.

Negative Pell equation

By the discussion in the previous section, the solutions of the negative Pell equation must also correspond to convergents of \(\sqrt{D}\), and can only appear where \((-1)^kQ_k=-1\). This can only happen at the end of the period. Therefore, the negative Pell equation has a solution if and only if the period length \(\ell\) is odd. When a solution exists, \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) is the fundamental solution of the negative Pell equation. The method of solving it is the same as in the previous section.

Using an approach similar to the earlier proof of the structure of the solutions of Pell's equation, the following result can be proved:

Theorem

Let the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) have a solution, with fundamental solution \((x_1,y_1)\). Then all integer solutions of \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) belong to the set

\[ \{(x,y):x+y\sqrt{D}=\pm(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^k,k\in\mathbf Z\}. \]

In particular, the integer solution \((x_2,y_2)\) satisfying \(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}=(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^2\) is exactly the fundamental solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=1\).

Proof

By symmetry, it suffices to consider positive integer solutions, i.e. the case \(x+y\sqrt{D}>1\). But since \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) consists of two hyperbolas, \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) cannot be put in one-to-one correspondence with \((x,y)\). To deal with this difficulty, we first prove that the \((x_2,y_2)\) above is the fundamental solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=1\).

Clearly, \((x_2,y_2)\) is a solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=1\). If \((z,w)\) is the fundamental solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=1\), then necessarily \(1<z+w\sqrt{D}\le x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}\). If the inequality on the right is strict, then dividing the inequality by \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) gives \(-x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}<(z+w\sqrt{D})(-x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})<x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). Expanding the middle term of this inequality gives an expression of the form \(x'+y'\sqrt{D}\), whose norm is \(-1\) and where \((x',y')\) is also an integer solution. Taking reciprocals in this inequality, we find that \(-x'+y'\sqrt{D}\) also falls between \(-x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) and \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). The quadratic integers \(\pm x'+y'\sqrt{D}\) are reciprocals of each other, so one of them must be greater than \(1\). But no other quadratic integer of norm \(-1\) should appear between \(1\) and \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\), which contradicts the minimality of \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). Hence necessarily \(x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}=z+w\sqrt{D}\), i.e. \((x_2,y_2)\) is the fundamental solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=1\).

Based on this, if there were a solution \((x,y)\) of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) not corresponding to any \((x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^k\), then there would exist \(k\) such that \((x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^{2k}<x+y\sqrt{D}<(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^{2k+2}\); removing the factor \((x_1+y_1\sqrt{D})^{2k+1}\) shows that there is a quadratic integer \(x'+y'\sqrt{D}\neq 1\) of norm \(\pm 1\) lying between \(-x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) and \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). Repeating the reciprocal argument of the previous paragraph, this contradicts the minimality of \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\). Thus the original claim is proved.

Since \((A_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) is the smallest positive integer solution of the negative Pell equation, and all positive integer solutions of \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) appear in the set

\[ \{(x,y):x+y\sqrt{D}=(A_{\ell-1}+B_{\ell-1}\sqrt{D})^k,k\in\mathbf N_+\} \]

and since these positive integer solutions must correspond to the convergents of \(\sqrt{D}\) at the end of a period (one position before it), and the numerators and denominators of the convergents are strictly increasing, for all \(k\in\mathbf N_+\) we always have

\[ (A_{\ell-1}+B_{\ell-1}\sqrt{D})^k = A_{k\ell-1}+B_{k\ell-1}\sqrt{D}. \]

Among all these positive integer solutions, those with odd \(k\) are solutions of the negative Pell equation and those with even \(k\) are solutions of Pell's equation; the two alternate.

To decide whether the negative Pell equation has a solution, one needs to compute the period length of the continued fraction expansion of \(\sqrt{D}\), which is not easy to compute, so one would like to find a simpler criterion. However, there is currently no criterion with concise, easily computable conditions5. Here we only give a simple result.

Theorem

If the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) has a solution, then \(4\) does not divide \(D\) and \(D\) has no prime factor of the form \(4k+3\). Conversely, if \(D=2\) or \(D\) is a prime of the form \(4k+1\), then the equation necessarily has a solution.

Proof

First, if the negative Pell equation has a solution, this means that \(-1\) is a quadratic residue modulo \(D\), hence \(-1\) is also a quadratic residue modulo any divisor \(d\) of \(D\), so \(d\neq 4\) and \(d\) is not a prime of the form \(4k+3\). Conversely, the equation \(x^2-2y^2=-1\) has the nontrivial solution \((1,1)\). What remains is the case where \(D\) is a prime of the form \(4k+1\).

Let \(D\) be a prime of the form \(4k+1\); we need to prove that the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) has a solution. The idea is to start from the fundamental solution \((u,v)\) of Pell's equation \(x^2-Dy^2=1\) and construct a solution \((\alpha,\beta)\) of \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\). If \(u\) is even, reducing both sides of \(u^2-Dv^2=1\) modulo \(4\) gives \(v^2\equiv -1\pmod 4\), but \(-1\) is not a quadratic residue modulo \(4\). This contradiction shows that \(u\) is odd. Consider the equation \(Dv^2=u^2-1=(u+1)(u-1)\). Since \(u\) is odd, \(\gcd(u+1,u-1)=\gcd(u+1,2)=2\). Based on this fact, distributing the factors of \(Dv^2\) between \(u+1\) and \(u-1\), one of them must be \(2\alpha^2\) and the other \(2D\beta^2\), where \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are coprime positive integers and \(v=2\alpha\beta\). Substituting \(u=\alpha^2+D\beta^2\) and \(v=2\alpha\beta\) into \(u^2-Dv^2=1\) gives \(\alpha^2-D\beta^2=\pm 1\). Since \((u,v)\) is the fundamental solution of Pell's equation and \((\alpha,\beta)\) is a pair of positive integers smaller than \((u,v)\), the right-hand side of this equation cannot be \(+1\), so it can only be \(-1\). This proves that \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) has the solution \((\alpha,\beta)\).

If \(D\) is composite, then having no prime factor of the form \(4k+3\) and being squarefree does not guarantee that the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) has a solution; for example, \(x^2-34y^2=-1\) has no solution.

Example

From the computations in the examples above, the equation \(x^2-14y^2=-1\) has no solution, and the smallest positive integer solution of the equation \(x^2-41y^2=-1\) is \((G_2,B_2)=(32,5)\).

The case of norm ±4

Next we discuss the solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Here the behavior of the solutions depends on the value of \(D\bmod 4\).

Some cases are easy. If \(D\equiv 0\pmod 4\), then \(x\) is even, so \((x/2,y)\) is a solution of the equation \(u^2-(D/4)v^2=\pm 1\). In the remaining cases, \(x,y\) must be both odd or both even. If \(x,y\) are both odd, reducing both sides of the equation modulo \(4\) gives \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\). So if \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\), then \(x,y\) can only be both even, hence \((x/2,y/2)\) is a solution of the equation \(u^2-Dv^2=\pm 1\). Therefore, except for the case \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), the solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) can all be obtained from the solutions of the corresponding (negative) Pell equation.

Now we discuss the case \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), which cannot simply be reduced to the cases already solved. To find the fundamental solution, we can apply the PQa algorithm to \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(1,2,D)\); when \(Q_\ell=2\) is obtained for the first time, we have reached the end of the first period. If the period length \(\ell\) is even, then \((G_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) is the fundamental solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=4\); otherwise, \((G_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\) is the fundamental solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\). Starting from \((G_{\ell-1},B_{\ell-1})\), all solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) can be obtained:

\[ \left\{(x,y):\dfrac{x+y\sqrt{D}}{2}=\pm\left(\dfrac{G_{\ell-1}+B_{\ell-1}\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^k,k\in\mathbf Z\right\}. \]

If the period length \(\ell\) is even, all of these are solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=4\); otherwise, when \(k\) is odd, \((x,y)\) is a solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\), and when \(k\) is even, \((x,y)\) is a solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=4\).

The correctness of this algorithm relies on the following facts:

Theorem

Let the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) have a positive integer solution \((x,y)\). If \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), then \(\dfrac{(x+y)/2}{y}\) must be a convergent of \(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\).

Proof

First note that in this case \(x,y\) must have the same parity, so \((x+y)/2\) is an integer. If \((x,y)\) is a solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=4\), then \(x>y\sqrt{D}>2y\), so

\[ \left|\dfrac{(x+y)/2}{y}-\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\right| = \dfrac{2}{y(x+y\sqrt{D})}<\dfrac{1}{2y^2}. \]

By Legendre's criterion, \(\dfrac{(x+y)/2}{y}\) is a convergent of \(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\).

If \((x,y)\) is a solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\), then to establish the inequality above it suffices to prove \(4y<x+y\sqrt{D}\). This holds at least for all cases except \(D=5,13\). For the cases \(D=5,13\), substituting \(x=\sqrt{Dy^2-4}\) into this inequality shows that it is equivalent to \(2(\sqrt{D}-2)y^2>1\). Except for \((D,y)=(5,1)\), this inequality holds for all \(D=5,13\) and positive integers \(y\). What remains is to verify the case \((D,y)=(5,1)\); here the solution of the equation \(x^2-5y^2=-4\) is \((x,y)=(1,1)\), and what needs to be verified is that \(\dfrac{1}{1}\) is a convergent of \(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}=[\overline{1}]\), which obviously holds.

Theorem

Let \(D\) be a positive integer that is not a perfect square. The continued fraction expansion of the quadratic irrational \(\omega=\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\) has the form

\[ \omega = [\lfloor\omega\rfloor,\overline{a_1,\cdots,a_{\ell-1},2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1}], \]

where \(\ell\) is the period length, and \(a_k=a_{\ell-k}\) holds for every \(1\le k<\ell\).

Proof

Since \(\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1+\omega>1\), and its conjugate equals \(\lfloor\omega\rfloor - \omega\), which lies between \(-1\) and \(0\), by Galois's result \(\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1+\omega\) is a purely periodic continued fraction and can be written as

\[ \lfloor\omega\rfloor-1+\omega = [\overline{2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1,a_1,\cdots,a_{\ell-1}}]. \]

And Galois's result on the reciprocal of the negative conjugate shows that

\[ \dfrac{1}{\omega-\lfloor\omega\rfloor} = [\overline{a_{\ell-1},\cdots,a_1,2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1}]. \]

Therefore, by the definition of a continued fraction, we have

\[ \lfloor\omega\rfloor-1+\omega = 2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1 + \dfrac{1}{\dfrac{1}{\omega-\lfloor\omega\rfloor}} = [2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1,\overline{a_{\ell-1},\cdots,a_1,2\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1}]. \]

The uniqueness of the continued fraction expansion shows that \(a_k=a_{\ell-k}\) holds for all \(1\le k<\ell\), and hence the expansion to be proved also holds.

Theorem

Let \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\). While running the PQa algorithm above on \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(1,2,D)\), \(Q_k=2\) necessarily implies \(\ell\mid k\).

Proof

In the continued fraction expansion of \(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}\), all remainders except the \(0\)-th are purely periodic continued fractions. Let \(Q_k=2\). By Galois's result, the conjugate of the remainder \(\omega_k=\dfrac{P_k+\sqrt{D}}{2}\) must satisfy \(-1<\dfrac{P_k-\sqrt{D}}{2}<0\), i.e. \(\sqrt{D}-2<P_k<\sqrt{D}\). Since in the PQa algorithm we always have \(Q_k\mid P_k^2-D\) (see the proof of correctness of the algorithm), \(P_k\) must be odd, which shows that the value of \(P_k\) is unique, namely \(P_k=P_0+2(\lfloor\omega\rfloor-1)\), that is, the remainder \(\omega_k=\omega_\ell\). But a repeated remainder means the continued fraction is periodic, so if \(k\) is not a multiple of \(\ell\), this contradicts \(\ell\) being the smallest positive period. Hence necessarily \(\ell\mid k\).

Theorem

Let \((x_1,y_1)\) be the smallest positive integer solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Then the set of all its solutions is

\[ \left\{(x,y):\dfrac{x+y\sqrt{D}}{2}=\pm\left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^k,k\in\mathbf Z\right\}. \]
Proof

By symmetry, it suffices to consider positive integer solutions \((x,y)\). The only thing to prove here is that the pairs of real numbers \((x,y)\) in the set are indeed integer solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). For the rest, it suffices to repeat the proof of the structure of the solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\).

What actually needs to be proved is that for any integer solutions \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\), the pair of positive real numbers \((x_3,y_3)\) defined by

\[ \dfrac{x_3+y_3\sqrt{D}}{2} = \dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\dfrac{x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}}{2}. \]

is still an integer solution. Expanding the right-hand side and comparing the coefficients of the rational and irrational terms, we get

\[ x_3=\dfrac{x_1x_2+Dy_1y_2}{2},\ y_3=\dfrac{x_1y_2+x_2y_1}{2}. \]

Since for \(i=1,2\) we have \(x_i\equiv x_i^2\equiv Dy_i^2\equiv Dy_i\pmod 2\), we get

\[ \begin{aligned} 2x_3 &= x_1x_2+Dy_1y_2 \equiv D^2y_1y_2+Dy_1y_2=D(D+1)y_1y_2 \equiv 0 \pmod 2,\\ 2y_3 &= x_1y_2+x_2y_1 \equiv Dy_1y_2+Dy_2y_1 = 2Dy_1y_2 \equiv 0 \pmod 2. \end{aligned} \]

This shows that \(x_3\) and \(y_3\) are both integers. Using the fact that the norm preserves multiplication, \((x_3,y_3)\) is a solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\).

Combining these facts and repeating the arguments of the previous sections, the correctness of the algorithm above for solving the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) can be established. These results show that the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) has a simple solution structure similar to that of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\): all its solutions can be expressed through its smallest positive integer solution, without solving other equations.

In fact, all solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) can be found among the solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\), so from this point of view the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) is more fundamental. Clearly, \((x,y)\) is a solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\) if and only if \((2x,2y)\) is a solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). The analysis above pointed out that when \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\), all solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) must be both even, and hence correspond to solutions of \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\).

When \(D\equiv 0\pmod 4\), in a solution \((x,y)\) of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\), \(x\) must be even, but \(y\) may be odd. If in the smallest positive integer solution \((x_1,y_1)\) of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\), \(y_1\) is even, then \(y\) is also even in all solutions, and these integer solutions are in one-to-one correspondence with the integer solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\); but if in the smallest integer solution \((x_1,y_1)\), \(y_1\) is odd, then the parity of \(y_k\) agrees with that of \(k\) and alternates, so only when \(k\) is even does it correspond to a solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\). If in the smallest positive integer solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\), \(y_1\) is odd and the norm of \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) is \(-4\), then for such \(D\) the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\) has a solution but \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) has none.

When \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), a solution \((x,y)\) of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) may be both odd or both even. If the smallest positive integer solution \((x_1,y_1)\) is already both even, then all its integer solutions must also be both even, so they always correspond to integer solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\). If the smallest positive integer solution \((x_1,y_1)\) is both odd, then the following result holds:

Theorem

Let \((x_1,y_1)\) be the smallest positive integer solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). If \(x_1\) and \(y_1\) are both odd, then \(D\equiv 5\pmod 8\), and an integer solution \((x,y)\) of this equation is both even if and only if

\[ \dfrac{x+y\sqrt{D}}{2} = \pm\left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^{3k},k\in\mathbf Z. \]
Proof

Reducing both sides of the equation \(x_1^2-Dy_1^2=\pm 4\) modulo \(8\) gives \(D\equiv 5\pmod 8\). To prove the second claim, we first prove that \((x_3,y_3)\) are both even; since

\[ \dfrac{x_3+y_3\sqrt{D}}{2} = \left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^{3} = \dfrac{x_1^3+3Dx_1y_1^2}{8}+\dfrac{3x_1^2y_1+Dy_1^3}{8}\sqrt{D}, \]

it suffices to prove that the right-hand side is an integer. Since the square of an odd number is congruent to \(1\) modulo \(8\), we have

\[ \begin{aligned} &x_1^3+3Dx_1y_1^2 = x_1(x_1^2+3Dy_1^2) \equiv x_1(1+3\times 5\times 1) = 16x_1 = 0 \pmod 8,\\ &3x_1^2y_1+Dy_1^3 = y_1(3x_1^2+Dy_1^2) \equiv y_1(3\times 1+5\times 1) = 8y_1 = 0 \pmod 8. \end{aligned} \]

This shows that \(x_3,y_3\) are both even. Furthermore, for all \(k\in\mathbf Z\) we have

\[ \dfrac{x+y\sqrt{D}}{2} = \pm\left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^{3k} = \pm\left(\dfrac{x_3+y_3\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^k \in \mathbf Z[\sqrt{D}], \]

so in this case \((x,y)\) are both even. Conversely, for \(r=1,2\) we always have

\[ \pm\left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^{3k+r} = \pm\left(\dfrac{x_3+y_3\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^k\left(\dfrac{x_r+y_r\sqrt{D}}{2}\right). \]

To prove that the corresponding \((x,y)\) are not integers, it suffices to prove that this expression is not an integer, i.e. that the second factor of the product on the right-hand side is not an integer. For \(r=1\) this is just the hypothesis; for \(r=2\), since

\[ \dfrac{x_2+y_2\sqrt{D}}{2} = \left(\dfrac{x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{x_1^2+Dy_1^2}{4} + \dfrac{x_1y_1}{2}\sqrt{D}, \]

and \(x_1^2+Dy_1^2\equiv 1+1\times 1=2\pmod 4\), \(x_1y_1\equiv 1\pmod 2\), this expression is not an integer either. This proves that the corresponding solutions are both even only when the exponent is a multiple of \(3\).

That is to say, one out of every three solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\) is both even, and it corresponds to an integer solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\). This also shows that for \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-4\) has a solution if and only if the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-1\) has a solution.

The discussion so far is already sufficient to compute the fundamental unit of a ring of real quadratic integers. Let \(D\) be a positive squarefree integer. For the case \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\), it suffices to find the smallest positive integer solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 1\); for the case \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), it suffices to find the smallest positive integer solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=\pm 4\). Once the smallest positive integer solution \((x,y)\) is obtained, for \(D\equiv 2,3\pmod 4\) the fundamental units are \(\pm x\pm y\sqrt{D}\); for \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\) the fundamental units are \(\dfrac{\pm x\pm y\sqrt{D}}{2}\).

General case

Finally, we discuss how to solve the generalized Pell equation.

For the case \(|N|<\sqrt{D}\) there is a simple method. The result above shows that a solution \((x,y)\) of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) must satisfy that \(\dfrac{x}{y}\) equals some convergent of \(\sqrt{D}\). Moreover, by the structure of the solutions discussed above, every fundamental solution \((x,y)\) satisfies that \(x+y\sqrt{D}\) is less than or equal to the fundamental solution \(x_1+y_1\sqrt{D}\) of the corresponding Pell equation \(x^2-Dy^2=1\). By the monotonicity of the sequence of denominators \(B_k\) in the PQa algorithm, these fundamental solutions of the generalized Pell equation must appear before the fundamental solution of the corresponding Pell equation appears. Therefore, it suffices to run the PQa algorithm on \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(0,1,D)\) until \(Q_{\ell'}=1\) with \(\ell'\) even, and along the way check for every \((A_k,B_k)\) that appears whether there is an integer \(f\) such that

\[ A_k^2-DB_k^2 = (-1)^{k+1}Q_{k+1} = N/f^2 \]

holds; if so, record \((fA_{k},fB_{k})\) as a smallest positive integer solution. All the \((fA_k,fB_k)\) recorded by this process are exactly all the smallest positive integer solutions of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\). Using \((A_{\ell'-1},B_{\ell'-1})\), i.e. the fundamental solution of the corresponding Pell equation, all solutions of the generalized Pell equation can be generated from these smallest positive integer solutions. Note that, depending on whether the period length \(\ell\) is even or odd, the \(\ell'\) above may be \(\ell\) or \(2\ell\).

For more general \(N\), the method above no longer applies. First, enumerate all square factors \(f^2\) of \(N\), let \(m=N/f^2\), and enumerate all solutions \(z\) of the congruence \(z^2\equiv D\pmod{|m|}\) satisfying \(-|m|/2<z \le |m|/2\). Then run the PQa algorithm on \((P_0,Q_0,D)=(z,|m|,D)\) until \(Q_k=\pm 1\) or a full period has been completed. In the second case, no solution of the equation associated with this pair \((f,z)\) exists. In the first case, one further needs to check whether \((-1)^kQ_k=N/|N|\). If the signs agree, then \((fG_{k-1},fB_{k-1})\) is a solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\). Otherwise, it is a solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=-N\), and a solution of \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) can be obtained by composing it with the fundamental solution of the corresponding negative Pell equation if and only if a solution of that negative Pell equation exists. After going through all pairs \((f,z)\), we obtain exactly one solution of the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) in each equivalence class of solutions, and this solution is the fundamental solution or the smallest positive integer solution of that equivalence class. Using them and the fundamental solution of the corresponding Pell equation, all integer solutions of the equation can be generated. This algorithm is called the Lagrange–Matthews–Mollin algorithm.

The correctness of this algorithm is guaranteed by the following theorem:

Theorem

Let the equation \(x^2-Dy^2=N\) have an integer solution \((x,y)\) with \(x\ge 0, y>0,\gcd(x,y)=1\). Let \(Q_0=|N|\); then \(\gcd(Q_0,y)=1\). Let \(P_0\) be a solution of the congruence \(x\equiv -P_0y\pmod{Q_0}\) with \(-Q_0/2<P_0\le Q_0/2\), and let the integer \(X\) be such that \(x=Q_0X-P_0y\) holds. Then \(P_0^2\equiv D\pmod{Q_0}\), \(\dfrac{X}{y}\) is a convergent \(\dfrac{A_{k-1}}{B_{k-1}}\) of \(\omega=\dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0}\), and \(Q_k=(-1)^k\dfrac{N}{|N|}\).

Proof

Using \(x\equiv -P_0y\pmod{Q_0}\) and \(x^2-Dy^2=N\equiv 0\pmod{Q_0}\), clearly \(P_0^2\equiv D\pmod{Q_0}\). Hence,

\[ P_0x+Dy\equiv -P_0^2y+Dy = (D-P_0^2)y\equiv 0\pmod{Q_0}. \]

Based on this, consider the integer matrix

\[ \begin{pmatrix}P & R \\ Q & S\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}X & \dfrac{P_0x+Dy}{Q_0} \\ y & x\end{pmatrix}. \]

Its determinant is

\[ PS-QR = \dfrac{x(x+P_0y)-y(P_0x+Dy)}{Q_0} = \dfrac{x^2-Dy^2}{Q_0} = \pm 1. \]

Moreover, letting \(\zeta =\sqrt{D} > 1\), we have

\[ \dfrac{P\zeta+R}{Q\zeta+S} = \dfrac{(x+P_0y)\sqrt{D}+(P_0x+Dy)}{(x+y\sqrt{D})Q_0} = \dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0} = \omega. \]

We now prove that \(\dfrac{P}{Q}\) is a convergent of \(\omega\). Suppose \(\dfrac{P}{Q}\) has the continued fraction expansion

\[ \dfrac{P}{Q} = [a_0,a_1,\cdots,a_k] \]

with \(PS-QR = (-1)^{k-1}\). If \(\dfrac{p_k}{q_k}\) denotes its \(k\)-th convergent, then \((p_k,q_k)=(P,Q)\), and by the difference formula for convergents, \(p_kq_{k-1}-q_kp_{k-1}=(-1)^{k-1}\). This shows that

\[ p_k(S-q_{k-1}) = q_k(R-p_{k-1}). \]

We distinguish cases:

  • If \(S=0\), then it is easy to verify that \(Q=R=1\), so \(\omega=P+\zeta^{-1}=[P,\zeta]\), and hence \(\dfrac{P}{Q}=P\) is the \(0\)-th convergent of \(\omega\);
  • If \(Q=S>0\), then \(Q=S=1\) and \(P-R=\pm 1\). In this case,
    • if \(P=R+1\), then \(\omega=R+\dfrac{1}{1+\zeta^{-1}}=[R,1,\zeta]\), so \(\dfrac{P}{Q}=\dfrac{R+1}{1}=[R,1]\) is the \(1\)st convergent of \(\omega\);
    • if \(P=R-1\), then \(\omega=R-1+\dfrac{1}{1+\zeta}=[R-1,\zeta-1]\), so \(\dfrac{P}{Q}=R-1\) is the \(0\)-th convergent of \(\omega\);
  • If \(Q\neq S>0\), then since \(Q=q_k\mid(S-q_{k-1})\), there always exists an integer \(\kappa\) such that \(S=\kappa q_k+q_{k-1}\) and \(R=\kappa p_k+p_{k-1}\) hold. Since \(q_k\ge q_{k-1}\) and \(S>0\), we have \(\kappa\ge 0\). Hence \(\omega=\dfrac{(\kappa+\zeta)p_k+p_{k-1}}{(\kappa+\zeta)q_k+q_{k-1}}=[a_0,a_1,\cdots,a_k,\kappa+\zeta]\), so \(\dfrac{P}{Q}\) is its \(k\)-th convergent.

In summary, \(\dfrac{X}{y}\) is always a convergent of \(\omega=\dfrac{P_0+\sqrt{D}}{Q_0}\), and in the notation of the PQa algorithm we write it as \(\dfrac{A_{k-1}}{B_{k-1}}\). Since \(A_{k-1}^2-DB_{k-1}^2=(-1)^kQ_0Q_k\), we have \(Q_k=(-1)^k\dfrac{N}{|N|}\).

This theorem guarantees that all positive solutions of the equation are found among the convergents of the corresponding quadratic irrationals. Since, when computing convergents with the PQa algorithm, the convergents are guaranteed to be positive once the period is entered, it suffices to enumerate all quadratic irrationals allowed by the conditions of the theorem and compute their convergents up to the end of one period to find a solution. Since two solutions appearing among the convergents of the same quadratic irrational must be equivalent, as soon as the first solution satisfying \((-1)^kQ_k=N/|N|\) is obtained, the subsequent computation can be stopped. Unlike all the previous algorithms, here the \(k\) satisfying the condition may appear before the period is entered.
