Uvod

U ovom se članku razmatraju rezultati vezani uz računanje faktorijela po zadanom modulu te se daje metoda računanja čija je vremenska složenost linearna u veličini modula, pa je ta metoda prikladna uglavnom kada modul nije prevelik (\(\sim 10^6\)). Osim metode opisane u ovom članku, ovisno o situaciji, za brzo računanje mogu se primijeniti i polinomne tehnike.

Prema kineskom teoremu o ostatcima, problem računanja faktorijela po modulu može se svesti na slučaj u kojem je modul potencija prostog broja \(p^\alpha\). Pri rješavanju takvih problema često je za prost broj \(p\) i prirodan broj \(n\) potrebno iz faktorijela \(n!\) izdvojiti sve faktore \(p\), čime se dobiva rastav:

\[ n! = p^{\nu_p(n!)}(n!)_p. \]

Ovdje \(\nu_p(n!)\) označava eksponent broja \(p\) u rastavu faktorijela \(n!\) na proste faktore, a \((n!)_p\) označava cijeli broj koji se dobije kada se iz vrijednosti faktorijela \(n!\) uklone sve potencije broja \(p\). U ovom se članku razmatra ostatak broja \((n!)_p\) po modulu koji je prost broj (ili njegova potencija) te konkretan način računanja eksponenta \(\nu_p(n!)\).

Ovaj je rastav osobito koristan pri rješavanju problema u kojima se faktorijeli istodobno pojavljuju u brojniku i nazivniku traženog izraza, npr. pri računanju binomnih koeficijenata po zadanom modulu. Kod takvih problema eksponenti broja \(p\) u brojniku i nazivniku mogu se izravno oduzeti, a dijelovi \((n!)_p\) relativno prosti s \(p\) mogu se izračunati pomoću modularnog inverza.

U članku se također predstavljaju Wilsonov teorem i njegovo poopćenje, Legendreova formula i Kummerov teorem, koji su povezani s gornjim problemom.

Wilsonov teorem

Wilsonov teorem daje nužan i dovoljan uvjet da prirodan broj bude prost.

Wilsonov teorem

Za prirodan broj \(n>1\) vrijedi \((n-1)!\equiv -1\pmod n\) ako i samo ako je \(n\) prost.

Dokaz

Najprije dokažimo da za prost broj \(p\) vrijedi \((p-1)!\equiv -1\pmod{p}\). Za to se pomoću kongruencija ili primitivnih korijena mogu dobiti dva kratka dokaza, koja ovdje izostavljamo. U nastavku dajemo dokaz koji zahtijeva manje predznanja:

Za \(p=2\) tvrdnja očito vrijedi. Neka je dalje \(p\geq 3\); treba dokazati da je umnožak svih nenul elemenata (tj. klasa ostataka) u \(\mathbf{Z}_p\) jednak \(\overline{-1}\). Budući da svaki nenul element \(\overline{a}\) u \(\mathbf{Z}_p\) ima inverz \(\overline{a}^{-1}\), umnožak međusobno inverznih elemenata u \(\mathbf{Z}_p\) jednak je \(\overline{1}\). No treba paziti da \(\overline{a}\) i \(\overline{a}^{-1}\) mogu biti jednaki: \(\overline{a}=\overline{a}^{-1}\) ako i samo ako je \(a^2\equiv 1\pmod p\), tj.

\[ 0\equiv a^2-1\equiv (a+1)(a-1)\pmod p, \]

odakle je \(a\equiv 1\pmod p\) ili \(a\equiv -1\pmod p\). To pokazuje da je umnožak svih elemenata iz \(\mathbf{Z}_p\setminus\{\overline{0},\overline{1},\overline{-1}\}\) jednak \(\overline{1}\), pa je umnožak svih nenul elemenata u \(\mathbf{Z}_p\) jednak \(\overline{-1}\).

Obratno, za složen broj \(n\) treba dokazati da je \((n-1)!\not\equiv-1\pmod{n}\). Dokazujemo kontradikcijom: pretpostavimo da je \((n-1)!\equiv -1\pmod{n}\), tj. da postoji cijeli broj \(k\) takav da je \((n-1)!=kn-1\). Budući da je \(n\) složen, nužno postoji prost broj \(p<n\) takav da je \(n=pm\), pa je \((n-1)!=kpm-1\equiv -1\pmod{p}\). Međutim, \(p\) se nužno već pojavljuje u umnošku \((n-1)!\), pa mora vrijediti \((n-1)!\equiv 0\pmod{p}\). Ta kontradikcija pokazuje da je \((n-1)!\not\equiv-1\pmod{n}\).

U oznakama ovog članka Wilsonov se teorem može zapisati kao \((p!)_p\equiv -1\pmod{p}\).

Poopćenje

Wilsonov se teorem može poopćiti na slučaj općeg modula.

Teorem (Gauss)

Za prirodan broj \(m>1\) vrijedi

\[ \prod_{1\le k<m,\ k\perp m} k \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{m}. \]

Štoviše, ostatak \(\pm 1\) jednak je \(-1\) ako i samo ako postoji primitivni korijen po modulu \(m\), tj. kada je \(m=2,4,p^\alpha,2p^\alpha\), gdje je \(p\) neparan prost broj, a \(\alpha\) prirodan broj.

Dokaz

Ovaj se teorem može jednostavno dokazati pomoću strukture multiplikativne grupe cijelih brojeva po modulu \(m\). Ovdje dajemo dokaz slične ideje, ali elementarniji.

Za \(m=2\) vrijedi \(1!=1\equiv -1\pmod{2}\). Za ostale slučajeve u kojima postoji primitivni korijen, neka je \(g\) primitivni korijen; tada se svaki prirodan broj \(k\) manji od \(m\) i relativno prost s njim može jednoznačno prikazati u obliku \(g^i\bmod m\), gdje je \(0\le i<\varphi(m)\), a \(\varphi(m)\) je Eulerova funkcija. Izravnom provjerom vidi se da je \(\varphi(m)\) nužno paran. Budući da su \(g^i\) i \(g^{\varphi(m)-i}\) međusobno multiplikativni inverzi, uparivanjem u umnošku dobivamo

\[ \prod_{1\le k<m,\ k\perp m} k \equiv \prod_{i=0}^{\varphi(m)-1}g^i = g^{\varphi(m)/2}\prod_{i=1}^{\varphi(m)/2-1}g^{i}g^{\varphi(m)-i} \equiv g^{\varphi(m)/2} \pmod{m}. \]

Budući da je \(g^{\varphi(m)/2}\bmod m\) jedini element različit od \(1\bmod{m}\) koji je sam sebi multiplikativni inverz, on je jednak \(-1\bmod{m}\). Time je pokazano da je u tom slučaju ostatak jednak \(-1\).

Za slučaj u kojem primitivni korijen po modulu \(m\) ne postoji treba dokazati da je ostatak jednak \(1\). U tu svrhu najprije napravimo rastav na proste faktore \(m=p_1^{e_1}p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_s^{e_s}\), a zatim primjenom kineskog teorema o ostatcima vidimo da je dovoljno dokazati da

\[ \prod_{1\le k<m,\ k\perp m} k\equiv 1\pmod{p_j^{e_j}} \]

vrijedi za sve faktore \(p_j^{e_j}\). Kineski teorem o ostatcima kaže da svaka moguća kombinacija ostataka \((r_1,r_2,\cdots,r_s)\), gdje je \(1\le r_j<p_j^{e_j}\) i \(p_j\perp r_j\), jednoznačno odgovara točno jednom broju takvom da je \(1\le k<m\), \(k\perp m\) i \(k\equiv r_j\pmod{p_j^{e_j}}\). Stoga za svaki ostatak \(r_j\) postoji točno \({\varphi(m)}/{\varphi(p_j^{e_j})}\) brojeva \(k\) za koje vrijedi \(k\equiv r_j\pmod{p_j^{e_j}}\). Pomoću toga umnožak možemo grupirati, pa dobivamo

\[ \prod_{1\le k<m,\ k\perp m} k\equiv\left(\prod_{1\le r_j<p_j^{e_j},\ r_j\perp p_j} r_j\right)^{{\varphi(m)}/{\varphi(p_j^{e_j})}}\pmod{p_j^{e_j}}. \]

Da bi ovdje eksponent \({\varphi(m)}/{\varphi(p_j^{e_j})}=\varphi(m/p_j^{e_j})\) bio neparan, nužno mora biti \(m/p_j^{e_j}=1,2\), jer je Eulerova funkcija \(\varphi(n)\) parna za sve \(n\ge 3\). Ako je \(p_j\) neparan prost broj, budući da primitivni korijen po modulu \(m\) ne postoji, nužno je \(m/p_j^{e_j}\neq 1,2\); ako je \(p_j^{e_j}=2,4\), budući da primitivni korijen po modulu \(m\) ne postoji, \(m/p_j^{e_j}\) nužno sadrži neki neparan prost faktor, pa je veći od \(2\): u oba je slučaja eksponent \({\varphi(m)}/{\varphi(p_j^{e_j})}\) paran. A za izraz u zagradi u gornjoj formuli već smo dokazali da daje ostatak \(-1\) po modulu \(p_j^{e_j}\), pa ostatak te potencije po modulu \(p_j^{e_j}\) mora biti \(1\). Preostaje samo slučaj \(p_j=2\) i \(e_j>2\), za koji se izravno može dokazati:

\[ \prod_{1\le r_j<2^{e_j},\ r_j\perp 2}r_j \equiv 1\pmod{2^{e_j}}. \]

Slijedeći ideju prethodnog dokaza, sve neparne brojeve \(r_j\) s \(1\le r_j<2^{e_j}\) možemo upariti tako da se pokrate; oni koji se ne mogu upariti nužno su rješenja jednadžbe \(x^2\equiv 1\pmod{2^{e_j}}\). Ta jednadžba znači \(2^{e_j}\mid (x-1)(x+1)\). Stavimo li \(x=2y+1\), nužno je \(2^{e_j-2}\mid y(y+1)\), a od brojeva \(y\) i \(y+1\) jedan je neparan, a drugi paran, pa je \(y=t2^{e_j-2}\) ili \(y=t2^{e_j-2}-1\). Dakle, \(x=t2^{e_j-1}\pm 1\), gdje je \(t\) cijeli broj. Među ostatcima po modulu \(2^{e_j}\) takva su samo četiri: \(\pm 1\) i \(2^{e_j-1}\pm 1\). Stoga vrijedi

\[ \prod_{1\le r_j<2^{e_j},\ r_j\perp 2}r_j \equiv (-1)(2^{e_j-1}-1)(2^{e_j-1}+1) \equiv 1\pmod{2^{e_j}}. \]

Time je dokaz dovršen za sve slučajeve.

U računanju je osobito važan slučaj u kojem je modul potencija prostog broja:

Korolar

Za prost broj \(p\) i prirodan broj \(\alpha\) vrijedi

\[ \prod_{1\le k<p^\alpha,\ k\perp p}k \equiv \begin{cases} 1, & p=2\text{ and }\alpha\ge3,\\ -1, &\text{otherwise} \end{cases} \pmod{p^\alpha}. \]

Uočite da lijeva strana nije \((p^\alpha!)_p\), jer potonji izraz uključuje i doprinos višekratnika broja \(p\).

Računanje ostatka faktorijela

U ovom se odjeljku razmatra računanje ostatka \((n!)_p\bmod p^{\alpha}\).

Slučaj prostog modula

Izraz \((n!)_p\) ima očitu rekurzivnu strukturu. Da bismo je uočili, najprije pogledajmo konkretan primjer:

Primjer

Da bismo izračunali \((32!)_5 \bmod{5}\), možemo provesti sljedeći rekurzivni račun:

\[ \begin{aligned} (32!)_5 &= 1\times 2\times 3 \times 4\times \underbrace{1}_{5}\times 6 \times 7 \times 8\times 9 \times \underbrace{2}_{10} \\ &\quad\times 11 \times 12 \times 13 \times 14\times \underbrace{3}_{15}\times 16 \times 17\times 18\times 19\times \underbrace{4}_{20} \\ &\quad\times 21 \times 22 \times 23 \times 24\times \underbrace{1}_{25}\times 26 \times 27\times 28\times 29\times \underbrace{6}_{30} \times 31 \times 32 \\ &\equiv 1\times 2\times 3 \times 4\times \underbrace{1}_{5}\times 1 \times 2 \times 3\times 4 \times \underbrace{2}_{10} \\ &\quad\times 1 \times 2 \times 3 \times 4\times \underbrace{3}_{15}\times 1 \times 2\times 3\times 4\times \underbrace{4}_{20} \\ &\quad\times 1 \times 2 \times 3 \times 4\times \underbrace{1}_{25}\times 1 \times 2\times 3\times 4\times \underbrace{1}_{30}\times 1\times 2\\ &= (1\times 2\times 3\times 4)^{6}\times(1\times 2)\times(\underbrace{1}_{5}\times \underbrace{2}_{10}\times \underbrace{3}_{15} \times \underbrace{4}_{20}\times \underbrace{1}_{25}\times \underbrace{1}_{30}) \pmod{5} \end{aligned} \]

Vidimo da se, zahvaljujući periodičnosti ostataka po modulu \(5\), ovaj umnožak može podijeliti na nekoliko blokova duljine \(5\), pri čemu je jedina razlika među blokovima ostatak posljednjeg elementa. Budući da dijeljenje \(32\) s \(5\) daje kvocijent \(6\) i ostatak \(2\), umnožak se može podijeliti na \(6\) potpunih blokova i posljednji nepotpuni blok duljine \(2\). Stoga iz prvih \(6\) blokova možemo izdvojiti dio bez posljednjeg elementa (upravo taj dio rješava Wilsonov teorem), zatim pomnožiti s umnoškom posljednjeg nepotpunog bloka i na kraju pomnožiti s umnoškom posljednjih elemenata prvih \(6\) blokova. Posljednji element svakog bloka višekratnik je broja \(5\) pa je, nakon uklanjanja potencija broja \(5\), njihov umnožak upravo \((6!)_{5}\pmod{5}\). Time je početni problem sveden na problem manje veličine.

Poopćavanjem rekurzivne strukture iz ovog primjera dobiva se sljedeća rekurzivna formula:

Rekurzivna formula

Za prost broj \(p\) i prirodan broj \(n\) vrijedi

\[ (n!)_p \equiv (-1)^{\left\lfloor n/p\right\rfloor}\cdot (n\bmod p)!\cdot\left(\left\lfloor n/p\right\rfloor!\right)_p\pmod{p}. \]
Dokaz

Označimo s \((n)_p\) rezultat uklanjanja svih potencija broja \(p\) iz rastava broja \(n\) na proste faktore. Tada vrijedi

\[ \begin{aligned} (n!)_p &= \prod_{k=1}^n(k)_p = \left(\prod_{1\le k\le n,\ k\perp p}(k)_p\right)\left(\prod_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/p\rfloor}(pk)_p\right) \\ &= \left(\prod_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor-1}\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}(ip+j)\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n\bmod p}(\lfloor n/p\rfloor p+j)\right)\left(\prod_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/p\rfloor}(k)_p\right) \\ &\equiv\left(\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}j\right)^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n\bmod p}j\right)(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p \\ &\equiv (-1)^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}\cdot(n\bmod p)!\cdot(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p \pmod p. \end{aligned} \]

Time je dokaz dovršen. U nastavku dajemo konkretno objašnjenje ovog formalnog dokaza.

Želimo izračunati vrijednost \((n!)_p\bmod p\). Po uzoru na gornji primjer imamo

\[ \begin{aligned} (n!)_p &= 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot \underbrace{1}_{p} \cdot (p+1) \cdot (p+2) \cdot \ldots \cdot (2p-1) \cdot \underbrace{2}_{2p} \\ &\quad \cdot (2p+1) \cdot \ldots \cdot (p^2-1) \cdot \underbrace{1}_{p^2} \cdot (p^2 +1) \cdot \ldots \cdot n \\ &\equiv 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot \underbrace{1}_{p} \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-1) \cdot \underbrace{2}_{2p} \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \\ &\quad \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-1) \cdot \underbrace{1}_{p^2} \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot \ldots \cdot (n \bmod p) \pmod{p}. \end{aligned} \]

Jasno se vidi da je faktorijel, osim posljednjeg bloka, podijeljen na nekoliko potpunih blokova jednake duljine.

\[ \begin{aligned} (n!)_p&\equiv \underbrace{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot 1}_{1\text{st}} \cdot \underbrace{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot 2}_{2\text{nd}} \cdot \ldots \\ &\quad \cdot \underbrace{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot 1}_{p\text{th}} \cdot \ldots \cdot \quad \underbrace{1 \cdot 2 \cdot \ldots \cdot (n \bmod p)}_{\text{tail}} \pmod{p}. \end{aligned} \]

Glavni dio potpunog bloka, bez posljednjeg elementa, tj. \((p-1)!\bmod p\), lako je izračunati: primjenjuje se Wilsonov teorem:

\[ (p-1)!\equiv -1\pmod p. \]

Ukupno ima \(\left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{p} \right\rfloor\) potpunih blokova, pa \(\left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{p} \right\rfloor\) treba staviti u eksponent broja \(-1\).

Vrijednost posljednjeg, nepotpunog bloka jest \((n\bmod p)!\bmod p\) i može se izračunati zasebno.

Preostaju još posljednji elementi svakog bloka. Ako sakrijemo već obrađene elemente, vidimo sljedeći obrazac:

\[ (n!)_p = \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 1 } \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 2} \cdot \ldots \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot (p-1)} \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 1 } \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 1} \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 2} \cdots \]

I to je modificirani faktorijel, samo znatno kraći. On iznosi:

\[ \left(\left\lfloor \frac{n}{p} \right\rfloor !\right)_p. \]

Množenjem svih dijelova dobiva se gornja rekurzivna formula.

Pri računanju po ovoj rekurzivnoj formuli dubina rekurzije iznosi \(O(\log_p n)\). Ako se srednji član svaki put računa iznova, složenost računanja na svakoj razini iznosi \(O(p)\), pa je ukupna vremenska složenost \(O(p\log_p n)\); ako se za sve \(n=1,2,\cdots,p-1\) unaprijed izračuna \(n!\bmod p\), složenost pretprocesiranja iznosi \(O(p)\), složenost računanja na svakoj razini iznosi \(O(1)\), a ukupna vremenska složenost iznosi \(O(p+\log_p n)\).

Budući da je rekurzija repna, implementacija može biti iterativna. Sljedeća implementacija unaprijed računa prvih \(p-1\) faktorijela; ako je funkciju potrebno pozivati više puta, pretprocesiranje se može izdvojiti izvan funkcije.

Referentna implementacija 
// Calculate (n!)_p mod p.
int factmod(int n, int p) {
  // Pretreatment.
  std::vector<int> f(p);
  f[0] = 1;
  for (int i = 1; i < p; ++i) {
    f[i] = (long long)f[i - 1] * i % p;
  }
  // Recursion.
  int res = 1;
  while (n > 1) {
    if ((n / p) & 1) res = p - res;
    res = (long long)res * f[n % p] % p;
    n /= p;
  }
  return res;
}

Ako je prostor ograničen pa se ne mogu pohraniti svi faktorijeli, može se također izračunati sve vrijednosti \(n\) iz faktorijela \(n!\bmod p\) koji se stvarno koriste u pozivima funkcije, zatim ih sortirati i na kraju odjednom izračunati vrijednosti tih faktorijela te ih uvrstiti u konačni rezultat, čime se izbjegava pohranjivanje vrijednosti svih faktorijela.

Slučaj modula koji je potencija prostog broja

Slučaj modula koji je potencija prostog broja može se riješiti po uzoru na slučaj prostog modula; dovoljno je Wilsonov teorem zamijeniti njegovim poopćenjem. U oba rezultata ovog odjeljka \(\pm 1\) označava upravo sljedeće: za \(p=2\) i \(\alpha\ge 3\) uzima se \(1\), a u ostalim slučajevima \(-1\).

Rekurzivna formula

Za prost broj \(p\) i prirodne brojeve \(\alpha,n\) vrijedi

\[ (n!)_{p} \equiv (\pm 1)^{\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor}\cdot\left(\prod_{1\le j\le (n\bmod p^\alpha),\ j\perp p}j\right)\cdot(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p\pmod{p^\alpha}. \]

Ovdje se vrijednost \(\pm 1\) određuje kao u poopćenju Wilsonova teorema.

Dokaz

Ideja dokaza potpuno je ista kao u slučaju prostog modula. Označimo s \((k)_p\) rezultat uklanjanja svih potencija broja \(p\) iz rastava broja \(k\) na proste faktore; tada je

\[ \begin{aligned} (n!)_p &= \prod_{1\le k\le n}(k)_p = \left(\prod_{1\le k\le n,\ k\perp p}(k)_p\right)\left(\prod_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/p\rfloor}(pk)_p\right) \\ &= \left(\prod_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor-1}\prod_{1\le j\le p^\alpha,\ j\perp p}(ip^\alpha+j)_p\right)\left(\prod_{1\le j\le (n\bmod p^\alpha),\ j\perp p}(\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor p^\alpha+j)_p\right)\left(\prod_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/p\rfloor}(k)_p\right)\\ &\equiv \left(\prod_{1\le j\le p^\alpha,\ j\perp p}j\right)^{\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor}\cdot\left(\prod_{1\le j\le (n\bmod p^\alpha),\ j\perp p}j\right)\cdot(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p\\ &\equiv (\pm 1)^{\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor}\cdot\left(\prod_{1\le j\le (n\bmod p^\alpha),\ j\perp p}j\right)\cdot(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p \pmod{p^\alpha}. \end{aligned} \]

Osim toga što \(-1\) možda treba zamijeniti s \(\pm 1\), razlika u odnosu na slučaj prostog modula jest i u podatcima koji se pretprocesiraju. U slučaju modula koji je potencija prostog broja za sve prirodne brojeve \(n\) koji ne premašuju \(p^\alpha\) treba unaprijed izračunati umnožak svih cijelih brojeva od \(1\) do \(n\) koji nisu višekratnici broja \(p\), tj.

\[ \prod_{1\le k\le n,\ k\perp p} k\bmod{p^\alpha}. \]

U slučaju prostog modula, za \(n<p\), taj se izraz svodi na \(n!\bmod p\), ali u općem slučaju potencije prostog broja to više ne vrijedi.

U nastavku dajemo primjer računanja ostatka faktorijela po modulu koji je potencija prostog broja, radi lakšeg razumijevanja gornje metode:

Primjer

Da bismo izračunali \((32!)_3\bmod 9\), možemo provesti sljedeći rekurzivni račun:

\[ \begin{aligned} (32!)_3 &= 1\times 2\times \underbrace{1}_{3} \times 4\times 5\times \underbrace{2}_{6}\times 7\times 8\times\underbrace{1}_{9}\\ &\quad\times 10\times 11\times\underbrace{4}_{12}\times 13\times 14\times\underbrace{5}_{15}\times 16\times 17\times\underbrace{2}_{18}\\ &\quad\times 19\times 20\times\underbrace{7}_{21}\times 22\times 23\times\underbrace{8}_{24}\times 25\times 26\times\underbrace{1}_{27}\\ &\quad\times 28\times 29\times\underbrace{10}_{30}\times 31\times 32\\ &\equiv 1\times 2\times \underbrace{1}_{3} \times 4\times 5\times \underbrace{2}_{6}\times 7\times 8\times\underbrace{1}_{9}\\ &\quad\times 1\times 2\times\underbrace{4}_{12}\times 4\times 5\times\underbrace{5}_{15}\times 7\times 8\times\underbrace{2}_{18}\\ &\quad\times 1\times 2\times\underbrace{7}_{21}\times 4\times 5\times\underbrace{8}_{24}\times 7\times 8\times\underbrace{1}_{27}\\ &\quad\times 1\times 2\times\underbrace{1}_{30}\times 4\times 5\\ &=(1\times 2\times 4\times 5\times 7\times 8)^{3}\times (1\times 2\times 4\times 5)\\ &\quad\times\begin{pmatrix}\underbrace{1}_{3}\times\underbrace{2}_{6}\times\underbrace{1}_{9}\times\underbrace{4}_{12}\times\underbrace{5}_{15}\times\underbrace{2}_{18}\\\times\underbrace{7}_{21}\times\underbrace{8}_{24}\times\underbrace{1}_{27}\times\underbrace{1}_{30}\end{pmatrix}\pmod{9}. \end{aligned} \]

Rastav izraza \((32!)_3\bmod 9\) također se može podijeliti na tri dijela:

  • potpuni blokovi: umnožak svih cijelih brojeva u rasponu \(1\sim 9\) koji nisu djeljivi s \(3\), ukupno \(\lfloor 32/9\rfloor=3\) bloka;
  • nepotpuni blok na kraju: umnožak svih cijelih brojeva koji nisu djeljivi s \(3\) od \(1\) do \(32\bmod 9\);
  • umnožak svih cijelih brojeva djeljivih s \(3\); usporedbom s rezultatom nakon pretposljednjeg znaka jednakosti vidi se da je to upravo prvih \(\lfloor 32/3\rfloor=10\) članova, tj. \((\lfloor 32/3\rfloor!)_3\bmod 9\).

Dio u posljednjoj zagradi rješava se rekurzivno; time je početni problem sveden na manji problem.

Odatle se dobiva sljedeći rekurzivni rezultat:

Rekurzivni rezultat

Za prost broj \(p\) i prirodne brojeve \(\alpha,n\) vrijedi

\[ (n!)_p \equiv (\pm 1)^{\sum_{j\ge\alpha}\lfloor{n}/{p^j}\rfloor}\prod_{j\ge 0}F(\lfloor n/p^j\rfloor\bmod p^\alpha)\pmod{p^\alpha}, \]

gdje je \(F(m) = \prod_{1\le k\le m,\ k\perp p} k\bmod{p^\alpha}\), a vrijednost \(\pm 1\) određuje se kao gore.

Implementacija za slučaj modula koji je potencija prostog broja slična je onoj za slučaj prostog modula, razlikuje se samo u nekim detaljima. Kao i gore, pretprocesiranje se može izdvojiti izvan funkcije.

Referentna implementacija 
// Calculate (n!)_p mod pa.
int factmod(int n, int p, int pa) {
  // Pretreatment.
  std::vector<int> f(pa);
  f[0] = 1;
  for (int i = 1; i < pa; ++i) {
    f[i] = i % p ? (long long)f[i - 1] * i % pa : f[i - 1];
  }
  // Recursion.
  bool neg = p != 2 || pa <= 4;
  int res = 1;
  while (n > 1) {
    if ((n / pa) & neg) res = pa - res;
    res = (long long)res * f[n % pa] % pa;
    n /= p;
  }
  return res;
}

Vremenska složenost pretprocesiranja iznosi \(O(p^\alpha)\), a vremenska složenost jednog upita \(O(\log_p n)\).

Računanje eksponenta

U ovom se odjeljku razmatra računanje eksponenta \(\nu_p(n!)\) broja \(p\) u faktorijelu \(n!\), koje se može iskoristiti za računanje ostatka binomnih koeficijenata. Naime, u binomnom koeficijentu faktorijeli se pojavljuju i u brojniku i u nazivniku, pa to hoće li se prost broj \(p\) u brojniku i nazivniku međusobno pokratiti postaje ključan čimbenik koji određuje konačni ostatak.

Legendreova formula

Eksponent prostog broja \(p\) u faktorijelu \(n!\) može se izračunati Legendreovom formulom i povezan je s prikazom broja \(n\) u bazi \(p\).

Legendreova formula

Za prirodan broj \(n\) eksponent \(\nu_p(n!)\) prostog broja \(p\) sadržanog u faktorijelu \(n!\) iznosi

\[ \nu_p(n!) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{p^i} \right\rfloor = \dfrac{n-S_p(n)}{p-1}, \]

gdje je \(S_p(n)\) zbroj znamenki broja \(n\) u bazi \(p\). Posebno, eksponent broja \(2\) u faktorijelu iznosi \(\nu_2(n!)=n-S_2(n)\).

Dokaz

Budući da je

\[ n! = 1\times 2\times \cdots \times p\times \cdots \times 2p\times \cdots \times \lfloor n/p\rfloor p\times \cdots \times n. \]

Ovdje je umnožak višekratnika broja \(p\) jednak \(p\times 2p\times \cdots \times \lfloor n/p\rfloor p=p^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor }\lfloor n/p\rfloor !\), a u \(\lfloor n/p\rfloor !\) mogu se i dalje pojavljivati višekratnici broja \(p\). Stoga za eksponent vrijedi rekurzivna relacija:

\[ \nu_p(n!) = \lfloor n/p\rfloor + \nu_p(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!). \]

Njezinim raspisivanjem dobiva se Legendreova formula.

Da bismo dokazali drugu jednakost, najprije raspišimo \(n\) u bazi \(p\), što je isto kao zapisati ga kao sljedeću sumu:

\[ n = n_\ell p^{\ell} + \cdots + n_1 p + n_0 = \sum_{k=0}^\ell n_kp^k. \]

Stoga vrijedi

\[ \begin{aligned} \nu_p(n!) &= \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^i}\right\rfloor = \sum_{i=1}^\ell\sum_{k=i}^{\ell}n_kp^{k-i} = \sum_{k=1}^\ell n_k\sum_{i=1}^kp^{k-i} \\ &= \sum_{k=1}^\ell n_k\dfrac{p^k-1}{p-1} = \dfrac{\sum_{k=0}^\ell n_kp^k - \sum_{k=0}^\ell n_k}{p-1} = \dfrac{n - S_p(n)}{p-1}. \end{aligned} \]

Referentna implementacija računanja eksponenta prostog broja u faktorijelu:

Referentna implementacija 
// Obtain multiplicity of p in n!.
int multiplicity_factorial(int n, int p) {
  int count = 0;
  do {
    n /= p;
    count += n;
  } while (n);
  return count;
}

Njezina vremenska složenost iznosi \(O(\log n)\).

Kummerov teorem

Ostatci binomnih koeficijenata po nekom modulu često tvore fraktalnu strukturu; npr. trokut Sierpińskog može se dobiti iz binomnih koeficijenata po modulu \(2\).

Pažljivom analizom vidi se da je pitanje dijeli li \(p\) binomni koeficijent zapravo povezano s time je li pri oduzimanju gornjeg i donjeg indeksa u bazi \(p\) potrebno posuđivanje. Odatle dolazi Kummerov teorem.

Kummerov teorem

Eksponent prostog broja \(p\) u binomnom koeficijentu \(\dbinom{m}{n}\) jednak je upravo broju posuđivanja potrebnih pri oduzimanju \(n\) od \(m\) u bazi \(p\), tj.

\[ \nu_p\left(\dbinom{m}{n}\right)=\frac{S_p(n)+S_p(m-n)-S_p(m)}{p-1}. \]

Posebno, eksponent broja \(2\) u binomnom koeficijentu iznosi \(\nu_2\left(\dbinom{m}{n}\right)=S_2(n)+S_2(m-n)-S_2(m)\).

Dokaz

Najprije dokažimo donji izraz. Prema Legendreovoj formuli vrijedi

\[ \begin{aligned} \nu_p\left(\dbinom{m}{n}\right) &=\nu_p(m!)-\nu_p(n!)-\nu_p((m-n)!)\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^\infty\left(\left\lfloor\dfrac{m}{p^i}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^i}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{m-n}{p^i}\right\rfloor\right)\\ &=\frac{S_p(n)+S_p(m-n)-S_p(m)}{p-1}. \end{aligned} \]

Taj se izraz može shvatiti kao broj posuđivanja potrebnih pri oduzimanju \(n\) od \(m\) u bazi \(p\). Naime, ako pri računanju \(i\)-te znamenke (najniža znamenka ima indeks \(1\)) oduzimanje nije moguće pa je potrebno posuđivanje, tada je broj koji u rezultatu oduzimanja čine znamenke iznad \(i\)-te, \(\left\lfloor\dfrac{m-n}{p^i}\right\rfloor\), zapravo razlika koja se dobije kada se od broja koji čine znamenke iznad \(i\)-te u \(m\), \(\left\lfloor\dfrac{m}{p^i}\right\rfloor\), oduzme jedan (tj. posuđena jedinica) i zatim oduzme broj koji čine znamenke iznad \(i\)-te u \(n\), \(\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^i}\right\rfloor\); stoga je razlika

\[ \left\lfloor\dfrac{m}{p^i}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^i}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{m-n}{p^i}\right\rfloor = 1 \]

ako i samo ako je došlo do posuđivanja; inače je ta razlika \(0\). Stoga se suma u gornjem izrazu može shvatiti kao broj posuđivanja. Time je dobivena riječima iskazana formulacija Kummerova teorema.

Introduction

This article discusses results related to computing factorials modulo a given number and provides a computation method whose time complexity is linear in the size of the modulus, so the method is mainly suitable when the modulus is not too large (\(\sim 10^6\)). Besides the method described in this article, depending on the situation, polynomial techniques can also be applied for fast computation.

By the Chinese remainder theorem, the problem of computing a factorial modulo a number can be reduced to the case where the modulus is a prime power \(p^\alpha\). When dealing with such problems, for a prime \(p\) and a positive integer \(n\) one often needs to extract all factors \(p\) from the factorial \(n!\), obtaining the decomposition:

\[ n! = p^{\nu_p(n!)}(n!)_p. \]

Here, \(\nu_p(n!)\) denotes the exponent of \(p\) in the prime factorization of the factorial \(n!\), and \((n!)_p\) denotes the integer obtained by removing all powers of \(p\) from the value of the factorial \(n!\). This article discusses the remainder of \((n!)_p\) modulo a prime (power) and the concrete way to compute the exponent \(\nu_p(n!)\).

This decomposition is especially useful when solving problems in which factorials appear simultaneously in the numerator and the denominator of the desired expression, for example when computing binomial coefficients modulo a given number. For such problems, the exponents of \(p\) in the numerator and the denominator can be subtracted directly, while the parts \((n!)_p\) coprime to \(p\) can be computed using the modular inverse.

This article also presents Wilson's theorem and its generalization, Legendre's formula and Kummer's theorem, which are related to the above problem.

Wilson's theorem

Wilson's theorem gives a necessary and sufficient condition for a natural number to be prime.

Wilson's theorem

For a natural number \(n>1\), \((n-1)!\equiv -1\pmod n\) holds if and only if \(n\) is prime.

Proof

First, we prove that for a prime \(p\) we have \((p-1)!\equiv -1\pmod{p}\). For this, two concise proofs can be obtained using congruences or primitive roots; we omit them here. Below we give a proof that requires fewer prerequisites:

For \(p=2\) the statement obviously holds. Now let \(p\geq 3\); we need to prove that the product of all nonzero elements (i.e. residue classes) of \(\mathbf{Z}_p\) equals \(\overline{-1}\). Since every nonzero element \(\overline{a}\) of \(\mathbf{Z}_p\) has an inverse \(\overline{a}^{-1}\), the product of mutually inverse elements of \(\mathbf{Z}_p\) equals \(\overline{1}\). However, note that \(\overline{a}\) and \(\overline{a}^{-1}\) may be equal: \(\overline{a}=\overline{a}^{-1}\) if and only if \(a^2\equiv 1\pmod p\), i.e.

\[ 0\equiv a^2-1\equiv (a+1)(a-1)\pmod p, \]

hence \(a\equiv 1\pmod p\) or \(a\equiv -1\pmod p\). This shows that the product of all elements of \(\mathbf{Z}_p\setminus\{\overline{0},\overline{1},\overline{-1}\}\) equals \(\overline{1}\), and therefore the product of all nonzero elements of \(\mathbf{Z}_p\) equals \(\overline{-1}\).

Conversely, for a composite \(n\) we need to prove that \((n-1)!\not\equiv-1\pmod{n}\). We argue by contradiction: suppose \((n-1)!\equiv -1\pmod{n}\), i.e. there exists an integer \(k\) such that \((n-1)!=kn-1\). Since \(n\) is composite, there necessarily exists a prime \(p<n\) such that \(n=pm\), so \((n-1)!=kpm-1\equiv -1\pmod{p}\). However, \(p\) necessarily already appears in the product \((n-1)!\), so we must have \((n-1)!\equiv 0\pmod{p}\). This contradiction shows that \((n-1)!\not\equiv-1\pmod{n}\).

In the notation of this article, Wilson's theorem can be written as \((p!)_p\equiv -1\pmod{p}\).

Generalization

Wilson's theorem can be generalized to the case of a general modulus.

Theorem (Gauss)

For a natural number \(m>1\), we have

\[ \prod_{1\le k<m,\ k\perp m} k \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{m}. \]

Moreover, the remainder \(\pm 1\) equals \(-1\) if and only if a primitive root exists modulo \(m\), i.e. when \(m=2,4,p^\alpha,2p^\alpha\), where \(p\) is an odd prime and \(\alpha\) is a positive integer.

Proof

This theorem can be proved simply via the structure of the multiplicative group of integers modulo \(m\). Here we give a proof with a similar idea but more elementary.

For \(m=2\), we have \(1!=1\equiv -1\pmod{2}\). For the other cases where a primitive root exists, let \(g\) be a primitive root; then every positive integer \(k\) less than \(m\) and coprime to it can be uniquely written in the form \(g^i\bmod m\), where \(0\le i<\varphi(m)\) and \(\varphi(m)\) is Euler's totient function. By direct verification, \(\varphi(m)\) is necessarily even. Since \(g^i\) and \(g^{\varphi(m)-i}\) are multiplicative inverses of each other, pairing them up in the product gives

\[ \prod_{1\le k<m,\ k\perp m} k \equiv \prod_{i=0}^{\varphi(m)-1}g^i = g^{\varphi(m)/2}\prod_{i=1}^{\varphi(m)/2-1}g^{i}g^{\varphi(m)-i} \equiv g^{\varphi(m)/2} \pmod{m}. \]

Since \(g^{\varphi(m)/2}\bmod m\) is the only element different from \(1\bmod{m}\) that is its own multiplicative inverse, it equals \(-1\bmod{m}\). This shows that the remainder in this case equals \(-1\).

For the case where no primitive root exists modulo \(m\), we need to prove that the remainder equals \(1\). To this end, first take the prime factorization \(m=p_1^{e_1}p_2^{e_2}\cdots p_s^{e_s}\), and then by the Chinese remainder theorem it suffices to prove that

\[ \prod_{1\le k<m,\ k\perp m} k\equiv 1\pmod{p_j^{e_j}} \]

holds for all factors \(p_j^{e_j}\). The Chinese remainder theorem states that every possible combination of remainders \((r_1,r_2,\cdots,r_s)\), where \(1\le r_j<p_j^{e_j}\) and \(p_j\perp r_j\), corresponds uniquely to one number with \(1\le k<m\) and \(k\perp m\) such that \(k\equiv r_j\pmod{p_j^{e_j}}\). Therefore, for every remainder \(r_j\) there are exactly \({\varphi(m)}/{\varphi(p_j^{e_j})}\) values of \(k\) such that \(k\equiv r_j\pmod{p_j^{e_j}}\). Using this, we can group the product and obtain

\[ \prod_{1\le k<m,\ k\perp m} k\equiv\left(\prod_{1\le r_j<p_j^{e_j},\ r_j\perp p_j} r_j\right)^{{\varphi(m)}/{\varphi(p_j^{e_j})}}\pmod{p_j^{e_j}}. \]

For the exponent \({\varphi(m)}/{\varphi(p_j^{e_j})}=\varphi(m/p_j^{e_j})\) here to be odd, we must have \(m/p_j^{e_j}=1,2\), because Euler's totient function \(\varphi(n)\) is even for all \(n\ge 3\). If \(p_j\) is an odd prime, since no primitive root exists modulo \(m\), necessarily \(m/p_j^{e_j}\neq 1,2\); if \(p_j^{e_j}=2,4\), since no primitive root exists modulo \(m\), \(m/p_j^{e_j}\) necessarily contains some odd prime factor and is therefore greater than \(2\): in both cases the exponent \({\varphi(m)}/{\varphi(p_j^{e_j})}\) is even. The term in parentheses in the formula above has already been shown to be congruent to \(-1\) modulo \(p_j^{e_j}\), so the remainder of this power modulo \(p_j^{e_j}\) must be \(1\). The only remaining case is \(p_j=2\) and \(e_j>2\), for which we can prove directly:

\[ \prod_{1\le r_j<2^{e_j},\ r_j\perp 2}r_j \equiv 1\pmod{2^{e_j}}. \]

Following the idea of the previous proof, all odd numbers \(r_j\) with \(1\le r_j<2^{e_j}\) can be paired up so that they cancel; those that cannot be paired are necessarily solutions of the equation \(x^2\equiv 1\pmod{2^{e_j}}\). This equation means \(2^{e_j}\mid (x-1)(x+1)\). Let \(x=2y+1\); then necessarily \(2^{e_j-2}\mid y(y+1)\), and since one of \(y\) and \(y+1\) is odd and the other even, we get \(y=t2^{e_j-2}\) or \(y=t2^{e_j-2}-1\). Hence \(x=t2^{e_j-1}\pm 1\) where \(t\) is an integer. Among the remainders modulo \(2^{e_j}\) there are only four such: \(\pm 1\) and \(2^{e_j-1}\pm 1\). Therefore,

\[ \prod_{1\le r_j<2^{e_j},\ r_j\perp 2}r_j \equiv (-1)(2^{e_j-1}-1)(2^{e_j-1}+1) \equiv 1\pmod{2^{e_j}}. \]

This completes the proof for all cases.

In computations, the case where the modulus is a prime power is especially important:

Corollary

For a prime \(p\) and a positive integer \(\alpha\), we have

\[ \prod_{1\le k<p^\alpha,\ k\perp p}k \equiv \begin{cases} 1, & p=2\text{ and }\alpha\ge3,\\ -1, &\text{otherwise} \end{cases} \pmod{p^\alpha}. \]

Note that the left-hand side is not \((p^\alpha!)_p\), because the latter also includes the contribution of the multiples of \(p\).

Computing the factorial remainder

This section discusses the computation of the remainder \((n!)_p\bmod p^{\alpha}\).

The prime modulus case

The expression \((n!)_p\) has an obvious recursive structure. To notice it, let us first look at a concrete example:

Example

To compute \((32!)_5 \bmod{5}\), we can perform the following recursive computation:

\[ \begin{aligned} (32!)_5 &= 1\times 2\times 3 \times 4\times \underbrace{1}_{5}\times 6 \times 7 \times 8\times 9 \times \underbrace{2}_{10} \\ &\quad\times 11 \times 12 \times 13 \times 14\times \underbrace{3}_{15}\times 16 \times 17\times 18\times 19\times \underbrace{4}_{20} \\ &\quad\times 21 \times 22 \times 23 \times 24\times \underbrace{1}_{25}\times 26 \times 27\times 28\times 29\times \underbrace{6}_{30} \times 31 \times 32 \\ &\equiv 1\times 2\times 3 \times 4\times \underbrace{1}_{5}\times 1 \times 2 \times 3\times 4 \times \underbrace{2}_{10} \\ &\quad\times 1 \times 2 \times 3 \times 4\times \underbrace{3}_{15}\times 1 \times 2\times 3\times 4\times \underbrace{4}_{20} \\ &\quad\times 1 \times 2 \times 3 \times 4\times \underbrace{1}_{25}\times 1 \times 2\times 3\times 4\times \underbrace{1}_{30}\times 1\times 2\\ &= (1\times 2\times 3\times 4)^{6}\times(1\times 2)\times(\underbrace{1}_{5}\times \underbrace{2}_{10}\times \underbrace{3}_{15} \times \underbrace{4}_{20}\times \underbrace{1}_{25}\times \underbrace{1}_{30}) \pmod{5} \end{aligned} \]

We can see that, thanks to the periodicity of remainders modulo \(5\), this product can be divided into several blocks of length \(5\), and the only difference between the blocks is the remainder of the last element. Since dividing \(32\) by \(5\) gives quotient \(6\) and remainder \(2\), the product can be divided into \(6\) complete blocks and a final incomplete block of length \(2\). Therefore, we can extract from the first \(6\) blocks the parts without their last elements (this part is exactly what Wilson's theorem handles), then multiply by the product of the last incomplete block, and finally multiply by the product of the last elements of the first \(6\) blocks. The last element of each block is a multiple of \(5\), so after removing the powers of \(5\), their product is exactly \((6!)_{5}\pmod{5}\). This reduces the original problem to a problem of smaller size.

Generalizing the recursive structure of this example, we obtain the following recurrence:

Recurrence

For a prime \(p\) and a positive integer \(n\), we have

\[ (n!)_p \equiv (-1)^{\left\lfloor n/p\right\rfloor}\cdot (n\bmod p)!\cdot\left(\left\lfloor n/p\right\rfloor!\right)_p\pmod{p}. \]
Proof

Let \((n)_p\) denote the result of removing all powers of \(p\) from the prime factorization of \(n\). Then we have

\[ \begin{aligned} (n!)_p &= \prod_{k=1}^n(k)_p = \left(\prod_{1\le k\le n,\ k\perp p}(k)_p\right)\left(\prod_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/p\rfloor}(pk)_p\right) \\ &= \left(\prod_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor-1}\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}(ip+j)\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n\bmod p}(\lfloor n/p\rfloor p+j)\right)\left(\prod_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/p\rfloor}(k)_p\right) \\ &\equiv\left(\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}j\right)^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n\bmod p}j\right)(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p \\ &\equiv (-1)^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}\cdot(n\bmod p)!\cdot(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p \pmod p. \end{aligned} \]

This completes the proof. Below we give a concrete explanation of this formal proof.

We want to compute the value of \((n!)_p\bmod p\). Following the example above, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} (n!)_p &= 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot \underbrace{1}_{p} \cdot (p+1) \cdot (p+2) \cdot \ldots \cdot (2p-1) \cdot \underbrace{2}_{2p} \\ &\quad \cdot (2p+1) \cdot \ldots \cdot (p^2-1) \cdot \underbrace{1}_{p^2} \cdot (p^2 +1) \cdot \ldots \cdot n \\ &\equiv 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot \underbrace{1}_{p} \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-1) \cdot \underbrace{2}_{2p} \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \\ &\quad \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-1) \cdot \underbrace{1}_{p^2} \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot \ldots \cdot (n \bmod p) \pmod{p}. \end{aligned} \]

It can be clearly seen that, except for the last block, the factorial is divided into several complete blocks of the same length.

\[ \begin{aligned} (n!)_p&\equiv \underbrace{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot 1}_{1\text{st}} \cdot \underbrace{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot 2}_{2\text{nd}} \cdot \ldots \\ &\quad \cdot \underbrace{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \ldots \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1) \cdot 1}_{p\text{th}} \cdot \ldots \cdot \quad \underbrace{1 \cdot 2 \cdot \ldots \cdot (n \bmod p)}_{\text{tail}} \pmod{p}. \end{aligned} \]

The main part of a complete block, excluding its last element, i.e. \((p-1)!\bmod p\), is easy to compute: Wilson's theorem applies:

\[ (p-1)!\equiv -1\pmod p. \]

There are \(\left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{p} \right\rfloor\) complete blocks in total, so \(\left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{p} \right\rfloor\) has to be put into the exponent of \(-1\).

The value of the last, partial block is \((n\bmod p)!\bmod p\) and can be computed separately.

What remains are the last elements of each block. If we hide the already processed elements, we can see the following pattern:

\[ (n!)_p = \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 1 } \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 2} \cdot \ldots \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot (p-1)} \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 1 } \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 1} \cdot \underbrace{ \ldots \cdot 2} \cdots \]

This is also a modified factorial, only of much shorter length. It is:

\[ \left(\left\lfloor \frac{n}{p} \right\rfloor !\right)_p. \]

Multiplying all the parts together, we obtain the recurrence above.

When computing with this recurrence, the recursion depth is \(O(\log_p n)\). If the middle term is recomputed every time, the computation at each level costs \(O(p)\), and the total time complexity is \(O(p\log_p n)\); if \(n!\bmod p\) is precomputed for all \(n=1,2,\cdots,p-1\), the precomputation costs \(O(p)\), the computation at each level costs \(O(1)\), and the total time complexity is \(O(p+\log_p n)\).

Since the recursion is a tail recursion, the implementation can be iterative. The following implementation precomputes the first \(p-1\) factorials; if the function needs to be called multiple times, the precomputation can be moved outside the function.

Reference implementation 
// Calculate (n!)_p mod p.
int factmod(int n, int p) {
  // Pretreatment.
  std::vector<int> f(p);
  f[0] = 1;
  for (int i = 1; i < p; ++i) {
    f[i] = (long long)f[i - 1] * i % p;
  }
  // Recursion.
  int res = 1;
  while (n > 1) {
    if ((n / p) & 1) res = p - res;
    res = (long long)res * f[n % p] % p;
    n /= p;
  }
  return res;
}

If memory is limited and not all factorials can be stored, one can also compute all the values \(n\) of the factorials \(n!\bmod p\) actually used in the function calls, then sort them, and finally compute the values of these factorials all at once and combine them into the final result, thereby avoiding storing the values of all factorials.

The prime power modulus case

The case of a prime power modulus can be solved following the prime modulus case; it suffices to replace Wilson's theorem by its generalization. In both results of this section, \(\pm 1\) refers specifically to the following definition: for \(p=2\) and \(\alpha\ge 3\) it is \(1\), and in all other cases it is \(-1\).

Recurrence

For a prime \(p\) and positive integers \(\alpha,n\), we have

\[ (n!)_{p} \equiv (\pm 1)^{\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor}\cdot\left(\prod_{1\le j\le (n\bmod p^\alpha),\ j\perp p}j\right)\cdot(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p\pmod{p^\alpha}. \]

Here, the value of \(\pm 1\) is as specified in the generalization of Wilson's theorem.

Proof

The idea of the proof is exactly the same as in the prime modulus case. Let \((k)_p\) denote the result of removing all powers of \(p\) from the prime factorization of \(k\); then

\[ \begin{aligned} (n!)_p &= \prod_{1\le k\le n}(k)_p = \left(\prod_{1\le k\le n,\ k\perp p}(k)_p\right)\left(\prod_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/p\rfloor}(pk)_p\right) \\ &= \left(\prod_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor-1}\prod_{1\le j\le p^\alpha,\ j\perp p}(ip^\alpha+j)_p\right)\left(\prod_{1\le j\le (n\bmod p^\alpha),\ j\perp p}(\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor p^\alpha+j)_p\right)\left(\prod_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/p\rfloor}(k)_p\right)\\ &\equiv \left(\prod_{1\le j\le p^\alpha,\ j\perp p}j\right)^{\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor}\cdot\left(\prod_{1\le j\le (n\bmod p^\alpha),\ j\perp p}j\right)\cdot(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p\\ &\equiv (\pm 1)^{\lfloor n/p^\alpha\rfloor}\cdot\left(\prod_{1\le j\le (n\bmod p^\alpha),\ j\perp p}j\right)\cdot(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!)_p \pmod{p^\alpha}. \end{aligned} \]

Apart from the fact that \(-1\) may need to be replaced by \(\pm 1\), the difference from the prime modulus case also lies in the data being precomputed. For the prime power modulus case, for all positive integers \(n\) not exceeding \(p^\alpha\) we need to precompute the product of all integers from \(1\) to \(n\) that are not multiples of \(p\), i.e.

\[ \prod_{1\le k\le n,\ k\perp p} k\bmod{p^\alpha}. \]

In the prime modulus case, for \(n<p\), this degenerates to \(n!\bmod p\), but this expression no longer applies in the general prime power case.

Below we give an example of computing the factorial remainder in the prime power modulus case, to help understand the method above:

Example

To compute \((32!)_3\bmod 9\), we can perform the following recursive computation:

\[ \begin{aligned} (32!)_3 &= 1\times 2\times \underbrace{1}_{3} \times 4\times 5\times \underbrace{2}_{6}\times 7\times 8\times\underbrace{1}_{9}\\ &\quad\times 10\times 11\times\underbrace{4}_{12}\times 13\times 14\times\underbrace{5}_{15}\times 16\times 17\times\underbrace{2}_{18}\\ &\quad\times 19\times 20\times\underbrace{7}_{21}\times 22\times 23\times\underbrace{8}_{24}\times 25\times 26\times\underbrace{1}_{27}\\ &\quad\times 28\times 29\times\underbrace{10}_{30}\times 31\times 32\\ &\equiv 1\times 2\times \underbrace{1}_{3} \times 4\times 5\times \underbrace{2}_{6}\times 7\times 8\times\underbrace{1}_{9}\\ &\quad\times 1\times 2\times\underbrace{4}_{12}\times 4\times 5\times\underbrace{5}_{15}\times 7\times 8\times\underbrace{2}_{18}\\ &\quad\times 1\times 2\times\underbrace{7}_{21}\times 4\times 5\times\underbrace{8}_{24}\times 7\times 8\times\underbrace{1}_{27}\\ &\quad\times 1\times 2\times\underbrace{1}_{30}\times 4\times 5\\ &=(1\times 2\times 4\times 5\times 7\times 8)^{3}\times (1\times 2\times 4\times 5)\\ &\quad\times\begin{pmatrix}\underbrace{1}_{3}\times\underbrace{2}_{6}\times\underbrace{1}_{9}\times\underbrace{4}_{12}\times\underbrace{5}_{15}\times\underbrace{2}_{18}\\\times\underbrace{7}_{21}\times\underbrace{8}_{24}\times\underbrace{1}_{27}\times\underbrace{1}_{30}\end{pmatrix}\pmod{9}. \end{aligned} \]

The result of decomposing the expression \((32!)_3\bmod 9\) can likewise be divided into three parts:

  • complete blocks: the product of all integers in the range \(1\sim 9\) not divisible by \(3\), \(\lfloor 32/9\rfloor=3\) blocks in total;
  • the incomplete tail block: the product of all integers not divisible by \(3\) from \(1\) up to \(32\bmod 9\);
  • the product of all integers divisible by \(3\); comparing with the result after the second-to-last equals sign, we see that this is exactly its first \(\lfloor 32/3\rfloor=10\) terms, i.e. \((\lfloor 32/3\rfloor!)_3\bmod 9\).

The part in the last parentheses is solved recursively; this reduces the original problem to a smaller one.

From this we obtain the following recursive result:

Recursive result

For a prime \(p\) and positive integers \(\alpha,n\), we have

\[ (n!)_p \equiv (\pm 1)^{\sum_{j\ge\alpha}\lfloor{n}/{p^j}\rfloor}\prod_{j\ge 0}F(\lfloor n/p^j\rfloor\bmod p^\alpha)\pmod{p^\alpha}, \]

where \(F(m) = \prod_{1\le k\le m,\ k\perp p} k\bmod{p^\alpha}\) and the value of \(\pm 1\) is as described above.

The implementation for the prime power modulus case is similar to that for the prime modulus case, differing only in some details. As above, the precomputation can be moved outside the function.

Reference implementation 
// Calculate (n!)_p mod pa.
int factmod(int n, int p, int pa) {
  // Pretreatment.
  std::vector<int> f(pa);
  f[0] = 1;
  for (int i = 1; i < pa; ++i) {
    f[i] = i % p ? (long long)f[i - 1] * i % pa : f[i - 1];
  }
  // Recursion.
  bool neg = p != 2 || pa <= 4;
  int res = 1;
  while (n > 1) {
    if ((n / pa) & neg) res = pa - res;
    res = (long long)res * f[n % pa] % pa;
    n /= p;
  }
  return res;
}

The time complexity of the precomputation is \(O(p^\alpha)\), and the time complexity of a single query is \(O(\log_p n)\).

Computing the multiplicity

This section discusses the computation of the exponent \(\nu_p(n!)\) of \(p\) in the factorial \(n!\), which can be used to compute remainders of binomial coefficients. In a binomial coefficient, factorials appear in both the numerator and the denominator, so whether the prime \(p\) in the numerator and the denominator cancels out becomes a key factor determining the final remainder.

Legendre's formula

The exponent of a prime \(p\) in the factorial \(n!\) can be computed by Legendre's formula, and it is related to the representation of \(n\) in base \(p\).

Legendre's formula

For a positive integer \(n\), the exponent \(\nu_p(n!)\) of the prime \(p\) contained in the factorial \(n!\) is

\[ \nu_p(n!) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left\lfloor \dfrac{n}{p^i} \right\rfloor = \dfrac{n-S_p(n)}{p-1}, \]

where \(S_p(n)\) is the sum of the digits of \(n\) in base \(p\). In particular, the exponent of \(2\) in the factorial is \(\nu_2(n!)=n-S_2(n)\).

Proof

Because

\[ n! = 1\times 2\times \cdots \times p\times \cdots \times 2p\times \cdots \times \lfloor n/p\rfloor p\times \cdots \times n. \]

Here, the product of the multiples of \(p\) is \(p\times 2p\times \cdots \times \lfloor n/p\rfloor p=p^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor }\lfloor n/p\rfloor !\), and \(\lfloor n/p\rfloor !\) may continue to contain multiples of \(p\). Therefore, for the exponent we have the recurrence relation:

\[ \nu_p(n!) = \lfloor n/p\rfloor + \nu_p(\lfloor n/p\rfloor!). \]

Expanding it gives Legendre's formula.

To prove the second equality, first expand \(n\) in base \(p\), which amounts to writing it as the following sum:

\[ n = n_\ell p^{\ell} + \cdots + n_1 p + n_0 = \sum_{k=0}^\ell n_kp^k. \]

Therefore, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \nu_p(n!) &= \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^i}\right\rfloor = \sum_{i=1}^\ell\sum_{k=i}^{\ell}n_kp^{k-i} = \sum_{k=1}^\ell n_k\sum_{i=1}^kp^{k-i} \\ &= \sum_{k=1}^\ell n_k\dfrac{p^k-1}{p-1} = \dfrac{\sum_{k=0}^\ell n_kp^k - \sum_{k=0}^\ell n_k}{p-1} = \dfrac{n - S_p(n)}{p-1}. \end{aligned} \]

A reference implementation for computing the exponent of a prime in a factorial is as follows:

Reference implementation 
// Obtain multiplicity of p in n!.
int multiplicity_factorial(int n, int p) {
  int count = 0;
  do {
    n /= p;
    count += n;
  } while (n);
  return count;
}

Its time complexity is \(O(\log n)\).

Kummer's theorem

The remainders of binomial coefficients modulo a number often form a fractal structure; for example, the Sierpiński triangle can be obtained from binomial coefficients modulo \(2\).

A careful analysis shows that whether \(p\) divides a binomial coefficient is actually related to whether borrowing is needed when subtracting the lower index from the upper index in base \(p\). This gives Kummer's theorem.

Kummer's theorem

The exponent of a prime \(p\) in the binomial coefficient \(\dbinom{m}{n}\) is exactly the number of borrows needed when subtracting \(n\) from \(m\) in base \(p\), i.e.

\[ \nu_p\left(\dbinom{m}{n}\right)=\frac{S_p(n)+S_p(m-n)-S_p(m)}{p-1}. \]

In particular, the exponent of \(2\) in the binomial coefficient is \(\nu_2\left(\dbinom{m}{n}\right)=S_2(n)+S_2(m-n)-S_2(m)\).

Proof

First we prove the expression below. For this, by Legendre's formula we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \nu_p\left(\dbinom{m}{n}\right) &=\nu_p(m!)-\nu_p(n!)-\nu_p((m-n)!)\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^\infty\left(\left\lfloor\dfrac{m}{p^i}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^i}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{m-n}{p^i}\right\rfloor\right)\\ &=\frac{S_p(n)+S_p(m-n)-S_p(m)}{p-1}. \end{aligned} \]

This expression can be understood as the number of borrows needed when subtracting \(n\) from \(m\) in base \(p\). Indeed, if when computing the \(i\)-th digit (the lowest digit has index \(1\)) the subtraction is not possible and a borrow is needed, then the number formed by the digits above the \(i\)-th in the result of the subtraction, \(\left\lfloor\dfrac{m-n}{p^i}\right\rfloor\), is actually the difference obtained by taking the number formed by the digits above the \(i\)-th in \(m\), \(\left\lfloor\dfrac{m}{p^i}\right\rfloor\), subtracting one (namely the borrowed one), and then subtracting the number formed by the digits above the \(i\)-th in \(n\), \(\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^i}\right\rfloor\); therefore, the difference

\[ \left\lfloor\dfrac{m}{p^i}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^i}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{m-n}{p^i}\right\rfloor = 1 \]

if and only if a borrow occurred; otherwise, this difference is \(0\). Therefore, the sum in the expression above can be understood as the number of borrows. This gives the verbal formulation of Kummer's theorem.

Example problem

Example problem HDU 2973 - YAPTCHA

Given \(n\), compute

\[ \sum_{k=1}^n\left\lfloor\frac{(3k+6)!+1}{3k+7}-\left\lfloor\frac{(3k+6)!}{3k+7}\right\rfloor\right\rfloor \]
Solution idea

If \(3k+7\) is prime, then

\[ (3k+6)!\equiv-1\pmod{3k+7} \]

Let \((3k+6)!+1=m(3k+7)\)

Then

\[ \left\lfloor\frac{(3k+6)!+1}{3k+7}-\left\lfloor\frac{(3k+6)!}{3k+7}\right\rfloor\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor m-\left\lfloor m-\frac{1}{3k+7}\right\rfloor\right\rfloor=1 \]

If \(3k+7\) is not prime, then it is a composite number greater than \(4\), and we have \((3k+7)\mid(3k+6)!\), i.e.

\[ (3k+6)!\equiv 0\pmod{3k+7} \]

Let \((3k+6)!=m(3k+7)\); then

\[ \left\lfloor\frac{(3k+6)!+1}{3k+7}-\left\lfloor\frac{(3k+6)!}{3k+7}\right\rfloor\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor m+\frac{1}{3k+7}-m\right\rfloor=0 \]

Therefore

\[ \sum_{k=1}^n\left\lfloor\frac{(3k+6)!+1}{3k+7}-\left\lfloor\frac{(3k+6)!}{3k+7}\right\rfloor\right\rfloor=\sum_{k=1}^n[3k+7\text{ is prime}] \]
Reference code 
#include <iostream>

constexpr int M = 1e6 + 5, N = 3 * M + 7;

bool not_prime[N];
int sum[M];

int main() {
  for (int i = 2; i < N; ++i)
    if (!not_prime[i])
      for (int j = 2; j * i < N; ++j) not_prime[j * i] = true;
  for (int i = 1; i < M; ++i) sum[i] = sum[i - 1] + !not_prime[3 * i + 7];

  int t;
  std::cin >> t;
  while (t--) {
    int n;
    std::cin >> n;
    std::cout << sum[n] << std::endl;
  }
}
