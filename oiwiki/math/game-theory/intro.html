Teorija igara (game theory) grana je ekonomije koja uglavnom proučava ponašanje pojedinaca u natjecateljskim ili suparničkim odnosima pod određenim pravilima. Teorija igara bavi se očekivanim i stvarnim ponašanjem sudionika u igri te proučava njihove optimalne strategije.

Jednostavno rečeno, teorija igara uglavnom proučava sljedeće: kako više igrača koji sudjeluju u nekoj igri bira svoje strategije.

Osnovni pojmovi

U ovom odjeljku kratko predstavljamo neke uobičajene pojmove iz teorije igara.

Kooperativne i nekooperativne igre

Kooperativna igra (cooperative game) jest igra u kojoj sudionici mogu sklapati saveze i međusobno surađivati. U takvim igrama nekooperativno ponašanje pojedinca obično kažnjava neki vanjski mehanizam. Nasuprot tome, u nekooperativnoj igri (noncooperative game) takav mehanizam ne postoji, pa sudionici ili uopće ne mogu sklapati saveze ili suradnju mogu održavati samo pomoću vjerodostojnih prijetnji.

U usporedbi s kooperativnim igrama, nekooperativne igre proučene su sustavnije i zrelije. Sve igre o kojima se govori u ovom članku nekooperativne su igre.

Simetrične i nesimetrične igre

U simetričnoj igri (symmetric game) različiti sudionici za isto ponašanje dobivaju istu isplatu, odnosno isplata ovisi samo o samom potezu, a ne o identitetu onoga tko ga povlači. Igra koja ne zadovoljava taj uvjet naziva se nesimetrična igra (asymmetric game).

Igre nulte i nenulte sume

Glavna stranica: Igre nulte sume

Igra nulte sume (zero-sum game) igra je u kojoj je zbroj isplata svih sudionika uvijek jednak nuli, bez obzira na to kako se strane ponašaju. Igre nulte sume o kojima se obično govori imaju dva sudionika; tada je dobitak jedne strane nužno gubitak druge. Nasuprot tome, igra nenulte sume (non-zero-sum game) dopušta da više strana zajedno dobije ili zajedno izgubi; tu spadaju igre pozitivne sume (positive-sum game), igre negativne sume (negative-sum game) i dr.

Simultane i sekvencijalne igre

U simultanoj igri (simultaneous game) svi sudionici odlučuju istodobno, ne znajući što su odabrali drugi. Tipičan primjer simultane igre jest kamen-škare-papir. Takve se igre često prikazuju matricom isplata i u pravilu ne uključuju pojam vremena.

Nasuprot tome stoji sekvencijalna igra (sequential game), u kojoj sudionici povlače poteze jedan za drugim. Valja napomenuti da onaj tko igra kasnije mora moći vidjeti barem dio poteza onih koji su igrali prije njega, inače redoslijed poteza ne bi imao nikakva smisla. Sekvencijalne igre obično se opisuju stablom igre.

Igre sa savršenom i nesavršenom informacijom

Savršena informacija (perfect information) znači da sudionik u trenutku donošenja bilo koje odluke u potpunosti poznaje sve što se dotad dogodilo, uključujući početno stanje igre. Primjerice, šah i go igre su sa savršenom informacijom, dok su mahjong i poker igre s nesavršenom informacijom jer igrači ne znaju koje karte (pločice) drže drugi. Savršena informacija obično se koristi za opis sekvencijalnih igara; budući da u simultanoj igri igrači ne mogu znati koji će potez protivnik upravo povući, obično se smatra da simultane igre nisu igre sa savršenom informacijom.

Igre s potpunom i nepotpunom informacijom

Potpuna informacija (complete information) znači da svi sudionici u potpunosti poznaju samu strukturu igre (uključujući dostupne odluke svih strana i konačne isplate) i da je ta informacija opće znanje (common knowledge). Nasuprot tome stoje igre s nepotpunom informacijom, u kojima su neki elementi igre (npr. protivnikove dostupne odluke ili funkcija isplate) sudionicima nepoznati.

Valja istaknuti da su „potpuna informacija” i „savršena informacija” dva nezavisna pojma koji jedan drugoga ne uključuju. Primjerice, mahjong je igra s potpunom, ali nesavršenom informacijom, jer su pravila i isplate javni, ali pločice nisu vidljive; s druge strane, neke igre sa skrivenim ciljevima, ali potpuno javnim potezima, igre su sa savršenom, ali nepotpunom informacijom.

Kombinatorna teorija igara

U algoritamskim natjecanjima najčešća je vrsta igre kombinatorna igra (combinatorial game). Taj se naziv obično odnosi na igre koje je teško riješiti zbog golemog broja stanja. Upravo zato što su opće kombinatorne igre prilično složene, kombinatorna teorija igara uglavnom se bavi sljedećom vrstom igara: igrama za dva igrača koji naizmjence povlače poteze, sa savršenom informacijom i bez slučajnih elemenata. Šah, go i slične igre tipične su kombinatorne igre.

Nepristrane kombinatorne igre

Glavna stranica: Nepristrane kombinatorne igre

Nepristrana igra (impartial game) kombinatorna je igra koja zadovoljava sljedeće uvjete:

  • u svakom određenom stanju svi sudionici imaju na raspolaganju potpuno iste poteze, koji ovise samo o trenutačnom stanju, a ne o identitetu igrača;
  • isto stanje igre ne može se dosegnuti više puta, igra završava kad sudionik ne može povući potez, a igra uvijek završava nakon konačno mnogo poteza i to bez neriješenog ishoda.

Nepristrane igre uvijek su simetrične igre.

Pristrane kombinatorne igre

Glavna stranica: Pristrane kombinatorne igre

Pojam suprotan nepristranoj igri jest pristrana (partizanska) igra (partizan game), u kojoj potezi koje sudionik može povući u nekom stanju ovise o njegovu identitetu. Većina igara na ploči (npr. šah, kineski šah, go, gomoku) pristrane su igre jer sudionici mogu pomicati samo vlastite figure.

Normalne igre i igre misère

U kombinatornim igrama pobjednik je obično sudionik koji je posljednji povukao potez prije završetka igre. To se naziva normalna igra (normal game). Nasuprot tome stoji igra misère (misère game), u kojoj sudionik koji posljednji povuče potez prije završetka igre gubi.

I nepristrane i pristrane kombinatorne igre mogu biti normalne igre ili igre misère.

Game theory is a branch of economics that mainly studies the behavior of individuals in competitive or adversarial situations under given rules. Game theory is concerned with the expected and actual behavior of the participants in a game and studies their optimal strategies.

Put simply, game theory mainly studies the following: how the several players taking part in a game choose their strategies.

Basic concepts

This section briefly introduces some common concepts of game theory.

Cooperative and noncooperative games

A cooperative game is a game in which the participants can form coalitions and cooperate with each other. In such games, noncooperative behavior of an individual is usually punished by some external mechanism. In contrast, no such mechanism exists in a noncooperative game, so the participants either cannot form coalitions at all or can only sustain cooperation through credible threats.

Compared with cooperative games, noncooperative games have been studied more systematically and are better understood. All games discussed in this article are noncooperative games.

Symmetric and asymmetric games

In a symmetric game, different participants receive the same payoff for the same action; that is, the payoff depends only on the action itself and not on the identity of the one who takes it. A game that does not satisfy this condition is called an asymmetric game.

Zero-sum and non-zero-sum games

Main page: Zero-sum games

A zero-sum game is a game in which the total payoff of all participants is always zero, no matter how the parties act. The zero-sum games usually discussed involve two participants; then one side's gain is necessarily the other side's loss. In contrast, a non-zero-sum game allows several parties to win or lose together; this includes positive-sum games, negative-sum games, and so on.

Simultaneous and sequential games

In a simultaneous game, all participants make their decisions at the same time, without knowing the others' choices. Rock-paper-scissors is a typical simultaneous game. Such games are often represented by a payoff matrix and usually do not involve the notion of time.

The opposite is a sequential game, in which the participants act one after another. Note that a player who moves later must be able to observe at least part of the actions of the players who moved earlier; otherwise the order of moves would be meaningless. Sequential games are usually described by a game tree.

Perfect and imperfect information games

Perfect information means that whenever a participant makes a decision, they know everything that has happened so far, including the initial state of the game. For example, chess and Go are games of perfect information, whereas mahjong and poker are games of imperfect information, since the players cannot see the other players' hands. Perfect information is usually used to describe sequential games; since in a simultaneous game the players cannot know the action the opponent is about to take, simultaneous games are usually not considered games of perfect information.

Complete and incomplete information games

Complete information means that all participants fully know the structure of the game itself (including the decisions available to each party and the final payoffs), and that this information is common knowledge. The opposite is a game of incomplete information, in which some elements of the game (such as the opponent's available decisions or payoff function) are unknown to the participants.

It is worth noting that "complete information" and "perfect information" are two independent concepts, neither of which implies the other. For example, mahjong is a game of complete but imperfect information, since its rules and payoffs are public but the tiles are not visible; on the other hand, some games with hidden goals but fully public actions are games of perfect but incomplete information.

Combinatorial game theory

In algorithmic competitions, the most common type of game is the combinatorial game. The term usually refers to games that are hard to solve because of their huge number of states. Precisely because general combinatorial games are quite complex, combinatorial game theory mainly focuses on the following type: two-player games with alternating moves, perfect information, and no element of chance. Chess, Go, and the like are typical combinatorial games.

Impartial combinatorial games

Main page: Impartial combinatorial games

An impartial game is a combinatorial game satisfying the following conditions:

  • in any given state, all participants have exactly the same moves available, which depend only on the current state and not on the identity of the player;
  • the same state of the game cannot be reached more than once, the game ends when a participant cannot move, and the game always ends after finitely many moves and never in a draw.

Impartial games are always symmetric games.

Partizan combinatorial games

Main page: Partizan combinatorial games

The concept opposite to an impartial game is a partizan game, in which the moves a participant can make in a given state depend on their identity. Most board games (such as chess, Chinese chess, Go, and Gomoku) are partizan games, since the participants can only move their own pieces.

Normal and misère games

In combinatorial games, the winner is usually the participant who makes the last move before the game ends. This is called a normal game. The opposite is a misère game, in which the participant who makes the last move before the game ends is the loser.

Both impartial and partizan combinatorial games can be normal or misère games.
