title: Introduction to the math section
---

This chapter introduces the mathematical knowledge that may be needed in OI. Computer science is closely related to mathematics, and algorithmic competitions put special emphasis on discrete, concrete mathematics represented by number theory, combinatorics, probability and expectation, and polynomials: it focuses on program implementation and real problems and can appear in almost any category of problems.

In fact, the algorithms, data structures, automata, etc. that appear in algorithmic competitions can also be considered part of mathematics, but for better understanding this content is split into specific chapters (such as the one on strings) together with its application background. This chapter mainly introduces some basic mathematical concepts, algebra, number theory, game theory, probability theory, and other knowledge. Applying this knowledge can help optimize other algorithms and data structures, for example:

-   using polynomials to optimize knapsack problems in convolution form in order to solve some string problems;
-   many recurrence-type problems have combinatorics or probability and expectation as their background, and they can be further derived and solved with generating functions, while the efficiency of the algorithm can be improved with FFT-based divide-and-conquer optimization;
-   using congruences and cycles to analyze the possible weight sums of non-simple paths in a graph modulo some number, and maintaining them with a weighted union–find structure.

In addition, high-school mathematics is the foundation of mathematics for informatics competitions; mastering the basic concepts and properties from the textbooks helps a lot in learning the content of this chapter.
