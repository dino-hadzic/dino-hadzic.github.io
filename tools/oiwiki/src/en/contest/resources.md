This page lists some resources related to algorithm competitions: online judges, problem collections, books, tools and more.

## Online judges

Online judges (Online Judging System, OJ for short) are generally used for practice by solving problems, participating in and organizing contests, and exchanging ideas among users.

### Chinese

-   [51Nod](https://www.51nod.com/): many math and puzzle problems worth trying.
-   [Comet OJ](https://www.cometoj.com): started in 2018, aiming to give algorithm enthusiasts a platform for competing, practicing and exchanging ideas; frequently holds high-quality contests with original problems and has a rich problem set.
-   [HDU Online Judge](http://acm.hdu.edu.cn/): started in 2005, the online judge of Hangzhou Dianzi University, with problems from multi-university training contests.
-   [HydroOJ](https://hydro.ac/): started in 2021, the official site of the open-source project [Hydro](https://hydro.js.org/). Users can create their own [domains](https://hydro.ac/discuss/6087cc44e098b0cd7dde1a0c), in which the problem set, contests, discussions and other features of the main site are available.
-   [Judge Duck Online](https://duck.ac/): based on the open-source project [JudgeDuck](https://github.com/JudgeDuck) developed by [松松松](https://github.com/wangyisong1996); it measures the running time of judged programs to the microsecond. (Few problems.)
-   [LibreOJ](https://loj.ac/): started in 2017. Based on the open-source project [Lyrio](https://github.com/lyrio-dev/lyrio); "Libre" means free. All test data of the problems and all submitted code are open to every user. Currently maintained by [Menci](https://github.com/Menci).
-   [CDOJ](https://cdoj.site/d/lutece/): the online judge of the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, started in 2012.
-   [Luogu](https://www.luogu.com.cn/): started in 2013, has a large community and fairly complete collections of past problems and exercises from all kinds of OI contests. Offers paid educational services.
-   [Nowcoder (牛客网)](https://www.nowcoder.com/): started in 2014, provides technical interview preparation, community exchange, corporate recruiting and other services.
-   [OpenJudge](http://openjudge.cn/): started in 2005, a group-oriented judging platform developed by the POJ team.
-   [POJ](http://poj.org/): the online judge of Peking University, started in 2003, one of the OJs with the longest history in China. It contains many problems in English, both basic ones and good ones worth trying. POJ problems can also be submitted in the [Bailian](http://bailian.openjudge.cn/practice/) problem set.
-   [PTA (拼题 A)](https://pintia.cn/): started in 2016, a product of Hangzhou PAT Education Technology, a spin-off of Zhejiang University.
-   [QOJ](https://qoj.ac/): collects many problems from Chinese and foreign OI and ICPC contests; valuable for training.
-   [Universal Online Judge](https://uoj.ac/): started in 2014; "Universal" means general-purpose, [the project is open source](https://github.com/UniversalOJ/UOJ-System); the OJ of [VFK](https://github.com/vfleaking): many original contest problems and CCF/THU problems, of higher difficulty.
-   [Vijos](https://vijos.org/): started in 2005. The [server](https://github.com/vijos/vj4), the [judge](https://github.com/vijos/jd4) and other components are open source.
-   [WZOI](https://wzoi.cn): started in 2017, an [open-source](https://github.com/massimodong/wzoj) judging system maintained by Wenzhou High School in Zhejiang Province.
-   [ZOJ](https://zoj.pintia.cn/home): the online judge of Zhejiang University, started in 2001.

### Foreign

-   [AizuOJ](https://onlinejudge.u-aizu.ac.jp): the online judge of the University of Aizu in Japan, started in 2004. It contains problems from several Japanese high school and university programming contests and comes with introductory courses on programming, data structures and algorithms.
-   [AtCoder](https://atcoder.jp/): a Japanese OJ; the Japanese version hosts contests of Japanese schools that are not shown in the English version. The problems are interesting and of high quality.
-   [CodeChef](https://codechef.com/): an Indian OJ that holds contests periodically. The system is based on SPOJ's Sphere Engine.
-   [Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/): a Russian OJ, started in 2010, founded by [Mike Mirzayanov](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mike-mirzayanov-31772a93/). It has several series of contests and supports individuals proposing problems and applying to organize contests. The problems are of high quality.
-   [CSES](https://cses.fi/problemset/) (Code Submission Evaluation System): a problem set organized by topic that [aims](https://cses.fi/problemset/text/2433) to be a comprehensive high-quality problem set; developed mainly by Antti Laaksonen, author of the [Competitive Programmer's Handbook](https://cses.fi/book/book.pdf), since 2013.
-   [CS Academy](https://csacademy.com/)
-   [DMOJ](https://dmoj.ca/): a Canadian open-source OJ with wide language support; the problem set is an archive of major contests, and it also holds its own contests regularly.
-   [HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/): has many contests.
-   [Kattis](https://open.kattis.com/): the problem set mainly contains ICPC-style problems; it rates users according to the problems they have solved and recommends problems suited to their level in four difficulty classes, trivial/easy/medium/hard, where problem difficulty is estimated with an [Elo-like rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating_system) system.
-   [LeetCode](https://leetcode.com/): a site for practicing programming interview problems, with a Chinese branch: [LeetCode China](https://leetcode.cn).
-   [Light OJ](https://lightoj.com)
-   [Open Trains](https://opentrains.opencup.org/): the training platform of the Russian Open Cup, built on the open-source system [ejudge](https://ejudge.ru/), supports virtual contests; the problem set contains Open Cup problems of past years and problems from the Petrozavodsk camps.
-   [SPOJ](http://www.spoj.com): started in 2003; its backend system [Sphere Engine](https://sphere-engine.com/) was commercialized in 2008; supports liking and tagging problems.
-   [TopCoder](https://www.topcoder.com/): started in 2001; its [competitive programming community](https://www.topcoder.com/community/competitive-programming/) has many contests; its main business is now technical crowdsourcing.
-   [TimusOJ](http://acm.timus.ru/): started in 2000, developed by Ural Federal University; it has the largest online judge problem set in Russia, with problems mainly from Ural Federal University contests, the Ural Championship, the ICPC Ural regional and the Petrozavodsk camps.
-   Online Judge (formerly [UVaOJ](https://uva.onlinejudge.org/)): started in 1995, the earliest internationally famous OJ, founded by Professor Miguel Ángel Revilla of the University of Valladolid (UVa) in Spain. Because [Professor Revilla sadly passed away in 2018](https://www.elnortedecastilla.es/valladolid/muere-profesor-miguel-20180402225739-nt.html) and the University of Valladolid stopped maintaining it, UVaOJ was renamed Online Judge in July 2019. The current maintainers of the platform [are building a new judging platform on GitHub](https://github.com/TheOnlineJudge/ojudge).
-   [Yandex](https://contest.yandex.ru/): archives the All-Russian Olympiad in Informatics of recent years.

## Books

This list gives the authors of the books, but not the translators. Since no two books share a title and they are easy to find, ISBNs are not given.

-   The Rujia Liu (刘汝佳) series
    -   "算法竞赛入门经典" (Beginning Algorithm Contests; the "purple book")
        -   [Companion resource repository for the 1st edition (mirror)](https://github.com/sukhoeing/aoapc-book/)
        -   [Companion resource repository for the 2nd edition](https://github.com/aoapc-book/aoapc-bac2nd)
        -   [Selected exercise solutions for the 2nd edition](https://github.com/sukhoeing/aoapc-bac2nd-keys)
    -   "算法竞赛入门经典 - 训练指南" (Beginning Algorithm Contests – Training Guide; the "white/blue book") – co-authored with 陈锋
    -   "算法艺术与信息学竞赛" (The Art of Algorithms and Informatics Competitions; the "blue/black book")
-   "算法竞赛进阶指南" (Advanced Guide to Algorithm Contests) – 李煜东
    -   [Companion resource repository](https://github.com/lydrainbowcat/tedukuri)
-   "啊哈算法" (Aha! Algorithms) – 纪磊

-   The CCF "Computer Programming for Secondary School Students" series
    -   "CCF 中学生计算机程序设计 - 入门篇" (introductory volume) – 陈颖, 邱桂香, 朱全民

    -   "CCF 中学生计算机程序设计 - 基础篇" (basic volume) – 江涛, 宋新波, 朱全民
    -   "CCF 中学生计算机程序设计 - 提高篇" (advanced volume) – 徐先友, 朱全民

-   The "深入浅出" (Simply Explained) series
    -   "深入浅出程序设计竞赛 - 基础篇" (Programming Contests Simply Explained – basic volume) – the Luogu online school teaching team
-   The "一本通" (All-in-One) series
    -   "信息学奥赛一本通" (Informatics Olympiad All-in-One) – 董永建
    -   "信息学奥赛一本通 - 提高篇" (advanced volume) – 黄新军, 董永建
        -   [Selective reading recommended.](https://www.zhihu.com/question/292926937)
    -   "信息学奥赛一本通 - 高手训练" (expert training) – 黄新军, 董永建
-   Other textbooks written by well-known Chinese OI coaches
    -   "信息学奥赛课课通" (Informatics Olympiad Lesson by Lesson) – 林厚从
    -   "聪明人的游戏：信息学探秘 - 提高篇" (A Game for Clever People: Exploring Informatics – advanced volume) – 江涛, 陈茂贤
    -   "计算概论：C++ 编程与信息学竞赛入门" (Introduction to Computing: C++ Programming and Informatics Competitions) – 金靖
    -   "算法竞赛宝典" (Treasury of Algorithm Contests) – 张新华
-   The "ACM International Collegiate Programming Contest" series
    -   "ACM 国际大学生程序设计竞赛系列 知识与入门" (Knowledge and Introduction) – 俞勇
    -   "ACM 国际大学生程序设计竞赛系列 算法与实现" (Algorithms and Implementation) – 俞勇
    -   "ACM 国际大学生程序设计竞赛系列 题目与解读" (Problems and Analysis) – 俞勇
-   "算法竞赛入门到进阶" (Algorithm Contests from Beginner to Advanced) – 罗勇军, 郭卫斌
-   "算法导论" (Introduction to Algorithms), 3rd edition – Thomas H. Cormen/Charles E. Leiserson/Ronald L. Rivest/Clifford Stein  
    The "black book", a classic university textbook. Original English title: *Introduction to Algorithms*
    -   [Solutions (English)](https://github.com/walkccc/CLRS)
-   "具体数学" (Concrete Mathematics), 2nd edition – Ronald L. Graham/Donald E. Knuth/Oren Patashnik  
    Original English title: *Concrete Mathematics*
-   "组合数学" (Combinatorics), 5th edition – Richard A. Brualdi  
    Original English title: *Introductory Combinatorics*
-   "挑战程序设计竞赛" (Programming Contest Challenge Book), the whole series – Takuya Akiba, Yoichi Iwata, Masatoshi Kitagawa
    Easy to understand.
-   "算法概论" (Algorithms) – Sanjoy Dasgupta/Christos Papadimitriou/Umesh Vazirani
    -   Concise and well structured, but with less content.
-   [Legend-K's notes on data structures and algorithms](http://web.archive.org/web/20180826111306/http://www.legend-k.com/Algorithm/Algorithm.pdf)
-   [acm-cheat-sheet](https://github.com/soulmachine/acm-cheat-sheet)
-   [Competitive Programmer's Handbook](https://cses.fi/book/book.pdf) – Antti Laaksonen
    -   The author spent three years of his own time completing it. Aimed at algorithm competitions, it covers a wide range of material with a good balance of detail.
-   ["Programming Challenges: The Programming Contest Training Manual"](http://acm.cs.buap.mx/downloads/Programming_Challenges.pdf) – Steven S. Skiena/Miguel A. Revilla
    -   Written by two professors of the University of Valladolid in Spain.
    -   Read the [translated online electronic edition](http://www.tup.com.cn/upload/books/yz/030502-01.pdf)
    -   Buy the [printed edition](http://www.tup.tsinghua.edu.cn/booksCenter/book_03050201.html)
-   "C++，挑战编程——程序设计竞赛进阶训练指南" (C++, Programming Challenges – Advanced Training Guide for Programming Contests) – 邱秋
    -   [Introduction page on the author's blog](https://blog.csdn.net/metaphysis/article/details/90288252)
-   ["数据结构（C++ 语言版 第 3 版）" (Data Structures, C++ edition, 3rd edition) – 邓俊辉](https://dsa.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/~deng/ds/dsacpp/index.htm)
    -   Recommended to be used together with the companion course, slides and exercise solutions.
-   "计算几何：算法与应用" (Computational Geometry: Algorithms and Applications) – by Berg, M. D., translated by 邓俊辉  
    Original English title: *Computational Geometry: Algorithms and Applications*
-   ["Handbook of Data Structures and Applications, 2nd Edition"](https://www.routledge.com/Handbook-of-Data-Structures-and-Applications/Mehta-Sahni/p/book/9780367572006)
    -   Co-authored by many famous professors such as Sartaj Sahni, Hanan Samet and Weiss; it is extensive and recommended for data structure enthusiasts who already have some background.
-   [The "Algorithms Illuminated" series](https://www.algorithmsilluminated.org/)
    -   A textbook for beginners who already know a programming language; recommended to be used together with the companion course
    -   "Algorithms Illuminated, Part 1: The Basics" – Tim Roughgarden
    -   "算法详解，卷 1：算法基础" (Chinese translation of Part 1) – translated by 徐波
    -   "Algorithms Illuminated, Part 2: Graph Algorithms and Data Structures" – Tim Roughgarden
    -   "算法详解，卷 2：图算法和数据结构" (Chinese translation of Part 2) – translated by 徐波
    -   "Algorithms Illuminated, Part 3: Greedy Algorithms and Dynamic Programming" – Tim Roughgarden
    -   "Algorithms Illuminated, Part 4: Algorithms for NP-Hard Problems" – Tim Roughgarden

## Tools

-   ["100 gdb tips"](https://github.com/hellogcc/100-gdb-tips)
-   [Algorithm Visualizer](http://algorithm-visualizer.org)
-   [cppreference](https://en.cppreference.com/w/): a comprehensive online reference for the C and C++ languages and their standard libraries
-   [Compiler Explorer](https://godbolt.org): view online the assembly corresponding to a compiled block of code, with a choice of different compilers
-   [C++ Insights](https://cppinsights.io/): see your C++ source code through the eyes of the compiler
-   [Inverse Symbolic Calculator](http://wayback.cecm.sfu.ca/projects/ISC/ISCmain.html): reverse lookup of an expression from a real number, useful for identifying constants
-   [$\rm\LaTeX$ handwritten symbol recognition](http://detexify.kirelabs.org/classify.html)
-   [$\rm\LaTeX$ math formula reference](http://www.mohu.org/info/symbols/symbols.htm)
-   [Mathpix](https://mathpix.com/): screenshot to $\rm\LaTeX{}$
-   [OEIS](https://oeis.org): search engine for integer sequences
-   [Python Tutor](https://pythontutor.com/): visualization of code execution
-   [Quick C++ Benchmark](https://quick-bench.com/): compare the running speed of two or more functions online
-   [Try It Online](https://tio.run): run code in 600+ languages online, supports interactive I/O, 60 s timeout, code can be shared
-   [Graph editor](https://csacademy.com/app/graph_editor/) and [GraphViz](http://www.graphviz.org/)
-   [uDebug](https://www.udebug.com): debugging assistance for problems from some OJs
-   [USF](https://www.cs.usfca.edu/~galles/visualization/) and [VisuAlgo](https://visualgo.net/en): algorithm visualization
-   [Wandbox](https://wandbox.org/): run code online, supports 30+ languages, code can be shared, supports different compiler versions
-   [Wolfram Alpha](https://www.wolframalpha.com/): can compute answers to questions on many topics, including mathematics, science and technology, society and culture…
